Definition / general

  * Very rare tumor of perianal skin, representing 0.2% of anorectal tumors and less than 0.5% of all basal cell carcinomas at all sites ([Am J Dermatopathol 1996;18:371](8879301), [Rev Gastroenterol Mex 2013;78:52](23375494)) 
  * Nodular subtype most common in this location 

Essential features

  * Rare perianal malignancy identical to basal cell carcinoma of skin 

Sites

  * Perianal skin (not anus proper) 

Pathophysiology

  * Appears to behave similarly to basal cell carcinoma at other locations 

Clinical features

  * Usually 1 - 2 cm but can be 10 cm or larger; recurrence uncommon ([Br J Surg 1981;68:856](7317766)) 
  * Patients may also have basal cell carcinomas elsewhere ([Dis Colon Rectum 1999;42:1200](10496562)) 

Diagnosis

  * Biopsy 

Case reports

  * 69 year old man with perianal basal cell carcinoma ([Indian J Dermatol 2010;55:178](20606890)) 
  * 88 year old woman with polypoid basal cell carcinoma on the perianal region ([J Dermatol 2004;31:51](14739505)) 

Treatment

  * Wide local excision with negative margins

Gross description

  * Indurated, ulcerated lesion with irregular borders 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Resembles basal cell carcinoma at other sites (see [Skin nonmelanocytic](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/skintumornonmelanocyticbcc.html) chapter) 
  * Retraction artifact helps distinguish from basaloid squamous cell carcinoma

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/anusbasalcellcarcinomagonzalez01.jpg

Which of the following is true about perianal basal cell carcinoma?   
  
  1. It accounts for 10% of basal cell carcinomas overall 
  2. It can also arise within the anus 
  3. It histologically resembles basal cell carcinoma at other sites 
  4. It is negative for BerEP4 and BCL2 

Practice answer #1

**C**. It histologically resembles basal cell carcinoma at other sites
