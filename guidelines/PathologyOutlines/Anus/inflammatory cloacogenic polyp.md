Definition / general

  * Benign polyp with colorectal, squamous and transitional epithelium with thickened and prolapsed muscularis mucosa associated with rectal prolapse 

Essential features

  * Benign polyp with colorectal, squamous and transitional epithelium and prolapsed muscularis mucosae 
  * Thought to be associated with mucosal prolapse or chronic inflammatory diseases 
  * Located at anorectal transition zone 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [K62.0](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K55-K64/K62-/K62.0) \- anal polyp 
  * ICD-11: [DB71](https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en#/http%3a%2f%2fid.who.int%2ficd%2fentity%2f2038426249) \- inflammatory anal polyp 

Epidemiology

  * Wide age range from pediatric to adult population 

Sites

  * Anorectal transition zone ([Eur J Gastroenterol Hepatol 2000;12:247](10741944)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Rectal mucosal prolapse due to repeated mucosal ischemia and regeneration are thought to contribute to the development ([Histopathology 2008;53:91](18484980)) 

Clinical features

  * Symptoms range from asymptomatic to rectal bleeding to constipation, tenesmus, anal swelling or anal itching ([Cureus 2022;14:e22014](35282506)) 
  * On endoscopy, these polyps can mimic hemorrhoids, solitary rectal ulcer, villous adenoma or anorectal carcinoma ([Cureus 2022;14:e22014](35282506)) 
  * Associated with solitary rectal ulcer syndrome, mucosal prolapse or chronic inflammatory conditions such as Crohn's disease and colorectal tumors ([Cureus 2022;14:e22014](35282506), [Eur J Gastroenterol Hepatol 2000;12:247](10741944)) 

Diagnosis

  * Colonoscopy or sigmoidoscopy typically identifies a sessile or pedunculated polyp ([Eur J Gastroenterol Hepatol 2000;12:247](10741944)) 

Laboratory

  * Patients sometimes present with a positive fecal immunochemical test (e.g., Cologuard) 

Prognostic factors

  * Benign entity with no increased risk for malignancy ([Eur J Gastroenterol Hepatol 2000;12:247](10741944)) 

Case reports

  * 15 year old boy with Marshall-Smith syndrome and perianal subcutaneous mass ([Pediatr Pathol 1993;13:409](8372025)) 
  * 34 year old woman with anal intraepithelial neoplasia in an inflammatory cloacogenic polyp ([J Clin Pathol 1999;52:393](10560365)) 
  * 38 year old woman and 41 year old woman with squamous cell carcinoma in situ arising in inflammatory cloacogenic polyp ([Histopathology 2008;53:91](18484980)) 

Treatment

  * Endoscopic polypectomy or mucosal resection ([Am J Gastroenterol 1994;89:438](8122661)) 
  * High fiber diet ([Cureus 2022;14:e22014](35282506))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Tubulovillous architecture with elongated crypts stretching into the submucosa 
  * Areas of stratified squamous, colorectal or transitional epithelium that are often eroded and contain mixed inflammation and granulation tissue ([Hum Pathol 1987;18:1120](3500106), [Am J Surg Pathol 1981;5:761](7337162)) 
  * Abundant regenerative epithelial changes can be seen 
  * Thickened muscularis mucosa with extension into the lamina propria 
  * Fibromuscular obliteration of the lamina propria can be seen

Practice question #2

What is the preferred treatment for an inflammatory cloacogenic polyp?   
  
  1. Chemotherapy 
  2. Colectomy 
  3. Endoscopic / surgical resection 
  4. Fecal immunohistochemical test 
  5. Routine endoscopic surveillance 

Practice answer #2

**C**. Endoscopic / surgical resection is the preferred treatment for an inflammatory cloacogenic polyp. Routine endoscopic surveillance is necessary after resection because of the risk of recurrence but it is not the treatment modality.
