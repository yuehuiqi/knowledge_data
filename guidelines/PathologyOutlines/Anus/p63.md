Definition / general

  * Transcription factor that is a member of the _p53_ gene family 
  * Master regulator of epidermal keratinocyte proliferation and embryonic epidermal growth 

Essential features

  * Encoded by the gene _TP63_ located on 3q27-29 ([Mol Cell 1998;2:305](9774969), [Development 2012;139:772](22274697)) 
  * Has more than 6 isoforms 
  * Marker for myoepithelial cells 

Terminology

  * Transcription factor 
  * Known as the guardian of reproduction ([Development 2012;139:772](22274697)) 
  * Formerly known as keratinocyte transcription factor (KET) in 1997 ([Oncogene 1997;15:1363](9315105)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Regulates embryonic stem cells development and commitment to epithelial tissue ([Genes Dev 2006;20:3185](17114587)) 
  * Functions through epigenetic regulation of Satb1, Brg1, Cbx4 and tissue specific chromatin factors ([Development 2014;141:101](24346698), [J Cell Biol 2011;194:825](21930775), [J Cell Biol 2016;212:77](26711500)) 
  * Limb malformation, ectodermal dysplasia and orofacial clefting are associated with _p63_ germline mutations ([Am J Med Genet A 2006;140:1419](16688749)) 
  * Multiple isoforms that regulate epithelial growth and development were generated from alternative splicing, including 
    * TA isoform: the full length and most predominant isoform ([BMC Genomics 2015;16:584](26251276)) 
      * Has 3 specific exons 
      * Plays a role in protein interactions through a sterile alpha motif (SAM) domain 
      * Includes a transactivation domain 
      * Acts as a transcription factor and a tumor suppressor gene 
      * Activates _BAX_ , _p21_ and other _p53_ target genes 
    * ΔN isoform: also known as p40 ([Mol Cell Biol 2002;22:8601](12446779), [Development 2012;139:772](22274697)) 
      * Was considered an inactive transcription factor 
      * Inhibits TAP63 isoform 
      * Essential for epithelial development 
      * Lacks the transactivation domain TA1 
      * Shorter than TAP63 
    * (β) isoform: lacks exon 13, the transcription inhibitory (TID) and sterile alpha motif domains ([Nucleic Acids Res 2009;37:6092](19700772)) 
    * (γ) isoform: lacks the exons 11 - 14, mainly expressed in muscle cells and cancer-like stem cells ([Nucleic Acids Res 2009;37:6092](19700772), [Mol Cell 1998;2:305](9774969)) 
    * ΔΔN isoform: a recently generated isoform, distinct from the ΔN isoform as it lacks its first 26 amino acids in epidermal keratinocytes ([Nucleic Acids Res 2009;37:6092](19700772), [Hum Mol Genet 2008;17:1968](18364388)) 
    * (δ) isoform: lacks exon 13 
    * (ε) isoform: detected first though generation from a stop codon in exon 10 ([Nucleic Acids Res 2009;37:6092](19700772), [Hum Mol Genet 2008;17:1968](18364388))

Clinical features

  * Investigate the primary origin of malignant tumors 
  * In most cases, p63 can help exclude the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinomas in the prostate and breast; however, there are exceptions 
    * Case of prostatic adenocarcinoma in 65 year old man with diffuse, nonbasal p63 staining ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2013;137:1179](23991727)) 
    * Low level of p63 staining in tumor cells in 2 cases of metaplastic breast carcinoma ([Hamdan Medical Journal 2023;16:278](https://doi.org/10.4103/hmj.hmj_78_23)) 

Interpretation

  * Mainly nuclear 
  * Could demonstrate cytoplasmic expression in Z bands in skeletal muscle cells ([Mod Pathol 2011;24:1320](21623385)) 

Uses by pathologists

  * In lung: detects squamous cell carcinoma and differentiates it from adenocarcinomas ([J Cytol 2021;38:151](34703092)) 
  * In the kidneys: distinguishes urothelial carcinoma from renal cell carcinoma ([Hum Pathol 2014;45:1824](24993315)) 
  * In the prostate: distinguishes prostatic hyperplasia from well differentiated prostatic adenocarcinoma ([Am J Surg Pathol 2002;26:1161](12218572)) 
  * In the bladder: distinguishes poorly differentiated urothelial carcinoma from metastatic prostatic adenocarcinoma ([J Pathol Transl Med 2016;50:345](27498545)) 
  * Favors the diagnosis of sarcomatoid carcinoma over sarcomas in the bladder ([Mod Pathol 2005;18:1471](15976812)) 
  * In most cases, p63 can help exclude the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinomas in the prostate and breast; however, there are exceptions 
    * Case of prostatic adenocarcinoma in 65 year old man with diffuse, nonbasal p63 staining ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2013;137:1179](23991727)) 
    * Low level of p63 staining in tumor cells in 2 cases of metaplastic breast carcinoma ([Hamdan Medical Journal 2023;16:278](https://doi.org/10.4103/hmj.hmj_78_23)) 
  * Differentiates cutaneous sweat gland carcinoma (p63+) from metastatic breast adenocarcinoma to skin (p63-) ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2011;135:975](21809988)) 
  * Differentiates olfactory neuroblastoma (p63- / calretinin+) from other small round blue cell tumors of sinonasal tract ([Am J Surg Pathol 2011;35:1786](22020045)) 
  * Differentiates renal collecting duct carcinoma (p63- / PAX8+) from upper tract urothelial carcinoma ([Am J Surg Pathol 2011;35:757](21451364)) 
  * p63 expression can be helpful in identifying primary adnexal neoplasms versus other metastatic processes ([J Cutan Pathol 2007;34:474](17518775)) 

Prognostic factors

  * Loss of p63 expression correlates with poor prognosis in urothelial carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma of the esophagus and larynx ([Hum Pathol 2014;45:1824](24993315), [Oncol Rep 2006;15:323](16391849), [ORL J Otorhinolaryngol Relat Spec 2010;72:319](20847581)) 
  * p63 expression is associated with poorer prognosis for Merkel cell carcinoma ([Mod Pathol 2011;24:1451](21765392)) 
  * Identifies false lymphatic invasion (tumor cells surrounded by D2-40+ / p63+ ducts), which has a good prognosis ([Mod Pathol 2011;24:502](21317878)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Nuclear marker for myoepithelial cells in benign tumors and noninvasive malignancies

Positive staining - normal

  * Squamous epithelial cells ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Thymic epithelial cells ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Urothelium ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Basal cells of the respiratory tract ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Basal cells of prostate ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Basal cells of seminal vesicle ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Gynecologic tract: basal and parabasal cells of mature cervical, vaginal and vulval squamous epithelium; cervical reserve cells at transformation zone, immature metaplastic and atrophic cervical squamous epithelium ([J Pathol Transl Med 2015;49:450](26459408)) 
  * Myoepithelial cells in breast ([Am J Surg Pathol 2001;25:1054](11474290)) 
  * Myoepithelial cells in parotid, submandibular and sublingual glands ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829), [Clin Cancer Res 2002;8:494](11839669)) 

Positive staining - disease

  * In the skin: squamous cell carcinomas, basal cell carcinomas ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Primary cutaneous adnexal neoplasms (with the exception of primary mucinous carcinoma in most cases) 
  * Tumors of the head and neck: squamous cell carcinoma of the larynx / oral cavity, pleomorphic adenoma of the parotid gland, Warthin tumor ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Tumors of the mediastinum: thymoma (in 65%) ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829), [Clin Cancer Res 2002;8:494](11839669)) 
  * In the lung: identification of squamous cell carcinoma, mainly the specific isoform p40 ΔNp63 ([PLoS One 2010;5:e12209](20808915), [Mod Pathol 2012;25:405](22056955)) 
  * Tumors of the female genital tract: squamous cell carcinoma of the vagina / vulva / cervix, Brenner tumor ([Biomark Res 2021;9:7](33494829)) 
  * Sarcomatoid carcinoma of the breast ([Histopathology 2003;42:94](12493033)) 
  * Tumors of the digestive system: squamous cell carcinoma of the esophagus / anal canal 
  * Tumors of the urinary system: urothelial carcinoma ([Hum Pathol 2014;45:1824](24993315)) 
  * Mild expression in diffuse large B cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma and chronic lymphocytic leukemia ([Appl Immunohistochem Mol Morphol 2005;13:237](16082248), [Cancer Sci 2006;97:1050](16918993), [J Clin Pathol 2009;62:77](19103865)) 
  * ALK negative ALCLs can express p63, which is associated with _TP63_ rearrangements ([Hum Pathol 2017;64:19](28153507)) 

Negative staining

  * Anus: anal gland carcinoma ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2007;131:1304](17683193)) 
  * Breast: normal epithelium, stromal cells, myofibroblasts; may be reduced or occasionally absent in benign apocrine lesions, benign sclerosing lesions ([Am J Surg Pathol 2011;35:202](21263240), [Histopathology 2016;68:1030](26497122)) 
  * Malignant mixed Müllerian tumors, endometrioid carcinoma, pancreatic, colorectal and cholangiocellular carcinoma (10 - 25%) ([Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A 2013;110:8105](23620512), [Oncotarget 2017;8:22741](28009986)) 
  * Melanoma ([Am J Surg Pathol 2012;36:1216](22534723)) 
  * Mesothelioma 
  * Prostate: adenocarcinoma, nephrogenic adenoma, partial atrophy ([Am J Surg Pathol 2012;36:1216](22534723)) 
  * Most soft tissue tumors ([Am J Clin Pathol 2011;136:762](22031315)) 

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * Encoded by the gene _TP63_ located on 3q27-29 
  * Multidomain protein with more than 6 isoforms 
  * Isoforms are generated through alternative splicing at the 3 site 
  * Tetramer that has a DNA binding domain (DBD), an N terminal transcactivation domain (TA), a tetramerization domain (TD) and a transcription inhibitory domain following C terminal sterile alpha motif (SAM) ([Mol Cell Biol 2002;22:8601](12446779), [Mol Cell 1998;2:305](9774969))

Sample pathology report

  * Prostate, core needle biopsy, measuring 2 x 1 x 1 cm and tan-brown in color: 
    * Benign prostatic hyperplasia (see comment) 
    * Comment: Microscopic examination of the biopsy revealed the proliferation of prostatic glands lined by epithelial cells with no signs of atypia in the lining of epithelial cells. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated a strong nuclear expression of p63 in the basal cells of the hyperplastic glands. These features correlate with benign prostatic hyperplasia. 

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/stainsp63Elshimali05.jpg

Which of the statements below regarding p63 is true?   
  
  1. Has only 3 isoforms 
  2. Loss of p63 expression correlates with poor prognosis in urothelial carcinoma 
  3. p63 is absent in squamous epithelial cells 
  4. p63 is a marker for smooth muscle cells 

Practice answer #1

**B.** Loss of p63 expression correlates with poor prognosis in urothelial carcinoma. Answer A is incorrect because p63 has more than 6 isoforms. Answer C is incorrect because p63 is a marker for squamous epithelial cells. Answer D is incorrect because p63 is a marker for basal epithelial cells.
