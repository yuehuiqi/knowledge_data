Definition / general

  * Hypertrophic herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection is a rare presentation most often seen in immunocompromised patients, particularly those with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) 
  * Common histologic features include squamous hyperplasia and a dense lymphoplasmacytic inflammatory infiltrate 

Essential features

  * Hypertrophic epidermis / mucosal epithelium with dense, often perivascular lymphoplasmacytic inflammation 
  * Virally infected cells can be focal and not be sampled on a biopsy specimen 
  * Clinically, the lesions often have a tumoral or mass-like appearance 

Terminology

  * Hyperplastic herpes, vegetative herpes, atypical herpes, pseudotumoral herpes 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [A60.9](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/A00-B99/A50-A64/A60-/A60.9) \- anogenital herpesviral infection, unspecified 

Epidemiology

  * Most commonly occurs in immunocompromised adults, particularly those with HIV / acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) 
  * No strong sex or geographic predilection has been established 
  * Strong causal association with HSV and HIV related immunosuppression 
    * HSV2 infection is more common in the anogenital region and HSV1 elsewhere, although there are exceptions 
  * References: [Int J STD AIDS 2022;33:212](34726092), [IDCases 2022;28:e01502](35479108)

Sites

  * Most cases have been reported in the anogenital region but there are reports of this lesion appearing in other cutaneous and mucosal surfaces, such as the oral cavity, facial skin and extremities ([J Am Acad Dermatol 1989;20:1125](2666462), [Br J Dermatol 1995;133:475](8547009), [J Hand Surg Am 1997;22:650](9260621)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Thought to involve dysregulated type 2 T helper (Th2) cell responses triggering a robust cytokine environment in response to HSV infection 
  * This immune activation may promote epithelial proliferation 
  * Dermal dendritic cells may produce tumor necrosis factor (TNF), which directly stimulates keratinocyte growth 
  * References: [J Am Acad Dermatol 1992;27:943](1362209), [Int J Gynecol Pathol 2012;31:286](22498948), [J Drugs Dermatol 2003;2:198](12852374)

Etiology

  * Dysregulation of type 2 T helper cells in the setting of immunosuppression leads to an abnormal immune response to HSV infection 

Clinical features

  * Often mass forming and can bear a striking clinical resemblance to a neoplasm ([Ann Clin Lab Sci 2014;44:208](24795061)) 

Diagnosis

  * Often clinically challenging to diagnose unless the patient has a history of similar lesions; generous tissue sampling is recommended 

Laboratory

  * CD4 count and viral load in HIV infected patients is variable 
  * HSV polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is typically positive 
    * Often HSV type 2 but type 1 has been reported as well 
  * References: [Int J STD AIDS 2022;33:212](34726092), [Int J Infect Dis 2020;95:153](32311452), [IDCases 2019;15:e00512](30937283)

Prognostic factors

  * Recurrence is common 

Case reports

  * 34 year old HIV positive man with painful penile plaques ([Clin Exp Dermatol 2023;48:43](36631433)) 
  * 65 year old man on anti-cancer medications with right posterior shoulder lesions ([IDCases 2022;28:e01502](35479108)) 
  * 71 year old man on azathioprine presented with perianal lesions ([Cureus 2022 ;14:e30649](36439566)) 

Treatment

  * Lesions are difficult to treat 
  * Common initial treatments include 
    * Surgery 
    * Extended courses of antiviral therapy 
      * Resistance testing could be considered 
    * Immunomodulators, such as imiquimod, have shown very positive results thus far in treating these lesions 
  * References: [Oral Surg Oral Med Oral Pathol Oral Radiol Endod 2004;97:376](15024363), [Int J STD AIDS 2011;22:181](21515747), [ J STD AID. 2007;18:218](17362560), [Sex Transm Dis 2010;37:126](19858782)

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Marked squamous hyperplasia with or without surface papillations 
  * Dense lymphoplasmacytic inflammation, typically around vessels and adnexal structures 
  * HSV infected areas are typically focal or multifocal and associated with ulceration 
    * HSV infected cells may be absent on small biopsies or incomplete excisions and may only be identified on resection specimens 
  * References: [J Low Genit Tract Dis 2007;11:173](17596763), [Int J Gynecol Pathol 2012;31:38](22123722)

Positive stains

Sample pathology report

  * Perianal skin, biopsy: 
    * Skin with hypertrophic herpes virus infection (see comment) 
    * Viral inclusions identified on routine stain or viral inclusions confirmed with HSV immunohistochemical stain 
    * Comment: Hypertrophic herpes virus infection (also known as hyperplastic herpes, atypical herpes or vegetative herpes) is a pattern of infection that is typically associated with an immunocompromised state. Clinical history of HIV is noted. This lesion can be difficult to treat but immunomodulator agents, such as imiquimod, have shown promise. Other options, such as surgical excision or extended courses of antiviral therapy, may be considered. Some of these lesions have been reported to demonstrate resistance to acyclovir and thus, if that treatment modality is preferred, resistance testing may be helpful. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/anushypertrophicherpesPr1Saulino1.jpg

An HIV positive patient with a history of multiple other sexually transmitted infections presents with a 2 month history of an enlarging, now 5 cm scrotal lesion. A punch biopsy is performed and reveals notable squamous hyperplasia and lymphoplasmacytic inflammation but no evidence of carcinoma or viral cytopathic effect. HPV PCR taken of the lesion is negative. A subsequent surgical excision of the mass lesion is performed and shows the findings in the image above. What is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. Condyloma acuminatum 
  2. Hypertrophic herpes simplex virus infection 
  3. IgG4 related disease 
  4. Vegetative fungal infection 

Practice answer #1

**B.** Hypertrophic herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection. In immunocompromised individuals, especially those with HIV, HSV can present in a hypertrophic form as a slowly enlarging mass-like lesion. Biopsy may show squamous hyperplasia and dense lymphoplasmacytic inflammation but viral cytopathic effect may be absent or focal and only evident in larger or excisional specimens. The lesion is often mistaken clinically and histologically for a tumor. The negative HPV PCR helps rule out other viral etiologies and the ultimate diagnosis is frequently confirmed on surgical excision.   
  
Answer A is incorrect because condyloma acuminatum is caused by low risk HPV types (most often 6 and 11) and typically shows papillomatosis, koilocytosis and hyperkeratosis on histology. It does not usually present with a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate or marked squamous hyperplasia without HPV evidence and HPV PCR would be expected to be positive. Answer C is incorrect because IgG4 related disease typically presents with storiform fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis and an increased number of IgG4 positive plasma cells, none of which were reported in the biopsy. Additionally, IgG4 related disease does not usually involve the scrotal skin with squamous hyperplasia or present as a large solitary epidermal mass. Answer D is incorrect because fungal infections typically show granulomatous inflammation, necrosis and identifiable fungal organisms, often highlighted with special stains. These features were not present in this biopsy and the pattern of inflammation and epithelial hyperplasia is more characteristic of a viral (rather than fungal) etiology.

Practice question #2

A perianal mass lesion is identified and biopsied with the diagnosis of hypertrophic herpes simplex virus infection. What is most likely to be true about this patient?   
  
  1. Body mass index (BMI) > 25 
  2. History of HIV infection 
  3. Long history of tobacco use 
  4. Long term sun exposure 
  5. These lesions are currently poorly understood and have no known associated risk factors 

Practice answer #2

**B.** History of HIV infection. Hypertrophic herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection most often occurs in immunocompromised individuals, especially those with HIV / AIDS. The immune dysfunction in these patients leads to an exaggerated response to HSV infection, resulting in mass forming lesions that may clinically mimic tumors, particularly in the anogenital region.   
  
Answer A is incorrect because obesity is not linked to the pathogenesis of hypertrophic HSV. While a high BMI may influence general immune function, there is no evidence that it contributes to the development of these lesions. Answer C is incorrect because tobacco use is not a known risk factor for hypertrophic HSV infection. It is more commonly associated with malignancies (particularly squamous cell carcinoma) but has no established connection to this viral condition. Answer D is incorrect because sun exposure is associated with ultraviolet (UV) induced skin damage and skin cancers, particularly in sun exposed areas. Hypertrophic HSV typically occurs in mucocutaneous or anogenital sites and has no association with sun exposure. Answer E is incorrect because although the exact pathophysiology is not fully defined, there is a well established association with immunosuppression, particularly in patients with HIV; therefore, known risk factors do exist.
