Definition / general

  * Lung carcinosarcoma is a high grade, rare, malignant non-small cell lung cancer that is made up of epithelial and mesenchymal cells ([Cureus 2022;14:e26901](35983399), [Clin Transl Oncol 2010;12:303](20462841)) 
  * It is poorly differentiated and has both carcinoma and sarcomatous features ([Cureus 2019;11:e5643](31700745)) 

Essential features

  * Lung carcinosarcoma is a high grade, rare, malignant non-small cell lung cancer ([Cureus 2022;14:e26901](35983399)) 
  * Poorly differentiated and has both carcinoma and sarcomatous features ([Cureus 2019;11:e5643](31700745)) 
  * More common in male smokers between 50 and 80 years old ([Clin Transl Oncol 2010;12:303](20462841)) 
  * Overall prognosis of carcinosarcoma is poor, with a 6 month survival rate of 27% ([Clin Transl Oncol 2010;12:303](20462841)) 
  * Best treatment is surgical resection of the entire tumor ([Cureus 2022;14:e26901](35983399)) 

Terminology

  * Lung carcinosarcoma is also known as pulmonary carcinosarcoma (PCS) and pulmonary sarcomatoid carcinoma (PSC) ([Cureus 2022;14:e26901](35983399)) 
  * According to the WHO classification, carcinosarcoma, together with pleomorphic carcinoma of the lung and pulmonary blastoma, is part of the sarcomatoid carcinoma group 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [C34.90](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/C00-D49/C30-C39/C34-/C34.90) \- malignant tumor of an unspecified area of bronchus or lung 

Epidemiology

  * Rare (carcinosarcoma represents < 1% of all primary pulmonary tumors) ([Cureus 2022;14:e26901](35983399)) 
  * More common in male smokers ([Clin Transl Oncol 2010;12:303](20462841)) 
  * Usually occurs between 50 and 80 years old ([Clin Transl Oncol 2010;12:303](20462841)) 

Sites

  * Solid mass in the peripheral lung ([Cureus 2019;11:e5643](31700745)) 
  * Polyp-like mass in the endobronchial tree ([Cureus 2019;11:e5643](31700745)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Lung carcinosarcomas are clonal tumors that form when a carcinoma undergoes sarcomatoid change ([J Thorac Oncol 2015;10:1243](26291008)) 
  * Mutations in carcinosarcoma: _TP53_ (very common), _KRAS_ (less common), _EGFR_ (very uncommon) ([J Thorac Oncol 2015;10:1243](26291008)) 

Etiology

  * Smoking ([Clin Transl Oncol 2010;12:303](20462841)) 
  * Genetic mutations ([J Thorac Oncol 2015;10:1243](26291008)) 

Clinical features

  * Patients can present with productive cough, hemoptysis and shortness of breath ([Respiration 1994;61:113](8008987), [Indian J Pathol Microbiol 2022;65:145](35074981), [Lung India 2014;31:164](24778484)) 

Diagnosis

  * Preliminary diagnosis of pulmonary carcinosarcoma is based on the morphology and immunohistochemistry from fine needle biopsy samples ([Clin Lung Cancer 2020;21:160](31455596)) 
  * Definitive diagnosis is possible after surgical resection, which shows both sarcomatous and carcinoma parts ([Clin Lung Cancer 2020;21:160](31455596)) 

Radiology description

  * Tumors are more often peripheral, as opposed to central ([Clin Lung Cancer 2020;21:160](31455596)) 
  * Lesions typically occur in the upper lobe ([Clin Lung Cancer 2020;21:160](31455596))

Prognostic factors

  * Overall prognosis of carcinosarcoma is poor ([Clin Transl Oncol 2010;12:303](20462841)) 
  * 6 month survival rate in lung carcinosarcoma is ~27% ([Clin Transl Oncol 2010;12:303](20462841)) 

Case reports

  * 60 year old woman with lung carcinosarcoma with a productive cough ([Respiration 1994;61:113](8008987)) 
  * 64 year old female smoker with lung carcinosarcoma with hemoptysis ([Indian J Pathol Microbiol 2022;65:145](35074981)) 
  * 71 year old woman with lung carcinosarcoma with a 13 cm lung mass ([Cureus 2019;11:e5643](31700745)) 
  * 90 year old man with pulmonary carcinosarcoma with productive cough and shortness of breath ([Lung India 2014;31:164](24778484)) 

Treatment

  * Main treatment for lung carcinosarcoma is surgical removal of the tumor ([Cureus 2022;14:e26901](35983399)) 
  * Early surgical resection can be combined with adjuvant therapy with platinum based combination chemotherapy and radiotherapy ([Cureus 2022;14:e26901](35983399)) 

Gross description

  * Large, white and yellow mass with a well defined border, variable appearance and a textured cut surface ([Lung Cancer (Auckl) 2019;10:131](31824199))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * On microscopic examination, there will be sarcomatous and carcinomatous elements present 
  * Carcinoma component can include small cell carcinoma or adenocarcinoma 
  * Sarcomatous component has a spindle cell morphology 
  * Reference: [Ann Transl Med 2014;2:41](25333016)

Sample pathology report

  * Lung, right upper lobe, lobectomy: 
    * Lung carcinosarcoma, 7.5 cm (see comment) 
    * Margins free of tumor 
    * Uninvolved lung with emphysema 
    * Comment: The resected lesion is a 7.5 cm x 4.5 cm x 2.0 cm tan-white tumor that is not well circumscribed. On microscopic examination, H&E stain of the specimen shows a malignant mixed tumor that has both carcinoma and sarcomatous components. The carcinoma component is an adenocarcinoma with gland-like structures. The sarcomatous component has a spindle cell morphology. The gland-like structures that make up the adenocarcinoma component stained positive for CAM 5.2, TTF1 and Napsin A. The spindle cell component did not stain with CAM 5.2, TTF1 or Napsin A. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/lungcarcinosarcomahusain01.jpg

A 55 year old man with a 50 pack per year smoking history presents to the emergency department with hemoptysis that has progressed over a 2 week period. CT scan shows a 6.5 cm x 4.5 cm x 2.5 cm mass in the lung right upper lobe. A right upper lobectomy is performed to remove the lesion. Histologic examination with H&E stain (see image above) shows a mixed malignant tumor that has 2 components: one of the components has epithelial cells and the other is an undifferentiated spindle cell component. Keratin stains are positive where there are epithelial cells and negative in the spindle cell component. p40, WT1 and calretinin stains are negative. What is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. Biphasic mesothelioma 
  2. Pulmonary carcinosarcoma 
  3. Small cell lung carcinoma 
  4. Squamous cell carcinoma of the lung 

Practice answer #1

**B**. Pulmonary carcinosarcoma. A malignant tumor that has both carcinoma (adenocarcinoma) and spindle cell (sarcomatous) components and stains positive for keratin in the adenocarcinoma component describes a pulmonary carcinosarcoma. Answer C is incorrect because the described tumor does not have features of a small cell lung carcinoma, which would show small, oval shaped cells. Answer D is incorrect because the tumor described has a sarcomatous component and stains negative for p40, which excludes squamous cell carcinoma of the lung. Answer A is incorrect because biphasic mesothelioma is normally found in the pleura in patients with a history of asbestos exposure. This answer is also incorrect because cells from a mesothelioma would most likely stain positive for WT1 and calretinin, which excludes mesothelioma as a likely diagnosis.
