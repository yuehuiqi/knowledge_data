Definition / general

  * Composite pheochromocytoma is a tumor in which pheochromocytoma or paraganglioma is found in conjunction with a neoplasm of related lineages, such as ganglioneuroma, ganglioneuroblastoma, neuroblastoma, schwannoma or malignant nerve sheath tumor 

Essential features

  * Tumor composition consists of both pheochromocytoma / paraganglioma and a neuroblastic component, such as ganglioneuroma, ganglioneuroblastoma or neuroblastoma 
  * As per WHO criteria, the presence of ≥ 5% of histologically different recognizable components is required; some authors have suggested that ≥ 10% of the tumor should be a neuroblastic component to diagnose, since scattered ganglion cells may be present in pure pheochromocytoma or healthy adrenal gland ([Endocr Connect 2021;10:926](34261040)) 
  * Similar presentation to pheochromocytoma, associated with hypertension, abdominal pain, headaches 
  * Pheochromocytoma / paraganglioma component is positive for synaptophysin, chromogranin, neuron specific enolase (NSE); neuroblastic component is positive for neurofilament 
  * _HRAS_ and _BRAF_ are driver mutations; majority have _SDHB_ and _ATRX_ retention 

Terminology

  * Pheochromocytoma: a tumor originating from the neuroendocrine chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla; it is defined as paraganglioma within the adrenal gland 
  * Paraganglioma: a tumor originating from the neuroendocrine chromaffin cells outside of the adrenal gland (also known as extra-adrenal pheochromocytoma) 
  * Composite pheochromocytoma: pheochromocytoma in conjunction with neuroblastic tumor component 
  * Composite paraganglioma: paraganglioma in conjunction with neuroblastic tumor component 
  * Neuroblastic tumors: tumors composed of embryonic nerve cells including neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma

Epidemiology

  * Median age is 48 years (most common in the fourth and fifth decades) but has a wide range from early childhood to late adulthood ([Langenbecks Arch Surg 2022;407:517](33651160)) 
  * Slight female predominance (56 - 57% female) ([Langenbecks Arch Surg 2022;407:517](33651160), [Endocr Pathol 2022;33:90](35285002)) 
  * Composite pheochromocytoma accounts for ~3% of pheochromocytoma cases ([BMC Res Notes 2015;8:257](26104921)) 
  * Strong genetic connections with neurofibromatosis type I, von Hippel-Lindau syndrome, multiple endocrine neoplasia type IIA 

Sites

  * Composite pheochromocytoma: arises in the adrenal medulla with most frequent metastases to liver and bone 
  * Composite paraganglioma: often found in retroperitoneum, urinary bladder, cauda equina, mediastinum, head and neck ([Semin Diagn Pathol 2013;30:207](24144290)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Kimura et al. 2002 proposed a model of pathogenesis in which loss of neurofibromin causes loss of inhibition of neurotrophin, which increases proliferation of Schwann cells and induces a concomitant increase in proliferation of ganglionic and pheochromocytoma cells ([Mod Pathol 2002;15:183](11904334)) 
  * Single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) analysis of a composite pheochromocytoma with neuroblastoma demonstrates identical chromosomal deletions and duplications, suggesting distinct components are clonal ([Virchows Arch 2017;471:553](28864906)) 
  * Composite pheochromocytoma shares _HRAS_ driver mutations with ordinary pheochromocytoma but has unique _BRAF_ and lacks _SDH_ and _ATRX_ driver mutations ([Endocr Connect 2021;10:926](34261040)) 

Etiology

  * Neurofibromatosis type I is the most common underlying genetic syndrome, associated with ~14 - 19% of composite pheochromocytoma, more common than in pure pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma ([Virchows Arch 2023;483:267](36645463)) 
  * Composite pheochromocytomas have stronger associations with underlying genetic syndromes than composite paraganglioma ([Semin Diagn Pathol 2013;30:207](24144290)) 

Clinical features

  * ~75% of patients are symptomatic ([Endocr Pathol 2022;33:90](35285002)) 
  * Most common symptoms of composite pheochromocytoma are hypertension, abdominal pain, headaches, weight loss, diarrhea and palpitations or tachycardia ([Langenbecks Arch Surg 2022;407:517](33651160), [Virchows Arch 2023;483:267](36645463)) 
  * Composite pheochromocytoma / paraganglioma components ([Langenbecks Arch Surg 2022;407:517](33651160)) 
    * Ganglioneuroma (65%) 
    * Ganglioneuroblastoma (16%) 
    * Neuroblastoma (11%) 
    * Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor 
    * Neuroendocrine carcinoma 

Diagnosis

  * Patient presents with indicatory symptoms, is found to have elevated blood or urine catecholamines and undergoes magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or computed tomography (CT) imaging 
  * Alternatively, an adrenal mass is incidentally found when a patient undergoes abdominal imaging for other concerns 
  * (123)I metaiodobenzylguanidine (MIBG) scintigraphy is utilized to identify pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma 
  * Laparoscopic adrenalectomy is performed to remove the adrenal mass 
  * Diagnosis of composite tumor can only be made with histological analysis of the surgical specimen demonstrating neuroblastic components ([BMC Res Notes 2015;8:257](26104921)) 

Laboratory

  * Elevated catecholamine levels (epinephrine, norepinephrine or dopamine) lead to associated symptoms, which are indistinguishable between typical pheochromocytoma / paraganglioma and composite pheochromocytoma / paraganglioma ([Endocr Pathol 2022;33:90](35285002)) 
  * Watery diarrhea, hypokalemia and achlorhydria have been identified in some cases of composite pheochromocytoma with elevated vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) production ([Virchows Arch 2023;483:267](36645463), [Endocr Pathol 2022;33:90](35285002)) 

Radiology description

  * CT 
    * Composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma is generally indistinguishable from ordinary pheochromocytoma ([Endocr Pract 2014;20:864](24641930)) 
    * Composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma demonstrates enhancement on postcontrast venous phase imaging ([Endocr Pract 2014;20:864](24641930)) 
    * On unenhanced CT, 70% (7/10) of composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma were homogenous and 30% (3/10) were heterogeneous ([Clin Endocrinol (Oxf) 2021;95:47](33721367)) 
    * On enhanced CT, 22% (2/9) of composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma were homogenous and 78% (7/9) were heterogeneous ([Clin Endocrinol (Oxf) 2021;95:47](33721367)) 
    * 60% (6/10) of composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma were round in appearance and 40% (4/10) were multilobular ([Clin Endocrinol (Oxf) 2021;95:47](33721367)) 
  * MRI 
    * Composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma demonstrates hypointensity on T1 weighted images, hyperintensity on T2 weighted images and contrast enhancement ([Clin Endocrinol (Oxf) 2021;95:47](33721367)) 
  * Nuclear imaging 
    * (123/131)I metaiodobenzylguanidine (MIBG) accumulation in the adrenal gland indicates pheochromocytoma but does not differentiate between composite or noncomposite ([BMC Res Notes 2015;8:257](26104921))

Prognostic factors

  * Considered to have similar prognosis as pure pheochromocytomas or paragangliomas 
  * Metastases are usually driven by less differentiated neuroblastic components (such as neuroblastoma or ganglioneuroblastoma) but both pheochromocytoma and neuroblastic components can exist in the same metastases or drive separate metastases ([Semin Diagn Pathol 2013;30:207](24144290)) 

Case reports

  * 40 year old woman with novel _MAX_ gene mutation ([Front Endocrinol (Lausanne) 2020;11:234](32508744)) 
  * 46 year old man with composite pheochromocytoma and distinct appearance on imaging ([BJR Case Rep 2022;8:20220079](36211605)) 
  * 54 year old man with neurofibromatosis found to have composite pheochromocytoma and gastrointestinal stromal tumor ([North Clin Istanb 2021;8:629](35284800)) 
  * 64 year old woman with composite paraganglioma of the celiac trunk ([Front Surg 2022;9:824076](35273997)) 

Treatment

  * Laparoscopic adrenalectomy is the primary management with preoperative regimen of alpha adrenergic blockade ([Langenbecks Arch Surg 2022;407:517](33651160), [J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2014;99:1915](24893135)) 

Gross description

  * Mean tumor size is 4.9 cm (0.9 - 14 cm) ([Endocr Pathol 2022;33:90](35285002)) 
  * Composite pheochromocytoma consists of patchy, firm, tan-yellow areas containing red-tan hemorrhagic foci ([Endocr Pract 2010;16:291](19833581))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Pheochromocytoma 
    * Classical zellballen pattern of polygonal chromaffin cells organized in nests surrounded by sustentacular cells in alveolar, trabecular or solid arrangement 
    * Nuclei: pleomorphic appearance 
    * Cytoplasm: basophilic and granular 
    * See [Pheochromocytoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/adrenalpheochromocytoma.html)
  * Neuroblastic component: ganglioneuroma, ganglioneuroblastoma, neuroblastoma ([CAP: Protocol for the Examination of Specimens from Patients with Paraganglioma and Pheochromocytoma [Accessed 11 June 2025]](https://documents.cap.org/documents/New-Cancer-Protocols-March-2025/Paragang_Pheochrom_1.0.0.0.REL.CAPCP.pdf), [Endocr Pathol 2022;33:90](35285002)) 
  * Ganglioneuroma 
    * Composed of only ganglion cells within Schwannian stroma (no neuroblasts) 
    * Maturing: some ganglion cells are immature 
    * Mature: all ganglion cells are mature 
    * See [Ganglioneuroma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/adrenalganglioneuroma.html)
  * Ganglioneuroblastoma 
    * Intermixed: > 50% Schwannian stroma, integrated neuroblasts and Schwannian stroma with mature ganglion cells 
    * Nodular: distinct nodular region with clear juxtaposition between Schwannian stroma (stromal poor or stromal dominant) and neuroblast components 
    * See [Ganglioneuroblastoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/adrenalganglioneuroblastoma.html)
  * Neuroblastoma 
    * Lacks prominent Schwannian stroma 
    * Undifferentiated: no differentiating neuroblasts, no neuropil 
    * Poorly differentiated: < 5% differentiating neuroblasts, has neuropil 
    * Differentiating: > 5% differentiating neuroblasts, has neuropil 
    * See [Neuroblastoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/adrenalneuroblastoma.html)

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * _HRAS_
    * Mutation identified in 47% with ganglioneuroma, higher than in cases without a composite component ([Endocr Connect 2021;10:926](34261040)) 
  * _BRAF_
    * Mutation identified in 20% with ganglioneuroma, higher than in cases without a composite component 
    * Mutations occurred more often in codon 601 than 600, as would be expected in pure pheochromocytoma ([Endocr Connect 2021;10:926](34261040)) 
  * _MYCN_
    * Though amplification of this gene is a negative prognostic factor in pure neuroblastomas, it was not found in composite pheochromocytoma, suggesting neuroblastic components do not necessarily worsen prognosis ([Am J Clin Pathol 2009;132:69](19864235)) 
  * _SDHB_
    * Majority of reported composite cases have not been associated with germline _SDHB_ mutations and all have retained SDH expression ([Virchows Arch 2023;483:267](36645463), [Endocr Connect 2021;10:926](34261040)) 
  * _ATRX_
    * All cases with ganglioneuroma showed retained expression ([Endocr Connect 2021;10:926](34261040)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Left adrenal, laparoscopic adrenalectomy: 
    * Composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma, 4.3 cm (see synoptic report) 
    * Surgical margins, negative for tumor 

  * Notes: 
    * The College of American Pathologists Protocol for the Examination of Specimens from Patients with Paraganglioma and Pheochromocytoma should be used ([CAP: Protocol for the Examination of Specimens from Patients with Paraganglioma and Pheochromocytoma [Accessed 11 June 2025]](https://documents.cap.org/documents/New-Cancer-Protocols-March-2025/Paragang_Pheochrom_1.0.0.0.REL.CAPCP.pdf)) 
    * However, the AJCC TNM staging system does not apply to composite adrenal tumors 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/adrenalcompositepheochromocytomaZynger08.jpg

A 46 year old woman presents with hypertension, abdominal pain, watery diarrhea, hypokalemia, achlorhydria and palpitations. Laboratory analysis reveals elevated plasma metanephrines and vasoactive intestinal peptides. A CT scan is ordered and shows a right sided adrenal mass, prompting laparoscopic adrenalectomy. The gross specimen is composed of white-yellow areas with intermixed hemorrhagic foci. Histopathologic slides demonstrate regions of polygonal cells with strong and diffuse chromogranin A and synaptophysin staining, as well as regions with maturing ganglion cells with focal and weak synaptophysin staining (see image above showing the maturing ganglion cell component). Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. Composite paraganglioma with ganglioneuroma 
  2. Composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma 
  3. Paraganglioma 
  4. Pheochromocytoma 

Practice answer #1

**B.** Composite pheochromocytoma with ganglioneuroma. Composite pheochromocytoma is composed of pheochromocytoma intermixed with regions of ganglioneuroma, ganglioneuroblastoma or neuroblastoma. It presents with similar symptoms to pheochromocytoma but many cases have demonstrated watery diarrhea, hypokalemia and achlorhydria syndrome. In this case, the photomicrograph shows the ganglioneuroma component with maturing ganglion cells and Schwannian stroma. Answer A is incorrect because composite paraganglioma is not in the adrenal gland. Answer C is incorrect because paraganglioma only has 1 component and is not in the adrenal gland. Answer D is incorrect because pheochromocytoma does not have neuroblastic regions of maturing ganglion cells, which classifies the tumor as composite pheochromocytoma.
