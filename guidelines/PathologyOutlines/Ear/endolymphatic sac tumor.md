# Ear

## Middle ear and inner ear tumors - malignant

### Endolymphatic sac tumor

[Definition / general](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#definitiongeneral129834) | [Terminology](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#terminology129835) | [Radiology description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#radiologydescription129836) | [Case reports](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#casereports129837) | [Treatment](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#treatment129838) | [Microscopic (histologic) description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#microscopichistologicdescription129839) | [Microscopic (histologic) images](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#microscopichistologicimages129840) | [Cytology description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#cytologydescription129841) | [Positive stains](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#positivestains129842) | [Negative stains](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#negativestains129843) | [Electron microscopy description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#electronmicroscopydescription129844) | [Differential diagnosis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#differentialdiagnosis129845) | [Additional references](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/earendolymphaticsac.html#additionalreferences129846)

Definition / general

  * Uncommon, associated with von Hippel-Lindau syndrome (11% have these tumors) and female adnexal tumor of presumed wolffian origin ([Am J Surg Pathol 1994;18:1254](7977949)); also somatic mutations of VHL gene in non VHL patients ([Cancer Res 2000;60:5963](11085513)) 
  * Most arise within intraosseous portion of the endolymphatic duct / sac, with precursor lesions present in VHL patients ([Cancer Res 2005;65:10847](16322231)) 
  * Median age 30s, range of 11 - 71 years 
  * **Symptoms:** early sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus and episodic vertigo 

Terminology

  * The 2017 WHO now separates aggressive papillary tumor from endolymphatic sac tumor 
  * Also called papillary adenoma of endolymphatic sac / temporal bone, adenocarcinoma of temporal bone / mastoid, low grade adenocarcinoma of probable endolymphatic sac origin, Heffner tumor 

Radiology description

  * Tumor in posterior medial petrous ridge of temporal bone (site of endolymphatic sac) 

Case reports

  * 20 year old woman with endolymphatic sac tumor associated with von Hippel-Lindau disease ([Mod Path 2001;14:727](11455007)) 
  * 32 year old woman with papillary neoplasm of the endolymphatic sac associated with Hippel-Lindau disease ([J Clin Pathol 1994;47:959](7962613)) 
  * 42 year old woman with endolymphatic sac tumor associated with von Hippel-Lindau disease ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2003;127:1387](14521446)) 
  * 77 year old man with aspirated cyst ([Mod Pathol 2001;14:920](11557790)) 
  * Patient with mutation of von Hippel-Lindau tumor suppressor gene in a sporadic endolymphatic sac tumor ([Hum Pathol 2001;32:1272](11727270)) 
  * Patients with low grade papillary adenomatous tumors of the temporal bone ([Mod Pathol 1995;8:603](8532691)) 

Treatment

  * Radical surgery including mastoidectomy and temporal bone resection with possible loss of cranial nerves 
  * Tumor grows slowly with only one reported metastasis ([J Neurosurg Spine 2005;3:68](16122027)) but may recur with inadequate excision 
  * Is infiltrative, destructive and may cause death 
  * Bleeds profusely at surgery 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Simple papillary structures composed of single layer of columnar to cuboidal epithelium, often with distinct cell boundaries 
  * May have apparent myoepithelial layer that actually is flattened stroma; epithelial cells have pale-clear cytoplasm, uniform central or luminal nuclei 
  * Often granulation tissue reaction with small vascular spaces and mixed inflammatory infiltrate is present next to tumor cells 
  * Occasional thyroid like hypercellular areas with cystic glandular spaces containing colloid like material 
  * May have areas of recent hemorrhage with cholesterol clefts 
  * Minimal pleomorphism 
  * No / rare mitotic figures or necrosis 
  * Resembles choroid plexus papilloma

Cytology description

  * Rare epithelial cell clusters, some with papillary features 
  * Foamy macrophages 
  * Epithelial cells have eosinophilic and focally vacuolated cytoplasm, some with pigmented granules resembling hemosiderin, well defined cell borders, bland nuclei
