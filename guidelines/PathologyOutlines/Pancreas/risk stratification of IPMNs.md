Definition / general

  * Accurate risk stratification of pancreatic intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMNs) is critical for the early detection of pancreatic cancer, enabling the development of more personalized surveillance intervals and guiding surgical decisions 

Essential features

  * IPMNs are the most common precancerous pancreatic cystic lesions with the potential to progress to pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma 
  * IPMNs are frequently multifocal and multiclonal, with spatial and temporal genetic heterogeneity 
  * IPMN epithelial subtypes are associated with different prognoses: the most favorable is gastric, followed by intestinal type and then pancreatobiliary type, which shows the highest risk of neoplastic progression 
  * Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) enhances the classification and risk stratification of pancreatic cysts beyond magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and computed tomography (CT); its novel endomicroscopic imaging and ability to obtain cyst fluid aspirate enable detailed cytologic, biochemical and molecular analysis 

Terminology

  * Old terminology: intraductal papillary mucinous adenoma 

ICD coding

  * ICD-O 
    * [8453/0](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/96612/9789241548496_eng.pdf?sequence=1#page=73) \- intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (benign) 
    * [8453/0](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/96612/9789241548496_eng.pdf?sequence=1#page=73) \- intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm with low grade dysplasia 
    * [8453/2](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/96612/9789241548496_eng.pdf?sequence=1#page=73) \- intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm with high grade dysplasia, noninvasive (in situ) 
    * [8453/3](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/96612/9789241548496_eng.pdf?sequence=1#page=73) \- intraductal papillary mucinous carcinoma (invasive) 

Epidemiology

  * Increasing incidental discovery and prevalence of pancreatic cysts (> 30% on cross sectional imaging after age 70), driven by the greater use of high resolution imaging and an aging population, underscore the urgent need for improved risk stratification and management strategies ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2024;22:1798](38423346))

Clinical features

  * Various guidelines have been published since 2006 to provide diagnostic, risk stratification, therapeutic and management recommendations for IPMNs ([JAMA Surg 2022;157:723](35731507)) 
    * 2006 Sendai guidelines 
      * For management of main and branch duct IPMNs 
      * It included features like cyst size, presence of symptoms related to the cyst, presence of mural nodules and dilation if the main pancreatic duct (MPD) is > 6 mm ([Pancreatology 2006;6:17](16327281)) 
    * 2012 Fukuoka guidelines 
      * Focused on providing recommendations for managing IPMNs and mucinous cystic neoplasms and identifying high risk and worrisome features ([Pancreatology 2012;12:183](22687371)) 
    * 2017 Revised Fukuoka 
      * Updated guidelines regarding prediction of invasive carcinoma and high grade dysplasia, surveillance and postoperative follow up of IPMNs and specified operative criteria for branch duct IPMNs ([Pancreatology 2017;17:738](28735806)) 
    * 2024 Kyoto guidelines 
      * International evidence based guidelines for the management of IPMNs; key updates included the following ([Pancreatology 2024;24:255](38182527)) 
        * Revision of high risk stigmata and worrisome features (see table below) 
        * EUS (including EUS fine needle aspiration [FNA], contrast enhanced EUS) has been accepted as an important modality to evaluate IPMN suspicious for high grade dysplasia (HGD) / invasive carcinoma (IC) 
        * Emerging role of newer cyst fluid markers 
          * Cyst fluid glucose (cutoff value of < 50 ng/dL to diagnose mucinous cyst) 
          * Molecular / genetic profiling of pancreatic cyst fluid: mutations for _TP53_ , _SMAD4_ , _CDKN2A_ and _PIK3CA_ associated with high grade dysplasia / invasive carcinoma, with low sensitivity (9 - 39%) but high specificity (92 - 98%) 
        * Recent pathologic observations (see [Microscopic (histologic) description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreasriskstratificationipmns.html#riskstratificationipmnsmicro)) 
        * Clarified surveillance of nonresected IPMNs and postresection IPMNs 

**Table 1: High risk stigmata and worrisome features of high grade dysplasia / invasive carcinoma in IPMNs according to the 2024 Kyoto guidelines** **Feature** | **Criteria** | **High risk stigmata** | 

  * Obstructive jaundice 
  * Enhancing mural nodule ≥ 5 mm or solid component 
  * Main pancreatic duct dilation ≥ 10 mm 
  * Cytology suspicious or positive for malignancy (if EUS FNA performed) 

| **Worrisome features** | 

  * Acute pancreatitis 
  * Increased serum CA 19-9 level 
  * New onset or acute exacerbation of diabetes mellitus within the past year 
  * Cyst diameter ≥ 30 mm 
  * Enhancing mural nodule < 5 mm 
  * Thickened or enhancing cyst walls 
  * Main pancreatic duct dilation ≥ 5 mm and < 10 mm 
  * Abrupt change in caliber of pancreatic duct with distal pancreatic atrophy 
  * Lymphadenopathy (regional) 
  * Cyst growth rate ≥ 2.5 mm per year

Diagnosis

  * For presurgical IPMN evaluation, classification and risk stratification require close clinicopathologic correlation of radiology, endoscopy, cyst fluid (biochemical or molecular) and FNA cytology findings 

Laboratory

  * See [Pancreatic cyst fluid analysis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreascysticfluidanalysis.html)

Radiology description

  * Primary imaging modalities for the assessment of IPMN: contrast enhanced MRI / magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) and multidetector CT 
  * EUS can be used for further investigation and risk stratification; contrast enhancement increases its diagnostic abilities 
  * Novel EUS guided needle confocal laser endomicroscopy (EUS nCLE) allows optical biopsies of IPMNs 
    * Real time, en face visualization of cyst lining epithelium at the microscopic level during EUS procedure ([Gastro Hep Adv 2022;1:160](39131123), [Cancers (Basel) 2024;16:1238](38539568), [Diagnostics (Basel) 2022;13:65](36611356)) 
    * On endomicroscopic images, pancreatic cysts are evaluated through a combined assessment of interstitial vascular pattern, cyst content fluorescence characteristics and the negative image / architecture of the cyst epithelium 
    * IPMN shows finger-like papillary projections composed of an outer epithelium and inner vascular core ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2020;18:432](31220640)) 
    * nCLE evaluation of the thickness and darkness of the cyst epithelia allows the grading of dysplasia and thus risk stratification of IPMNs, which can be further augmented by machine learning / artificial intelligence ([Gastrointest Endosc 2020;91:551](31542380), [Biomimetics (Basel) 2023;8:496](37887627), [Gastrointest Endosc 2021;94:78](33465354))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * IPMN epithelial histologic subtypes and associated dysplasia / prognosis ([Pancreatology 2024;24:255](38182527)) 
    * Gastric subtype: most common type, considered low grade or associated with low grade dysplasia and has the most favorable prognosis 
    * Intestinal subtype: considered intermediate grade or less favorable compared to gastric subtype 
    * Pancreaticobiliary subtype: considered to have the highest risk of neoplastic progression 
    * Though gastric subtype is considered low grade, recent molecular studies have shown that gastric subtype is considered an ancestor of co-occurring high grade lesions, including pancreatobiliary and intestinal subtypes, so gastric subtype with low grade dysplasia should not be disregarded as having no risk 
    * If there are several subtypes in the same lesion, the subtype should be determined as that of the highest grade of dysplasia 
  * Other pathology updates from 2024 Kyoto guidelines 
    * Carcinoma in situ can be used interchangeably with high grade dysplasia in IPMNs 
    * The term malignant IPMN is discouraged to avoid confusion 
    * Neoplastic components of high grade dysplasia and invasive carcinoma should be reported separately 
    * Low grade dysplasia at the margin does not justify completion pancreatectomy 
    * Absence of epithelial cells at the transection margin (i.e., a denuded duct) is not equivalent to a negative margin; an additional resection should be considered if possible

Positive stains

  * Mutated [p53](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsp53.html) staining pattern, loss of [SMAD4](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsdpc4.html) or [ARID1A](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsarid1a.html) and high [Ki67](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainski67.html) proliferation are typically associated with high grade dysplasia / invasive adenocarcinoma 

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * IPMN molecular landscape: genetic heterogeneity and multifocal neoplasia are prevalent ([Nat Rev Gastroenterol Hepatol 2021;18:457](34099908), [Cancers (Basel) 2024;16:1183](38539517)) 
    * Recent evidence supports the presence of field effect affecting the pancreatic ductal system, such as the germline genetic background or external risk factors (e.g., inflammation), that leads to the multifocality in IPMNs 
  * Somatic activating mutations in _KRAS_ and _GNAS_
    * Most prevalent genetic alterations seen in IPMN, with either or both seen in > 95% of all IPMNs 
    * _KRAS_ and _GNAS_ mutations are not reliably associated with prognosis of IPMNs 
    * _KRAS_ mutation (predominantly codon 12; occasionally codons 13 and 61): an early event in IPMN tumorigenesis; however, the presence of _KRAS_ mutations does not correlate with the degree of dysplasia or histologic subtype of IPMNs 
    * Activating _GNAS_ mutations affecting codon 201: most prevalent in intestinal type IPMNs; not seen in mucinous cystic neoplasms 
  * Hotspot mutations in _KLF4_ (K409 and S411): in > 50% of low grade IPMNs but in only 15% of high grade lesions, suggesting a role in early tumorigenesis 
  * Inactivating mutations of _RNF43_ : up to 75% of IPMNs, found mainly in noninvasive high grade lesions and likely represent a later event involved in the progression from low grade to high grade lesions in the intestinal pathway 
  * _ATM_ germline and somatic mutations: up to 17% of IPMN cases 
  * Other genes involved in IPMNs 
    * _GLI3_ , _SF3B1_ , _BRG1_ , _LKB1_ , _PTEN_ , _PI3K_ , _PIK3CA_ , _STK11_
    * Mitogen activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway, including _BRAF_ , _ERBB2_ , _HRAS_ and _MAPK1_
  * Advanced neoplasia (high grade dysplasia / invasive carcinoma) 
    * Tumor suppressor genes (_TP53_ , p16 / _CDKN2A_ , _SMAD4_ and _TGFBR2_) 
    * mTOR pathway genes (_PTEN_ , _PIK3CA_ and _AKT1_) 
    * _TP53_ , _SMAD4_ and _TGFBR2_ abnormalities frequently restricted to invasive carcinoma 
  * Epigenetic inactivation via DNA methylation is also present in IPMN 
    * Hypermethylation is more prevalent with adenocarcinoma, in which multiple genes could be methylated

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/pancreasriskstratificationipmnsChenChalla04.jpeg

A pancreatic cyst resected from a 70 year old man demonstrates intraductal neoplastic proliferation with papillary architecture and mucin production involving the main pancreatic duct. Histological examination reveals the lining epithelium to be composed of tall columnar cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and scattered goblet cells. This pattern is consistent with which of the following histologic subtypes of intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN)?   
  
  1. Gastric 
  2. Intestinal 
  3. Oncocytic 
  4. Pancreatobiliary 

Practice answer #1

**B.** Intestinal. The described epithelial morphology is classic for intestinal type epithelium, which is commonly seen in main duct IPMN, as in this case. Answers A and D are incorrect because they represent the other 2 epithelial subtypes of IPMNs. Answer C is incorrect because intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm (IOPN) has been separated from IPMNs due to its distinctive molecular pathogenesis and presenting features.

Practice question #2

Which of the following represents a worrisome feature for high grade dysplasia / invasive carcinoma in intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN), according to 2024 Kyoto guidelines?   
  
  1. Cyst size ≥ 30 mm 
  2. Cytology suspicious or positive for malignancy 
  3. Enhancing mural nodule ≥ 5 mm or solid component 
  4. Main pancreatic duct diameter ≥ 10 mm 
  5. Obstructive jaundice 

Practice answer #2

**A.** Cyst size ≥ 30 mm is a worrisome feature for high grade dysplasia / invasive carcinoma in IPMN according to the 2024 Kyoto guidelines. Answers B, C, D and E are incorrect because these represent high risk stigmata or direct evidence of malignancy.
