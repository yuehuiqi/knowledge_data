Definition / general

  * Several familial and inherited syndromes increase the risk of pancreatic neoplasms 
  * Associated conditions include recurrent pancreatitis, benign cysts, neuroendocrine tumors and familial pancreatic cancer, which accounts for 10% of pancreatic ductal carcinoma cases 

Essential features

  * Recognizing familial syndromes is critical for targeted screening, early detection and personalized therapy 

Terminology

  * Syndromes associated with familial pancreatic neoplasms are listed below ([J Natl Cancer Inst 2022;114:996](35445726), [Gastroenterology 2022;163:386](35398344)) 
    * Ataxia telangiectasia 
    * Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (BWS) 
    * Cystic fibrosis (CF) 
    * Familial adenomatous polyposis syndrome (FAP) 
    * Familial atypical multiple mole melanoma syndrome (FAMMM) 
    * Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome 
    * Hereditary pancreatitis 
    * Li-Fraumeni syndrome (LFS) 
    * Lynch syndrome 
    * McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS) 
    * Multiple endocrine neoplasia, type 1 (MEN1) 
    * Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) - von Recklinghausen disease 
    * Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (PJS) 
    * Tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC) 
    * Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) 
  * Reference: [Molecular genetics of pancreatic ductal carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreasmolecularaspects.html)

Ataxia telangiectasia

Definition / general  

  * Rare autosomal recessive inherited condition caused by biallelic germline mutations in the _ATM_ gene on chromosome 11q22-23, primarily characterized by neurologic and immunologic manifestations ([Ataxia telangiectasia](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsatm.html)) 

Essential features  

  * Individuals with ataxia telangiectasia have a significantly increased risk for a variety of cancers, especially in childhood or early adulthood, including leukemia, lymphoma, breast cancer, gastrointestinal cancers, pancreatic cancers and brain tumors 

Pathophysiology  

  * Mutations of the _ATM_ gene are responsible for ataxia telangiectasia 
  * Nonsense, frameshift, missense and insertion deletion mutations of the _ATM_ gene have been described 
  * In most cases, mutations lead to a truncated and, thus, nonfunctional protein product 
  * Mechanism underlying telangiectasia formation remains unknown; this condition results in dilated blood vessels and is one of the pathognomonic hallmarks of the disorder 
  * Irreversible cerebellar ataxia is commonly the first outcome in ataxia telangiectasia due to selective cerebellar Purkinje neuronal degeneration 
  * Recent reviews describe how cerebellar Purkinje neurons become selectively vulnerable when all other cell types in the brain are affected by the same defects in _ATM_ function 
  * Reference: [StatPearls: Ataxia-Telangiectasia [Accessed 22 July 2025]](30137827)

Treatment  

  * No cure for ataxia telangiectasia exists; an interprofessional team is crucial for symptom management and ongoing surveillance 
  * Physical therapy, acetyl-DL-leucine and immunoglobulin treatments may improve symptoms but disease modifying treatments are needed 
  * Ongoing research explores antioxidants, glucocorticoids and new therapies for ataxia telangiectasia's neurological and cancer complications 
  * Reference: [StatPearls: Ataxia-Telangiectasia [Accessed 22 July 2025]](30137827)

Molecular / cytogenetics description  

  * Heterozygous carriers of _ATM_ mutations (i.e., individuals with a single mutated _ATM_ allele) have an increased risk of developing pancreatic cancer ([JAMA Oncol 2021;7:1664](34529012)) 
  * Multicenter cohort study estimated that individuals with a germline pathogenic _ATM_ variant have a cumulative risk of pancreatic cancer of 1.1% by age 50, 6.3% by age 70 and 9.5% by age 80 ([JAMA Oncol 2021;7:1664](34529012)) 

Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (BWS)

Definition / general  

  * Genetic overgrowth disorder characterized by an increased risk of childhood tumors and congenital abnormalities ([Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/adrenalbeckwith.html)) 

Essential features  

  * Overgrowth syndrome with characteristic features such as hemihypertrophy (unequal growth of body parts), macroglossia (enlarged tongue) and intra-abdominal tumors 
  * Associated with increased risk of developing pancreatoblastoma ([Am J Med Genet A 2019;179:1878](31231953)) 

Pathophysiology  

  * It results from epigenetic and genetic alterations affecting chromosome 11p15.5, which contains genes involved in growth regulation, including _IGF2_ (insulin-like growth factor 2) and _CDKN1C_

Clinical features  

  * Pancreatoblastoma in BWS is typically congenital and often presents as a palpable abdominal mass in neonates ([Pediatr Pathol 1988;8:331](2845376)) 
  * Up to 50% of pancreatoblastomas may have liver metastasis at the time of diagnosis 

Cystic fibrosis (CF)

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal recessive disorder caused by _CFTR_ gene mutation ([Cystic fibrosis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreascysticfibrosis.html)) 
  * Primarily affecting the lungs, pancreas, liver and intestines 

Essential features  

  * Cystic fibrosis (CF) is considered a risk factor for developing pancreatic cancer, particularly pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) ([Cancer 2010;116:203](19885835), [Gut 2007;56:1327](17698876)) 

Pathophysiology  

  * Caused by mutations in the _CFTR_ (cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator) gene, which encodes a chloride ion channel for ion transport across cell membranes and maintains the function of epithelial cells in multiple organs, including the pancreas 
  * Characterized by defective chloride and sodium transport across epithelial cells, resulting in thick, sticky secretions 
  * CF leads to exocrine pancreatic insufficiency and chronic pancreatitis, which are well established risk factors for pancreatic cancer ([Int J Epidemiol 2015;44:186](25502106)) 

Molecular / cytogenetics description  

  * Mutations in _CFTR_ may contribute to pancreatic tumorigenesis, possibly by affecting cell proliferation and inflammation 

Familial adenomatous polyposis syndrome (FAP)

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal dominant familial polyposis syndrome caused by a germline mutation in the _APC_ gene on chromosome 5q21 characterized by development of numerous adenomatous polyps (> 100) in the colon and rectum ([Familial adenomatous polyposis syndrome](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumorFAP.html)) 

Essential features  

  * FAP is associated with an increased risk of developing pancreatic cancer, though the absolute lifetime risk remains relatively low ([Gut 1993;34:1394](8244108)) 
  * Most peripancreatic neoplasms in FAP are duodenal or ampullary adenocarcinomas, not true PDAC 

Treatment  

  * PDAC screening is not recommended for FAP patients due to the rarity of PDAC in this population ([Gut 1993;34:1394](8244108)) 
  * Surveillance focuses on monitoring colorectal, gastric and duodenal polyps, as well as desmoid tumors 

Microscopic (histological) description  

  * Primary pancreatic tumors in FAP are rare and include neuroendocrine tumor, acinar cell carcinoma or pancreatoblastoma 

Molecular / cytogenetics description  

  * Most mutations lead to truncation of the APC (adenomatous polyposis coli) protein, causing dysregulated beta catenin accumulation within the Wnt signaling pathway 

Familial atypical multiple mole melanoma syndrome (FAMMM)

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal dominant condition associated with mutations in the cyclin dependent kinase inhibitor 2A (_CDKN2A_) gene (p16) 
  * Characterized by usually > 50 melanocytic nevi 

Essential features  

  * Associated with multiple nevi, cutaneous and ocular malignant melanomas, as well as a high risk for pancreatic cancers ([Lung Cancer 2016;97:48](27237027)) 

Epidemiology  

  * People with FAMMM syndrome may develop pancreatic cancer at an earlier age compared to the general population 

Diagnosis  

  * Presence of both melanoma and pancreatic cancer in the same patient may raise suspicion for FAMMM syndrome 
  * Genetic testing for _CDKN2A_ mutations may be considered in affected families 

Treatment  

  * For individuals with a family history of pancreatic cancer or those with _CDKN2A_ mutations, screening for pancreatic cancer may be considered 

Microscopic (histological) description  

  * Pancreatic adenocarcinoma typically presents with conventional histologic features of PDAC 
  * Other variations in pancreatic carcinoma that have been reported include adenosquamous carcinoma and undifferentiated carcinoma with osteoclast-like giant cells ([Am J Surg Pathol 2008;32:1905](18813118)) 

Molecular / cytogenetics description  

  * FAMMM pancreatic carcinoma syndrome variant has been identified in families with a specific 19 base pair deletion in the p16 gene (the p16 Leiden mutation), which is associated with a cumulative pancreatic carcinoma risk of up to 21% by age 75 ([Cancer 2002;94:84](11815963), [Pancreas 1991;6:127](1886881), [Int J Cancer 2000;87:809](10956390)) 

Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome 

Definition / general  

  * Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome is an inherited cancer predisposition syndrome caused by mutations in the _BRCA1_ or _BRCA2_ genes ([_BRCA_ associated carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastmalignantbrca1.html)) 

Essential features  

  * Presence of _BRCA1_ or _BRCA2_ gene mutation 
  * It is associated with a significantly increased risk of breast, ovarian, pancreatic cancer and other cancers 

Pathophysiology  

  * Both germline and sporadic mutations in _BRCA1/2_ have been found to play a role in the development of PDAC, with a stronger association observed in _BRCA2_ mutations 
  * Pancreatic cancer risk in _BRCA2_ gene mutation carriers is elevated with a relative risk (RR) that ranges from 3.3 to 10 ([J Natl Cancer Inst 2022;114:996](35445726), [J Clin Oncol 2022;40:1529](35077220), [J Natl Cancer Inst 1999;91:1310](10433620)) 
  * Germline mutations in the partner and localizer of _BRCA2_ (_PALB2_) gene also confer an increased risk of pancreatic cancer ([Science 2009;324:217](19264984), [J Clin Oncol 2020;38:674](31841383)) 

Treatment  

  * Current National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend pancreatic screening for patients with germline _BRCA2_ mutations and a family history of pancreatic ducal carcinoma ([J Natl Compr Canc Netw 2021;19:77](33406487)) 
  * Emerging evidence suggests _BRCA1_ and _BRCA2_ associated PDAC demonstrates increased sensitivity to platinum based therapy ([Lung Cancer 2016;97:48](27237027)) 

Microscopic (histological) description  

  * Pancreatic cancers in individuals with _BRCA_ mutations may show a higher incidence of poorly differentiated or undifferentiated tumors, scant desmoplastic stromal reaction or mucinous features 

Hereditary pancreatitis

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal dominant disorder caused by serine protease 1 (_PRSS1_), characterized by recurrent episodes of pancreatitis and increased risk of PDAC ([Hereditary pancreatitis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreashereditary.html)) 

Essential features  

  * Accounts for a small fraction of cases of chronic pancreatitis 
  * Most common subtype of familial hereditary pancreatitis (80%) 
  * Patients have increased risk of developing pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) and PDAC 

Epidemiology  

  * Recurrent bouts of pancreatitis begin at a young age, typically before 30 years 
  * Magnitude of lifetime risk of pancreatic cancer is estimated to be 25 - 44% ([J Natl Cancer Inst 1997;89:442](9091646)) 
  * In one study, the cumulative risk of pancreatic cancer in hereditary pancreatitis at 50, 60 and 75 years was 10%, 19% and 54%, respectively ([Am J Gastroenterol 2008;103:111](18184119)) 

Pathophysiology  

  * Most often associated with mutations in serine protease 1 (_PRSS1_) gene, which is involved in the production and inactivation of trypsinogen, a pancreatic enzyme 
  * Individuals with chronic pancreatic injury are at increased risk for developing both pseudotumors caused by fibrosis and PDAC ([Mod Pathol 2007;20:S113](17486047)) 

Li-Fraumeni syndrome (LFS)

Definition / general  

  * Inherited autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline mutation in the tumor suppressor gene _TP53_
  * Significantly increases the risk of developing multiple types of cancers at an early age 

Essential features  

  * Mutations in the _TP53_ gene are associated with an increased risk of several cancers, including breast cancer, brain tumors, soft tissue sarcomas, osteosarcoma, leukemia, adrenocortical carcinoma and pancreatic cancer 
  * While pancreatic cancer is not among the most common cancers linked to LFS, studies have reported cases of pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PanNETs) in individuals with LFS, suggesting a broader spectrum of pancreatic neoplasms associated with the syndrome ([Ecancermedicalscience 2022;16:1487](36819813)) 

Pathophysiology  

  * Li-Fraumeni syndrome (LFS) is caused by germline mutations in the _TP53_ gene, which encodes a tumor suppressor protein responsible for regulating apoptosis, DNA repair and the cell cycle 
  * Most mutations are missense point mutations in exons 5 - 8, leading to a stable but inactive p53 protein that accumulates in tumor cells 

Treatment  

  * No treatment or procedure currently exists to repair mutations in the _TP53_ gene and p53 is not a drug target 
  * Radiation therapy, computed tomography (CT) scans and Xrays should be avoided in LFS patients due to the high risk of radiation induced secondary cancers 
  * Reference: [StatPearls: Li-Fraumeni Syndrome [Accessed 22 July 2025]](30335319)

Molecular / cytogenetics description  

  * _TP53_ mutations result in loss of protection against genetic alterations and abnormal interactions with ubiquitin ligases (HDM2 and UBE4B) contribute to tumorigenesis; some families lack _TP53_ mutations, indicating potential involvement of post-translational modifications or other genes 
  * Mutations associated with a younger age of onset in Li-Fraumeni syndrome include short telomeres and genetic modifiers like the MDM2-SNP309 polymorphism 
  * Genetic anticipation may also contribute to earlier onset in affected individuals 

Lynch syndrome

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal dominant hereditary disorder caused by inherited germline mutations in the mismatch repair (MMR) genes: _MLH1_ , _MSH2_ , _MSH6_ and _PMS2_ ([Lynch syndrome](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumorlynch.html)) 

Essential features  

  * Presence of pancreatic cancer in individuals with Lynch syndrome is often associated with a family history of other cancers, including colorectal cancer, endometrial cancer, ovarian cancer, stomach cancer, urinary tract cancer 

Epidemiology  

  * More likely to develop pancreatic cancer at a younger age 

Treatment  

  * Regular cancer screening such as endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) are being explored 
  * Treatment for pancreatic cancer generally follows standard approaches, including surgery (if resectable), chemotherapy (e.g., FOLFIRINOX, gemcitabine) and radiation therapy 
  * Promising new treatment regimens, including immunotherapy drugs such as PD-1 inhibitors, are currently under study for microsatellite unstable malignancies 
  * Reference: [Immunology 2024;173:209](38517066)

Microscopic (histological) description  

  * Associated with a higher risk of PDAC, the most common conventional type, with some cases exhibiting a microsatellite instability (MSI) phenotype characterized by a medullary pattern 
  * May show greater genetic heterogeneity compared to sporadic pancreatic tumors, including mucinous features or necrosis 
  * May also show histological subtypes, such as signet ring cell carcinoma and solid papillary neoplasms (SPN) as well as acinar cell carcinomas 
  * Can predispose to intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMN) or mucinous cystic neoplasms (MCN), potentially progressing to PDAC ([Lung Cancer 2016;97:48](27237027), [GeneReviews: Lynch Syndrome [Accessed 7 July 2025]](20301390)) 

Molecular / cytogenetics description  

  * Certain genes critical to PDAC development contain microsatellite sequences and are particularly vulnerable to MSI driven mutations 
    * Loss of function in the MMR genes _MLH1_ and _MSH2_ results in increased genomic instability and progressive accumulation of mutations in pancreatic ductal cells 
    * TGFβ receptor type 2 (_TGFBR2_): normally inhibits cell proliferation; MSI related frameshift mutations in _TGFBR2_ result in a loss of function, leading to uncontrolled cell growth 
    * _BAX_ (proapoptotic gene): helps regulate programmed cell death (apoptosis); MSI leads to mutations in _BAX_ , reducing apoptosis and allowing damaged cells to survive and proliferate 
    * _KRAS_ and _TP53_ mutations: though MSI driven PDACs often have distinct molecular profiles, they can still acquire mutations in key oncogenes like _KRAS_ and tumor suppressor genes like _TP53_ , further promoting cancer progression 
  * Reference: [Br J Cancer 2023;128:726](36434153)

McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS)

Definition / general  

  * Genetic nonheritable disorder caused by somatic mutation in the _GNAS_ gene, characterized by triad of symptoms: polyostotic fibrous dysplasia, café au lait skin spots and endocrine abnormalities ([McCune-Albright syndrome](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/bonefibrousdysplasiamccunealbright.html)) 

Essential features  

  * _GNAS_ gene mutations are frequently found in IPMNs of the pancreas ([Medicine (Baltimore) 2019;98:e18102](31852070)) 
  * _GNAS_ mutations are highly specific for IPMNs among pancreatic neoplasms, reinforcing the link between MAS and pancreatic cystic lesions ([Sci Rep 2011;1:161](22355676)) 

Pathophysiology  

  * McCune-Albright syndrome is a rare disorder caused by somatic activating mutations in the _GNAS_ gene, leading to increased intracellular cAMP signaling, which drives diverse clinical manifestations, including hepatobiliary and pancreatic neoplasms 
  * In bone, elevated cAMP causes osteoblasts to differentiate into stromal cells, leading to fibrous dysplasia and increased secretion of FGF23, resulting in renal phosphate wasting 
  * In the skin, increased cAMP stimulates melanin production, causing café au lait macules, while in endocrine tissues it leads to overproduction of specific hormones in the gonads, thyroid, pituitary, parathyroid and adrenal glands 
  * References: [StatPearls: McCune-Albright Syndrome [Accessed 22 July 2025]](30725777), [J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2014;99:E97](24170100)

Treatment  

  * It has been suggested that individuals with MAS might benefit from screening for pancreatic lesions to enable early detection and management ([J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2014;99:E97](24170100)) 

Multiple endocrine neoplasia, type 1 (MEN1)

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal dominant syndrome characterized by inactivating _MEN1_ gene mutations, leading to multiple endocrine tumors (NETs) and other tumor types ([Multiple endocrine neoplasia, type 1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreasMEN1.html)) 
  * Classic triad of pituitary, parathyroid and pancreatic tumors (the 3 Ps) 

Essential features  

  * Characteristic tumors develop in the following endocrine glands: parathyroid glands, anterior pituitary gland, pancreas (PanNETs: pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors) 

Epidemiology  

  * 100% of MEN1 patients have microscopic neuroendocrine tumors in the pancreas 
  * Up to 70% of MEN1 patients will develop macroscopic PanNETs in their lifetime 
  * Age of diagnosis for macroscopic PanNETs is usually < 50 years 
  * Reference: [Surg Pathol Clin 2016;9:595](27926362)

Clinical features  

  * Most PanNETs in MEN1 are nonfunctional but gastrinomas and insulinomas (hormone secreting) are seen in a substantial percentage of cases 
  * Functional gastrinomas in MEN1 may be located in the duodenum, not the pancreas 
  * Almost all patients with MEN1 eventually die from complications of the disease, often related to PanNET metastases or comorbidities ([Medicine (Baltimore) 2013;92:135](23645327)) 

Treatment  

  * For functioning gastroentero pancreatic tumors, surgical removal is the primary treatment 
  * For carcinoid tumors, somatostatin analogs like octreotide are used for treatment and surgery is preferred for tumors 
  * Reference: [StatPearls: Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 1 [Accessed 22 July 2025]](30725665)

Molecular / cytogenetics description  

  * MEN1 syndrome is caused by germline inactivating mutations of the _MEN1_ or menin gene on chromosome 11 and is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern 
  * ~90% of cases are inherited from an affected parent, while 10% result from a de novo mutation; clinical diagnostic criteria are critical for diagnosis 
  * A study by Marini et al. identified 99 different _MEN1_ mutations, with gastroentero pancreatic tumors more common in nonsense mutations and thoracic neuroendocrine tumors in splice site mutations 
  * _CDKN1B_ mutations have also been linked to pituitary adenomas, parathyroid adenomas and PanNETs, suggesting a potential role in MEN1 syndromes without _MEN1_ mutations 

Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) - von Recklinghausen disease

Definition / general  

  * Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1), also called von Recklinghausen disease ([Neurofibromatosis type 1 - von Recklinghausen disease](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/softtissueneurofibroma.html)) 
  * Autosomal dominant condition caused by a mutation in the _NF1_ gene 

Essential features  

  * Primary manifestations are multiple neurofibromas, café au lait spots (pigmented skin lesions) and Lisch nodules 
  * NF1 is associated with PanNETs ([Cancer 2008;113:1807](18798544)) 

Pathophysiology  

  * _NF1_ gene on chromosome 17q encodes neurofibromin, a tumor suppressor that inhibits the Ras protein, thereby limiting cell growth 

Clinical features  

  * Particular type of functional neuroendocrine tumor associated with NF1, known as a somatostatinoma; more commonly located in the duodenum or periampullary region, where it may cause pancreatic obstruction and mimic a pancreatic neoplasm 
  * Although somatostatinoma in patients with NF1 can be associated with increased morbidity, it is not a significant cause of mortality in this population ([Cancer 2008;113:1807](18798544)) 

Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (PJS)

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal dominant polyposis syndrome caused by a germline mutation in the _STK11 / LKB1_ gene at chromosome 19p13.3, characterized by gastrointestinal hamartomatous polyps and melanocytic mucocutaneous pigmentation ([Peutz-Jeghers syndrome](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumorPeutz.html)) 

Essential features  

  * Gastrointestinal hamartomatous polyps are the most common feature 
  * Patients are at risk for multiple neoplasms, including gastrointestinal (GI) malignancies (colorectal, small bowel and pancreatic carcinomas) and non-GI tumors (breast, lung, ovary, uterus) 

Epidemiology  

  * Pancreatic carcinoma risk: ~10% chance by age 70 ([Clin Cancer Res 2006;12:3209](16707622)) 

Pathophysiology  

  * Germline mutation in the _STK11 / LKB1_ gene can result in a truncated protein, leading to loss of tumor suppressive activity 

Treatment  

  * Screening for pancreatic cancer is recommended for all PJS patients, regardless of family history 

Microscopic (histological) description  

  * Most pancreatic tumors in PJS are conventional PDAC but acinar cell carcinoma and IPMN (precursors to PDAC) have also been reported ([Biomedicines 2022;10:1475](35884779)) 
  * Increased risk for duodenal and ampullary malignancies, which must be differentiated from PDAC 

Tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC)

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal dominant condition caused by mutations in _TSC1_ (hamartin) or _TSC2_ (tuberin) in chromosomes 9 and 16, respectively, characterized by the growth of benign tumors (hamartomas) in multiple organs, including the brain, skin, kidneys, heart, lungs and eyes ([Tuberous sclerosis complex](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsTSC1/2.html)) 

Essential features  

  * Tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC) is associated with an increased risk of developing PanNETs (~2% frequency) ([Cancer 2008;113:1807](18798544)) 
  * Association between TSC and PDAC, the most common type of pancreatic cancer, is less clear 

Pathophysiology  

  * Mutations in _TSC2_ are more common than in _TSC1_ and ~15% of TSC patients have no identifiable genetic mutations 
  * _TSC1_ mutations affect hamartin production, while _TSC2_ mutations affect tuberin production; these genes regulate cell growth and tumor suppression 
  * _TSC1_ / _TSC2_ mutations lead to mTOR pathway hyperactivation, disrupting cell growth regulation and favoring solid tumor formation 

Treatment  

  * Lifetime surveillance is recommended, with imaging studies every 1 - 3 years, focusing on multisystemic complications 
  * mTOR inhibitors are used to treat subependymal giant cell astrocytomas (SEGA), renal angiomyolipomas and neural angiofibromas 
  * Reference: [StatPearls: Tuberous Sclerosis [Accessed 22 July 2025]](30860727)

Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL)

Definition / general  

  * Autosomal dominant disorder caused by mutations in the tumor suppressor gene _VHL_ located on chromosome 3p25, which predisposes individuals to the development of multiple tumors and cysts in various organs ([von Hippel-Lindau](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsvhl.html)) 

Essential features  

  * Pancreatic manifestations in VHL: pancreatic serous cystadenomas and PanNETs ([Handb Clin Neurol 2015;132:139](26564077), [Cancer 2022;128:435](34735022)) 

Pathophysiology  

  * VHL is caused by an autosomal dominant mutation in the von Hippel–Lindau tumor suppressor gene on chromosome 3, leading to abnormal pVHL protein 
  * pVHL normally regulates HIF-1-alpha, which is involved in the cellular response to hypoxia 
  * _VHL_ mutations alter the pVHL-HIF-1-alpha binding, preventing effective binding and causing upregulation of growth factors, including erythropoietin, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and platelet derived growth factor B (PDGF-B), as well as genes related to glucose uptake and metabolism 

Clinical features  

  * Pancreatic serous cystadenomas are the most common pancreatic manifestation 
    * Serous cystadenomas are benign but can be multiple and cause ductal obstruction or gland replacement 
    * Serous cystadenomas can be the first sign of de novo VHL in patients without a family history ([Indian J Cancer 2004;41:159](15659868)) 
  * PanNETs in VHL ([Surg Pathol Clin 2016;9:595](27926362)) 
    * Occur in ~10 - 17% of VHL patients 
    * Typically, solitary and nonfunctional (in contrast to MEN1) 
    * Often detected through routine surveillance imaging, primarily for renal cell carcinoma risk 
    * PanNETs in VHL rarely exhibit clear cell features or combined serous endocrine neoplasms 
    * Some PanNETs can grow rapidly and cause symptoms due to size 
    * Risk of metastasis increases if the tumor is > 3 cm 

Treatment  

  * Surgical removal is considered for PanNETs with a high risk of metastasis, based on tumor size, growth rate or genetic mutations 
  * Reference: [StatPearls: Von Hippel-Lindau Syndrome [Accessed 22 July 2025]](29083737)

Practice question #2

In von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) syndrome, which of the following pancreatic tumors is most commonly observed?   
  
  1. Mucinous cystic neoplasms (MCNs) 
  2. Pancreatic acinar cell carcinoma 
  3. Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)

Practice answer #2

**E.** Pancreatic serous cystadenomas. These are the most common pancreatic tumors associated with VHL syndrome. They are benign, cystic lesions that often present as multiple, small cysts in the pancreas. They can cause ductal obstruction or gland replacement but are generally asymptomatic. Serous cystadenomas are the hallmark pancreatic manifestation in VHL and are detected through routine imaging surveillance for other VHL related conditions, such as renal cell carcinoma ([Handb Clin Neurol 2015;132:139](26564077)).

Answer B is incorrect because pancreatic acinar cell carcinoma is a rare and aggressive form of pancreatic cancer that arises from acinar cells in the pancreas. While acinar cell carcinoma can occur in various genetic conditions, it is not typically associated with VHL syndrome, where the predominant pancreatic lesions are benign cystic tumors and PanNETs. Answer C is incorrect because PDAC, although a common form of pancreatic cancer, is not commonly associated with VHL syndrome. PDAC typically occurs sporadically or in genetic conditions like hereditary pancreatitis or _BRCA_ mutations, not VHL. Answer D is incorrect because PanNETs are indeed seen in VHL syndrome but are less common than serous cystadenomas. ~10 - 17% of VHL patients may develop PanNETs. Practice question #3

A 42 year old woman presents with jaundice and unintentional weight loss. She has a strong family history of early onset colorectal and endometrial cancers and her father had pancreatic cancer at age 50. A CT scan reveals a 2.8 cm mass in the head of the pancreas. Genetic testing reveals a germline mutation in the _MLH1_ gene, confirming a diagnosis of Lynch syndrome. Further workup is planned for cancer screening and management. In this patient with Lynch syndrome, which of the following molecular alterations is most likely to contribute to the development of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)?   
  
  1. Accumulation of mutations in the p16 (_CDKN2A_) tumor suppressor gene 
  2. Constitutive activation of the _KRAS_ oncogene via a point mutation 
  3. Loss of function mutations in the TGFβ receptor type 2 (_TGFBR2_) due to microsatellite instability (MSI) 
  4. Mutation induced activation of the PI3K / Akt pathway 
  5. Upregulation of _MYC_ oncogene expression leading to cell proliferation 

Practice answer #3

**C.** Loss of function mutations in the TGFβ receptor type 2 (_TGFBR2_) due to microsatellite instability (MSI). Lynch syndrome, caused by mutations in mismatch repair (MMR) genes like _MLH1_ , leads to MSI. MSI results in frameshift mutations in key genes, including _TGFBR2_ , which plays a crucial role in regulating cell proliferation and apoptosis. Loss of _TGFBR2_ function contributes to tumorigenesis, particularly in Lynch syndrome associated pancreatic cancer ([Lung Cancer 2016;97:48](27237027)).   
  
Answer A is incorrect because mutations in _CDKN2A_ are more commonly associated with familial atypical multiple mole melanoma (FAMMM) syndrome, not Lynch syndrome. _CDKN2A_ mutations do not drive the development of PDAC in Lynch syndrome. Answer B is incorrect because _KRAS_ mutations are common in sporadic pancreatic cancers and other hereditary cancer syndromes but they are not specifically associated with Lynch syndrome. Lynch syndrome related PDAC typically involves MSI, not _KRAS_ mutations. Answer D is incorrect because the PI3K / Akt pathway is often activated in pancreatic cancers but it is not a primary molecular alteration in Lynch syndrome associated PDAC. MSI driven mutations are the primary cause of tumorigenesis in Lynch syndrome. Answer E is incorrect because _MYC_ amplification is seen in various cancers but it is not a defining feature of Lynch syndrome associated pancreatic cancer. _MYC_ is not specifically linked to MSI driven pancreatic cancer.
