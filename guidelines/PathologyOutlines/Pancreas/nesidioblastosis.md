Definition / general

  * Nesidioblastosis is a term that describes various nonneoplastic, focal or diffuse alterations of the neuroendocrine compartment of pancreatic parenchyma 
  * In adults, nesidioblastosis may represent the microscopic correlate of hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia, although some patients remain clinically asymptomatic ([Endocr Relat Cancer 2023;30:e230034](37279235)) 

Essential features

  * Nesidioblastosis is a microscopic alteration of pancreatic islets 
  * Suspicion of nesidioblastosis may appear in cases of glycemic imbalance, particularly in the absence of focal lesions on radiological examinations 
  * Thorough understanding of the diagnostic criteria and clinical implications of nesidioblastosis has been complicated by evolving definitions over the years and by the frequent (but inappropriate) use of the term as a synonym for hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia 

Terminology

  * Old definitions included the presence of islet cells budding off from pancreatic ducts for the diagnosis of nesidioblastosis; nowadays, this finding is considered frequent but not necessary for its diagnosis ([Endocr Pathol 2004;15:241](15640550)) 
  * Islet cell hyperplasia is a discouraged term ([Endocr Relat Cancer 2023;30:e230034](37279235)) 
  * Nesidioblastoma is a term used in reference to focal nesidioblastosis ([Biomedicines 2023;11:1732](37371827)) 
  * Some authors inappropriately use the term nesidioblastosis as a synonym for clinical syndromes, including congenital hyperinsulinism of infancy and adult nonneoplastic hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia (also known as noninsulinoma pancreatogenous hypoglycemia syndrome) and vice versa 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10 
    * [E16](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/E00-E89/E15-E16/E16-/E16) \- other disorders of pancreatic internal secretion 
    * [E16.1](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/E00-E89/E15-E16/E16-/E16.1) \- other hypoglycemia 
  * ICD-11: [5A45](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#402589098) \- persistent hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia of infancy 

Epidemiology

  * F:M = 1.7:1 ([Biomedicines 2023;11:1732](37371827)) 
  * 2 fundamental clinical conditions can be distinguished 
    * Congenital hyperinsulinism / persistent hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia of infancy and childhood, typically before the age of 3 years old, with an incidence of 1/40,000 - 50,000 live births ([Biomedicines 2023;11:1732](37371827), [J Pediatr 1971;79:226](4104455), [Endocr Relat Cancer 2023;30:e230034](37279235)) 
    * Noninsulinoma pancreatogenous hypoglycemia syndrome / adult onset nesidioblastosis with hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia in patients older than 3 years old, with an estimated incidence of 9/100,000,000/year; it is reasonable to believe that the actual incidence is higher because of a lack of diagnosis ([Biomedicines 2023;11:1732](37371827)) 

Sites

  * Nesidioblastosis involves orthotopic and heterotopic pancreatic parenchyma 

Pathophysiology

  * Congenital nesidioblastosis: small endocrine lesions in the pancreas derive from the confluence of hyperplastic islets; all identified genes are involved in the pathways regulating insulin secretion ([Endocr Relat Cancer 2023;30:e230034](37279235)) 
    * Focal form of congenital nesidioblastosis is related to a loss of heterozygosity of 11p15 on the maternal allele and a sulfonylurea receptor on the paternal allele ([Am J Pathol 2001;158:2177](11395395)) 
    * Diffuse forms of congenital nesidioblastosis are related to mutation of the genes of the ATP dependent K channel, including _ABCC8_ , _KCNJ11_ ([Diabetes 2000;49:667](10871207)) 
    * Additional genes are linked to either the focal or diffuse form of congenital nesidioblastosis, such as _GLUD1_ , _HNF1A_ , _HNF4A_ , _HADH_ , _UCP2_ , _SLC16A1_ , _GCK_ , _HK1_ ; nevertheless, in some cases, no genetic alteration is found ([Endocr Relat Cancer 2023;30:e230034](37279235)) 
  * Adult nesidioblastosis: genetic alterations do not play a significant role in the pathogenesis of nesidioblastosis; some authors maintain that some genetic defects could be present with low penetrance ([Endocr Relat Cancer 2023;30:e230034](37279235)) 

Etiology

  * Congenital nesidioblastosis is usually related to concomitant genetic alterations (see [Pathophysiology](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreasnesidio.html#nesidiopathophysiology)) 
  * Partial gastrectomy, particularly for morbid obesity, may predispose to nesidioblastosis through altered digestion and pre-existing endocrine imbalances 

Clinical features

  * Adrenergic signs: trembling, sweating, palpitation, tachycardia, hunger, pallor 
  * Neuroglycopenic symptoms: dizziness, aphasia, visual impairment, atypical behavior, paresthesia, transient hemiplegia, psychosis, convulsion, loss of consciousness, coma ([Europace 2013;15:1506](23478090), [Pancreas 2015;44:1162](26355554)) 
  * Atypical symptoms: abdominal pain, vomiting, blurred vision, lethargy, dehydration, bradycardia, hypotension, electrocardiogram (ECG) alterations ([Postgrad Med J 1996;72:231](8733533)) 
  * It is important to recognize that nesidioblastosis may also be found in normoglycemic patients; the clinical syndrome of noninsulinoma pancreatogenous hypoglycemia syndrome is not univocally related to nesidioblastosis 
  * Although rare, some authors reported the association of nesidioblastosis with the secretion of hormones other than insulin, such as glucagon, gastrin and vasoactive intestinal peptide ([Biomedicines 2023;11:1732](37371827)) 

Diagnosis

  * Evaluation of general hypoglycemia symptoms and their situational occurrence (i.e., postprandial, fasting, spontaneous, exercise induced) to prove hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia 
  * Results of a 4 - 6 hour oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) and 72 hour fasting test to define endogenous / hyperinsulinism 
  * Optional (might replace the next point in the future): results of functional imaging studies (where available; e.g., 68Ga-DOTA-Exendin-4 PET / CT or somatostatin receptor scintigraphy) or selective arterial calcium stimulation (SACS) with proof of an increased nonlocalized insulin gradient (also useful to define the extent of pancreatic resection if surgery is planned) 
  * Exclusion of other conditions 
    * Hirata disease 
    * Insulin secretagogues 
    * Hypoglycemia factitial ([Postgrad Med 1981;69:237](7005884), [Pediatrics 2005;116:e145](15995015), [BMC Emerg Med 2018;18:53](30537938)) 
    * Critically ill with hepatic, renal or cardiac failure 
    * Pituitary / adrenal insufficiency, glucagon insufficiency 
    * Paraneoplastic syndrome (e.g., Doege-Potter syndrome) 
    * Late onset inborn deficiencies (e.g., glycogenolysis disorders, fatty acid oxidation defects, gluconeogenesis disorders) ([Biomedicines 2023;11:1732](37371827)) 

Laboratory

  * See [Diagnosis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreasnesidio.html#nesidiodiagnosis)

Radiology description

  * Nesidioblastosis does not present with a typical radiological presentation 
  * Imaging plays an important role in excluding the presence of masses 

Case reports

  * 53 year old woman with initial symptom of fasting hypoglycemia ([Case Rep Endocrinol 2022;2022:2802975](36248221)) 
  * 55 year old woman with a pancreatic mass ([J Endocr Soc 2021;6:bvab185](35024540)) 
  * 62 year old woman with a suspicion of insulinoma ([Cureus 2023;15:e38197](37252561)) 
  * 78 year old man with altered mental status with history of Roux-en-Y gastric bypass surgery ([Cureus 2023;15:e47349](38021505)) 

Treatment

  * Low carbohydrate diet 
  * Alpha glucosidase inhibitors 
  * Calcium channel antagonists 
  * Somatostatin analogs 
  * Glucocorticoids 
  * Beta blockers 
  * Surgery: with diagnostic and therapeutic purpose ([J Endocrinol Invest 2017;40:9](27624297)) 

Gross description

  * Nesidioblastosis is a microscopic finding; the presence of any macroscopic mass should raise suspicion of the presence of a neuroendocrine neoplasm 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Major criteria (all essential for diagnosis) 
    * Exclusion of an insulinoma by macroscopic, microscopic and immunohistochemical examination 
    * Multiple β cells with an enlarged and hyperchromatic nucleus and abundant clear cytoplasm 
    * Islets with normal spatial distribution of the various cell types 
    * No proliferative activity of endocrine cells 
  * Minor criteria (supports the diagnosis but not necessary) 
    * Irregular shape and occasional enlargement of islets 
    * Increased number of islets 
    * Macronucleoli in β cells 
  * Old definitions of nesidioblastosis also include the association with ductuloinsular complexes and the presence of peliosis; however, even if their presence is no longer required, their observations on slides support the diagnosis of nesidioblastosis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2005;29:524](15767809), [Mod Pathol 2009;22:239](18931647)) 
    * Ductuloinsular complex: intimate associations between small pancreatic ductules and clusters of islet cells, likely reflecting islet cell regeneration or neogenesis from ductal epithelium ([Surg Pathol Clin 2016;9:619](27926363), [Am J Surg Pathol 2004;28:813](15166675)) 
    * Peliosis: blood filled cystic spaces within the pancreatic parenchyma, representing vascular ectasia with focal endothelial disruption rather than a true neoplasm ([Forensic Sci Int 2005;149:25](15734106), [Am J Pathol 1950;26:1](15399218))

Cytology description

  * Because no focal mass is present, there is no indication for cytologic evaluation 
  * Diagnosis of nesidioblastosis is exclusively histological 

Positive stains

  * Neuroendocrine markers (i.e., [chromogranin A](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainschromogranin.html), [synaptophysin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainssynaptophysin.html), [INSM1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsinsm1.html), [NSE](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsNSE.html), etc.) are invariably positive 
  * At least 2 pancreatic hormones should be tested to exclude the diagnosis of neuroendocrine neoplasm 

Negative stains

Sample pathology report

  * Pancreas, distal pancreatectomy: 
    * Nesidioblastosis (see comment) 
    * Comment: The extended sampling and histological evaluation of the pancreatic parenchyma excluded the presence of neuroendocrine neoplasms. The presence of an increased number of islets, composed of various cell types, in which β cells show nuclear enlargement and clear cytoplasm, without significant proliferative activity (Ki67 < 1%), is consistent with adult nesidioblastosis.

Practice question #1

Which of the following is a possible predisposing factor for nesidioblastosis in an adult patient?   
  
  1. Prior gastric surgery 
  2. Prior neurosurgical intervention 
  3. Prior orthopedic surgery 
  4. Prior rectal surgery 

Practice answer #1

**A**. Prior gastric surgery. The underlying mechanism is still not clear but recent studies suggest an increased risk of nesidioblastosis in patients with a history of gastric surgery, compared with the general population. Answers B, C and D are incorrect because prior orthopedic surgery, neurosurgical intervention and rectal surgery do not present a correlation with nesidioblastosis thus far.
