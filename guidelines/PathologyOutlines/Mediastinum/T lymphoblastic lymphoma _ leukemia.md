Definition / general

  * Aggressive hematopoietic neoplasm of precursor lymphoid cells with T lineage 

Essential features

  * Neoplastic proliferation of precursor lymphoid cells of T lineage 
  * Considered T cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T ALL) when the primary site of involvement is the peripheral blood / bone marrow and T cell lymphoblastic lymphoma (T LBL) when the primary site of involvement is a solid organ, often the mediastinum ([Blood Adv 2020;4:3466](32722786), [Biomedicines 2021;9:1621](34829849)) 
  * Positive for T lineage defining antigen (cCD3 or sCD3) 
  * Positive for markers of immaturity: TdT, CD1a, CD34, CD117 (KIT) or CD99 
  * Does not meet the criteria for diagnosis of early T cell precursor lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma (ETP ALL) 

Terminology

  * T lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma, NOS (T LBL / ALL) 
  * T cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T ALL) 
  * T cell lymphoblastic lymphoma (T LBL) 
  * Precursor T cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoblastic lymphoma 

ICD coding

  * ICD-O: [9837/3](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/96612/9789241548496_eng.pdf?sequence=1#page=99) \- T lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma 
  * ICD-11: [2A71 & XH50W7](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#803161771%20%26%201717411707) \- precursor T lymphoblastic neoplasms & T lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma 

Epidemiology

  * Male predominance; M:F = ~2:1 ([J Clin Oncol 2003;21:3616](14512392)) 
  * Majority of cases occur in children, adolescents or young adults ([J Clin Oncol 2003;21:3616](14512392), [Biomedicines 2021;9:1621](34829849), [Leuk Lymphoma 2019;60:1171](30407885)) 
  * Accounts for 15% of childhood ALL and 20 - 25% of adult ALL cases ([Hematology Am Soc Hematol Educ Program 2016;2016:580](27913532), [N Engl J Med 2004;350:1535](15071128)) 
  * T LBL comprises 85 - 95% of lymphoblastic lymphomas ([Cancer Res 1983;43:4486](6191861), [Blood 1986;67:474](3080041)) 

Sites

  * Majority of T LBL cases (between 70 and 90%) involve the mediastinum and can present as a large mass ([Eur J Haematol 2016;96:447](26679753)) 
  * Other commonly involved areas in T LBL include lymph nodes, skin / subcutis and bone 
  * Involvement of the gonads and the central nervous system is less common but may occur ([Eur J Haematol 2016;96:447](26679753)) 
  * By definition, T ALL involves peripheral blood and bone marrow 
  * 25% or more blasts in the bone marrow is often used as the cutoff for diagnosis of T ALL 

Pathophysiology

  * Pathogenesis of T ALL / LBL involves a series of genetic alterations that disrupt normal T cell development and lead to neoplastic proliferation (the genetic pathways involved in this process are currently being investigated) 
  * Signaling pathways activation 
    * 60% of patients have activating mutations in _NOTCH1_ , leading to ligand independent cellular signaling ([Expert Rev Anticancer Ther 2011;11:1403](21929314), [Science 2004;306:269](15472075), [Lancet 2008;371:1030](18358930)) 
    * Constitutive NOTCH1 signaling leads to activation of the MYC oncoprotein, which may play an important role in leukemogenesis ([Lancet 2008;371:1030](18358930), [Blood Sci 2021;3:65](35402840)) 
    * Hyperactivation of the PI3K / Akt / mTOR signaling pathway due to inactivation of a negative regulator, such as PTEN, is common in T ALL ([J Clin Invest 2008;118:3762](18830414)) 
  * Transcription factor expression 
    * > 50% of T ALL cases exhibit enhanced expression of transcription factors such as _TAL1_ , _TLX1_ , _TLX3_ , _LYL1_ , _HOXA_ , _LMO1_ or _LMO2_ , often due to translocations that juxtapose transcription factor genes with T cell receptor (TCR) loci (_TRA_ / _TRD_ at 14q11 or _TRB_ at 7q34) resulting in aberrant overexpression and driving cell proliferation ([Cold Spring Harb Perspect Med 2020;10:a035246](31570389)) 
    * Gene fusions such as _SET::NUP214_ , _PICALM::MLLT10_ , _NUP214::ABL1_ and _KMT2A::AFDN_ are present in ~25% of cases and lead to HOXA dysregulation ([Blood Cancer J 2022;12:14](35082269)) 
  * Cell cycle regulators 
    * Inactivation of tumor suppressor genes _CDKN2A_ , _CDKN2B_ or _RB_ is seen in a majority of T ALL cases, causing dysregulation of the cell cycle and increased proliferation ([Blood 1994;84:4038](7994022)) 
  * Cell adhesion proteins 
    * T ALL cells often express LFA1 in its active conformation, which mediates extravasation through interactions with ICAM1 ([Blood Rev 2018;32:36](28830639)) 
    * Gene expression profiles and next generation sequencing (NGS) have shown differences between T LBL and T ALL; T LBL has increased expression of cell adhesion proteins and upregulation of genes related to angiogenesis ([Eur J Haematol 2016;96:447](26679753)) 

Etiology

  * Etiology remains largely unknown 
  * Exposure to ionizing radiation or certain chemicals has been proposed as a risk factor for other leukemic neoplasms but there is a lack of strong evidence directly linking these processes with T ALL / LBL ([Blood Rev 2023;58:101017](36220737))

Clinical features

  * B symptoms, including fever, weight loss or night sweats ([Eur J Cancer Clin Oncol 1988;24:1609](3208805)) 
  * Mediastinal mass is present in the majority of T LBL cases ([Eur J Haematol 2016;96:447](26679753)) 
    * Respiratory symptoms, such as cough, dyspnea or chest pain, may be present 
    * Superior vena cava syndrome has been reported ([Cureus 2024;16:e54729](38524018)) 
    * Mediastinal masses can be seen in combined presentations (T ALL / LBL) but are less common and may not be as large ([Clin Radiol 1986;37:257](3486737)) 
  * Lymphadenopathy is present in the majority of cases ([Eur J Cancer Clin Oncol 1988;24:1609](3208805)) 
  * Hepatosplenomegaly is present in a subset of cases ([Eur J Cancer Clin Oncol 1988;24:1609](3208805)) 
  * Extranodal involvement can occur in skin, testis and central nervous system (CNS) ([Eur J Cancer Clin Oncol 1988;24:1609](3208805)) 
  * Bone marrow involvement presents with bone pain and symptoms related to cytopenia ([Dan Med J 2014;61:A4883](25162442)) 
    * Fatigue and pallor from anemia 
    * Bleeding from thrombocytopenia 
    * Recurrent infections due to granulocytopenia 
  * Mediastinal mass and CNS involvement are more common in T ALL compared to B ALL ([Lancet Oncol 2019;20:e142](30842058)) 

Diagnosis

  * World Health Organization (WHO) has 2 categories of precursor T cell neoplasms 
    * ETP ALL 
    * T lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma, NOS 
  * International Consensus Classification (ICC) has 3 categories of precursor T cell neoplasms and 8 provisional entities ([Virchows Arch 2023;482:11](36422706)) 
    * T lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma 
    * ETP ALL with _BCL11B_ rearrangement 
    * ETP ALL, NOS 
    * Provisional entities: _TAL1_ -R / _TAL2-R_ , _TLX-R_ , _TLX3-R_ , _HOXA_ , _LMO1-R_ / _LMO2-R_ , _NKX2-R_ , _SPI1-R_ and _BHLH_ , other 
  * WHO and ICC have essentially the same criteria for diagnosis of T ALL 
    * Hematopoietic progenitors expressing T lineage specific markers (cCD3; occasionally sCD3) that have an aberrant immunophenotype and do not meet the criteria for the diagnosis of ETP ALL 
    * > 20% blasts in the peripheral blood or bone marrow or involvement of an extramedullary site are desirable but not required for the diagnosis 

Laboratory

  * White blood cell count is significantly elevated (commonly > 50 x109/L) in T ALL while frequently normal in T LBL ([Eur J Haematol 2011;86:38](21077959), [Clin Radiol 1986;37:257](3486737)) 
  * Anemia and thrombocytopenia are more common in T ALL cases due to bone marrow involvement ([Clin Radiol 1986;37:257](3486737)) 

Radiology description

  * T LBL radiographic findings ([Clin Radiol 1986;37:257](3486737), [Int J Hematol 2004;80:43](15293567)) 
    * Mediastinal widening (often with a mediastinum - thoracic ratio > 0.75) on chest Xray and computed tomography (CT) 
    * Systemic lymphadenopathy on CT 
    * Pleural effusion, pericardial effusion and airway compression may be appreciated 
  * T ALL radiographic findings ([Clin Radiol 1986;37:257](3486737), [Pediatr Hematol Oncol 1998;15:121](9592839)) 
    * Bone lesions are present in some cases 
    * Metaphyseal banding, periosteal reactions, osteolytic lesions, osteosclerosis or osteopenia 
    * Mediastinal widening can be present but is typically not as pronounced as in T LBL

Prognostic factors

  * Favorable 
    * Mutated _NOTCH1_ or _FBWX7_ ([J Clin Oncol 2013;31:4333](24166518)) 
    * _TLX1_ rearrangements ([Leukemia 2024;38:1213](38744920)) 
    * _LMO_ rearrangements ([Blood 2024;143:1891](38295337)) 
    * _NKX2-1_ rearrangements ([Leukemia 2024;38:1213](38744920)) 
    * Low end induction minimal residual disease ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2021;68:e28719](33026184)) 
  * Intermediate 
    * _HOXA_ rearrangements ([Leukemia 2024;38:1213](38744920)) 
  * Unfavorable 
    * _NOTCH1_ or _FBWX7_ wild type ([J Clin Oncol 2013;31:4333](24166518)) 
    * PIK3K pathway mutations ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2021;68:e28719](33026184)) 
    * _TAL1_ rearranged ([Virchows Arch 2023;482:11](36422706), [Leukemia 2024;38:1213](38744920), [Review Blood 2017;129:1113](28115373)) 
    * CNS involvement 
  * In pediatric patients there is a 5 year event free survival of 83.5% with modern protocols and a relapse rate of 12.7% ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2021;68:e28719](33026184), [Leukemia 2024;38:2382](39261601)) 
  * Adults have a worse 5 year event free survival of 45 - 70% ([Exp Hematol 2017;51:7](28412288)) 

Case reports

  * 8 year old boy with facial swelling, shortness of breath and lymphadenopathy ([J Hematop 2024;17:103](38528212)) 
  * 9 year old boy with T LBL showing cutaneous involvement ([Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol 2022;15:2027](36176942)) 
  * 15 year old boy with a painless thyroid mass ([Int J Clin Exp Pathol 2013;7:443](24427370)) 
  * 18 year old man with T ALL / LBL that demonstrated no _TCR_ gene rearrangement on molecular testing ([Transl Cancer Res 2021;10:1603](35116486)) 
  * 18 year old man with pleural effusion and mediastinal mass ([Respir Med Case Rep 2014;12:55](26029543)) 
  * 67 year old man with history of B1 thymoma and painless soft tissue mass on the right chest wall ([Acta Haematol 2022;145:106](34537764)) 
  * 73 year old woman with bilateral breast masses and lymphadenopathy ([BJR Case Rep 2024;10:uaae013](38746651)) 

Treatment

  * Intensive multiagent chemotherapy 
  * Stem cell transplantation 
    * Typically reserved for high risk patients 
    * Allogeneic stem cell transplantation is preferred over autologous stem cell transplantation because of its lower relapse rates ([Br J Haematol 2024;204:2390](38664918)) 
  * CNS prophylaxis with intrathecal chemotherapy, often methotrexate or cytarabine ([Curr Treat Options Oncol 2022;23:1829](36510037)) 
  * Immunotherapy currently has a limited role in treatment although preclinical and clinical studies are ongoing ([Haematologica 2024;109:1677](38832423)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Blastoid, intermediate to large sized cells with high N:C ratio, fine chromatin and nucleoli range from inconspicuous to prominent nucleoli in large blasts (WHO) 
  * Cells are monotonous in appearance with back to back nuclei due to the scant cytoplasm 
  * Mitotic figures are readily appreciated 
  * Bone marrow 
    * Hypercellular bone marrow 
    * Displacement of normal marrow elements although some residual trilineage hematopoiesis is typically present 
    * Bone marrow necrosis has been reported in rare cases of ALL ([Am J Hematol 1986;22:341](3460326)) 
    * Reticulin or collagen fibrosis may be present in rare cases ([Blood Res 2021;56:46](33504686)) 
  * Lymph node or soft tissue sections (WHO) 
    * Complete effacement of nodal architecture and capsular involvement 
    * Less commonly, partial paracortical involvement sparing germinal centers 
    * Starry sky pattern due to interspersed tingible body macrophages is present in some cases ([Blood 2021;137:3002](34042977))

Cytology description

  * Blast lineage cannot be reliably determined without flow cytometry or immunohistochemistry; cytologic features seen in a bone marrow aspirate or peripheral blood smear can help guide management and ordering of appropriate ancillary testing ([Mediterr J Hematol Infect Dis 2014;6:e2014073](25408859), [Br J Haematol 1997;97:372](9163604)) 
  * Cytologic features include 
    * Intermediate to large sized cells with a high N:C ratio 
    * Scant basophilic cytoplasm with occasional vacuolation 
    * Cytoplasmic azurophilic granules are uncommon but can be seen 
    * Round to oval nuclei that may be convoluted with grooves 
    * Chromatin is typically fine and dispersed but can be condensed in small lymphoblasts 
    * Nucleoli may be prominent in large lymphoblasts but are often indistinct or absent in smaller lymphoblasts ([Br J Haematol 1981;47:553](6938236)) 

Peripheral smear description

  * Lymphoblast predominance 
  * Anemia, thrombocytopenia and granulocytopenia may be present

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * NOTCH1 signaling pathway activation (through _NOTCH1_ or _FBXW7_ mutations) are the most common genetic alteration in T ALL / LBL, occurring in over 50% of cases in both children and adults and are associated with a favorable prognosis ([Leuk Lymphoma 2022;63:1624](35129045), [Review Blood 2017;129:1113](28115373)) 
  * PI3K signaling pathway alterations (involving genes like _PI3KCA_ , _PTEN_ , _KRAS_ , _NRAS_ and _mTOR_) are found in 5 - 20% of cases and are linked to a poor prognosis ([Review Blood 2017;129:1113](28115373), [J Clin Oncol 2013;31:4333](24166518), [Pediatr Blood Cancer 2021;68:e28719](33026184)) 
  * Transcription factor gene rearrangements play a significant role, with examples including _TAL1_ , _TAL2_ , _TLX1_ , _TLX3_ , _LMO1_ , _LMO2_ , _LYL1_ , _HOXA_ , _HOA13_ , _NKX2_ , _SPI1_ ([Review Blood 2017;129:1113](28115373), [Leukemia 2024;38:1213](38744920)) 
    * _TLX1_ , _NKX2_ and _LMO1_ translocations have a more favorable prognosis 
    * _HOXA13_ , _TAL1_ , _TLX3_ , _LMO1_ , _LMO2_ and _SPI1_ translocations have a worse prognosis 
  * Deletions in cell cycle regulators _CDKN2A_ , _CDKN2B_ and _RB_ are highly prevalent (occurring in over 60% of cases) but have an unclear impact on prognosis 
  * Inactivating mutations or deletions in cell cycle regulators including _DNMT3A_ , _PHF6_ and others are present in a subset of cases and have a variable impact on prognosis ([Review Blood 2017;129:1113](28115373), [Haematologica 2019;104:1617](30655366)) 
  * Numerous other genetic abnormalities have been reported in T ALL

Sample pathology report

  * Mass, anterior mediastinum, core needle biopsy: 
    * T lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma (see comment) 
    * Comment: H&E sections show a diffuse proliferation of crowded intermediate sized lymphoid cells. The lymphoid cells have scant cytoplasm, round to irregular nuclear contours and fine chromatin with 1 or more nucleoli. 
    * Immunohistochemical staining was performed for CD1a, CD2, CD3, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, CD10, CD20, CD34, CD79a, CD99, CD117, TdT and PAX5. The neoplastic cells are positive for CD1a, CD2, CD3, CD4, CD7, CD8, CD10 (weak patchy), CD99 (weak) and TdT. They are negative for CD20, CD34, CD79a, CD117 and PAX5. 
    * Flow cytometric analysis was performed using a cell suspension from a small portion of the core biopsy and showed an abnormal T lymphoblastic population positive for cytoplasmic CD3, TdT, CD1a, CD2, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, CD10 (dim) and CD38. The abnormal population is negative for surface CD3, CD34, HLA-DR and B cell antigens. 
    * Overall, the morphologic and immunophenotypic findings are consistent with T lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma. Results of cytogenetic testing are pending. Correlation with clinical, radiographic and pending genetic testing is advised.

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/leukemiaTallDanielson14.jpg

This is a representative H&E image of a mediastinal mass removed from a 16 year old boy. The cells express CD1a, CD2, cytoplasmic CD3, CD7, CD8, CD99 and TdT. Pancytokeratin stain is negative. What is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. B1 thymoma 
  2. Early T cell precursor lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma 
  3. Ewing sarcoma 
  4. T cell lymphoblastic lymphoma 

Practice answer #1

**D.** T cell lymphoblastic lymphoma. CD2, cytoplasmic CD3 and CD7 positivity indicate that the cells are of a T cell lineage. CD1a, cytoplasmic CD3, CD99 and TdT are immaturity markers. Pancytokeratin is negative. Answer A is incorrect because a B1 thymoma will have thymocytes that are positive for T lineage markers and immaturity markers but pancytokeratin will highlight polygonal epithelial cells and there should be a mix of CD4 and CD8 positive thymocytes. Answer B is incorrect because early T cell precursor lymphoblastic leukemia / lymphoma must be negative for CD1a and CD8. Answer C is incorrect because Ewing sarcoma lacks T lineage markers or other immaturity markers.
