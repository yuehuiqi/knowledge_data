Definition / general

  * Cystic fibrosis (CF) is an autosomal recessive disorder with CF transmembrane conductance regulator (_CFTR_) gene mutation affecting the apical membrane of cells 

Essential features

  * _CFTR_ gene mutations lead to a defective chloride transport protein, resulting in inspissated mucus accumulation and a distended appendiceal lumen 
  * Clinical presentation of appendiceal disease in patients with cystic fibrosis can be indolent or nonspecific (high level of suspicion is warranted) 
  * Other manifestations include dilated appendix with inspissated mucin, wall thickening and inflammation 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10 
    * [E84.1](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/E00-E89/E70-E88/E84-/E84.1) \- cystic fibrosis with intestinal manifestations 
    * [K38.8](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K35-K38/K38-/K38.8) \- other specified diseases of appendix 
  * ICD-11 
    * [CA25.Z](https://icd.who.int/browse/2025-01/mms/en#514403112%2Funspecified) \- cystic fibrosis, unspecified 
    * [CA25.0](https://icd.who.int/browse/2025-01/mms/en#290257878) \- classical cystic fibrosis 

Epidemiology

  * Total incidence (U.S.): 1 in 1,900 - 3,700 live births (predominantly non-Hispanic White populations) ([J Cyst Fibros 2023;22:436](36922288)) 
  * Appendiceal disease in cystic fibrosis is typically diagnosed in infancy or early childhood; occasionally reported in young adults ([J Surg Case Rep 2023;2023:rjad117](36937795), [Cystic Fibrosis Foundation: 2023 Patient Registry Annual Data Report [Accessed 8 May 2025]](https://www.cff.org/medical-professionals/patient-registry#read_time)) 
  * Acute appendicitis occurs with an incidence of 1 - 2% compared with an overall incidence of 7% ([J Pediatr Surg 1990;25:835](2205712)) 

Sites

  * Confined to appendix or involving other organs due to complications such as appendicocutaneous fistula, appendicocolic fistula, intussusception or intra-abdominal abscess ([Pediatr Surg Int 2004;20:151](14986036)) 

Pathophysiology

  * _CFTR_ gene mutation produces a defective chloride transport protein 
  * Defective protein causes decreased luminal Cl secretion and increased Na absorption at the apical membrane of appendiceal epithelial cells, resulting in thick, viscid mucus accumulation in the appendiceal lumen ([J Pediatr Surg 2012;47:772](22498395)) 
  * Hypersecretion of the intestinal goblet cells is a feature of cystic fibrosis ([Eur J Pediatr 2016;175:881](27055450)) 
  * Increased luminal distension with mucus and decreased intraluminal fluid predispose to infection and appendiceal luminal obstruction 

Etiology

  * Autosomal recessive disorder associated with mutation in _CFTR_ gene, with ΔF508 being the most common mutation 
  * _CFTR_ gene encodes a cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) regulated chloride channel protein present in the apical membrane of appendiceal epithelial cells, leading to dehydrated luminal mucus in the appendix 

Clinical features

  * Can present with atypical or indolent symptoms, right iliac pain, intestinal obstruction, appendiceal mass 
  * Complications: appendicocutaneous fistula, appendiceal abscess, appendicocolic fistula, intussusception, intra-abdominal abscess or perforation ([Pediatr Surg Int 2004;20:151](14986036), [J Pediatr Surg 1990;25:835](2205712)) 
  * Appendicitis in patients with cystic fibrosis is associated with delayed diagnosis because it is often misdiagnosed as distal intestinal obstruction syndrome 
  * Appendix is larger in adults with cystic fibrosis than in those without it and appears hyperattenuating on unenhanced computed tomography (CT) in 75% of patients, more commonly in those with ΔF508 homozygous mutation ([AJR Am J Roentgenol 2014;202:759](24660703)) 

Diagnosis

  * Diagnosis of appendicitis should be considered in patients with cystic fibrosis who present with pain, prolonged fever and abdominal mass or fluid discharge from the right flank 
  * Further evaluation with radiology, endoscopy and histopathology could confirm the diagnosis 

Radiology description

  * CT: a distended appendix with inspissated mucus, wall thickening and periappendiceal inflammation ([Gut 2007;56:1799](17998329), [J Formos Med Assoc 2012;111:580](23089694)) 
  * Contrast enema: soft tissue mass, which displaces the ileum, together with a fistula between the sigmoid colon and the cecum; appendiceal nonfilling due to mucus plugging 

Case reports

  * 3.5 year old girl with cystic fibrosis was found to have acute appendicitis caused by a mucus plug, despite initially inconclusive findings ([Hamidiye Med J 2020;1:77](https://hamidiyemedj.com/articles/doi/hamidiyemedj.galenos.2020.44154)) 
  * 13 year old cystic fibrosis patient presented with abdominal pain and atypical imaging and lab results but clinical signs led to appendectomy; histopathology confirmed acute appendicitis with ulceration and inflammation ([J Surg Med 2019;3:91](https://jsurgmed.com/article/view/470193)) 
  * 20 year old man with cystic fibrosis presented with right iliac fossa pain due to rare appendiceal intussusception, which resolved spontaneously ([J Surg Case Rep 2023;2023:rjad117](36937795)) 
  * 36 year old man with cystic fibrosis and recurrent bowel obstruction was found to have appendiceal intussusception mimicking a cecal polyp ([Am J Gastroenterol 2018;113:S861](https://journals.lww.com/ajg/fulltext/2018/10001/appendiceal_intussusception_due_to_cystic.1500.aspx)) 

Treatment

  * Early appendicectomy is likely to reduce morbidity ([Pediatr Surg Int 2004;20:151](14986036)) 

Gross description

  * Appendix: distended and firm with a sausage shaped appearance, with or without perforation; irregular serosal surface with focal areas of fibrinopurulent exudate and fibrous adhesions 
  * Cross sectioning reveals diffuse luminal dilatation along the entire appendix, with compacted, hardened, brown-gray, waxy material

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Diffuse distention of the appendix, compressed mucosa with intracryptic mucin and mild appendicitis ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2009;7:A24](19539553)) 
  * Thinned appendiceal mucosa from compression by an expanded central lumen, filled with impacted, inspissated, eosinophilic secretions ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2010;8:A27](20621198)) 
  * Goblet cells within the dilated glands appear swollen (hypersecretion of goblet cells) 
  * Thickened muscularis mucosae and fibrous expansion of the lamina propria with acute and chronic inflammation (can be the leading point for intussusception) ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2010;50:1](19934768))

Positive stains

  * Special stains are not needed to confirm diagnosis 

Sample pathology report

  * Appendix, appendectomy: 
    * Dilated appendix with inspissated luminal mucin (see comment) 
    * Comment: The patient's history of cystic fibrosis is noted. The appendix shows a large amount of inspissated luminal mucin and sloughed cellular debris. There is also focal acute inflammation (cryptitis and crypt abscesses). The findings in the appendix are consistent with changes in response to the inspissated mucin due to patient's known cystic fibrosis. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

A 20 year old man with known cystic fibrosis presents with abdominal pain localized to the right lower quadrant. Imaging reveals an enlarged, mucus filled appendix. What is the most likely histologic finding in the appendix of this patient?   
  
  1. Appendiceal dilation with inspissated mucus 
  2. Epithelial dysplasia with fibrotic changes 
  3. Hyperplasia of the lymphoid tissue 
  4. Mucinous neoplasms 

Practice answer #1

**A**. Appendiceal dilation with inspissated mucus. Cystic fibrosis often leads to mucus obstruction in the appendix, resulting in appendiceal dilation and mucus impaction with or without acute inflammation. Answer D is incorrect because although mucinous neoplasms can present with appendiceal dilation, they do not typically arise as a result of cystic fibrosis. Answer B is incorrect because epithelial dysplasia with fibrotic changes is not a hallmark of cystic fibrosis in the appendix. Answer C is incorrect because hyperplasia of lymphoid tissue is unrelated to cystic fibrosis related appendiceal changes and is more typical in lymphoid hyperplasia.

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/appendixcysticfibrosispezhouh01.jpg

A 44 year old man with cystic fibrosis presents with abdominal pain and distension. Surgery reveals a distended appendix filled with inspissated mucin. A representative histologic image is shown above. What complication is most commonly associated with these findings in cystic fibrosis?   
  
  1. Adenocarcinoma 
  2. Appendiceal perforation 
  3. Carcinoid tumor 
  4. Mucinous neoplasms 

Practice answer #2

**B**. Appendiceal perforation. Inspissated mucus in cystic fibrosis can obstruct the appendiceal lumen, leading to increased intraluminal pressure and secondary inflammation, which commonly results in appendicitis. On very rare occasions, this can cause perforation. Answer A is incorrect because appendiceal adenocarcinoma is a neoplastic process that invades the appendiceal wall and often presents as a mass forming tumor. Answer D is incorrect because mucinous neoplasms are not a typical finding in cystic fibrosis related appendix pathology. Answer C is incorrect because carcinoid tumors are unrelated to mucus retention and appendicitis seen in cystic fibrosis patients.
