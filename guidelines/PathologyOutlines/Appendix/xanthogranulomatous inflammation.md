Definition / general

  * Xanthogranulomatous inflammation (XGI) of the appendix is a rare, chronic inflammatory condition characterized by accumulation of foamy histiocytes and mixed inflammatory cells within the appendiceal wall 
  * It is most commonly identified in specimens from interval appendectomy following a previous episode of complicated or treated appendicitis 

Essential features

  * Usually associated with interval appendectomy 
  * Prominent lipid laden histiocytes in the background of mixed inflammatory cell infiltrates 
  * May appear as a mass lesion, mimicking neoplasms on imaging 
  * Often accompanied by fibrosis and dystrophic calcification 

Terminology

  * Xanthogranulomatous appendicitis (XGA) 
  * Xanthogranulomatous inflammation (XGI) of the appendix 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10 
    * [K36](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K35-K38/K36-/K36) \- other appendicitis 
    * [K37](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K35-K38/K37-/K37) \- unspecified appendicitis 
    * [K35.8](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K35-K38/K35-/K35.8) \- other and unspecified acute appendicitis 
    * [K35.80](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K35-K38/K35-/K35.80) \- unspecified acute appendicitis 
    * [K35.89](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K35-K38/K35-/K35.89) \- other acute appendicitis 
    * [K35.890](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K35-K38/K35-/K35.890) \- other acute appendicitis without perforation or gangrene 
    * [K35.891](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K35-K38/K35-/K35.891) \- other acute appendicitis without perforation, with gangrene 

Epidemiology

  * Rare condition; identified in < 1% of total appendicectomy specimens ([Bangladesh Journal of Medical Science 2014;13:302](https://doi.org/10.3329/bjms.v13i3.16266)) 
  * Usually identified in interval appendectomy specimens ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2022;147:546](36084245), [Histopathology 2022;80:965](35076982), [Am J Surg Pathol 2003;27:1147](12883248)) 
  * Reported in patients ranging from 3 to 78 years old with no clear gender predilection ([World J Gastrointest Surg 2021;13:76](33552395)) 

Sites

  * Appendix 

Pathophysiology

  * Recurrent obstruction of the appendiceal lumen leads to chronic inflammation ([Cureus 2024;16:e68322](39350865), [Ultrastruct Pathol 2000;24:33](10721150)) 
  * Prolonged chronic inflammation, local hypoxia, necrosis, hemorrhage, defective lipid transport and immune response to low virulence pathogens promote histiocyte recruitment, lipid accumulation and xanthogranulomatous inflammation ([World J Gastrointest Surg 2021;13:76](33552395), [Pathol Res Pract 1988;183:395](3054826)) 

Etiology

  * Chronic appendicitis ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2022;147:546](36084245), [Histopathology 2022;80:965](35076982)) 
  * Prior acute appendicitis, periappendicitis, appendiceal abscess, appendiceal perforation and systemic chemotherapy ([Cureus 2024;16:e68322](39350865), [Am J Surg Pathol 2003;27:1147](12883248), [J Clin Diagn Res 2014;8:ND01](25653988), [North Clin Istanb 2024;11:579](39650323), [Int J Surg Case Rep 2024;120:109800](38823225), [Ann Coloproctol 2016;32:83](27218100)) 

Clinical features

  * Usually an incidental finding in interval appendectomy specimens 
  * Can have similar presentation as chronic appendicitis 
  * Can be asymptomatic 
  * Rarely presents with an acute clinical course ([J Clin Diagn Res 2014;8:ND01](25653988), [Case Rep Med 2013;2013:498191](24093025)) 
  * Occasionally presents as a mass lesion on imaging, mimicking an appendiceal tumor ([Int J Surg Case Rep 2024;120:109800](38823225), [Asian J Surg 2022;45:1650](35393225), [J Surg Case Rep 2021;2021:rjab274](34345400), [Med J Malaysia 2021;76:114](33510122)) 

Diagnosis

  * Definitive diagnosis relies on histologic examination 

Laboratory

  * Nonspecific; normal or variably elevated white blood cell count and C reactive protein 

Radiology description

  * Usually, nonspecific radiological findings, including normal to enlarged appendix 
  * Occasionally, a solid or solid / cystic mass can be identified by computed tomography (CT) scan in the right lower quadrant ([Int J Surg Case Rep 2024;120:109800](38823225), [Asian J Surg 2022;45:1650](35393225), [J Surg Case Rep 2021;2021:rjab274](34345400), [Med J Malaysia 2021;76:114](33510122), [J Cytol 2017;34:48](28182060), [Indian J Surg 2013;75:405](24426487)) 
  * Positron emission tomography (PET) may show hypermetabolic lesion in the right lower quadrant ([J Surg Case Rep 2022;2022:rjac482](36324762), [Ann Coloproctol 2016;32:83](27218100))

Prognostic factors

  * Generally favorable prognosis with no recurrence after appendectomy 

Case reports

  * 8 year old boy with Burkitt lymphoma underwent appendectomy ([North Clin Istanb 2024;11:579](39650323)) 
  * 41 year old woman with right iliac fossa pain found to have a right pelvic mass on imaging studies ([Med J Malaysia 2021;76:114](33510122)) 
  * 64 year old woman with recurrent appendicitis ([Cureus 2024;16:e68322](39350865)) 
  * 76 year old woman with right lower abdominal pain found to have a cecal mass on CT ([J Surg Case Rep 2021;2021:rjab274](34345400)) 
  * 78 year old woman with a history of hilar cholangiocarcinoma found to have a mass in the pelvis on CT ([Int J Surg Case Rep 2024;120:109800](38823225)) 

Treatment

  * Generally treated with appendectomy

Gross description

  * Appendix may appear grossly normal, erythematous, thickened, fibrotic or mass-like 
  * Yellowish discoloration can be seen ([North Clin Istanb 2024;11:579](39650323), [Int J Surg Case Rep 2024;120:109800](38823225)) 
  * May contain areas of necrosis and hemorrhage

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Nodular aggregates or diffuse sheets of foamy, lipid laden histiocytes (xanthoma type cells) ([World J Gastrointest Surg 2021;13:76](33552395), [Am J Clin Pathol 2024;161:418](38217538)) 
  * Background mixed inflammatory cell infiltrates (lymphocytes, plasma cells and neutrophils) and scattered multinucleated giant cells 
  * Fibrosis 
  * Dystrophic calcifications can be seen 
  * Epithelioid granulomas not classified as xanthogranulomatous inflammation ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2022;147:546](36084245))

Cytology description

  * Touch preparation (TP) cytology slides may be prepared to evaluate mass-like xanthogranulomatous lesions 
  * Benign glandular epithelial cell groups and sheets of foamy histiocytes along with multinucleated giant cells in the background of mixed inflammation ([J Cytol 2017;34:48](28182060))

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/appendixxanthogranulomatousPQYu1.jpg

Which clinical scenario is most likely associated with the histologic diagnosis of the appendectomy specimen shown above?   
  
  1. Acute appendicitis managed with immediate surgery 
  2. Appendiceal well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor 
  3. Complicated appendicitis treated with antibiotics followed by interval appendectomy 
  4. Pediatric patient with viral gastroenteritis 

Practice answer #1

**C.** Complicated appendicitis treated with antibiotics followed by interval appendectomy. The histologic image shows xanthogranulomatous inflammation of the appendix, which is usually associated with interval appendicectomy. Answer A is incorrect because this is not an acute inflammatory process; acute appendicitis with immediate surgery would not show the chronic inflammatory process of xanthogranulomatous appendicitis (foamy, lipid laden histiocytes, mixed inflammatory cell infiltrates, fibrosis and dystrophic calcifications). Answer B is incorrect because there is no evidence of tumor. Answer D is incorrect because pediatric viral gastroenteritis primarily affects the small intestines, not the appendix as shown in the image.

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/appendixxanthogranulomatousPQYu2.jpg

Which histologic feature distinguishes malakoplakia from xanthogranulomatous appendicitis (shown above)?   
  
  1. Foamy macrophages 
  2. Intracytoplasmic granules in histiocytes with positive PAS stain 
  3. Multinucleated giant cells 
  4. Neutrophilic infiltrate 

Practice answer #2

**B.** Intracytoplasmic granules in histiocytes with positive PAS stain. While both malakoplakia and xanthogranulomatous inflammation are associated with chronic inflammatory conditions, they are histopathologically distinguished by the presence of Michaelis-Gutmann bodies in malakoplakia with positive PAS and von Kossa stains. Answers A, C and D are incorrect because foamy macrophages, multinucleated giant cells and neutrophilic infiltrate can be present in both malakoplakia and xanthogranulomatous appendicitis; therefore, their presence does not help distinguish between these conditions.
