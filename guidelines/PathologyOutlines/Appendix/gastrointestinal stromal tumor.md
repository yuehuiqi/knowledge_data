Definition / general

  * Gastrointestinal stromal tumor arising in the appendix, analogous to those from other digestive sites 

Essential features

  * Extremely rare location for gastrointestinal tumor (GIST) 
  * Almost always incidental / small and indolent 
  * Harbors _KIT_ mutations, as do most GISTs elsewhere 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [C49.A0](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/C00-D49/C45-C49/C49-/C49.A0) \- gastrointestinal stromal tumor, unspecified site 

Epidemiology

  * Very rare, with only 4 cases at Armed Forces Institute of Pathology (AFIP) during 1970 - 1998 ([Am J Surg Pathol 2001;25:1433](11684962)) 
  * F > M in largest series ([Mod Pathol 2022;35:554](34702994)) 
  * Mean age roughly 65 years 

Pathophysiology

  * Activating mutations in _KIT_ or _PDGRFA_ lead to neoplastic proliferation of interstitial cells of Cajal 

Etiology

  * Unknown; these appendiceal tumors are rare and appear sporadic 

Clinical features

  * May be incidental or associated with appendicitis-like symptoms 
  * Nearly all cases appear to follow indolent course, though aggressive behavior (invasion and perforation of adjacent bowel) has been reported ([Mod Pathol 2022;35:554](34702994), [J Gastrointest Cancer 2010;41:9](19960274)) 

Diagnosis

  * Microscopic examination with immunohistochemical confirmation 

Prognostic factors

  * Presumably size and mitotic rate (as for other GISTs) but no large case series has validated this for the appendix 

Case reports

  * 7 year old boy with appendix GIST ([Pediatr Surg Int 2014;30:457](24292407)) 
  * 37 year old man with acute appendicitis ([Cureus 2025;17:e83413](40462812)) 
  * 56 year old man with appendiceal hemorrhage ([World J Gastroenterol 2007;13:3265](17589911)) 
  * 67 year old man with 22 cm appendix mass ([Mol Clin Oncol 2017;7:399](28894578)) 
  * 68 year old man with cystic appendix mass ([Int J Surg Case Rep 2016;21:20](26895113)) 
  * 74 year old man with appendiceal mass on imaging ([Cureus 2024;16:e59780](38846198)) 
  * 74 year old man with incidental appendiceal lesion ([Diagnostics (Basel) 2025;15:924](40218274)) 
  * 75 year old man with acute appendicitis ([Hum Pathol 2013;44:651](23084635)) 

Treatment

  * Surgery 
  * Responds to imatinib if necessary 

Gross description

  * Firm nodule (usually small) expanding and potentially obliterating appendiceal wall

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Resemble GISTs at other sites 
  * Bland eosinophilic spindle cell tumor, often with extracellular collagen globules (skeinoid fibers) ([Diagnostics (Basel) 2025;15:924](40218274)) 
  * Generally no atypia and little / no mitotic activity 
  * May be well circumscribed 
  * Artifactual perinuclear vacuoles may be seen

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * _KIT_ mutations at exon 11 ([Hum Pathol 2008;39:1252](18547614), [Hum Pathol 2013;44:651](23084635)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Appendix, appendectomy: 
    * Acute appendicitis 
    * Incidental gastrointestinal stromal tumor (1.1 cm) (see comment) 
    * Comment: Immunostains show the GIST is positive for _KIT_ and _DOG1_. These tumors are rare in the appendix. This small GIST has no appreciable mitotic activity and therefore likely has no risk of progression. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

Which of the following is true about GISTs of the appendix?  
  
  1. Likely more common in women 
  2. Negative for DOG1 by immunohistochemistry 
  3. Often large with abundant mitotic activity 
  4. Typically have _PDGFRA_ mutations 

Practice answer #1

**A.** Likely more common in women. GISTs of the appendix are rare. The largest series of them included twice as many female patients as male patients. Answer B is incorrect because like at other sites, appendiceal GIST is almost always positive for DOG1. Answer C is incorrect because appendiceal GISTs are almost always small with minimal mitotic activity. Answer D is incorrect because appendiceal GISTs typically have _KIT_ mutations.

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/appendixGIST_Gonzalez02.jpg

This mesenchymal tumor was found incidentally within an appendix removed for appendicitis. What immunohistochemical stains would most likely be positive in this tumor?   
  
A. CD117 and DOG1  
B. Desmin and SMA  
C. S100  
D. Synaptophysin and chromogranin   

Practice answer #2

**A.** CD117 and DOG1. This lesion is a gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST), as evidenced by the bland spindled cells, perinuclear vacuoles and skeinoid fibers. The appendix is a rare location but as with GISTs elsewhere in the gastrointestinal tract, they are positive for CD117 and DOG1 by immunohistochemistry. Answer B is incorrect because leiomyoma, not GIST, would be positive for desmin and SMA. Answer C is incorrect because schwannoma, not GIST, would be positive for S100. Answer D is incorrect because neuroendocrine tumor, not GIST, would be positive for synaptophysin and chromogranin.
