Definition / general

  * Rare, benign tumor of the urinary bladder showing delicate fibrovascular cores lined by squamous epithelium 

Essential features

  * Rare, benign bladder lesion with female predilection 
  * Presents with gross hematuria and irritative bladder symptoms 
  * Recurrence is rare 
  * Not related to condyloma acuminatum 

Terminology

  * Squamous papilloma 

ICD coding

  * ICD-O: [8052/0](https://apps.who.int/iris/bitstream/handle/10665/96612/9789241548496_eng.pdf?sequence=1#page=63) \- squamous cell papilloma, NOS 
  * ICD-10: [D30.3](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/C00-D49/D10-D36/D30-/D30.3) \- benign neoplasm of bladder 

Epidemiology

  * Rare, benign urothelial lesion with < 20 cases reported in known literature 
  * F > M (6:1) ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227)) 
  * Fourth to seventh decade ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227)) 
  * Can arise de novo or along with previous urothelial lesions 

Sites

  * Most cases involve the dome and the lateral and posterior walls of the bladder ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Largely unknown 

Etiology

  * Smoking 
  * Occupational exposure to amines 

Clinical features

  * Macroscopic hematuria 
  * Irritative voiding symptoms (e.g., stress incontinence, neurogenic bladder, dysuria) ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227), [Am J Surg Pathol 2006;30:883](16819332)) 

Diagnosis

  * Cystoscopy: papillary / exophytic, whitish plaque, erythematous variable size lesions, often unifocal ([Case Rep Pathol 2013;2013:486312](24159399)) 

Laboratory

  * Urine cytology is negative for malignant cells 

Radiology description

  * Ultrasonography may show thickened bladder wall 

Prognostic factors

  * Recurrence is very rare and progression to carcinoma has not been reported ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227)) 

Case reports

  * 32 - 82 year old patients with squamous cell papilloma of urinary bladder, including squamous papilloma related to HPV ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227)) 
  * 74 year old man with irritative voiding symptoms and exophytic mass found at the mucosa of the floor and the posterior wall of the bladder ([Case Rep Pathol 2013;2013:486312](24159399)) 
  * Noninvasive squamous lesions in the urinary bladder; a case series with 5 reported cases of squamous cell papilloma of the urinary bladder ([Am J Surg Pathol 2006;30:883](16819332)) 

Treatment

  * Resection: transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT) ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227))

Cystoscopy finding: whitish exophytic lesion

Gross description

  * Small, whitish, polypoid lesion 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Multiple, free floating papillary fronds with thin, well developed fibrovascular cores composed of bland, benign appearing squamous cells ([Case Rep Pathol 2013;2013:486312](24159399)) 
  * No dysplasia seen 
  * Stromal invasion is not seen 
  * Hyperplasia and mitotic activity restricted to basal / parabasal layers ([Case Rep Pathol 2013;2013:486312](24159399)) 
  * Koilocytes are rare or absent ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227))

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * DNA ploidy: diploid ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227)) 
  * Negative for HPV DNA ([Cancer 2000;88:1679](10738227)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Urinary bladder, lateral wall, transurethral resection of bladder tumor: 
    * Squamous papilloma (see comment) 
    * Comment: Microscopic examination shows multiple papillae with fibrovascular core lined by keratinized squamous epithelium. No evidence of stromal invasion, atypia or koilocytic changes seen in the biopsy submitted. Clinical correlation with follow up is advised. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

Squamous papilloma of the urinary bladder is which of the following?   
  
  1. Benign lesion 
  2. Carcinoma in situ 
  3. Malignant 
  4. Papillary urothelial neoplasm of unknown malignant potential (PUNLMP) 

Practice answer #1

**A**. Benign lesion

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/bladdersquamouspapillomabatra02.jpg https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/bladdercondylomaEpsteinupdated3.jpg

Which of the following histopathological features distinguishes squamous papilloma of the bladder from condyloma acuminatum?   
  
  1. Absence of fibrovascular cores in the papillae 
  2. Absence of koilocytes 
  3. Presence of invasion into submucosa 
  4. Presence of umbrella cells 

Practice answer #2

**B**. Absence of koilocytes. Squamous papilloma shows absence of koilocytic change, whereas koilocytes are present in [condyloma acuminatum](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/bladdercondyloma.html).
