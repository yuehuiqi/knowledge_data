Definition / general

  * Variant of urothelial carcinoma; morphologically indistinguishable from sarcoma 
  * Heterologous elements may be present ([Histopathology 2019;74:77](30565299)) 

Essential features

  * Biphasic malignant neoplasm with morphologic and immunohistochemical evidence of both epithelial and mesenchymal differentiation 
  * Rare (0.3 - 0.6% of all urothelial carcinomas) and biologically aggressive variant 
  * Associated with radiation therapy or cyclophosphamide 
  * If present, heterologous elements should be mentioned in the report 

Terminology

  * WHO recommends the term sarcomatoid variant of urothelial carcinoma for these biphasic malignant neoplasms 
  * Terms carcinosarcoma, metaplastic carcinoma, spindle cell carcinoma and malignant mixed tumor have been used interchangeably but can lead to confusion and are therefore discouraged

Epidemiology

  * Estimated to represent 0.3 - 0.6% of all urothelial carcinomas ([Mod Pathol 2009;22:S96](19494856), [Eur Urol Focus 2020;6:653](31530497)) 
  * Seen in up to 2.4% of all high grade urothelial carcinomas in renal pelvis 
  * Age 45 - 82 years (mean 71.6); M > F (2 - 3:1) ([Oncol Lett 2014;8:1208](25120689), [Pathol Res Pract 1996;192:1218](9182292)) 
  * Although rare, sarcomatoid carcinoma is more common than primary sarcoma 

Sites

  * Kidney pelvis, proximal ureter and bladder 

Pathophysiology

  * _TERT_ C228T promoter mutations in 35% of sarcomatoid carcinoma ([Histopathology 2019;74:77](30565299), [Eur Urol Focus 2020;6:653](31530497)) 

Etiology

  * Probably has a common malignant clonal origin, with epithelial and mesenchymal differentiation ([J Pathol 2007;211:420](17236170)) 
  * Common final pathway of all forms of bladder tumors, supported by molecular and morphologic evidence ([Histopathology 2019;74:77](30565299)) 
  * Risk factors include previous exposure to radiotherapy and intravesicular cyclophosphamide ([Eur Urol Focus 2020;6:653](31530497)) 

Clinical features

  * Gross hematuria, flank pain, an abdominal mass and hydronephrosis 
  * Similar to those of conventional urothelial tumors 
  * Reference: [ISRN Urol 2014;2014:794563](24587922)

Diagnosis

  * Cystoscopy 
  * CT scan 
  * MRI 
  * Reference: [ISRN Urol 2014;2014:794563](24587922)

Laboratory

  * Hematuria 

Radiology description

  * Thickened bladder wall with or without intraluminal papillary or nodular mass; heterologous elements such as calcification in osteosarcoma may be demonstrated ([Radiographics 2006;26:553](16549617)) 

Prognostic factors

  * Poor prognosis, as frequently presents at an advanced stage and is associated with a worse overall survival when compared with pure urothelial carcinoma ([Eur Urol Focus 2020;6:653](31530497)) 
  * Similar to bladder, sarcomatoid urothelial carcinoma of renal pelvis has a worse prognosis on univariate analysis ([ISRN Urol 2014;2014:794563](24587922), [Oncol Lett 2014;8:1208](25120689), [J Urol 2012;188:398](22698626)) 
  * Associated with higher tumor stage, multifocality, tumor necrosis, frequent metastases at presentation; however, insufficient case numbers in kidney to confirm independent negative prognostic impact on multivariate analysis ([J Urol 2012;188:398](22698626)) 
  * May have heterologous elements, without definite prognostic significance ([Mod Pathol 2009;22:S96](19494856)) 

Case reports

  * 49 year old man with urothelial carcinoma arising in renal pelvis with exuberant chondrosarcomatous element ([Indian J Pathol Microbiol 2014;57:284](24943767)) 
  * 63 year old man with squamous cell and sarcomatoid variant urothelial carcinoma ([BMC Surg 2021;21:96](33612111)) 
  * 68 year old man with sarcomatoid carcinoma arising in renal pelvis ([Open J Pathol 2013;3:96](http://www.scirp.org/JOURNAL/PaperInformation.aspx?PaperID=30269)) 
  * 72 year old man with sarcomatoid urothelial carcinoma of the ureter with heterologous elements of chondrosarcoma and osteosarcoma and divergent squamous differentiation ([Urol Case Rep 2020;34:101484](33224730)) 
  * Sarcomatoid carcinoma of renal pelvis with giant cell tumor-like features ([Int J Urol 2005;12:199](15733116)) 

Treatment

  * Chemotherapy resistant ([Eur Urol Focus 2020;6:653](31530497)) 
  * No standard treatment due to rarity ([Cancer Cell Int 2020;20:550](33292281)) 

Gross description

  * Gray fleshy cut surface with infiltrative margins similar to sarcoma in appearance

Frozen section description

  * Not typically diagnosed on frozen section 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Most common component is undifferentiated high grade spindle cell sarcoma ([Histopathology 2019;74:77](30565299)) 
  * Sarcomatoid areas admixed with conventional high grade urothelial carcinoma 
  * Spindle cell component may account for 10% to > 60% of tumor 
  * Epithelial component has urothelial differentiation or less commonly squamous or glandular differentiation 
  * Highly variable and can mimic nonepithelial neoplasms ([J Biol Chem 2019;294:1579](30530497)) 
  * Most common heterologous element is osteosarcoma, followed by chondrosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, liposarcoma and angiosarcoma ([Histopathology 2019;74:77](30565299))

Negative stains

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * Overexpression of markers representative of epithelial - mesenchymal transition, including vimentin, FOXC2, SNAIL and ZEB1 ([Histopathology 2019;74:77](30565299)) 
  * _TERT_ C228T promoter mutations in 35% of sarcomatoid carcinoma ([Histopathology 2019;74:77](30565299), [Eur Urol Focus 2020;6:653](31530497)) 
  * Studies in urinary bladder showed: 
    * Similar patterns of allelic losses ([J Pathol 2007;211:420](17236170)) 
    * Similar _TP53_ point mutations between the carcinomatous and sarcomatous elements ([Mod Pathol 2009;22:113](18997737)) 
  * This suggests a common clonal origin with divergent differentiation ([J Pathol 2007;211:420](17236170)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Bladder, transurethral resection: 
    * High grade urothelial carcinoma with sarcomatoid differentiation (80%) (see comment) 
    * Comment: Invasive of muscularis propria (T2) 
    * Angiolymphatic invasion absent 
    * Muscularis propria present, involved by tumor

A 65 year old presented with bladder wall thickening on ultrasound. Transurethral resection of bladder tumor is performed and shows a lesion with features on H&E as seen in the image shown above. Which of the following is likely in the patient’s medical history?   
  
  1. Asbestos exposure 
  2. Family history of prostate adenocarcinoma 
  3. High nitrate consumption in their diet 
  4. Intravesical cyclophosphamide 
  5. Woodworking hobby 

Practice answer #1

**D**. Intravesical cyclophosphamide has been associated with the development of sarcomatoid variant of urothelial carcinoma ([Eur Urol Focus 2020;6:653](31530497))
