Definition / general

  * Nonspecific inflammatory disease of the bladder characterized by lymphoid follicles in lamina propria, often accompanied by other chronic inflammatory changes 

Essential features

  * Diagnosis established by histopathologic finding of lymphoid follicles in lamina propria 
  * Most commonly associated with chronic urinary tract infection but can also accompany a number of pathologies associated with chronic inflammation 
  * Low grade lymphomas of the bladder, while rare, represent the main differential diagnosis to exclude in florid cases, which can present as mass forming lesions 

Terminology

  * Lymphofollicular cystitis 
  * Previous term: cystitis follicularis 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [N30.2](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/N00-N99/N30-N39/N30-/N30.2) \- other chronic cystitis 

Epidemiology

  * Female predominant, mirroring rates of urinary tract infections ([Diagn Cytopathol 2019;47:1223](31587518), [J Urol 2005;173:933](15711342)) 
  * More common in postmenopausal period ([EJIFCC 2024;35:294](39810893), [J Urol 2023;209:928](36715657)) 
  * Present in 35% of patients with chronic urinary tract infection ([J Clin Pathol 1974;27:297](4855039)) 

Sites

  * While the trigone is more commonly affected, some case series report a higher frequency of involvement at the dome ([Paraplegia 1995;33:565](8848310), [Diagn Cytopathol 2019;47:1223](31587518)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Arises in a background of nonspecific chronic inflammatory changes 
  * May indicate an ineffective and overexuberant immune response to uropathogens 
  * Associated with increasing age and reproductive senescence ([J Urol 2023;209:928](36715657)) 

Etiology

  * Most commonly associated with prolonged urinary tract infection (typically bacterial) 
  * Can also arise in setting of urothelial malignancy, bladder outlet obstruction, neurogenic or muscular dysfunction of the bladder, intravesical chemotherapy and Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) therapy ([Clin Cancer Res 2003;9:5550](14654535), [J Urol 1990;143:330](2405189)) 
  * Reported in association with intradetrusor botulinum toxin injection ([Urol Case Rep 2017;14:38](28808621)) 

Clinical features

  * Nonspecific symptoms including dysuria and increased urinary frequency ([Arch Esp Urol 2007;60:77](17408179)) 
  * In the setting of urinary tract infection, follicular cystitis is a feature of chronic inflammation ([Diagn Cytopathol 2007;35:537](17636498)) 
  * May be associated with an increased risk for bladder cancer but data is inconsistent ([Cancer Causes Control 2007;18:839](17593531), [World J Urol 2018;36:839](29404674)) 

Diagnosis

  * Histopathologic examination required for diagnosis 
  * Urine cytology has low sensitivity and high specificity ([Diagn Cytopathol 2019;47:1223](31587518)) 

Laboratory

  * Urinalysis may show bacteriuria with elevated nitrites, leukocytes or erythrocytes 
  * Urine culture useful in identifying potential infectious etiologic agents 
    * Most common uropathogens: _Escherichia coli, Proteus mirabilis, Klebsiella pneumoniae_ and _Staphylococcus saprophyticus_ ([Clin Infect Dis 2011;52:e103](21292654), [Curr Infect Dis Rep 2013;15:109](23378123))

Radiology description

  * Urinary tract ultrasonography and computed tomography (CT) urograms are typically normal ([Asian J Urol 2021;8:235](33996482)) 
  * Can present as a pseudotumor ([Int J Urol 2004;11:1028](15509211), [Nihon Hinyokika Gakkai Zasshi 1989;80:899](2796107)) 

Prognostic factors

  * Associated with shorter symptom free intervals in patients with recurrent urinary tract infections ([J Urol 2023;209:928](36715657)) 

Case reports

  * 12 year old boy with dysuria, hematuria and hypogastric pain ([EJIFCC 2024;35:294](39810893)) 
  * 46 year old man with dysuria and urothelial nodularity on cystoscopy ([Asian J Urol 2021;8:235](33996482)) 
  * 87 year old woman presenting emergently with gross hematuria ([Int J Surg Case Rep 2024;126:110776](39731792)) 

Treatment

  * Antibiotic therapy associated with resolution ([J Urol 2023;209:928](36715657)) 
  * Electrofulguration and radiotherapy have been explored as possible adjuncts in antibiotic refractory cases ([Expert Rev Anti Infect Ther 2024;22:1157](39387179), [Rare Tumors 2012;4:e25](22826782))

Gross description

  * Significant overlap with cystitis cystica in gross appearance ([J Urol 2023;209:928](36715657)) 
  * Bladder mucosa may appear erythematous or nodular ([J Turgut Ozal Med Cent 2017:24:117](http://doi.org/10.5455/jtomc.2016.03.041)) 
  * Can present as a pseudotumor ([Int J Urol 2004;11:1028](15509211), [Nihon Hinyokika Gakkai Zasshi 1989;80:899](2796107)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Dense lymphoplasmacytic inflammation in lamina propria with tertiary lymphoid structure formation ([J Urol 2023;209:928](36715657)) 
  * Florid lesions may resemble Peyer patches of the intestine, with prominent lymphoid follicles subadjacent to surface epithelium 
  * Lymphocytes are polymorphous: germinal centers contain centroblasts admixed with smaller centrocytes and tingible body macrophages; T cells are concentrated perifollicularly ([Front Immunol 2024;15:1413067](39026670)) 
  * Commonly accompanies other chronic reactive conditions, such as cystitis cystica et glandularis, papillary cystitis, Von Brunn nests, nephrogenic adenoma and malakoplakia 
  * May accompany urothelial carcinoma, both noninvasive and invasive

Cytology description

  * Urothelial cells with reactive and degenerative changes, including anisonucleosis without increased nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio and glandular metaplasia 
  * Increased number of immature, mature and reactive lymphocytes ([J Am Soc Cytopathol 2015;4:107](31051690)) 
  * Tingible body macrophages are rarely seen but are highly suggestive of the diagnosis when present 
  * Significance and reproducibility of identifying follicular dendritic cells is less established ([Diagn Cytopathol 2019;47:1223](31587518))

Positive stains

  * B cells express typical markers ([CD45](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd45.html), [PAX5](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspax5.html), [CD20](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd20.html), [CD19](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd19.html), [CD79a](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd79a.html)) 
  * Activated B cells express [CD10](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd10.html) (confirms presence of germinal centers) 

Negative stains

  * Lacks [kappa](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainskappa.html) or [lambda light chain](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainskappa.html) restriction 
  * Lacks [CD10](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd10.html) and [BCL2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsbcl2.html) coexpression 

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * Lymphoid proliferation lacks monoclonal _IgH_ rearrangements 

Sample pathology report

  * Bladder, biopsy: 
    * Follicular cystitis (see comment) 
    * Muscularis propria sampled 
    * Negative for malignancy 
    * Comment: The histologic finding of lymphoid follicles in the lamina propria is diagnostic of follicular cystitis. The sampled urothelium is otherwise unremarkable. 
  
  * Bladder mass (trigone), biopsy: 
    * Follicular cystitis (see comment) 
    * Muscularis propria sampled 
    * Negative for malignancy 
    * Comment: The histologic finding of lymphoid follicles in the lamina propria is diagnostic of follicular cystitis. In florid cases, follicular cystitis can appear as a pseudotumor on cystoscopy. The morphologic and immunohistochemical evaluation shows no evidence of lymphoma. The sampled urothelium is otherwise unremarkable.

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/bladderfollicularDownes02.jpg

A 68 year old woman with an unremarkable medical history had been experiencing dysuria with urinary frequency for 3 months. Physical examination and laboratory investigations, including urine analysis and culture, showed evidence of a urinary tract infection. She was prescribed a course of antibiotics but presented a month later with the same symptoms seen previously. Abdominal / pelvic ultrasound was negative. A cystoscopy was performed and showed areas of urothelial nodularity. No mass or lesion suspicious for malignancy was seen and urine cytology was negative. Bladder biopsies showed the histology above. What is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. Follicular cystitis 
  2. Granulomatous cystitis 
  3. Interstitial cystitis 
  4. Lymphoma 

Practice answer #1

**A**. Follicular cystitis. Prolonged urinary tract infection is the most common risk factor for follicular cystitis. Histopathology showing the presence of lymphoid follicles in the lamina propria of the bladder is diagnostic of this condition. Answer C is incorrect because the presence of secondary lymphoid follicles excludes the diagnosis of interstitial cystitis. Answer D is incorrect because the lymphoid proliferation is nonexpansile and consists predominantly of small polymorphous lymphocytes. Answer B is incorrect because there are no collections of epithelioid histiocytes.

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/bladderfollicularDownes03.jpg

A 70 year old man with known bladder outlet obstruction presents with worsening dysuria. Urine culture is positive for growth of _Klebsiella pneumoniae_. A cystoscopy was performed and showed patchy urothelial erythema at the trigone. Bladder biopsies showed the histology above. Which is true regarding follicular cystitis?   
  
  1. Considered a premalignant lesion 
  2. Has a characteristic appearance on cystoscopy 
  3. Lymphoid follicles are an essential histologic feature 
  4. More common in men 

Practice answer #2

**C**. Lymphoid follicles are an essential histologic feature. The diagnosis of follicular cystitis is established by the histologic finding of lymphoid follicles in the lamina propria of the bladder. Answer D is incorrect because follicular cystitis is more common in women. Answer A is incorrect because while follicular cystitis is considered a risk factor for the development of urothelial carcinoma, it is not considered to be a premalignant lesion. Answer B is incorrect because follicular cystitis can exhibit multiple appearances on cystoscopy, including erythema, diffuse nodularity or more rarely, a mass forming lesion.
