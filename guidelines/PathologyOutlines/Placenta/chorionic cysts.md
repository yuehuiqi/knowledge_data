Definition / general

  * Chorionic cysts are found within the chorionic plate, placental septa and membranes and are composed of extravillous trophoblast with serous, serosanguinous or colloid-like contents 

Essential features

  * Pseudocysts composed of extravillous trophoblast with serous, serosanguinous or colloid-like contents 
  * Chorionic plate and septal cysts can be large and seen on gross examination 
  * Chorionic cysts in membranes are typically microscopic findings in the cellular chorion 
  * Can be associated with intrauterine growth restriction and conditions causing hypoxia (diabetes, maternal floor infarction, massive perivillous fibrin deposition, RhD alloimmunization, high altitude, hypertensive disorders) 

Terminology

  * Septal cysts 
  * Extravillous trophoblast cysts 
  * Chorionic plate cysts 
  * Subchorionic cysts 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [O43.19](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/O00-O9A/O30-O48/O43-/O43.19) \- other malformation of placenta 
  * ICD-11: [JA8A.1](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#2006514776) \- malformation of placenta 

Epidemiology

  * Prevalence of chorionic cysts is reported at 14 - 28% in delivered placentas ([Baergen: Manual of Pathology of the Human Placenta, 2nd Edition, 2011](https://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1441974938/pathologyoutl-20), [Pediatr Dev Pathol 2012;15:213](22372477)) 

Sites

  * Placenta 

Pathophysiology

  * Extravillous trophoblast layer constitutes part of the chorionic plate, placental septa and membranes; 2 different theories explain the formation of chorionic cysts 
    * Active secretion of substances by extravillous trophoblastic cells with secondary formation of these spaces ([Pathology 2010;42:125](20085513)) 
    * In the presence of hypoxia, the diffusion of nutrients is impaired, which results in cellular degeneration and liquefaction of cellular aggregates; the cystic degeneration of trophoblast can enlarge and form ovoid structures that are grossly identifiable ([Pediatr Dev Pathol 2007;10:192](17535086)) 

Etiology

  * Etiology is uncertain 
  * Possible explanations include 
    * Active secretion of substance by trophoblastic cells forming these spaces 
    * Damage to the trophoblast with secondary deposition of acellular material forming the cysts 
  * Chorionic cysts can be associated with intrauterine growth restriction and conditions that cause hypoxia, such as ([Pediatr Dev Pathol 2011;14:1](20465421), [Pediatr Dev Pathol 2007;10:192](17535086)) 
    * Diabetes mellitus 
    * Maternal floor infarction 
    * Massive perivillous fibrin deposition 
    * Rh alloimmunization 
    * High altitude 
    * Hypertensive disorders 

Diagnosis

  * Chorionic plate cysts can be diagnosed on prenatal ultrasound scans or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) 
  * Gross examination and histological evaluation of the placenta can diagnose chorionic cysts postnatally 

Radiology description

  * Prenatal ultrasound: multiple or single anechoic cystic formation(s) along the fetal surface of the placenta 
  * MRI: relatively well circumscribed, fluid filled lesion(s) located along the chorionic plate or within the interlobar septa with high signal intensity on T2 weighted images, consistent with simple fluid content and low signal intensity on T1 weighted images 
  * Reference: [J Ultrasound Med 2002;21:641](12054300)

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Chorionic cysts are pseudocysts formed secondary to the distension of extravillous trophoblast chorionic layer by intracystic contents 
  * Cysts lined by extravillous trophoblast located at chorionic plate, placental septa and membranes 
  * Cyst content can be serous, serosanguinous, eosinophilic or proteinaceous-like 
  * References: [Pediatr Dev Pathol 2007;10:192](17535086), [Pathology 2010;42:125](20085513), [Pediatr Dev Pathol 2011;14:1](20465421)

Sample pathology report

  * Placenta, delivery: 
    * Chorionic cyst (chorionic plate), measuring 0.5 cm in greatest dimension 

  * Placenta, delivery: 
    * Chorionic cyst (septal), measuring 2 cm in greatest dimension 

Differential diagnosis

  * Subamniotic / subchorionic [hematoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/placentahematoma.html) and subchorionic **thrombi** : 
    * Predominant components in hematomas and thrombi are blood and fibrin 
    * Location above or below the cellular chorion argues against chorionic cysts 
  * Intervillous and septal **thrombi** : 
    * Laminated thrombus with lateral displacement of the chorionic villi

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/placentacystsZanfagnin06.jpg

A placenta from a 37 year old gravida 1 para 0 woman with type 2 diabetes at 36 weeks of gestational age was examined. 3 cysts up to 1 cm are seen on gross examination. Microscopic appearance of the cyst is represented in the picture above. What are the cells lining the cysts composed of?   
  
  1. Decidual stromal cells 
  2. Endothelial cells 
  3. Extravillous trophoblast 
  4. Syncytiotrophoblast 

Practice answer #1

**C**. Extravillous trophoblast. Chorionic cysts can be associated with maternal diabetes mellitus and are composed of extravillous trophoblast. Answer B is incorrect because endothelial cells are present in vessels and are not involved in the formation of chorionic cysts. Answer D is incorrect because syncytiotrophoblast cells are the main cellular interface / barrier between the maternal blood filled space and the placental villi and do not form chorionic cysts. Answer A is incorrect because decidual stromal cells are derived from the endometrial stroma and do not form chorionic cysts.

Practice question #2

What are chorionic cysts associated with?   
  
  1. Intrauterine growth restriction 
  2. Placenta accreta spectrum 
  3. Repeated C sections 
  4. TORCH infections 

Practice answer #2

**A**. Intrauterine growth restriction. Chorionic cysts can be associated with intrauterine growth restriction and conditions that cause hypoxia. Answer C is incorrect because repeated C sections are not involved in the pathogenesis of chorionic cysts. Answer D is incorrect because TORCH infections do not cause formation of chorionic cysts. Answer B is incorrect because an abnormal implantation of the placenta is not the pathogenetic cause of chorionic cysts.
