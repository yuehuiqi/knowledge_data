Definition / general

  * Vasculitis of the cervix is an uncommon inflammatory condition characterized by leukocytic infiltration and destruction of predominately medium sized muscular arteries within the uterine cervix 
  * Usually an incidental finding and not associated with a systemic (autoimmune) vasculitis 

Essential features

  * Rare and often incidental finding on cervical or gynecologic specimens ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
  * 2 predominant histologic patterns 
    * Polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis 
      * Transmural neutrophilic inflammation and fibrinoid necrosis of medium sized arteries 
      * Seen in younger women 
      * Usually focal, confined to the cervix ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
    * Giant cell arteritis type vasculitis 
      * Granulomatous inflammation with multinucleated giant cells and elastic lamina disruption of medium to large arteries 
      * More common in postmenopausal women 
      * More widespread involvement (cervix, uterus, adnexa) ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
  * Most cases are not associated with systemic (autoimmune) vasculitis ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 
  * No further treatment is indicated beyond complete surgical removal ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175), [Eur J Obstet Gynecol Reprod Biol 1980;10:401](7190939)) 

Terminology

  * Cervical vasculitis 
  * Isolated vasculitis of the cervix 
  * Single organ vasculitis of the female genital tract 
  * Gynecologic vasculitis 
  * Necrotizing arteritis of the cervix 
  * Giant cell arteritis of the uterine cervix 
  * Granulomatous cervicitis with vasculitis 
  * Leukocytoclastic vasculitis 
  * Hypersensitivity vasculitis 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [N72](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/N00-N99/N70-N77/N72-/N72) \- inflammatory disease of cervix uteri 
  * ICD-11 
    * [4A43.Y](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#636081762%2Fother) \- other specified overlap non-organ specific systemic autoimmune disease 
    * [GA04](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#1433247301) \- cervicitis 

Epidemiology

  * Rare; seen in 0.04% of gynecologic specimens ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
  * Average age is 50.5 years; however, can occur in wide age range (18 - 83 years) ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
    * Polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis 
      * Predilection for younger women ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
      * More common subtype, accounting for 75 - 80% of cases ([Mod Pathol 1994;7:610](7937728)) 
    * Giant cell arteritis type vasculitis 
      * More likely in postmenopausal women ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
      * Accounts for 10 - 20% of cases ([Mod Pathol 1994;7:610](7937728)) 

Sites

  * Typically confined to the cervix (65%, 30/46 cases) ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
  * Polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis is more likely to be isolated to the cervix ([Mod Pathol 1994;7:610](7937728)) 
  * Giant cell arteritis type vasculitis is more often multifocal and involving parametrial or uterine arteries ([Mod Pathol 1994;7:610](7937728)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis 
    * Immune complex mediated destruction of medium sized muscular arteries ([Autoimmun Rev 2016;15:564](26884100)) 
    * Activation of complement cascade → neutrophil recruitment → fibrinoid necrosis ([Autoimmun Rev 2016;15:564](26884100)) 
    * Vessel wall ischemia and thrombosis from transmural inflammation ([Autoimmun Rev 2016;15:564](26884100)) 
    * Likely represents a localized variant of polyarteritis nodosa confined to uterine or cervical arteries ([Curr Rheumatol Rep 2021;23:49](34196889)) 
  * Giant cell arteritis type vasculitis 
    * Autoimmune T cell mediated attack on vessel wall proteins (elastin in internal elastic lamina) ([Circ Res 2023;132:238](36656970)) 
    * Chronic granulomatous inflammation → multinucleated giant cells form in arterial wall ([Circ Res 2023;132:238](36656970)) 
    * Intimal hyperplasia → luminal narrowing or obliteration ([Circ Res 2023;132:238](36656970)) 
    * Immunopathology mirrors that of giant cell arteritis but is localized to uterine or cervical arteries ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 

Etiology

  * Etiology is largely unknown but it is thought to be an immune mediated process 
  * May occur after cervical cone biopsy or loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP) ([Interv Med Appl Sci 2016;8:93](28386466)) 
  * May be attributable to drug exposure (antibiotics such as penicillin and vancomycin, antihypertensives, hormone replacement therapy, aspirin) ([Eur J Obstet Gynecol Reprod Biol 1980;10:401](7190939), [Pharmacotherapy 2009;29:846](19558258), [Int J Gynecol Pathol 1998;17:193](9656113)) 
  * Nonneoplastic, neoplastic and infectious pathologies may serve to trigger vasculitis 
    * Nonneoplastic 
      * Endometriosis, polyps, uterine prolapse, complications after cesarean section ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 
    * Neoplastic 
      * Co-existing leiomyomas are frequent ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 
      * Local or metastatic tumors to the gynecologic tract 
        * Primary pathology in the hysterectomy specimen (any sort of uterine, fallopian tube, ovarian or cervical neoplasia) ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
    * Infectious 
      * Sexually transmitted infections 
        * Syphilis, chlamydia, neisseria, HPV, HIV, hepatitis B ([StatPearls: Leukocytoclastic Vasculitis [Accessed 3 November 2025]](29489227)) 
      * Other 
        * _Mycobacterium tuberculosis_ , _Staphylococcus aureus_ , hepatitis C ([Rheumatology (Oxford) 2025;64:1400](38507705), [Cureus 2024;16:e60867](38910712), [J Adv Res 2017;8:99](28149646)) 

Clinical features

  * Mostly asymptomatic or with abnormal uterine bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding or pelvic pain, most commonly due to uterine leiomyomas or prolapse ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
  * May have a concurrent dysplastic or malignant process (uterine, fallopian tube, ovarian or cervical neoplasia or carcinoma) 
  * Systemic (autoimmune) vasculitic syndromes that may be found in association 
    * Polyarteritis nodosa with or without rheumatoid arthritis (RA) may occur after discovering Polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis in the cervix ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
    * Giant cell arteritis has been seen in association with gynecologic giant cell arteritis type arteritis ([South Med J 2005;98:469](15898526)) 
    * Antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA) associated vasculitis 
      * Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA) may rarely involve the gynecologic tract in < 1% of cases ([J Pers Med 2022;12:289](35207776)) 
    * Chronic systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) or rheumatoid arthritis may be associated with leukocytoclastic or polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis in various organs ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
    * Behçet disease, with genital or cervical mucosal ulcers ([Am J Obstet Gynecol 2024;231:657](39097130)) 

Diagnosis

  * Cervical excision procedures (cone biopsy, LEEP) or hysterectomy specimens 
  * Vasculitis diagnosis is rarely, if ever, made on simple colposcopic biopsy ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 

Laboratory

  * Vasculitis seen in the gynecologic tract is usually an isolated finding ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
  * Negative for serologic evidence of systemic (autoimmune) vasculitis ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 
  * Extensive lab workup may be warranted in the setting of systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss, arthralgias) ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 
    * Autoimmune workup: antinuclear antibody (ANA), rheumatoid factor (RF), cryoglobulins, complement levels (C3, C4), perinuclear and cytoplasmic antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies ([Hawaii J Health Soc Welf 2024;83:81](38456159)) 
    * Infectious workup: Venereal Disease Research Laboratory (VDRL) test, hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), hepatitis C virus antibodies, HIV ([Hawaii J Health Soc Welf 2024;83:81](38456159)) 
    * General markers of systemic inflammation (erythrocyte sedimentation rate [ESR], C reactive protein [CRP]) ([Hawaii J Health Soc Welf 2024;83:81](38456159)) 

Prognostic factors

  * Vasculitis isolated to the gynecologic tract has an excellent prognosis, with a mortality rate of essentially zero related to the lesion itself ([Eur J Obstet Gynecol Reprod Biol 1980;10:401](7190939), [Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 
  * Many (91%; 42 / 46) patients do not have concurrent nor do they develop systemic vasculitis ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
  * Recurrence is rare but may be seen in subsequent gynecologic specimens if hysterectomy was not performed ([Hawaii J Health Soc Welf 2024;83:81](38456159)) 

Case reports

  * 25 year old woman with low grade squamous intraepithelial neoplasia (LSIL) on Pap and leukocytoclastic vasculitis (LCV) of the cervix on cervical biopsy ([Hawaii J Health Soc Welf 2024;83:81](38456159)) 
  * 29 year old woman with a cervical ulcer revealing inflammatory granulation tissue and an ultimate diagnosis of Behçet disease ([Am J Obstet Gynecol 2024;231:657](39097130)) 
  * 39 year old woman with isolated polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis confined to the cervix associated with human papillomavirus (HPV) infection ([Interv Med Appl Sci 2016;8:93](28386466)) 
  * 48 year old postmenopausal woman presented with symptoms of pelvic inflammatory disease; a Pap smear revealed _Aspergillus_ fruiting bodies and subsequent biopsy revealed cervical squamous cell carcinoma ([J Cytol 2009;26:123](21938172)) 
  * 75 year old woman with an exophytic vaginal mass was diagnosed with limited granulomatosis with polyangiitis of the cervix and upper vagina ([J Pers Med 2022;12:289](35207776)) 

Treatment

  * Isolated genital tract vasculitis should be managed conservatively ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 
  * Complete resection is usually considered curative ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2014;18:199](24846840)) 
  * No immunosuppressive therapy is necessary ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
  * If systemic (autoimmune) disease is concurrent or develops, it should be treated according to the specific vasculitis; for example 
    * Polyarteritis nodosa with high dose corticosteroids (i.e., prednisone) and cyclophosphamide for moderate to severe disease ([Arthritis Care Res (Hoboken) 2021;73:1061](34235889)) 
    * Giant cell arteritis with high dose corticosteroids (i.e., prednisone) ([Curr Neurol Neurosci Rep 2024;24:285](38913278)) 

Gross description

  * Gross findings are usually absent in vasculitis; notable exception is Behçet disease, which may manifest as cervical ulcers ([Am J Obstet Gynecol 2024;231:657](39097130)) 

Frozen section description

  * Usually no distinctive gross lesions to target intraoperatively 
  * Microscopic and incidental finding 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Affected vessels are predominately medium sized muscular arteries 
  * Lesions can be focal (sometimes only a single artery) or multifocal within the specimen 
  * 2 predominant histologic patterns have been described 
    * Polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis 
    * Giant cell arteritis type vasculitis 
  * Polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis 
    * Transmural necrotizing inflammation of small to medium arteries or arterioles 
    * 1 or few arteries affected, most likely in the cervix ([Int J Gynecol Pathol 2000;19:258](10907175)) 
    * Fibrinoid necrosis of the media ([Am J Dermatopathol 2008;30:319](18645302)) 
    * Heavy neutrophilic infiltrates 
    * Often with lymphocytes and occasional eosinophils 
    * Luminal thrombi 
  * Giant cell arteritis type vasculitis 
    * Granulomatous inflammation of medium to large arteries 
    * Multifocal involvement (uterus, adnexa) ([South Med J 2005;98:469](15898526)) 
      * Aggregates of histiocytes and multinucleated Langhans or foreign body type giant cells 
      * Often concentrated near the internal elastic lamina, dissecting through the media and inner adventitia 
    * Elastic lamina disruption is often seen ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2021;52:151728](33798926)) 
    * Luminal narrowing due to intimal proliferation 
    * Lymphocytes and macrophages predominate 
    * Few neutrophils may be present 
    * May see concurrent age related vascular changes (atherosclerosis) 
  * Background cervical epithelium may be dysplastic (cervical intraepithelial neoplasia) or have chronic cervicitis or granulomatous inflammation 
  * Adjacent lymphoid follicles or stromal plasma cells from chronic inflammation may be present

Cytology description

  * No cytologic features of vasculitis 
  * Inflammatory changes on Pap (abundance of neutrophils, repair) are nonspecific and cannot diagnose vasculitis 

Immunofluorescence description

  * Performed for renal or cutaneous vasculitis but not in routine cervical specimens 
  * See [Cutaneous vasculitis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/skinnontumorvasculitis.html) for direct immunofluorescence (DIF) findings if systemic involvement is present 

Positive stains

  * Polyarteritis nodosa-like and giant cell arteritis type: [elastin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainselastic.html) stain shows patchy destruction 
  * Mixed inflammatory infiltrate consisting of T cells, macrophages, with or without neutrophils

Sample pathology report

  * Cervix, cone biopsy: 
    * Diffuse and chronic granulomatous inflammation with giant cell reaction and focal necrotizing vasculitis (see comment) 
    * Comment: Microscopic examination reveals granulomatous inflammation with multinucleated giant cells infiltrating medium sized arteries within the deep cervical stroma. This finding raises a broad differential, including infection, hypersensitivity or drug reaction, systemic vasculitis or other etiology. Special stains (acid fast bacillus, GMS) are negative for mycobacterial and fungal organisms; however, these special stains are not sensitive tests and clinical laboratory testing (quantiferon, serologies) may be indicated.

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/cervixvasculitisMicroPelkey2.jpg

A cervical biopsy taken for evaluation of postmenopausal bleeding reveals inflamed medium sized muscular arteries, seen in the photomicrograph above. Which of the following histologic features best supports a diagnosis of polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis of the cervix?   
  
  1. Atypical endothelial cells with preserved N:C ratio, without mitoses 
  2. Concentric intimal hyperplasia with minimal inflammation 
  3. Fibrinoid necrosis of the vessel wall with transmural neutrophils 
  4. Fungal forms disrupting the muscularis layer 

Practice answer #1

**C.** Fibrinoid necrosis of the vessel wall with transmural neutrophilic inflammation is characteristic of polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis. This pattern typically involves medium sized muscular arteries and reflects immune mediated vascular injury with segmental involvement and vessel wall destruction. Importantly, cervical polyarteritis nodosa type vasculitis is most often focal and incidental, not associated with systemic autoimmune disease, and may be idiopathic. Answer A is incorrect because atypical endothelial cells with preserved N:C ratio without mitoses are typical of radiation induced vasculopathy, not autoimmune vasculitis. These changes are reactive and may be seen following pelvic radiation ([Stolnicu: Gynecologic Pathology, 1st Edition, 2023](https://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/3030973778/pathologyoutl-20)). Answer D is incorrect because fungal forms (pseudohyphae, hyphae) disrupting the muscularis layer are diagnostic of invasive fungal vasculitis, an infectious rather than autoimmune process. This may be caused by _Candida_ or _Aspergillus_ spp. in immunocompromised patients ([J Intensive Med 2024;4:299](39035612), [J Cytol 2009;26:123](21938172)). Answer B is incorrect because concentric intimal hyperplasia with minimal inflammation is seen in hypertension, which is a reactive or adaptive response of vessel walls to a chronic high pressure state ([Age Ageing 1979;8:99](463685), [Arterioscler Thromb Vasc Biol 2017;37:191](27908890)).
