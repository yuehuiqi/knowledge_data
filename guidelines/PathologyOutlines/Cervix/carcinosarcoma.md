Definition / general

  * Biphasic tumor with malignant epithelial and sarcomatous components 

Essential features

  * Extremely rare biphasic tumor with malignant epithelial and sarcomatous components 
  * Metastasis or direct extension from the uterus must be excluded 
  * Clinically aggressive with a poor prognosis ([Gynecol Oncol 2005;99:348](16051326), [J Gynecol Oncol 2020;31:e25](31912680), [Gynecol Oncol 2010;118:134](20541244)) 

Terminology

  * Not recommended: malignant mesodermal mixed tumor, malignant mixed Müllerian tumor, metaplastic carcinoma 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [C53.9](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/C00-D49/C51-C58/C53-/C53.9) \- malignant neoplasm of cervix uteri, unspecified 
  * ICD-11: [2C77.Y & XH2W45](https://icd.who.int/browse/2025-01/mms/en#1256072522%2Fother%20%26%2035175765) \- other specified malignant neoplasms of cervix uteri & carcinosarcoma, NOS 

Epidemiology

  * Extremely rare; < 100 cases reported 
  * Vast majority in postmenopausal women but age at initial diagnosis ranges from 12 to 94 years ([J Gynecol Oncol 2020;31:e25](31912680)) 

Sites

  * Uterine cervix 

Pathophysiology

  * Sarcomatous component is likely derived from the carcinomatous component via transdifferentiation or metaplasia (epithelial - mesenchymal transition) 

Etiology

  * A portion of tumors are associated with high risk human papillomavirus (HPV) infection, particularly types 16 and 18 ([Am J Surg Pathol 2001;25:338](11224604)) 

Clinical features

  * Symptoms are nonspecific and include abnormal vaginal bleeding and watery discharge 
  * Vaginal exam may show a bulky, necrotic or hemorrhagic cervix or a polypoid mass within the cervix 

Diagnosis

  * Diagnosis may be made on biopsy or resection specimens 

Radiology description

  * Ultrasound or abdominal CT may show tumor mass centered at the endocervical junction

Prognostic factors

  * Poor prognosis with overall survival rate of 60% at 2 years ([Int J Gynecol Cancer 2017;27:1957](28708788)) 
  * Tumor stage at diagnosis is the single most important prognostic indicator ([Int J Gynecol Cancer 2017;27:1957](28708788)) 

Case reports

  * 35 year old woman with vaginal bleeding and cervical mass ([Int J Surg Case Rep 2024;124:110349](39332220)) 
  * 58 year old woman with vaginal bleeding and cervical mass ([Clin Exp Obstet Gynecol 2021;48:448](https://doi.org/10.31083/j.ceog.2021.02.2219)) 
  * Woman in her 60s with vaginal bleeding and cervical mass ([BMJ Case Rep 2022;15:e249302](35705302)) 

Treatment

  * No evidence based guidelines on optimal treatment approaches, given rarity 
  * For early stage patients, surgery with adjuvant radiotherapy (with or without chemotherapy) may be associated with improved survival ([Int J Gynecol Cancer 2017;27:1957](28708788)) 
  * Primary radiotherapy may be recommended in advanced disease ([Int J Gynecol Cancer 2017;27:1957](28708788)) 
  * Chemotherapy for patients with metastatic tumor ([World J Surg Oncol 2006;4:36](16813659)) 

Gross description

  * Tumor may present as an exophytic mass or ulceration involving the distal cervix 
  * In cases with endophytic, diffusely infiltrative growth, the cervix may appear enlarged and indurated, resulting in a barrel shaped configuration 
  * Cut surface often shows variable degrees of hemorrhage and necrosis

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Characterized by a biphasic admixture of intimately juxtaposed carcinomatous and sarcomatous components 
  * Carcinomatous component 
    * Represented by subtypes commonly seen in primary cervical epithelial malignancies 
    * Most commonly adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma 
    * Rarer components include adenoid basal carcinoma, mesonephric carcinoma and neuroendocrine carcinoma 
  * Sarcomatous component may exhibit either homologous differentiation (fibrosarcoma, endometrial stromal sarcoma) or heterologous differentiation (chondrosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, osteosarcoma) 
  * References: [Int J Gynecol Pathol 1998;17:211](9656116), [Am J Surg Pathol 2001;25:338](11224604)

Positive stains

  * Immunohistochemistry (IHC) is not required to establish a diagnosis but can be used to classify heterologous elements 
    * Rhabdomyosarcoma: positive for [desmin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsdesmin.html) and [myogenin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmyogenin.html)
    * Liposarcoma and chondrosarcoma: positive for [S100](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainss100.html)
  * Positive [p16](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsp16.html) immunostaining is useful in establishing an association with HPV 
  * Reference: [Case Rep Pathol 2013;2013:630859](23533892)

Sample pathology report

  * Cervical mass, biopsy: 
    * Carcinosarcoma (see comment) 
    * Comment: The tumor consists of an adenocarcinoma component (55%) and a sarcomatous component (45%), the majority of which demonstrates rhabdomyoblastic differentiation.

Practice question #1

Which of the following is a homologous component in cervical carcinosarcoma?   
  
  1. Chondrosarcoma 
  2. Fibrosarcoma 
  3. Liposarcoma 
  4. Osteosarcoma 
  5. Rhabdomyosarcoma 

Practice answer #1

**B**. Fibrosarcoma. Cervical carcinosarcoma is characterized by a biphasic admixture of carcinomatous and sarcomatous components. The sarcomatous component in cervical carcinosarcoma can show either homologous (uterine) differentiation, including fibrosarcoma and endometrial stromal sarcoma or heterologous (nonuterine) differentiation. Answer A is incorrect because chondrosarcoma represents heterologous differentiation (cartilage tissue), which is not normally found in the uterus. Answer C is incorrect because liposarcoma shows fatty differentiation, which is also not normally found in uterine histology, making it a heterologous sarcomatous element. Answer D is incorrect because osteosarcoma demonstrates bone formation, which does not occur in the normal uterine stroma. Answer E is incorrect because rhabdomyosarcoma shows skeletal muscle differentiation, which is also not a component of the uterus.

A 34 year old woman presented with abnormal vaginal bleeding and a bulky cervical mass. The patient underwent a radical hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy. A tumor, as illustrated in the photo shown above, was found to be confined within the cervix. Which of the following statements is true regarding this tumor?   
  
  1. Adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma are the most common epithelial components 
  2. All cases are human papillomavirus (HPV) associated 
  3. Immunohistochemistry is required to establish a diagnosis 
  4. It is generally indolent, with a good to intermediate prognosis 
  5. It is more common than uterine and ovarian counterparts 

Practice answer #2

**A**. Adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma are the most common epithelial components in cervical carcinosarcoma. Answers D and E are incorrect because these tumors are extremely rare and aggressive, with a poor prognosis. Answer B is incorrect because some but not all cases are associated with HPV. Answer C is incorrect because while immunohistochemistry can be helpful in characterizing heterologous sarcomatous elements, it is not required to establish a diagnosis in all cases.
