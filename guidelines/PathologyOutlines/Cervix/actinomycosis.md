Definition / general

  * _Actinomyces_ spp. are filamentous, Gram positive, anaerobic bacteria that are commensal in the female genital tract 
  * Usually of little clinical significance but may cause chronic infection and rarely, severe disease 

Essential features

  * Characteristic appearance of sulfur granules (actinomycotic granules) 
  * Associated with intrauterine devices (IUDs), though relationship is controversial 
  * Rarely, _Actinomyces_ spp. can cause invasive and severe infections 
  * Main differential diagnosis on H&E is pseudoactinomycotic radiate granules 

Terminology

  * Actinomycotic granules 
  * Actinomyces-like organisms 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10 
    * [A42.8](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/A00-B99/A30-A49/A42-/A42.8) \- other forms of actinomycosis 
    * [A42.9](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/A00-B99/A30-A49/A42-/A42.9) \- actinomycosis, unspecified 
    * [R87.5](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/R00-R99/R83-R89/R87-/R87.5) \- abnormal microbiological findings in specimens from female genital organs 

Epidemiology

  * Primarily seen in women of reproductive age 
  * More commonly identified in women with intrauterine devices (IUDs) than in women without IUDs ([Am J Obstet Gynecol 1983;145:338](6824023)) 
    * Relationship of _Actinomyces_ spp. to IUDs is controversial ([Am J Obstet Gynecol 1999;180:265](9988785)) 
  * _Actinomyces_ species found in the female genital tract in the absence of true infection include _A. meyeri, A. neuii, A. radingae, A. turicensis_ and _A. urogenitalis_ ([Int J Syst Bacteriol 1998;48:503](9731291)) 

Sites

  * Within female genital tract: cervix and endometrium 

Pathophysiology

  * _Actinomyces_ spp., particularly _Actinomyces israelii_ , reside on the mucosal surface and can gain access to deeper tissues after trauma, such as surgical procedures or foreign bodies that disrupt the mucosal surface ([Clin Microbiol Rev 2015;28:419](25788515), [Infect Drug Resist 2014;7:183](25045274)) 

Etiology

  * Long term IUD use 
  * Surgical procedures / instrumentation 
    * Due to presence of foreign body (e.g., suture material, synthetic mesh) 
  * Reference: [Can J Infect Dis Med Microbiol 2017;2017:9428650](28684963)

Clinical features

  * Asymptomatic colonization is the most common presentation 
  * Symptoms include malodorous vaginal discharge, vaginal bleeding and pelvic pain 
  * Rarely, may cause chronic pelvic inflammatory disease, pelvic mass and abscess formation ([N Engl J Med 2015;372:2039](25992748)) 
    * Pelvic actinomycosis has been reported to mimic pelvic malignancy ([Am J Case Rep 2020;21:e922601](32532952), [Infect Dis Obstet Gynecol 2011;2011:747059](21904441), [Rev Bras Ginecol Obstet 2019;41:463](31250417)) 

Diagnosis

  * Pap smear 
  * Tissue biopsy 
  * Culture 
  * Reference: [Clin Microbiol Rev 2015;28:419](25788515)

Case reports

  * 44 year old woman with bilateral adnexal masses ([Acad Pathol 2022;9:100058](36444377)) 
  * 57 year old woman with pelvic abscess 3 years after suture placement ([Case Rep Womens Health 2021;32:e00359](34611518)) 
  * 71 year old woman with postmenopausal bleeding and weight loss ([Cureus 2025;17:e80839](40255735)) 

Treatment

  * Neither antibiotic treatment nor IUD removal is required for incidentally found _Actinomyces_ in asymptomatic patients ([Am J Obstet Gynecol 1999;180:265](9988785)) 
  * Replacement of IUD or antibiotics may be considered in symptomatic patients 
  * Surgical intervention may be required for management of severe infections (e.g., fistulas or abscesses) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * H&E stain: sulfur granules (or actinomycotic granules) are characteristic 
    * Thin, filamentous organisms radiating from a dense center, often with a background of mixed inflammatory cells 
  * Gram and GMS stain: highlight the filaments at the edge of the cluster 
  * Reference: [J Clin Pathol 2009;62:1123](19946099)

Positive stains

Sample pathology report

  * Endocervix, curettage: 
    * Fragments of benign endocervical glands 
    * Fragments consistent with actinomycotic granules with surrounding acute inflammation (see comment) 
    * Comment: GMS and Gram (Brown-Hopps) special stains highlight the filamentous bacteria, supporting the above diagnosis. Clinical correlation is recommended. 

Differential diagnosis

  * **Pseudoactinomycotic radiate granules** ([J Clin Pathol 2006;59:17](16394276), [Am J Clin Pathol 1981;75:822](7020400), [J Clin Pathol 2009;62:1123](19946099)): 
    * Known mimic of sulfur granules 
    * Noninfectious entities that are often seen in women with and without IUDs 
    * H&E stain: pseudoactinomycotic radiate granules have thick, retractile, club-like (rather than filamentous) peripheral projections without a central dense core

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/cervixactinomycosisGoldberg01A.jpg

A 30 year old woman undergoes routine Pap smear screening. The Pap shows the finding in the image above. What is the diagnosis?   
  
  1. _Actinomyces_ spp. 
  2. Atypical squamous cells 
  3. _Candida_ spp. 
  4. Herpes simplex virus (HSV) 
  5. Pseudoactinomycotic radiate granules 

Practice answer #1

**A**. _Actinomyces_ spp. The imaging depicts a fuzzy basophilic clump with thin, filamentous bacteria noted at the periphery, which is consistent with _Actinomyces_ spp. Answer B is incorrect because the cytology of the surrounding epithelial cells shows no cytologic atypia. Answer C is incorrect because _Candida_ appears as pseudohyphae with reactive epithelial cells in a "shish kebab" form. Answer D is incorrect because HSV causes specific viral cytopathic changes such as multinucleation, nuclear molding and margination. Answer E is incorrect because pseudoactinomycotic radiate granules demonstrate thick, irregular club-like peripheral projections.

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/cervixactinomycosisQuannMa01.jpg

A 50 year old woman presents to her doctor's office for abnormal uterine bleeding. Office endometrial biopsy shows the finding above. Which of the following statements is most likely true?   
  
  1. Progesterone therapy may be recommended 
  2. The patient has an IUD 
  3. The patient is infertile 
  4. The patient requires genetic testing 
  5. The patient requires hysterectomy 

Practice answer #2

**B**. The patient has an IUD. The image depicts _Actinomyces_ spp., which is often incidentally found in the setting of prolonged IUD use. Answer A is incorrect because progesterone therapy would not be indicated for _Actinomyces_. Answer C is incorrect because the presence of _Actinomyces_ does not impact fertility. Answer D is incorrect because there is no genetic predisposition or inheritance pattern of _Actinomyces_. Answer E is incorrect because _Actinomyces_ often does not require treatment and would only require surgical intervention if the disease was severe.
