Definition / general

  * Stratified mucin producing intraepithelial lesion (SMILE) is a rare, human papillomavirus (HPV) related premalignant lesion of the uterine cervix, with features of both high grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (SIL) and adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS) 
  * Arises from transformation zone reserve cells with multidirectional differentiation potential ([Am J Surg Pathol 2000;24:1414](11023104)) 
  * Categorized as a subtype of AIS both in 2014 and 2020 WHO classification 

Essential features

  * High risk HPV related neoplastic precursor lesion of uterine cervix 
  * Demonstrates features of both SIL (stratification, nuclear atypia) and AIS (intracytoplasmic mucin); categorized as a subtype of AIS 
  * Most often coexists with other precursor lesions (SIL, AIS) as well as invasive carcinoma 
  * Invasive form present: invasive stratified mucin producing carcinoma ([Am J Surg Pathol 2016;40:262](26523540)) 
  * No consensus on management; generally treated like AIS 

ICD coding

  * ICD-O 
    * [8140/2](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/f63d7652-1254-47f4-8ab7-3c65ad60d57d/content?sequence=1#page=65) \- adenocarcinoma in situ, NOS 
    * [8483/2](https://www.iccr-cancer.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/ICCR-Cervix-4th-edn-v4-bookmark.pdf#page=17) \- adenocarcinoma in situ, HPV associated 

Epidemiology

  * Reported incidence of SMILE is 2.7% among all the high grade noninvasive cases (HSIL, AIS, SMILE) and invasive carcinomas, with median patient age of 29 - 33 years ([Histopathology 2015;66:658](25039487), [Cells 2021;10:2039](34440808), [Hum Pathol 2016;55:174](27237368)) 
  * Frequently associated with other intraepithelial lesions, including SIL and AIS as well as invasive carcinoma 

Sites

  * Predominantly arises at the cervical transformation zone 

Pathophysiology

  * Clonal proliferation of transformation zone reserve cells driven by high risk HPV infection 

Etiology

  * High risk HPV subtypes, mostly 16, 18, 31, 33, 39, 51 

Clinical features

  * Mostly asymptomatic 
  * Abnormal cervical cytology with high grade SIL or atypical glandular cells (AGC) 

Diagnosis

  * Cervical cytologic or histologic examination 

Prognostic factors

  * Kahn et al. showed no recurrence of SMILE in patients with fertility sparing excisional biopsies with negative margins over a median follow up of 29 months ([Int J Gynecol Cancer 2020;30:1298](32448806)) 
  * Coexisting invasive carcinoma is associated with an unfavorable prognosis 

Case reports

  * 30 year old woman underwent routine cervical screening and was diagnosed with atypical glandular cells ([Virol J 2019;16:76](31159808)) 
  * 39 year old woman presented with vaginal discharge ([Infect Disord Drug Targets 2024;24:e290124226396](38288809)) 
  * 48 year old woman underwent routine cervical screening and was diagnosed with atypical glandular cells ([Anticancer Res 2022;42:1175](35093923)) 

Treatment

  * Currently no international consensus guidelines regarding management 
  * Generally managed as AIS ([Anticancer Res 2022;42:1175](35093923)) 
  * Kahn et al. showed no recurrence of SMILE in patients with fertility sparing excisional biopsies with negative margins over a median follow up of 29 months ([Int J Gynecol Cancer 2020;30:1298](32448806)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Stratified epithelium similar to high grade SIL with nuclear atypia, high N:C ratio and hyperchromasia throughout the layers 
  * Cytoplasmic mucin is also present as either discrete vacuoles or cytoplasmic clearing throughout the full epithelial thickness 
    * Amount of intracytoplasmic mucin varies, being conspicuous in some and subtle in others 
    * In mucin rich cases, nuclei appear less atypical due to peripheral compression and displacement 
  * Gland formation is typically absent, in contrast to AIS ([Histopathology 2015;66:658](25039487))

Cytology description

  * 3D clusters with nuclear stratification and moderate crowding 
  * Jagged borders without classical feathering of AIS 
  * Distinct cytoplasmic border 
  * Spherical rather than elongated nuclear shape 
  * Moderate to marked nuclear membrane irregularity 
  * Fine rather than coarse chromatin ([Acta Cytol 2016;60:225](27442040), [Diagn Cytopathol 2016;44:20](26493276))

Practice question #1

[ /cervixSMILEMicroBarroeta2.jpeg)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/cervixSMILEMicroBarroeta2.jpeg)

Which of the following features is characteristic of SMILE?   
  
  1. Detection of high risk HPV 
  2. Mucin confined to the superficial layer 
  3. Negative p16 immunostaining 
  4. True gland formation with pseudostratified columnar epithelium 

Practice answer #1

**A.** Detection of high risk HPV. SMILE is a high risk HPV associated lesion, mostly linked to HPV types 16 and 18. Answer C is incorrect because HPV DNA testing and surrogate marker p16 overexpression (block type, nuclear and cytoplasmic positivity) support this viral etiology. Answer D is incorrect because SMILE lacks true glandular architecture; this feature is typical of AIS. Answer B is incorrect because in SMILE, mucin is distributed throughout the full epithelial thickness, not just the superficial layer. This helps differentiate it from immature squamous metaplasia.

Practice question #2

Which of the following staining profiles would be expected for SMILE?   
  
  1. p16 block, Ki67 high, mucicarmine positive, IMP3 negative 
  2. p16 block, Ki67 low, mucicarmine negative, IMP3 negative 
  3. p16 block, Ki67 low, mucicarmine negative, IMP3 positive 
  4. p16 focal, Ki67 high, mucicarmine positive, IMP3 positive 

Practice answer #2

**A.** p16 block, Ki67 high, mucicarmine positive, IMP3 negative. SMILE shows block nuclear and cytoplasmic p16 staining and mucicarmine positivity as well as has a high Ki67 proliferation index. Block p16 positivity is also seen in high grade SIL and HPV associated AIS. It is generally negative for IMP3, which helps distinguish it from invasive carcinoma. Mucicarmine is positive in AIS but not in SIL.   
  
Answer B is incorrect because it describes a low Ki67 index and negative mucicarmine staining, which are not typical features of SMILE. SMILE usually shows high proliferative activity and mucin production. Answer C is incorrect because although it describes positive p16 block staining (which is seen in SMILE), it also shows low Ki67 and mucicarmine negativity, both of which are uncharacteristic of SMILE. In addition, IMP3 positivity may raise concern for more aggressive lesions or invasive carcinoma, which is not expected in SMILE. Answer D is incorrect because it indicates focal p16 staining, whereas SMILE classically shows diffuse (block) p16 positivity due to high risk HPV association. Although the high Ki67 and mucicarmine positivity are consistent with SMILE, the IMP3 positivity and lack of block p16 expression make this staining profile inconsistent.
