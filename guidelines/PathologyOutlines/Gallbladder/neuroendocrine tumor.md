Definition / general

  * Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumors occur in gallbladder and extrahepatic bile ducts 
  * Tumor cells with neuroendocrine differentiation and well differentiated morphology 

Essential features

  * Accounts for 0.5 - 2% of all neuroendocrine tumors and ~2% of all gallbladder tumors 
  * Has well differentiated neuroendocrine cell morphology 
  * 2019 WHO grading system for neuroendocrine tumors should be applied 

Terminology

  * Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor in gallbladder and extrahepatic bile ducts 

ICD coding

  * ICD-11 
    * [2C14.2](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#1018801334) \- neuroendocrine neoplasms of cystic duct 
    * [2C15.2](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#298109934) \- neuroendocrine neoplasms of distal bile duct 
    * [2C17.2](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#1508454837) \- neuroendocrine neoplasms of other or unspecified parts of biliary tract 
    * [2C13.Y](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#922082116%2Fother) \- other specified malignant neoplasm of gallbladder 

Epidemiology

  * Accounts for 0.5 - 2% of all neuroendocrine tumors ([Am J Clin Oncol 2008;31:64](18376230)) 
  * ~2% of all gallbladder tumors ([J Clin Oncol 2008;26:3063](18565894), [Ann Surg Oncol 2019;26:3577](31102094)) 
  * Mean age of diagnosis: ~60 years 
  * Slight female predominance (M:F = 1:2.4 for gallbladder and 1:1.6 for extrahepatic bile ducts) ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2009;13:378](19917473)) 

Sites

  * Gallbladder neuroendocrine tumor (GB NET): fundus most frequent (~55%), body, neck ([Gastroenterol Res Pract 2021;2021:5592525](34122537)) 
  * Extrahepatic bile duct neuroendocrine tumor (EHBD NET): hepatic duct and distal common bile duct (CBD) (19.2%), middle CBD (17.9%), cystic gall duct (16.7%), proximal CBD (11.5%) ([Surgery 2016;160:525](26873297)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Chronic inflammation due to cholelithiasis or congenital malformation causes metaplasia of the bile duct epithelial cells, which might transform into neuroendocrine tumor ([World J Gastroenterol 2013;19:4616](23901240)) 
  * Activation of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), which upregulates downstream effector protein kinase B and extracellular signal regulates kinase expression ([J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 2014;21:288](24002888), [J Clin Oncol 2010;28:245](19917848)) 

Etiology

  * See [Pathophysiology](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladderneuroendocrinetumor.html#gallbladderneuroendocrinetumorpatho)

Clinical features

  * Incidental finding 
  * Abdominal pain 
  * Obstructive jaundice 
  * Fever 
  * May be functional: elevated serum gastrin and urinary 5-HIAA have been reported ([Digestion 1999;60:187](10095161), [Br J Surg 1968;55:147](5635925)) 

Diagnosis

  * Combination of tumor markers and imaging for preoperative diagnosis 
    * Tumor markers: CA19-9, CEA and CA125 are often negative ([J Gastrointest Oncol 2018;9:111](29564177)) 
    * Imaging: ultrasound, computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) 
  * Brush cytology obtained by ERCP, percutaneous transhepatic cholangiography or endoscopic ultrasound guided fine needle aspiration for preoperative histological result ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2010;134:1075](20586641)) 

Laboratory

  * See [Clinical features](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladderneuroendocrinetumor.html#gallbladderneuroendocrinetumorclin) and [Diagnosis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladderneuroendocrinetumor.html#gallbladderneuroendocrinetumordiagn)

Radiology description

  * GB NET 
    * Ultrasound: thickening of GB walls, a low echoic nodule 
    * CT and MRI: a thickening of the cystic wall on 1 side, with the mass protruding into or out of the cavity; more enhancement compared with that of adenocarcinoma ([Oncol Lett 2020;19:3381](32269610), [Intern Med 1996;35:953](9030993)) 
    * ERCP: the defect of the tumor in the gallbladder 
  * EHBD NET 
    * CT: well circumscribed wall enhancing on arterial phase, a polyp-like appearance of a small size 
    * MRI: NET with a lower signal intensity (SI) than that of the liver parenchyma on T1 weighted images (T1WI) and mostly with a higher SI than that of the liver parenchyma on T2 weighted images (T2WI) ([Abdom Imaging 2015;40:181](25008023))

Prognostic factors

  * Depends mainly on Ki67 proliferation index 
  * Age, tumor size and staging might be independent variables associated with survival ([Front Oncol 2021;11:654439](34350109)) 
  * Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) of the gallbladder and bile ducts are generally staged like other gallbladder carcinomas 
  * 5 year overall survival: 30 - 50%, a few studies suggested GB NET had a worse outcome than EHBD NET ([J Clin Gastroenterol 2010;44:687](20375728)) 

Case reports

  * 42 year old woman presented with colicky pain and an incidental finding on cholecystectomy ([Int J Surg Case Rep 2023;111:108882](37793232)) 
  * 50 year old man with a polypoid lesion in the gallbladder neck ([Mol Clin Oncol 2020;12:225](32064098)) 
  * 60 year old man with a pedunculated polyp in the gallbladder neck ([World J Surg Oncol 2021;19:3](33388069)) 
  * 68 year old woman with a mass in the biliary tract ([J Gastrointest Surg 2021;25:3007](34159552)) 
  * 75 year old man with a common bile duct lesion ([Surg Case Rep 2020;6:204](32770496)) 

Treatment

  * Surgical resection is the mainstay, with or without lymph node dissection ([J Clin Gastroenterol 2010;44:687](20375728)) 
  * Adjuvant chemotherapy ([J Clin Gastroenterol 2010;44:687](20375728)) 
  * Somatostatin analogs for functional tumors ([N Engl J Med 2014;371:1556](25317881)) 

Gross description

  * Gray to whitish tumor with either ill or well defined border in the wall of gallbladder or biliary tract 
  * Size is usually small (~1 cm)

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Uniform population of tumor cells arranged in nests, trabeculae or tubules 
  * Tumor cells with abundant eosinophilic and granular cytoplasm 
  * Nuclei with salt and pepper chromatin and small nucleoli 
  * Clear cell morphology has been reported ([World J Surg Oncol 2021;19:3](33388069)) 
  * WHO 2019 well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor grading 
    * Grade 1: < 2 mitoses/2 mm2 or Ki67 index < 3% 
    * Grade 2: 2 - 20 mitoses/2 mm2 or Ki67 index 3 - 20% 
    * Grade 3: > 20 mitoses/2 mm2 or Ki67 > 20%

Cytology description

  * Tumor cells arranged in rosettes 
  * Monomorphic at low magnification with rare anisonucleosis 
  * Tumor cells may contain round to oval nuclei with salt and pepper chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli and granular cytoplasm 
  * Mitotic figures might be present but infrequent 
  * No nuclear molding 
  * Reference: [Cytopathology 2016;27:97](25689921)

Sample pathology report

  * Gallbladder, cholecystectomy: 
    * Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor (1.5 cm), grade 1, invading submucosa (see comment) 
    * All margins are negative 
    * No lymphovascular or perineural invasion is identified 
    * Background gallbladder mucosa with mild chronic cholecystitis 
    * 1 pericystic lymph node, negative for metastatic tumor (0/1) 
    * Pathologic stage: pT1 N0 
    * Comment: The tumor cells are positive for pancytokeratin, synaptophysin and chromogranin with Ki67 proliferation index < 3%, supporting the above diagnosis. 

Differential diagnosis

  * [Neuroendocrine carcinoma]( https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumorneuroendocrinecarcinoma.html): 
    * Poorly differentiated cytomorphology 
    * Brisk mitosis 
    * Necrosis 
    * Nuclear molding (small cell carcinoma)

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/gallbladderneuroendocrinetumorMicroHsiao2.jpg

An incidental lesion was found in gallbladder wall with the histology as shown above. The tumor cells are positive for pancytokeratin and synaptophysin. What is the diagnosis?   
  
  1. Adenoma 
  2. Cholangiocarcinoma 
  3. Papillary hyperplasia 
  4. Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor 

Practice answer #1

**D.** Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor. The monomorphic tumor cells with trabecular growth, salt and pepper chromatin and positive neuroendocrine markers on immunostains support the diagnosis. Answer A is incorrect because in adenoma, the cells are predominantly gland forming and negative for synaptophysin. Answer B is incorrect because in cholangiocarcinoma, the glands are angulated and infiltrative and the nuclei lack salt and pepper features. This entity is negative for synaptophysin. Answer C is incorrect because in papillary hyperplasia, the cells form papillae and are negative for synaptophysin.
