Definition / general

  * Chronic inflammation of the gallbladder, typically secondary to gallstones 

Essential features

  * Most common disease of the gallbladder, typically secondary to cholelithiasis 
  * Variety of histologic findings, including variable amounts of mononuclear cell predominant inflammation, mucosal changes including metaplasia, muscular hypertrophy and transmural fibrosis 
  * Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses and ducts of Luschka should not be mistaken for invasive adenocarcinoma 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [K81.1](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K80-K87/K81-/K81.1) \- chronic cholecystitis 
  * ICD-11: [DC12.1](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#507077490) \- chronic cholecystitis 

Epidemiology

  * Female predominance ([StatPearls: Chronic Cholecystitis [Accessed 20 August 2025]](29261986), [Am Surg 2025;91:1258](40542547)) 
  * Associated with [cholelithiasis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladdercholelithiasis.html) in > 90% of cases 

Sites

  * Generally a diffuse process with no specific predilection for any 1 region (e.g., fundus, body, neck) of the gallbladder 

Pathophysiology

  * Can be a sequela of recurrent [acute cholecystitis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladderacutecholecystitis.html)
  * Typically related to [cholelithiasis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladdercholelithiasis.html), either through direct mucosal irritation or via intermittent mechanical obstruction with associated alteration of bile chemistry 
  * Altered mechanics of gallbladder emptying plays a crucial role 
  * Up to 33% of patients have bile cultures positive for bacteria (e.g., _Escherichia coli_ , _Enterococci_ , _Helicobacter pylori_ , etc.), the significance of which is uncertain ([Br J Surg 2010;97:532](20169571), [Scand J Gastroenterol 2005;40:96](15841721), [Helicobacter 2018;23:e12457](29266548), [ Ann Med Surg (Lond) 2021;70:102822](34540214)) 

Etiology

Clinical features

  * Does not always cause clinical symptoms 
  * Can present with dull right upper quadrant pain that radiates to mid back or right scapula ([StatPearls: Chronic Cholecystitis [Accessed 20 August 2025]](29261986)) 
  * Murphy sign: right upper abdominal pain with deep palpation 
  * Abdominal discomfort often related to fatty food ingestion 
  * Nausea, vomiting, bloating, flatulence 

Diagnosis

  * Abdominal ultrasound ([StatPearls: Chronic Cholecystitis [Accessed 20 August 2025]](29261986)) 
  * Abdominal CT with contrast 
  * HIDA (hepatobiliary iminodiacetic acid) scan demonstrating a reduced ejection fraction (< 35%) 

Laboratory

  * Laboratory testing is neither sensitive or specific for diagnosing chronic cholecystitis 

Radiology description

  * Gallbladder wall thickening with associated [cholelithiasis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladdercholelithiasis.html)
  * Gallbladder may appear contracted or distended ([Medicine (Baltimore) 2018;97:e11851](30113479))

Prognostic factors

  * Majority of uncomplicated cases have an excellent outcome ([Am J Surg 2003;185:91](12559435)) 
  * With elective cholecystectomy, bile duct injuries do occur with regular frequency ([Endoscopy 2018;50:577](29351705)) 
  * Complicated postoperative course most likely to occur in patients who experience common bile duct injury, conversion to open cholecystectomy or subtotal cholecystectomy ([Am Surg 2025;91:1258](40542547)) 
  * Biliary leakage reported in up to 3% of cases ([Visc Med 2017;33:184](28785565)) 
  * Generally, a very low risk (< 0.8%) of associated incidental [carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladdercarcinoma.html) ([Rev Col Bras Cir 2020;46:e20192279](31967243), [Surgery 2001;129:699](11391368), [Gastro Hep Adv 2025;4:100655](40487276)) 

Case reports

  * 38 year old woman with acalculous chronic cholecystitis secondary to dead _Ascaris lumbricoides_ worm within the gallbladder lumen ([Int J Surg Case Rep 2025;133:111668](40652865)) 
  * 44 year old man with gallstones and chronic cholecystitis revealing metachronous gallbladder metastasis from renal clear cell carcinoma ([ANZ J Surg 2020;90:E87](31230403)) 
  * 45 year old woman with porcelain gallbladder secondary to chronic cholecystitis, without adenocarcinoma ([Mymensingh Med J 2019;28:694](31391447)) 
  * 53 year old man with history of colorectal adenocarcinoma with progressively elevating CEA secondary to chronic cholecystitis ([J Surg Case Rep 2019;2019:rjz138](31214306)) 
  * 70 year old man with chronic cholecystitis secondary to biliary taeniasis ([Am J Trop Med Hyg 2019;100:135](30457101)) 

Treatment

  * Elective cholecystectomy ([Am J Surg 2003;185:91](12559435)) 

Gross description

  * Nearly normal to thickened gallbladder wall ([StatPearls: Chronic Cholecystitis [Accessed 20 August 2025]](29261986)) 
  * Gallbladder may appear shrunken due to marked fibrosis 
  * Severe cases show adhesions to adjacent organs 
  * Variable mucosal appearance: can be granular, ulcerated, polypoid

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Variable amounts of predominantly mononuclear inflammatory infiltrate in lamina propria, which may extend into the muscularis and pericholecystic tissues 
  * Inflammatory infiltrate predominantly consists of T lymphocytes, with some plasma cells, histiocytes and occasional eosinophils ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2003;7:147](12808565)) 
  * Inflammation typically rather minimal; occasional lymphoid follicles may be seen in lamina propria 
  * Hypertrophy of muscularis and variable degrees of mural fibrosis, elastosis, neural hyperplasia 
  * Accentuation of Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses (pseudodiverticula)

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/gallbladderchroniccholecystitismchugh06.jpg

A 40 year old woman with a body mass index (BMI) of 36 undergoes cholecystectomy for intermittent, dull right upper quadrant abdominal pain, reproducible on physical examination with deep palpation. Sections of the gallbladder wall are shown in the image above. What is the best interpretation of these findings?   
  
  1. Chronic cholecystitis 
  2. Dilated ducts of Luschka 
  3. IgG4 related cholecystitis 
  4. Intracholecystic papillary neoplasm 
  5. Invasive adenocarcinoma 

Practice answer #1

**A**. Chronic cholecystitis. The image above shows a gallbladder wall thickened secondary to muscularis hypertrophy, with a dilated Rokitansky-Aschoff sinus and an adjacent transmural lymphoid aggregate. These findings are consistent with chronic cholecystitis. Answer C is incorrect because there is no predominance of plasma cells within the chronic inflammation, no storiform fibrosis and no evidence of obliterative phlebitis. Answer B is incorrect because the ducts of Luschka are restricted to within the subserosal tissue of the serosa lined gallbladder wall or at the interface of the gallbladder with the adjacent liver. Ducts of Luschka can appear hyperplastic or florid secondary to inflammation; they are not glandular structures that involve the surface mucosa with protrusion into or extension beyond gallbladder muscularis. Answer D is incorrect because there is no evidence of intraluminal growth, which is characteristic of intracholecystic papillary neoplasms. Generally, back to back epithelial unity arranged in either papillary or tubulopapillary configurations are appreciable and only rarely are intracholecystic papillary neoplasms seen extending into Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses. Answer E is incorrect because there is no evidence of infiltrative glandular growth set within an altered desmoplastic stroma. Most typically, invasive adenocarcinoma of the gallbladder has a pancreatobiliary type morphology composed of infiltrative or angulated tubular units that are widely spaced, set within a variably cellular to collagenized desmoplastic stroma, none of which is seen here.

Practice question #2

Which of the following is the most common inflammatory cell seen in cholecystectomy specimens with conventional chronic cholecystitis?   
  
  1. Eosinophils 
  2. Histiocytes 
  3. Lymphocytes 
  4. Neutrophils 
  5. Plasma cells 

Practice answer #2

**C**. Lymphocytes. Chronic cholecystitis is classically defined as having a lymphocyte predominant inflammatory infiltrate. Specifically T cell lymphocytes account for the majority of the lymphocytes seen. While there may be some plasma cells, histiocytes and eosinophils admixed with the lymphocytes, these cell types generally do not predominate except in certain subtypes of cholecystitis. Answer E is incorrect because plasma cells are the predominant cell type in lymphoplasmacytic cholecystitis, not in conventional chronic cholecystitis. Answer A is incorrect because eosinophils are the predominant cell type in eosinophilic cholecystitis, not conventional chronic cholecystitis. Answer B is incorrect because histiocytes are the predominant cell type in xanthogranulomatous cholecystitis, not conventional chronic cholecystitis. Answer D is incorrect because neutrophils are the predominant cell type in acute cholecystitis, not conventional chronic cholecystitis.

Practice question #3

Which of the following variants of chronic cholecystitis has an established increased risk of associated adenocarcinoma?  
  
  1. Adenomyomatous chronic cholecystitis 
  2. Diffuse lymphoplasmacytic cholecystitis 
  3. Follicular cholecystitis 
  4. Hyalinizing cholecystitis 
  5. Xanthogranulomatous cholecystitis 

Practice answer #3

**D**. Hyalinizing cholecystitis. While a history of gallstones or chronic cholecystitis both increase the risk for development of malignancy within the gallbladder, it is the hyalinizing subtype of cholecystitis that is associated with a more marked risk of associated adenocarcinoma. The incidence of gallbladder carcinoma associated with hyalinizing cholecystitis is estimated to be ~6%. In contrast, the risk of incidental carcinoma in chronic cholecystitis is estimated to be < 0.8%.   
  
Answer A is incorrect because while adenomyomatous chronic cholecystitis is associated with gallbladder wall thickening and nodule formation, there is no increased risk of malignancy associated with this benign change. Answer B is incorrect because diffuse lymphoplasmacytic cholecystitis is not associated with an increased risk of carcinoma or any other type of malignancy (e.g., hematopoietic malignancies, etc.). Answer C is incorrect because follicular cholecystitis is not associated with an increased risk of carcinoma or any other type of malignancy (e.g., hematopoietic malignancies, etc.). In fact, follicular cholecystitis is associated with a lower risk of malignancy (< 0.5%) comparably. Answer E is incorrect because although xanthogranulomatous cholecystitis may radiologically, serologically and intraoperatively mimic a gallbladder malignancy, it is not inherently associated with an increased risk of gallbladder malignancy.
