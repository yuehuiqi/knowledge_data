Definition / general

  * Rare malignant adenocarcinoma arising from extrahepatic bile ducts 
    * Biliary adenocarcinomas (cholangiocarcinomas) typically divided into [intrahepatic bile duct origin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/livertumorcholangiocarcinoma.html), [gallbladder origin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/gallbladdercarcinoma.html), perihilar bile origin (this topic) and distal extrahepatic bile duct origin (this topic), including right and left hepatic ducts, common hepatic duct and common bile duct 

Essential features

  * Adenocarcinoma arising from the biliary system outside the liver, with several risk factors 
  * Histology is similar to intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, particularly large duct type 
  * Poor prognosis 

Terminology

  * Some prefer to restrict the term cholangiocarcinoma to intrahepatic adenocarcinomas only but others use the term for any adenocarcinoma of the biliary system 
  * Klatskin tumor refers to a hilar cholangiocarcinoma arising at the bifurcation of the right and left hepatic ducts 

ICD coding

  * ICD-11 
    * [2C15.0](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#1393178521) \- adenocarcinoma of biliary tract, distal bile duct 
    * [2C18.0](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#1571104786) \- hilar cholangiocarcinoma 

Epidemiology

  * 0.53 - 2 per 100,000 in population 
    * Incidence is decreasing in recent years ([StatPearls: Biliary Tract Cholangiocarcinoma [Accessed 17 November 2023]](32809543)) 
    * Slight male predominance due to [primary sclerosing cholangitis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/liverPSC.html) being more common in men ([StatPearls: Biliary Tract Cholangiocarcinoma [Accessed 17 November 2023]](32809543), [Ann Hepatobiliary Pancreat Surg 2023;27:151](36588169)) 
  * Occurs primarily in sixth and seventh decades of life ([Ann Hepatobiliary Pancreat Surg 2023;27:151](36588169)) 

Sites

  * Can develop at any level of the extrahepatic biliary tree, including within the pancreas 
  * Bismuth-Corlette classification relates to extent of infiltration of ducts 

Pathophysiology

  * Risk factors such as infections, genetics and primary sclerosing cholangitis can play a role in disease pathogenesis ([StatPearls: Biliary Tract Cholangiocarcinoma [Accessed 17 November 2023]](32809543)) 
  * Often arises from precursor lesions, namely biliary intraepithelial neoplasia and [intraductal papillary neoplasm of the bile ducts](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/livertumorintraductalpap.html)
  * Various mutations in oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes transform normal epithelium into premalignant lesions 

Etiology

  * Several risk factors exist for development of extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma ([Liver Int 2019;39:19](30851228))

Clinical features

  * Signs of biliary obstruction may occur, including jaundice (most characteristic and common symptom), pruritis, acholic stools and dark urine ([Liver Int 2019;39:98](30831002)) 
  * Patients can present with nonspecific symptoms, including abdominal pain, nausea / vomiting, anorexia, weight loss and malaise ([Liver Int 2019;39:98](30831002)) 
  * Many patients present with unresectable disease 

Diagnosis

  * Early diagnosis is challenging and is often delayed due to asymptomatic early stage disease ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:166](33708349)) 
    * Lack of a standardized screening protocol 
  * MRI and magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) can assess size and extent of tumor 
  * Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) and EUS guided FNA can be utilized in the diagnosis and staging of cholangiocarcinoma 
  * Intraductal ultrasound is suggested for patients with obstructive jaundice for local tumor staging and bile duct stricture assessment 

Laboratory

  * Elevated serum CA 19-9, CEA-M, CA125 ([BJS Open 2021;5:zrab127](34935900)) 

Radiology description

  * May form a mass or cause thickening of bile duct walls 
  * CT and MRI ([Liver Int 2019;39:98](30831002)) 
    * Duct wall thickening and lumen obliteration, indicating infiltration 
    * Mass lesion 
  * Ultrasound ([Liver Int 2019;39:98](30831002)) 
    * Duct dilation, indicating obstruction 
  * Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) 
    * Stricture of the bile duct

Prognostic factors

  * Favorable prognostic factors 
    * Absence of comorbidities and presence of dysplasia are favorable prognostic factors ([Hepatobiliary Pancreat Dis Int 2020;19:153](31974040)) 
    * Increase in albumin and a decrease in total bilirubin improve the prognosis of patients with hilar cholangiocarcinoma ([Hepatobiliary Pancreat Dis Int 2020;19:153](31974040)) 
    * Intrapancreatic cases have a better prognosis than pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma but a worse prognosis than ampullary adenocarcinoma ([Mod Pathol 2016;29:1358](27469329)) 
  * Prognostic biomarkers 
    * Albumin, ALT, CA 19-9 and CEA are independent prognostic biomarkers for overall survival ([Cancers (Basel) 2022;14:1026](35205774), [Medicine (Baltimore) 2019;98:e14556](30813168)) 
    * Preoperative C reactive protein to albumin ratio (CAR) is an accurate prognostic biomarker, predicting overall survival and disease free survival ([J Surg Oncol 2020;122:1516](32960983)) 
    * CAR is the most valuable prognostic biomarker in patients with resected extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma ([Dig Surg 2022;39:65](35051946)) 
  * Lymph node metastasis is one of the most important prognostic factors ([J Surg Oncol 2020;122:1516](32960983)) 
  * Presence of venous, lymphatic and perineural invasion indicate a worse prognosis ([J Surg Oncol 2020;122:1516](32960983)) 
  * pT category staging for extrahepatic cases is based on depth of invasion into bile duct wall ([Am J Surg Pathol 2007;31:199](17255764)) 

Case reports

  * 17 year old boy with perihilar mass in an explanted liver ([Pathol Int 2020;70:888](32936992)) 
  * 57 year old man with obstructive jaundice ([Front Oncol 2022;12:948799](36237325)) 
  * 71 year old woman with extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and thigh mass ([Front Surg 2022;9:922834](36034354)) 

Treatment

  * Surgical resection is the only hope for long term survival ([Rozhl Chir 2022;101:416](36257799)) 
  * Chemotherapy can be used as adjuvant treatment or for palliation in unresectable cases ([J Clin Oncol 2019;37:1015](30856044)) 

Gross description

  * Various configurations possible, including stenotic, nodular, polypoid, sclerosing and diffusely infiltrating 
  * Mass forming cases have a firm, white, gritty cut surface

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Generally resembles the large duct type of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma ([Mod Pathol 2014;27:1163](24406866)) 
  * Invasive malignant adenocarcinoma with variable nuclear and cytologic pleomorphism ([World J Gastrointest Oncol 2022;14:607](35321284)) 
  * Widely spaced glands haphazardly infiltrate duct wall 
  * Glands may be well formed, irregular, solid, cord-like, cribriform, abortive or forming papillary structures 
  * Individual infiltrating cells 
  * Variable necrosis can be present 
  * Often a prominent associated neutrophilic infiltrate 
  * May show intraductal growth with papillary, tubular or superficial spreading patterns ([World J Gastrointest Oncol 2022;14:607](35321284)) 
  * Precursor lesion may be visible (biliary intraepithelial neoplasia, intraductal papillary neoplasm of the bile ducts)

Common bile duct

Cytology description

  * Cytologic features include ([Cytopathology 2022;33:257](34729840)) 
    * Irregular, variably sized sheets of atypical to malignant glandular cells 
    * Peripheral palisading nuclei 
    * Branching tapered columns (resembling bile duct epithelium) 
    * Disorderly growth with crowding and piling up 
    * Loss of nuclear polarity (drunken honeycomb appearance) 

Positive stains

Negative stains

  * Generally negative for markers indicating origin in other organ systems (e.g., [TTF1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsttf1.html), [GATA3](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsgata3.html), [PAX8](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspax8.html)) 

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * _KRAS_ , _TP53_ , _ARID1A_ and _SMAD4_ mutations most common ([J Hepatol 2020;73:315](32173382)) 
  * 4 molecular classes have been proposed: metabolic class, proliferation class, mesenchymal class and immune class 

Sample pathology report

  * Extrahepatic bile duct, excision: 
    * Segment of bile duct with extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (2.5 cm), extending 0.7 cm into bile duct wall (pT2) (see synoptic report) 
    * Margins of resection unremarkable 
    * Lymphovascular and perineural invasion present 
    * One lymph node positive for carcinoma (1/1) 
    * Background bile duct with acute and chronic inflammation 

Differential diagnosis

  * Secondary involvement of [pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/pancreasductal.html) or [ampullary carcinoma](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/ampullaadenocarcinoma.html) ([Cancers (Basel) 2023;15:1454](36900245)): 
    * Most relevant in pancreatoduodenectomy specimens 
    * Requires careful gross examination and correlation with clinical and imaging findings 
    * May be impossible to definitively determine for advanced lesions 
  * **Reactive change** : 
    * Can involve biliary surface epithelium or peribiliary glands 
    * On cytology specimens, features of malignancy include 3 dimensional clusters, pleomorphism, 2 cell population and chromatin pattern changes ([Mod Pathol 2017;30:1273](28664934)) 

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/Gallbladdercarcinomaextrahepaticgonzalez11.jpg

This image shows a cholangiocarcinoma arising within the pancreas. Which of the following is true regarding extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma?   
  
  1. It has a good prognosis 
  2. It often shows a prominent neutrophilic infiltrate 
  3. It resembles the small duct type of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma 
  4. Peak onset is in the third decade of life 
  5. Primary biliary cholangitis is a risk factor 

Practice answer #1

**B.** It often shows a prominent neutrophilic infiltrate. This finding is often seen in pancreatobiliary adenocarcinomas in general, including extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. Answer A is incorrect because this disease has a poor prognosis. Answer D is incorrect because peak onset is in the sixth and seventh decades of life. Answer E is incorrect because primary sclerosing cholangitis is a risk factor, not primary biliary cholangitis. Answer C is incorrect because histologically, it most resembles the large duct type of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma.

Practice question #2

Which of the following immunohistochemical stains is typically positive in extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma?   
  
  1. CDX2 
  2. CK7 
  3. CK20 
  4. PAX8 
  5. TTF1 

Practice answer #2

**B.** CK7. As with most pancreatobiliary adenocarcinomas, extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma is positive for CK7. Answers A and C are incorrect because CDX2 and CK20 are variable but more often negative than positive. Answer E is incorrect because TTF1 positivity would suggest lung origin. Answer D is incorrect because PAX8 positivity would suggest renal or gynecologic origin.
