Definition / general

  * Abrupt destructive process of gallbladder 
    * Not typical inflammation; neutrophils often absent 
    * Most often due to stones blocking cystic duct 
    * ~50% of cases complicated by aerobic bacterial infection 

Essential features

  * Inflammation of gallbladder induced by choleliths, ischemia or substances 
  * Gallbladder wall thickening and pericholecystic fluid seen on transabdominal ultrasound 
  * Complications, such as perforation, seen most often in acalculous cholecystitis 
  * Cholecystectomy within 72 hours is typically curative 

ICD coding

  * ICD-9: 
    * [574.00](http://www.icd9data.com/2015/Volume1/520-579/570-579/574/574.00.htm) \- calculus of gallbladder with acute cholecystitis, without mention of obstruction 
    * [574.01](http://www.icd9data.com/2015/Volume1/520-579/570-579/574/574.01.htm) \- calculus of gallbladder with acute cholecystitis with obstruction 
    * [574.30](http://www.icd9data.com/2015/Volume1/520-579/570-579/574/574.30.htm) \- calculus of bile duct with acute cholecystitis, without mention of obstruction 
    * [574.31](http://www.icd9data.com/2015/Volume1/520-579/570-579/574/574.31.htm) \- calculus of bile duct with acute cholecystitis with obstruction 
    * [574.60](http://www.icd9data.com/2015/Volume1/520-579/570-579/574/574.60.htm) \- calculus of gallbladder and bile duct with acute cholecystitis, without mention of obstruction 
    * [574.61](http://www.icd9data.com/2014/Volume1/520-579/570-579/574/574.61.htm) \- calculus of gallbladder and bile duct with acute cholecystitis, with obstruction 
    * [575.0](http://www.icd9data.com/2015/Volume1/520-579/570-579/575/575.0.htm) \- acute cholecystitis 
  * Reference: [Gastroenterol Rep (Oxf) 2017;5:36](27174434)

Epidemiology

  * 10 - 15% of American population 
  * Native American women have highest rates 
    * As high as 73% in women > 30 years ([Surg Clin North Am 2014;94:455](24679431))

Sites

  * Gallbladder 

Pathophysiology

  * Calculous 
    * Blockage of neck or cystic duct 
      * Typically by gallstones or biliary sludge 
      * Continued production of mucus by gallbladder with no outlet
    * Increased pressure within gallbladder 
    * Venous stasis 
    * Arterial stasis 
    * Gallbladder ischemia and necrosis ([Surg Clin North Am 2014;94:455](24679431))
  * Acalculous 
    * Bile stasis and ischemia not provoked by a physical blockade of cystic duct 
      * Ischemia due to: 
        * Activated factor XII 
        * No collateral blood supply; complete reliance on cystic artery ([Surg Clin North Am 2014;94:455](24679431))

Etiology

  * Calculous 
    * Due to stones physically blocking cystic duct
  * Acalculous 
    * 2 - 15% of cases ([Histopathology 2005;47:485](16241996)) 
    * Greater complication risk 
      * More likely to get gangrenous cholecystitis and subsequent perforation ([Infect Dis Clin North Am 2010;24:885](20937456))
  * AIDS related 
    * Similar clinical picture 
    * Due to cytomegalovirus, cryptosporidia or microsporidia 
  * Cocaine induced ([Mills: Sternberg's Diagnostic Surgical Pathology 5th Edition, 2012](https://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/B004Z9662S/pathologyoutl-20)) 
    * Seen in young, healthy patients 
    * Suspect when concurrent vascular thrombosis

Clinical features

  * Classic: right upper quadrant pain, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, fever 

Diagnosis

  * Combination of history, physical, laboratory and imaging findings ([Surg Clin North Am 2014;94:455](24679431)) 

Laboratory

  * Leukocytosis 
  * Hyperbilirubinemia and elevated liver function tests 
    * May indicate choledocholithiasis ([Surg Clin North Am 2014;94:455](24679431))

Radiology description

  * Transabdominal ultrasound 
    * Gallbladder wall > 4 mm 
    * Pericholecystic fluid
  * Cholescintigraphy 
    * Hepatic 2,6-dimethyl-iminodiacetic acid (HIDA) injected intravenously 
    * Gallbladder not visualized ([Surg Clin North Am 2014;94:455](24679431))

Prognostic factors

  * Complications 
    * Perforation, pericholecystic abscess, fistula
  * Mortality from untreated complications is 20% ([BMJ Clin Evid 2008;2008:0411](19445789)) 

Case reports

  * 53 and 58 year old women with empyematous cholecystitis and a 75 year old man with perforated cholecystitis, who were treated with robotic cholecystectomy in the emergency setting ([Medicine (Baltimore) 2019;98:e16010](31348226)) 
  * 59 year old man with COVID-19 pneumonia and ischemic gangrenous cholecystitis ([World J Emerg Surg 2020;15:43](32615987)) 
  * 66 year old man with COVID-19 pneumonia and acute acalculous cholecystitis ([Ann Med Surg (Lond) 2020;58:73](32895611)) 
  * 68 year old woman with COVID-19 pneumonia and concurrent acute cholecystitis ([BMC Infect Dis 2020;20:437](32571224)) 

Treatment

  * Antibiotics 
  * Cholecystectomy 
    * Within 72 hours preferred for surgical candidates
  * Cholecystostomy drain for critically ill, poor candidates ([Infect Dis Clin North Am 2010;24:885](20937456))

Gross description

  * Distention / edema of gallbladder 
  * Serosa 
    * Exudate 
    * Color alteration 
    * Hemorrhagic
  * Wall 
    * Thickened up to 2 cm 
    * Edematous 
    * Hemorrhagic
  * Mucosa 
    * May see ulcers
  * Cholelith(s) 
    * In neck or cystic duct
  * Lumen 
    * May see bile, hemorrhage, fluid mixture of cholesterol and calcium carbonate ([J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Surg 2007;14:15](17252293))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * May not see acute inflammatory cells unless there is a secondary bacterial infection or choledocholelithiasis 
  * Erosion of mucosa 
  * Edema 
  * Myofibroblasts, lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils and pigment laden macrophages 
  * Fibrin 
    * Thrombi in small veins 
    * Sometimes fibrinoid necrosis ([Int J Clin Exp Pathol 2013;6:91](23236547))

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/gallbladderacutecholecystitisHuber01.jpg

A patient undergoes removal of the pictured organ. What symptoms or presentation would suggest that the etiology of the condition that leads to removal of this organ is related to cocaine use?   
  
  1. Amaurosis fugax 
  2. Hematemesis 
  3. Melena 
  4. Vascular thrombosis 

Practice answer #1

**D**. Cocaine induced cholecystitis is seen in young, otherwise healthy patients and should be suspected when there is vascular thrombosis along with involvement of other parts of the gastrointestinal tract.

Practice question #3

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/gallbladderacutecholecystitisHuber02.jpg

What factors make the pictured organ susceptible to ischemia necessitating removal, even in the absence of physical blockade of the duct?  
  
  1. Location in zone 3, furthest from the hepatic artery 
  2. Location within a watershed zone 
  3. No collateral blood supply 
  4. Sensitivity to atherosclerosis of the inferior mesenteric artery 

Practice answer #3

**C**. Cases of acalculous cholecystitis may occur because the gallbladder is completely reliant on the cystic artery, with no collateral blood supply.
