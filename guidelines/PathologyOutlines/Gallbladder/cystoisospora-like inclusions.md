Definition / general

  * Cystoisospora-like inclusions (CLIs) are cytoplasmic inclusions within gallbladder epithelium that mimic _Cystoisospora belli_ infection but are not true organisms ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2019;143:1259](30969156), [Am J Surg Pathol 2018;42:1346](30020094), [Int J Surg Pathol 2021;29:169](33016162), [Am J Clin Pathol 2020;153:88](31600399)) 

Essential features

  * CLIs were previously believed to be indicative of an infection with _Cystoisospora belli_
  * Studies utilizing molecular and electron microscopy have shown that CLIs are more likely artifacts resulting from bile mediated cytolysis rather than true infection with _Cystoisospora belli_
  * Characterized by prominent intraepithelial vacuoles within the gallbladder epithelium which have subnuclear or supranuclear eosinophilic inclusions 
  * No treatment is required for CLIs 
  * References: [Arch Pathol Lab Med 2019;143:1259](30969156), [Am J Surg Pathol 2018;42:1346](30020094), [Int J Surg Pathol 2021;29:169](33016162), [Am J Clin Pathol 2020;153:88](31600399)

Terminology

  * Cystoisospora-like inclusions 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10 
    * [K82](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K80-K87/K82-/K82) \- other diseases of gallbladder 
    * [K82.9](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K80-K87/K82-/K82.9) \- disease of gallbladder, unspecified 

Epidemiology

  * _Cystoisospora belli_ (formerly _Isospora belli_) is an obligate intracellular coccidian protozoan parasite that causes intestinal disease 
  * _C. belli_ has a worldwide distribution but is more common in tropical and subtropical regions; it is seen in the Caribbean, Central and South America, India, Africa and Southeast Asia 
  * Cystoisosporiasis is uncommon in the United States and other developed countries but is often seen in immunocompromised patients with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection or other cellular immunodeficiency ([Am J Trop Med Hyg 1995;53:656](8561272)) 
  * Involvement of the gallbladder is rare 
    * In 2015, a case of _Cystoisospora belli_ infection in an immunocompetent patient with biliary dyskinesia was reported; it lead to several series reporting an increase in incidence of cystoisosporiasis in acalculous gallbladders of immunocompetent patients ([BMJ Case Rep 2015;2015:bcr2015209966](26065551), [Am J Clin Pathol 2019;151:100](30285068), [Am J Surg Pathol 2016;40:1070](27158759)) 
    * Subsequently, through both molecular and ultrastructural analyses, it was proven that these were not true organisms but CLIs ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2019;143:1259](30969156), [Am J Surg Pathol 2018;42:1346](30020094), [Am J Clin Pathol 2020;153:88](31600399), [Int J Surg Pathol 2021;29:169](33016162)) 

Sites

  * Gallbladder 

Pathophysiology

  * Ultrastructural studies have shown that CLIs are formed from aggregates of degenerated or condensed cytoplasmic structures ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2019;143:1259](30969156), [Am J Clin Pathol 2020;153:88](31600399)) 
  * Mechanism of CLI formation is still not entirely clear but they may be secondary to bile mediated cytolysis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2018;42:1346](30020094)) 
  * Molecular studies have also confirmed that CLIs do not represent infectious organisms ([Am J Surg Pathol 2018;42:1346](30020094), [Am J Clin Pathol 2020;153:88](31600399)) 

Etiology

  * CLIs are thought to be mediated by bile mediated cytolysis within the gallbladder epithelium and may occur more frequently in gallbladder specimens that have less fixation time; however, their etiology is not entirely clear ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2019;143:1259](30969156), [Am J Clin Pathol 2020;153:88](31600399), [Am J Clin Pathol 2019;151:100](30285068)) 

Clinical features

  * CLIs are discovered incidentally in gallbladder specimens 

Diagnosis

  * CLIs are discovered incidentally in gallbladder specimens during histologic examination 

Prognostic factors

  * CLIs are of no clinical significance 

Case reports

  * 25 year old woman with prolonged corticosteroid therapy and 35 year old man with abdominal discomfort and diarrhea with HIV ([Pol J Pathol 2016;67:270](28155976)) 
  * 39 year old HIV positive man with acalculous cholecystitis ([Ann Intern Med 1994;121:663](7944075)) 
  * 42 year old man with abdominal pain and abnormal intrahepatic and extrahepatic bile ducts ([Hum Pathol 2009;40:1342](19447468)) 
  * 47 year old woman with chronic cholecystitis with _Cystoisospora belli_ ([BMJ Case Rep 2015;2015:bcr2015209966](26065551)) 
  * 63 year old Vietnamese man with intermittent abdominal pain and diarrhea ([Medicine (Baltimore) 2016;95:e3071](26962840)) 
  * 66 year old man with multiple myeloma alongside severe diarrhea and _Cystoisospora_ infection in the duodenum ([Turk J Haematol 2021;38:171](33112100)) 

Treatment

  * CLIs require no treatment 
  * Trimethoprim and sulfamethoxazole is the treatment of choice for true _C. belli_ infection ([Parasitology 2019;146:1490](31303182)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Cystoisospora-like inclusions morphologically mimic true infection and are composed of clear cytoplasmic vacuoles with subnuclear or supranuclear eosinophilic intraepithelial inclusions ([Int J Surg Pathol 2021;29:169](33016162))

PASD

Positive stains

  * Routine H&E stain is adequate for identification of CLIs in most cases; however, [GMS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsgms.html), [PAS with diastase (PASD)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspas.html) and [mucicarmine](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmucins.html) stains highlight the inclusions ([Int J Surg Pathol 2021;29:169](33016162), [Am J Surg Pathol 2018;42:1346](30020094)) 
  * True _Cystoisospora belli_ organisms identified in the small bowel are negative for [GMS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsgms.html), [mucicarmine](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmucins.html) and [PASD](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspas.html); they are subtly highlighted by [Giemsa](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsgiemsa.html) stain and occasionally [PASD](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspas.html) stain highlights the organism's capsule along with some punctate perinuclear internal staining ([Am J Surg Pathol 2018;42:1346](30020094), [Int J Surg Pathol 2021;29:169](33016162)) 

Electron microscopy description

  * CLIs appear on electron microscopy as dense cytoplasmic aggregates without organelles and with a clear electron lucent rim ([Am J Clin Pathol 2020;153:88](31600399), [Arch Pathol Lab Med 2019;143:1259](30969156))

Sample pathology report

  * Gallbladder, cholecystectomy: 
    * Chronic cholecystitis and intracytoplasmic eosinophilic inclusions (see comment) 
    * Comment: The gallbladder has numerous intracytoplasmic eosinophilic inclusions in subnuclear and supranuclear locations. These are essentially incidental artifactual inclusions that may be seen in the gallbladder epithelium and have no significance clinically. 

  * Alternatively, one could recognize these as CLIs and not mention them in the report since they are of no clinical significance. 

Differential diagnosis

  * **_Cystoisospora belli_** : 
    * Organisms will appear in various stages of the life cycle having different sizes and will be present in both the epithelium and lamina propria ([Am J Clin Pathol 2020;153:88](31600399)) 
Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/gallbladdercystoisosporaHuber7.jpg

A healthy 30 year old woman underwent a cholecystectomy for biliary dyskinesia. Tissue sections from the gallbladder showed intraepithelial eosinophilic inclusions and clear vacuoles. What is the diagnosis?   
  
  1. Artifact 
  2. Cryptosporidiosis 
  3. _Cystoisospora belli_
  4. Fungal infection 
  5. _Microsporidia_

Practice answer #1

**A.** Artifact. The findings represent an artifact of epithelial cytolysis likely related to tissue preservation and do not represent a true infection with _Cystoisospora belli_. Answers B and E are incorrect because _Cryptosporidia_ and _Microsporidia_ organisms are much smaller than CLIs. Answer C is incorrect because CLIs represent a mimicker of true infection with _Cytoisospora belli_. True _Cystoisospora belli_ organisms would be seen in the various stages of the life cycle and not only in the epithelium but also the lamina propria. Answer D is incorrect because fungal organisms would most likely demonstrate hyphae or yeast forms and would be GMS positive.
