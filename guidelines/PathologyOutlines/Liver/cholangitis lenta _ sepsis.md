Definition / general

  * Cholangitis lenta was originally a term referring to a rare clinical form of chronic sepsis ([Hum Pathol 1982;13:19](7076191)) 
  * Now typically used by pathologists to refer to microscopic bile ductular cholestasis, often in the context of sepsis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2017;41:1607](28984672)) 

Essential features

  * Cholangitis lenta currently functions as a synonym for bile ductular cholestasis 
  * Ductular reaction with bile ductular cholestasis is concerning (but not specific) for sepsis 
  * Generally seen in the context of additional histologic changes 

Terminology

  * Additional terms include cholangiolar cholestasis or subacute nonsuppurative cholangitis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2017;41:1607](28984672)) 
  * Lenta is Latin for slow or indolent 

Pathophysiology

  * Cause of biliary abnormalities in septic patients is complex, likely multifactorial ([Crit Care 2012;16:235](23134597)) 

Diagnosis

  * Microscopic examination 

Laboratory

  * Positive microbiology cultures are common ([Am J Surg Pathol 2017;41:1607](28984672)) 
  * Bilirubin levels are often high ([Lefkowich: Scheuer's Liver Biopsy Interpretation, 9th Edition, 2015](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0702055484/pathologyoutl-20)) 

Case reports

  * 2 year old girl with sepsis and hepatorenal syndrome ([Fetal Pediatr Pathol 2018;37:424](30351239)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Dilated, peripherally located bile ductular reaction that contains inspissated bile 
    * Ductular cholestasis can occur in nonseptic patients ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2022;146:1329](35196387)) 
  * Other common findings include portal tract inflammation, duct injury, hepatocyte ballooning or necrosis and canalicular cholestasis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2017;41:1607](28984672))

Other findings

Sample pathology report

  * Liver, biopsy: 
    * Biliary pattern injury with ductular reaction, prominent ductular cholestasis and moderate portal inflammation (see comment) 
    * Comment: The clinical concern for liver failure is noted. The histologic findings raise the possibility of sepsis, though they are not specific for that diagnosis. 

Differential diagnosis

  * Ductular cholestasis can be seen in: 
    * **Sepsis** : 
      * Histology is not entirely specific, with other biliary pattern injury and inflammation often seen 
      * Clinical correlation needed 
Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/livercholangitislentaGonzalezbr.jpg

A patient presents with jaundice and general malaise. Workup indicates acute liver failure, along with an elevated white blood cell count. Liver biopsy shows the findings in the image above. What is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. Acetaminophen toxicity 
  2. Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency 
  3. Primary biliary cholangitis 
  4. Sepsis 

Practice answer #1

**D.** Sepsis. The clinical history, combined with the histologic findings, indicate a diagnosis of sepsis. Answer A is incorrect because this usually causes hepatic necrosis. Answer B is incorrect because such patients would have alpha-1 antitrypsin globules on liver biopsy (best appreciated on PASD stain). Answer C is incorrect because primary biliary cholangitis forms florid duct lesions.

Practice question #2

What is the term typically used by pathologists to refer to bile ductular cholestasis?   
  
  1. Biliary cholangitis 
  2. Cholangitis lenta 
  3. Fibrosing cholestatic hepatitis 
  4. Obstructive cholestasis 

Practice answer #2

**B.** Cholangitis lenta. While this term was originally used clinically, it is now primarily used to describe bile ductular cholestasis, typically in septic patients. Answers A, C and D are incorrect because these clinical scenarios rarely lead to microscopic bile ductular cholestasis.
