Definition / general

  * Progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis (PFIC) is a spectrum of neonatal cholestatic liver diseases resulting from inherited genetic defects 

Essential features

  * Spectrum of hereditary cholestatic liver disorders caused by different gene mutations 
  * Most severe manifestations in pediatric population with marked phenotypic variability in adults 
  * Defective bile secretion and metabolism 
  * Typically presents with intrahepatic cholestasis, jaundice and pruritus during the neonatal period, infancy or early childhood, as well as during adolescence and adulthood 
  * Eventually causes progressive fibrogenic liver disease, leading to cirrhosis and end stage disease with liver failure, requiring liver transplantation 

Terminology

  * Byler disease / PFIC1 / FIC1 deficiency 
  * Byler syndrome / PFIC2 / BSEP deficiency 
  * PFIC3 / MDR3 deficiency 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [P59.9](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/P00-P96/P50-P61/P59-/P59.9) \- neonatal jaundice, unspecified 

Epidemiology

  * Constitutes 10 - 15% of cases of pediatric cholestatic liver disease 
  * Incidence is between 1:50,000 - 100,000 births ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2025;20:80](39984942)) 
  * No sex or geographical predilection 

Sites

  * Liver 

Pathophysiology / molecular genetics

  * Various PFIC types related to different gene mutations that result in defective bile acid homeostasis, transport or excretion 
  * Involved genes behave as autosomal recessive in pediatric PFIC, whereas the same genes behave as autosomal dominant, often as the compound heterozygous state in adult onset cholestatic disease (AOCD) 
  * Null variants (frameshift, nonsense, large deletions) leading to a completely nonfunctional protein are usually associated with PFIC 
  * Missense variants in PFIC related genes resulting in proteins with reduced functionality are associated with milder adult onset cholestatic disease 
  * PFIC1 
    * > 80 mutations in _ATP8B1_ gene on chromosome 18 (18q21), which encodes transmembrane lipid transporter protein FIC1 (flippase) located in the cell membrane 
    * G308V in the Amish population and D554N in the Greenland Inuit people were the first discovered mutations ([Hepatology 2009;50:1597](19731236)) 
    * FIC1 protects the canalicular membrane from the detergent action of high bile salt concentrations by flipping the cell membrane phospholipids 
    * Lower expression of _FIC1_ gene causes downregulation of the nuclear receptor farnesoid X, leading to upregulation of bile acid synthesis, increased bile acid reabsorption in the intestine and secondary reduction in the expression of bile salt export pump (BSEP) 
    * Accumulation of intracellular bile salts coupled with the absence of the protective lipid flipping damages the hepatocytes ([Dig Liver Dis 2019;51:922](31105019)) 
    * FIC1 is expressed in intestines, pancreas and kidney 
  * PFIC2 
    * > 200 mutations in the _ABCB11_ gene on chromosome 2q24 - 31, which encodes the adenosine triphosphate (ATP) dependent BSEP at the canalicular membrane 
    * BSEP is an ATP binding cassette transporter involved in active pumping of conjugated bile salts across the canalicular membrane of hepatocytes ([Semin Liver Dis 2011;31:3](21344347)) 
    * G238V, D482G, G982R, R1153C, R1286Q and ΔGly are some of the common mutations 
    * E297G / D482G mutations in _ABCB11_ gene is found in 30% of Europeans ([J Hepatol 2010;53:170](20447715)) 
  * PFIC3 
    * Mutations in _ABCB4_ gene encoding class III multidrug resistance p-glycoprotein (MDR3) result in defective transport of phosphatidylcholine across the canalicular membrane 
    * High bile salt to phospholipid ratio with hydrophobic bile acids injure the cholangiocytes and cause cholangitis 
    * Cholesterol can crystallize into stones, leading to cholelithiasis 
  * PFIC4 
    * _TJP2_ gene encodes for tight junction protein 2 or zona occludens 2 (ZO2), which maintains cell to cell adhesion and seal canaliculi 
    * Loss of function mutations of TJP2 cause failure of localization of claudin2 (CLDN2) at the membrane resulting in leakage of cytotoxic bile salts in paracellular space and damage to hepatocytes and cholangiocytes ([Front Pharmacol 2023;14:1173542](37324459)) 
  * PFIC5 
    * Farnesoid X receptor (FXR) is a bile acid activated nuclear transcription factor that regulates bile acid metabolism by inducing the expression of BSEP and MDR3 
    * Loss of function mutations in _NR1H4_ gene (chromosome 12q23), encoding FXR result in secondary loss of BSEP and MDR3 expression, accumulation of toxic bile and hepatocellular damage, with rapidly progressive cholestasis in the neonatal period 
    * Homozygous and compound heterozygous variants have been reported, most common in exon 5 ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2024;19:171](38641832)) 
  * PFIC6 
    * Solute carrier family 51 alpha subunit (_SLC51A_) gene encodes the organic solute transporter subunit alpha - beta (OSTα - OSTβ) complex, which is involved in intestinal bile acid reabsorption 
    * Homozygous mutation in _SLC51A_ gene results in abnormal bile acid reabsorption in enterohepatic circulation ([Hepatology 2021;73:2577](33222321)) 
  * PFIC7 
    * Biallelic mutations in the _USP53_ gene on chromosome 14q24.3, which encodes for inactive ubiquitin carboxyl terminal hydrolase 53, involved in degradation of proteins 
    * USP53 plays a role in maintaining the integrity of tight junctions by interaction with TJP2 ([Front Pharmacol 2023;14:1173542](37324459)) 
  * PFIC8 
    * Mutations in the _KIF12_ gene, encoding for a microtubule motor protein required for intracellular transport of microtubules and maintaining the cellular polarity in hepatocytes ([J Pediatr 2022;240:284](34555379)) 
  * PFIC9 
    * Biallelic mutations in the _ZFYVE19_ gene encoding for the zinc finger FYVE type containing 19 protein result in a cholangiocyte specific ciliary dysfunction ([J Med Genet 2021;58:514](32737136)) 
  * PFIC10 
    * Missense or frameshift mutations in the _MYO5B_ gene encoding the motor protein myosin Vb (myoVb) 
    * MYO5B interacts with RAB11A to facilitate normal trafficking of ABC transporter proteins, including BSEP, to the canalicular membrane 
    * MYO5B plays a role in plasma membrane recycling and epithelial cell polarization in enterocytes and respiratory epithelial cells ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2022;74:e115](35129155)) 
  * PFIC11 
    * _SEMA7A_ gene encodes semaphorin 7A protein that is involved in cell signaling and migration 
    * Gain of function mutation at R148W in the _SEMA7A_ gene can lead to disrupted hepatocyte polarity, cholestasis and reduced BSEP and multidrug resistance associated protein 2 (MRP2) expression in hepatocytes ([EMBO Mol Med 2021;13:e14563](34585848)) 
  * PFIC12 
    * _VPS33B_ gene encodes the vacuolar sorting associated protein 33B, which is involved in intracellular transport and lysosomal function ([Front Pharmacol 2023;14:1173542](37324459)) 
    * Mutations in the _VPS33B_ gene can result in abnormal liver cell polarity, leading to bile flow disruption and cholestasis ([Hum Mutat 2019;40:2247](31479177)) 
  * PFIC13 
    * Recently, PFIC13 with a phenotype of hepatorenal ciliopathy and abnormal transport, associated with _PSKH1_ gene mutation has been identified ([Genet Med 2024;26:101231](39132680)) 

Etiology

  * Inherited genetic defects as described in [Pathophysiology](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/liverpfic.html#liverpficpathophysiology)

Molecular mechanisms

Clinical features

  * Unremitting or persistent cholestasis with jaundice and intractable pruritus 
  * PFIC1 
    * Onset in first 3 - 6 months of life 
    * Diarrhea with malabsorption of fats and fat soluble vitamins leads to coagulopathy and rickets 
    * Extrahepatic manifestations include elevated sweat chloride concentrations, sensorineural hearing loss, persistent diarrhea, cholecystitis, pancreatitis, failure to thrive, short stature and respiratory symptoms ([World J Hepatol 2019;11:450](31183005)) 
  * PFIC2 
    * Early onset from birth to 6 months of life 
    * Jaundice, hepatomegaly and severe pruritis 
    * Early progression to advanced fibrosis and end stage liver disease in childhood 
    * No extrahepatic manifestations 
  * PFIC3 
    * Presents in late infancy to young adulthood (2 - 33 years) 
    * Variable jaundice, hepatomegaly and acholic stools 
    * Mental impairment, growth retardation, reduced bone density and learning disabilities 
    * May manifest as adult biliary cirrhosis, low phospholipids cholelithiasis, intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, transient neonatal cholestasis and drug induced cholestasis 
    * Those with homozygous status have a rapidly progressive cholestatic liver disease with potentially life threatening complications, whereas heterozygous status has less severe manifestations ([World J Hepatol 2022;14:98](35126842)) 
  * PFIC4 
    * Varying spectrum ranging from mild anicteric illness and recurrent jaundice to severe progressive liver disease based on remnant functional TJP2 activity ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:2024](35070006)) 
    * Homozygous mutation results in complete loss of TJP2 protein with severe manifestations requiring liver transplantation 
    * ~25 cases have been reported in literature, mostly affecting infants; rare reports in childhood and adults ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:2024](35070006), [BMJ Case Rep 2020;13:e234193](32636225)) 
  * PFIC5 
    * Very rare; ~13 cases reported in literature ([World J Gastroenterol 2020;26:7470](33384548)) 
    * Apart from liver and intestine, NR1H4 mRNA is expressed across a wide range of tissues 
    * Typically presents in neonatal period with normal gamma glutamyl transferase (GGT) associated cholestasis 
    * Early onset vitamin K independent coagulopathy due to loss of FXR dependent coagulation factors and markedly elevated AFP levels ([Nat Commun 2016;7:10713](26888176)) 
    * Rapid progression into micronodular cirrhosis and liver failure, requiring liver transplantation ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2024;19:171](38641832)) 
  * PFIC6 
    * Presents with unexplained elevated liver transaminases, cholestasis and congenital diarrhea with increased fecal loss of bile acids and fat soluble vitamin deficiency in the pediatric age group ([Hepatology 2020;71:1879](31863603)) 
    * < 10 cases have been reported 
  * PFIC7 
    * Age of onset ranges from infancy to adolescence 
    * Cholestasis is generally mild and intermittent, with pruritus and is usually responsive to rifampicin 
    * Associated with hearing loss in childhood and hypocalcemia ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2021;72:667](33075013)) 
  * PFIC8 
    * Marked phenotypic variability ranging from neonatal cholestasis with complete clinical remission to progressive liver disorder requiring liver transplant 
  * PFIC9 
    * Neonatal cholestasis with severe pruritus and hepatosplenomegaly with portal hypertension 
  * PFIC10 
    * _MYO5B_ defects associated with microvillous inclusion disease (MVID) and diarrhea 
    * Can present with isolated cholestasis, isolated MVID or both ([Hepatology 2017;65:164](27532546)) 
    * Typically jaundice, pruritus, chronic malabsorptive diarrhea, coagulopathy, fat malabsorption, rickets and hepatomegaly in pediatric patients ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2022;74:e115](35129155)) 
    * ~30 cases have been reported in literature ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:2024](35070006)) 
  * PFIC11 
    * Infantile cholestasis with transaminitis and hypercholanemia ([EMBO Mol Med 2021;13:e14563](34585848)) 
  * PFIC12 
    * Associated with arthrogryposis renal dysfunction cholestasis (ARC) syndrome, a rare, autosomal recessive disorder characterized by joint contractures, renal dysfunction and cholestasis in infants ([Ital J Pediatr 2014;40:77](25239142)) 
  * PFIC13 
    * Renal failure associated with unilateral renal agenesis and glomerulosclerosis 

Diagnosis

  * Correlation of clinical presentation with imaging, biochemical, histopathological, ultrastructural and genetic findings 
  * Molecular diagnosis 
    * Next generation sequencing remains the gold standard for the diagnosis of novel PFIC variants 
    * Panel gene sequencing (PGS) and whole exome sequencing / whole genome sequencing are the high throughput modalities for discovery and validation of novel genetic variants 
  * References: [J Pediatr 2023;258:113408](37019333), [Orphanet J Rare Dis 2025;20:80](39984942)

Laboratory

  * Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia 
  * Raised serum alkaline phosphatase 
  * GGT: normal or low in all types of PFIC except type 3, 6, 8 and 9 (raised to > 10 times the normal levels) 
  * Elevated alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) levels > 2 times normal limits suggests PFIC2 ([J Hepatol 2010;53:170](20447715)) 
  * Coagulation profile: deranged in PFIC1 and PFIC5 
  * Serum alpha protein levels: elevated in PFIC2 and PFIC5 
  * Serum bile acids: usually elevated in most of PFICs but can be in normal range in PFIC3 and PFIC6 
  * Bile analysis: technically demanding but helpful in exact subtyping 
  * Low bile acids with normal phospholipids seen in PFIC1 and PFIC2 
  * Phospholipids are markedly reduced in PFIC3 (< 15% of total biliary lipids; normal: 19 - 24%) ([World J Hepatol 2022;14:98](35126842)) 

Radiology description

  * Ultrasound 
    * Normal or marked enlargement in gallbladder size with presence of stones can be seen in PFIC2 and PFIC3 
    * Rules out extrahepatic causes of obstructive jaundice, like extrahepatic biliary atresia (EHBA), choledochal cysts, tumor and lymphadenopathy 
  * Cholangiography 
    * Normal biliary tree 
    * To rule out extrahepatic biliary atresia and sclerosing cholangitis 
    * For sampling bile 

Prognostic factors

  * PFIC1 
    * Presence of cirrhosis is a poor prognostic factor requiring liver transplant 
    * Extrahepatic symptoms (like diarrhea and fat malabsorption) persist or worsen after liver transplantation 
    * Refractory bile acid diarrhea, severe steatosis and graft fibrosis leading to graft failure and necessitating retransplant in liver allografts ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2021;72:425](33264179)) 
  * PFIC2 
    * More severe, with rapidly progressive fibrosis and end stage liver disease in initial few years 
    * High risk of progression to hepatocellular carcinoma and cholangiocarcinoma (15%) ([World J Hepatol 2022;14:98](35126842)) 
    * Screening for liver tumors is recommended from the first year of life 
    * Development of BSEP specific alloreactive antibodies, leading to recurrence in ~8% of patients undergoing liver transplantation 
    * E297G / D482G mutations in _ABCB11_ gene is associated with milder and slower disease progression and good response to biliary diversion procedures ([Hepatol Commun 2018;2:515](29761168)) 
  * PFIC3 
    * Hepatocellular carcinoma and cholangiocarcinoma have been associated with PFIC3 ([World J Hepatol 2022;14:98](35126842)) 
    * Biliary diversion procedures are not as effective but liver transplantation is curative 
  * PFIC4 
    * Can present with rapidly progressive cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma during adolescence 
    * Liver transplantation is successful 
  * PFIC5 
    * Worse prognosis due to rapid progression to cirrhosis and liver failure in the first 2 years of life ([Nat Commun 2016;7:10713](26888176)) 
    * Liver transplantation is the only curative option, survival with native liver has not been reported ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2024;19:171](38641832)) 
    * Posttransplant graft liver steatosis due to altered enterohepatic circulation and FXR signaling ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:2024](35070006)) 
  * PFIC6 
    * Due to limited number of cases reported, the biological behavior and prognosis remains elusive ([Hepatology 2018;68:590](28898457)) 
  * PFIC7 
    * Clinical course is usually mild and has a good outcome, with native liver survival in 82.7% 
    * ~17% of cases require liver transplantation ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2024;79:1199](39440620)) 
  * PFIC10 
    * Lifelong total parenteral nutrition is required but is associated with increased risk of sepsis and may require small bowel transplantation 
    * Ongoing cholestatic liver disease worsens after the intestinal transplant, leading to progressive liver fibrosis; therefore, combined liver and intestinal transplantation are preferred ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:2024](35070006)) 
  * PFIC12 
    * Prognosis remains very poor, with death in the first year of life due to fluid loss, infections and bleeding 
    * Very few case reports of novel mutations with prolonged survival ([JIMD Rep 2019;47:4](31240160)) 

Case reports

  * Female infant with a homozygous R148W mutation in semaphorin 7A and progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis ([EMBO Mol Med 2021;13:e14563](34585848)) 
  * 3 month old infant born to first degree consanguineous parents presented with partial and intermittent cholestasis that developed 7 days after birth ([JPGN Rep 2024;5:552](39610437)) 
  * 4 month old girl with severe jaundice, pruritus and clay colored stools ([Medicine (Baltimore) 2019;98:e15593](31083246)) 
  * 7 month old girl with cholestasis for the last 6 months ([Pediatr Gastroenterol Hepatol Nutr 2019;22:479](31555573)) 
  * 2.5 year old Pakistani boy born to first cousin parents was found to have organic solute transporter alpha deficiency ([Hepatology 2020;71:1879](31863603)) 
  * 3 year old boy and 7.7 year old girl (siblings) with novel mutation in _VPS33B_ gene causing a mild ARC syndrome phenotype with prolonged survival ([JIMD Rep 2019;47:4](31240160)) 
  * 19 year old man with cirrhosis and variceal bleeding ([World J Gastroenterol 2020;26:550](32089630)) 
  * 3 boys with rapidly progressing neonatal intrahepatic cholestasis caused by mutations in _NR1H4_ ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2024;19:171](38641832)) 
  * 6 patients with _MYO5B_ gene mutations causing intrahepatic cholestasis of infancy ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2022;74:e115](35129155)) 

Treatment

  * Choleretics such as ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) are first line medical treatment in all PFIC types 
    * UDCA is a hydrophilic bile acid that protects cholangiocytes against toxic hydrophobic bile acids and has immunomodulatory and cytoprotective role ([World J Hepatol 2019;11:450](31183005)) 
  * Apart from skincare, medications such as ursodeoxycholic acid, cholestyramine, rifampicin and naltrexone are used for controlling pruritus ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2025;20:80](39984942)) 
  * Odevixibat, a selective and reversible oral inhibitor of the ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) has been recently approved for the treatment of PFIC patients aged ≥ 6 months 
    * It interrupts enterohepatic circulation and reduces bile acid reuptake, leading to improvement in bile acid levels and pruritis ([Clin Res Hepatol Gastroenterol 2021;45:101751](34182185)) 
  * 4 phenylbutyrate (4PB) increases the expression of the affected transporter proteins on the canalicular membrane and improves bile flow and pruritus in PFIC ([J Hepatol 2016;64:1339](26879107)) 
  * Chemical chaperones can improve MDR3 trafficking in PFIC3 
  * Biliary diversion is effective in 80% of patients who don't respond to medical therapy and are not eligible for liver transplantation 
  * Liver transplantation is the last resort in patients who develop cirrhosis, liver failure or hepatocellular carcinoma or have refractory pruritus 
  * Liver transplantation improves cholestasis in 75 - 100% of patients over a short term follow up of 3 - 5 years, irrespective of PFIC subtype ([World J Hepatol 2022;14:98](35126842)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * PFIC essential diagnostic features 
    * Intralobular cholestasis with ductular reaction; hepatocellular ballooning; giant cell transformation, variable degree of periportal fibrosis with progression to micronodular cirrhosis ([World J Gastroenterol 2020;26:7470](33384548)) 
  * PFIC1 
    * Preserved lobular architecture and bland canalicular cholestasis devoid of inflammation 
    * Cholestasis, hepatocellular ballooning, acinar pseudorosettes 
    * Hepatocytes are orderly and small in early stage 
    * Giant cell transformation is infrequent and focal 
    * Interlobular bile duct may be hypoplastic with paucity 
    * Fibrosis is not characteristic in early biopsies 
    * Progression to micronodular cirrhosis by second to third decades of life ([Semin Liver Dis 2011;31:3](21344347)) 
  * PFIC2 
    * Morphological heterogeneity 
    * Deranged lobular architecture 
    * Zone 3 predominant canalicular and hepatocellular cholestasis with khaki colored bile 
    * Neonatal hepatitis: giant cell transformation, lobular inflammation, hepatocyte necrosis, apoptosis and hepatocyte ballooning with bile ductular reaction (in early biopsies) 
    * Centrilobular fibrosis progressing to bridging fibrosis and eventually to micronodular cirrhosis ([Semin Liver Dis 2011;31:3](21344347)) 
      * Commonly seen within first decade of life 
  * PFIC3 
    * Diffuse hepatocellular cholestasis with occasional canalicular and ductular cholestasis 
    * Portal fibrosis, expansion and florid ductular proliferation without bile epithelial injury, closely resembling extrahepatic biliary obstruction 
    * Extensive portal fibrosis progresses to biliary cirrhosis in advanced stages ([Semin Liver Dis 2011;31:3](21344347)) 
  * PFIC9 
    * Liver biopsy shows cholestasis, periportal fibrosis, bile duct proliferation and ductal plate malformations (DPM) / congenital liver fibrosis ([J Med Genet 2021;58:514](32737136))

Immunohistochemistry & special stains

  * Antibody against **ATP8B1** protein is not validated yet 
  * PFIC1 
    * Canalicular markers [GGT](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd220to229.html#cd224), [CD10](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd10.html) and polyclonal [CEA](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainscea.html) show a diminished or absent expression 
  * PFIC2 / PFIC5 
    * Lack of canalicular expression of [BSEP](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsBSEP.html) protein due to protein truncating mutations 
  * PFIC3 
    * Absent [MDR3](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsMDR.html) protein expression ([J Clin Exp Hepatol 2014;4:25](25755532)) 
  * Weak / normal expression of [BSEP](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsBSEP.html) and [MDR3](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsMDR.html) can be seen in missense mutations; does not rule out functional deficiency ([World J Gastroenterol 2020;26:7470](33384548)) 
  * PFIC4 
    * Loss of **TJP** expression, abnormal [claudin1 (CLDN1)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsclaudins.html#claudin1) localization and normal **claudin 2 (CLDN2)** localization ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:2024](35070006)) 
  * PFIC5 
    * Undetectable expression of [BSEP](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsBSEP.html) and **FXR** in the bile canaliculi, [MDR3](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsMDR.html) expression may be reduced ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2024;19:171](38641832)) 
  * PFIC6 
    * **MYO5B** and **RAB11A** show reduced canalicular staining and cytoplasmic expression 
Electron microscopy description

  * PFIC1 
    * Characteristic coarse granular Byler bile ([J Pediatr 1972;81:484](5049817)) 
  * PFIC2 
    * Amorphous fine filamentous bile 
  * PFIC3 
    * Presence of cholesterol crystals with angulated cleft-like lucencies ([J Clin Exp Hepatol 2014;4:25](25755532)) 
  * PFIC4 
    * Tight junctions appear elongated and lack the densest part of the zona occludens ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:2024](35070006)) 
  * PFIC10 ([World J Hepatol 2021;13:2024](35070006)) 
    * Dilatation of the bile canalicular lumen, canalicular thickening and disappearance of the microvilli apart from cholestasis 
    * No inclusion bodies in hepatocytes, unlike intestinal epithelial cells

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/liverpfic_SakhujaGoyal11.jpeg

A 1 year old boy presented with severe jaundice, watery diarrhea, failure to thrive and intractable pruritus. The investigations revealed normal liver transaminases, raised alkaline phosphatase and normal gamma glutamyl transferase (GGT), normal alpha-1 antitrypsin protein levels, reduced serum albumin and raised serum bile acids. Liver biopsy histology is shown above. Immunohistochemistry for bile salt export pump (BSEP) is retained. Which of the following is true regarding this entity?   
  
  1. Diarrhea can get worse after liver transplantation 
  2. Differential diagnosis includes serum bile acid synthetic defects in this case 
  3. Histology shows lobular disarray with prominent giant cell transformation of hepatocytes and inflammation 
  4. There is a risk of developing hepatocellular carcinoma or cholangiocarcinoma later in life 

Practice answer #1

**A**. Diarrhea can get worse after liver transplantation. Based on the history, investigations, histology and normal BSEP expression, the diagnosis in this case is progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis type 1 (PFIC1). Extrahepatic manifestations, like diarrhea, liver steatosis and short stature, do not improve and may even worsen after liver transplantation in PFIC1. Answer B is incorrect because serum bile acid concentration is low or absent in bile acid synthetic defects and therefore would not be included this differential diagnosis. Answer C is incorrect because lobular disarray with prominent giant cell transformation of hepatocytes and inflammation is a feature of PFIC2. Answer D is incorrect because PFIC1 does not have a risk of developing hepatocellular carcinoma or cholangiocarcinoma later in life.

Practice question #2

Which of the following is true regarding progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis type 3 (PFIC3)?   
  
  1. Bile acid analysis shows low bile acids with normal phospholipids 
  2. Early onset vitamin K independent coagulopathy and markedly elevated alpha fetoprotein (AFP) levels are characteristic of this entity 
  3. Gamma glutamyl transferase (GGT) levels are normal or low 
  4. Normal immunoexpression of multidrug resistance protein 3 (MDR3) does not exclude the diagnosis of PFIC3 

Practice answer #2

**D**. Normal immunostaining of MDR3 does not preclude the diagnosis of PFIC, as some missense mutations are associated with only functional defect in protein. Answer A is incorrect because bile analysis shows markedly reduced phospholipids in PFIC3. Answer B is incorrect because early onset vitamin K independent coagulopathy and markedly elevated AFP levels are characteristic of PFIC5. Answer C is incorrect because GGT levels are not normal or low in PFIC3; rather, they are typically raised.
