Definition / general

  * Decrease in bile flow due to hepatocellular dysfunction or biliary obstruction 

Essential features

  * Microscopically visible bile, usually in hepatocyte cytoplasm or canaliculi 
  * Seen in most forms of biliary pattern injury 
  * Typically indicates biliary process or drug induced liver injury 

Terminology

  * Bland cholestasis refers to cholestasis as an isolated microscopic finding 
  * Cholestatic hepatitis refers to microscopic cholestasis alongside inflammatory findings (i.e., hepatitis) 
  * Histologic cholestasis is sometimes referred to as bilirubinostasis 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [K71.0](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K70-K77/K71-/K71.0) \- toxic liver disease with cholestasis 

Pathophysiology

  * Bile is produced in hepatocytes and flows as follows: hepatocyte canaliculi → canals of Hering → bile ductules → interlobular bile ducts → larger bile ducts → duodenum 
  * Injury or obstruction at any point along biliary flow can lead to cholestasis 

Etiology

  * Most typically seen in biliary disease (primary sclerosing cholangitis, primary biliary cirrhosis) or drug induced liver injury ([Hepatology 2011;53:1377](21480339)) 
  * Other causes include pregnancy, benign familial recurrent cholestasis, sepsis ([World J Gastroenterol 2009;15:2049](19418576)) 

Clinical features

  * Patients with cholestasis may have jaundice, pruritus (due to bile acid deposition in skin), skin xanthomas (due to hyperlipidemia), deficiencies of fat soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) 
  * Clinical cholestasis may not correlate with histologic cholestasis 

Diagnosis

  * Cholestasis is observed microscopically on surgical pathology specimens 

Laboratory

  * Elevated serum alkaline phosphatase (present in bile duct epithelium and hepatocyte canalicular membrane) 
  * Elevated serum bilirubin ([Am J Gastroenterol 2017;112:18](27995906)) 
  * Elevated gamma glutamyl transferase ([Medicine (Baltimore) 2022;101:e28443](35029891)) 

Case reports

  * 7 year old boy with GVHD induced cholestasis ([Sultan Qaboos Univ Med J 2025;25:592](40717930)) 
  * 39 and 58 year old men with cholestasis from hepatitis E virus infection ([World J Hepatol 2025;17:92426](40308830)) 
  * 42 year old man with prolonged cholestasis ([ACG Case Rep J 2025;12:e01803](40761329)) 
  * 43 year old man with cholestasis and multiorgan failure ([Cureus 2025;17:e86217](40677506)) 

Treatment

  * Varies depending on etiology 
  * Ursodeoxycholic acid and obeticholic acid can improve symptoms of cholestatic liver disease ([Hepatology 2002;36:525](12198643)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Bile pigment (yellow / green / brown) is visible histologically within hepatic parenchyma, usually perivenular 
  * May be present within hepatocyte cytoplasm, hepatocyte canaliculi, ductule lumens or large duct lumens 
  * Chronic cholestasis leads to cholate stasis / feathery degeneration of periportal hepatocytes - enlarged, swollen cells with clear / granular cytoplasm 
  * References: [Clin Liver Dis 2004;8:27](15062191), [Semin Liver Dis 2004;24:21](15085484)

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/liver/livercholestasis0GonzalezBRQ1.jpg

Which of the following typically causes bland cholestasis as seen on a liver biopsy?  
  
  1. Amoxicillin / clavulanate injury 
  2. Anabolic steroid injury 
  3. Chronic large duct obstruction 
  4. Primary biliary cholangitis 
  5. Primary sclerosing cholangitis 

Practice answer #1

**B.** Anabolic steroid injury. A classic finding in drug induced liver injury secondary to anabolic steroid use is bland cholestasis. Answer A is incorrect because amoxicillin / clavulanate can cause cholestatic hepatitis. Answer C is incorrect because features of duct obstruction, such as ductular reaction, should be seen. Answer D is incorrect because primary biliary cholangitis causes florid duct lesions and inflammation. Answer E is incorrect because primary sclerosing cholangitis causes periductal fibrosis along with mild inflammation.

Practice question #2

Liver injury with cholestasis typically results in an increase of what laboratory value?  
  
  1. Alanine aminotransferase 
  2. Albumin 
  3. Alkaline phosphatase 
  4. Aspartate aminotransferase 

Practice answer #2

**C.** Alkaline phosphatase. Alkaline phosphatase is present in bile duct epithelium and hepatocyte canalicular membranes, so cholestasis can lead to increased alkaline phosphatase. Answers A and D are incorrect because alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase are most typically elevated in hepatitis. Answer B is incorrect because albumin is not particularly associated with cholestasis.
