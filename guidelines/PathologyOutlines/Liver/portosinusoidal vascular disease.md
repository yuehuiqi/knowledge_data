Definition / general

  * Portosinusoidal vascular disease (PSVD) is a rare, noncirrhotic liver disease, characterized by changes in the hepatic microvasculature, specifically in the portal / periportal venous system, leading to portal hypertension in some but not all patients ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019;4:399](30957754)) 
  * PSVD is not a recently described entity but rather a reclassification of previously known vascular liver disorders, unified under a single terminology to better capture their shared clinical and histological features ([Liver Int 2025;45:e16196](39665793)) 

Essential features

  * May or may not be associated with portal hypertension 
  * PSVD does not include conditions affecting the hepatic veins, causing microvascular damage or associated with vascular malformations 
  * Pathologists should recognize histologic features that can guide a multidisciplinary diagnosis of PSVD ([Liver Int 2025;45:e16196](39665793)) 

Terminology

  * Several different terminologies for this disease have been proposed based on either histology or clinical presentation (see [table 1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/liverhepatoportalsclerosis.html#liverhepatoportalsclerosistable) and [figure 1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/liverhepatoportalsclerosis.html#liverhepatoportalsclerosisfig1)) ([Gastroenterology Res 2021;14:49](34007347), [Liver Int 2025;45:e16196](39665793)) 
  * Terminologies are often used interchangeably in the literature 
    * Noncirrhotic portal fibrosis / idiopathic portal hypertension terminologies have been used by Asian Pacific Association for the Study of the Liver guidelines ([Hepatol Int 2024;18:1684](39546143)) 
    * Idiopathic noncirrhotic portal hypertension is the preferred terminology in Western countries ([J Hepatol 2016;64:179](26516032)) 
  * PSVD is the most recent terminology for this disease, proposed by the Vascular Liver Disease Interest Group in 2019 ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019;4:399](30957754)) 
    * PSVD is considered more inclusive than other applied terminologies 
    * PSVD terminology can help organize data and facilitate collaborative studies 
  * Portosinusoidal vascular disorder is a similar new term that has been suggested ([Liver Int 2025;45:e16196](39665793)) 
    * Some authors argue that the term disease is associated with specific signs and symptoms, whereas PSVD is not always, hence the suggestion of the term disorder

Epidemiology

  * Prevalence varies geographically and is higher in developing countries ([Dig Dis Sci 2013;58:2767](23812828), [Hepatol Int 2007;1:398](19669336), [Gut 2002;51:275](12117894)) 
  * Globally, accounts for 15 - 35% of all cases of portal hypertension ([Korean J Radiol 2023;24:31](36606618)) 
  * Decreasing incidence and prevalence due to improvement in sanitation, hygiene and socioeconomic factors ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2015;10:67](26025214)) 
  * Age of onset and gender distribution vary by region ([Dig Liver Dis 2023;55:178](35906168)) 
    * Highest prevalence is in Japan (33%), where PSVD is more common in women, unlike most other countries 
  * Pediatric cases are rare and less well documented in the literature ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2022;74:e132](35258501)) 

Sites

  * Liver 

Pathophysiology

  * Remains poorly understood 
  * 2 main theories are hypothesized, with pros and cons to each ([Hepatology 2018;68:2413](30066417)) 
    * Increased portal flow hypothesis aligns with clinical observations of splenomegaly preceding portal hypertension 
    * Intrahepatic vascular resistance theory is supported by histologic findings of portal vein sclerosis 
  * Other speculative theories with limited evidence have been proposed ([Hepatology 2018;68:2413](30066417)) 
    * Autoimmune mechanisms (high levels of antiendothelial cell antibodies) 
    * Coagulation imbalances may lead to obliteration of terminal portal venules 
  * Various mechanisms are involved in intrahepatic vascular damage ([Clin Liver Dis 2019;23:781](31563222)) 
    * Dysregulation of adaptative immunity and autoantibody production 
    * Activated inflammatory cells secrete cytokines (TNFα, IL6, IL1 beta) that interact with receptors and activate endothelial cells 
    * Occlusion of the intrahepatic portal microvasculature 
    * Increased resistance to portal blood flow (consequent presinusoidal type of portal hypertension) 

Etiology

  * > 50% of patients have an associated disease ([Clin Exp Hepatol 2023;9:297](38774199)) 
  * More than 1 disease may be simultaneously present in patients with PSVD ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019;4:399](30957754)) 
  * Significant proportion of PSVD cases remain idiopathic ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019;4:399](30957754))

Clinical features

  * PSVD may or may not be associated with portal hypertension ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2022;20:e251](33279774)) 
    * Up to 65% of patients with portal hypertension may be asymptomatic until complications develop ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019;4:399](30957754), [J Hepatol 2025;82:72](39181213)) 
    * Portal hypertension complications 
      * Gastroesophageal varices, with or without bleeding 
        * Initial manifestation in 20 - 40% of cases 
      * Splenomegaly 
      * Ascites (20 - 50% of cases) 
      * Encephalopathy (rare) 
      * Portosystemic collaterals visible on physical examination 
      * Portal vein thrombosis (33% of cases in 5 years) 
    * PSVD must always be suspected if signs of portal hypertension do not match the severity of liver fibrosis 
  * Patients without portal hypertension are frequently asymptomatic ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2022;20:e251](33279774)) 
    * Abnormalities detected incidentally during routine blood tests 
      * 20% of patients with abnormal liver function tests ([J Hepatol 2011;54:455](21087805), [Liver Int 2016;36:454](26264219)) 
      * 67% of patients with nodular regenerative hyperplasia (NRH) ([JHEP Rep 2024;6:101150](39263328)) 
    * May present with mild splenomegaly without clinical signs of portal hypertension 

Diagnosis

  * Multidisciplinary approach is mandatory, as no individual aspect can establish the diagnosis on its own 
  * Relies on an algorithm based on the presence of specific or nonspecific clinical signs of portal hypertension and specific or nonspecific histological features of PSVD (see [Microscopic (histologic) description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/liverhepatoportalsclerosis.html#liverhepatoportalsclerosismicro)) ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019;4:399](30957754), [J Hepatol 2022;77:1124](35690264)) 
    * It has been suggested to revisit and possibly remove the descriptors specific and nonspecific in the future ([Liver Int 2025;45:e16196](39665793)) 
  * Tissue analysis is required for diagnosis 
    * Ruling out cirrhosis is mandatory 
    * Allows evaluation of the hepatic microvasculature 
    * Large liver specimens containing enough portal tracts are needed ([J Hepatol 2016;64:179](26516032)) 
    * Liver tissue requirements for analyses 
      * ≥ 20 mm in length 
      * ≥ 10 portal tracts 
      * Little or no fragmentation 
      * Larger and deeper wedge biopsies may be better than needle biopsies ([Virchows Arch 2018;473:23](29644430)) 
  * Patients more frequently demonstrate portal hypertension 
    * Hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) measurement is the gold standard for assessing the severity of portal hypertension 
      * Normal value of HVPG ranges from 1 to 5 mmHg 
      * HVPG measurement is used to indirectly assess portal pressure 
        * In pre and postsinusoidal forms of portal hypertension, prognostic value is limited but still holds diagnostic significance 
        * Measurement of HVPG is considered questionable in some studies ([Hepatology 2019;70:2228](31318454)) 
          * Lack of reliable predictive value for key clinical outcomes 
      * This procedure also allows for transjugular liver biopsy 
        * May be considered for patients with contraindications for percutaneous biopsy 
  * Clinical signs of portal hypertension considered specific in PSVD ([J Hepatol 2022;77:1124](35690264)) 
    * Gastric, esophageal or ectopic varices 
    * Portal hypertensive bleeding 
    * Portosystemic collaterals at imaging 
  * Clinical signs of portal hypertension considered nonspecific in PSVD ([J Hepatol 2022;77:1124](35690264)) 
    * Ascites 
    * Spleen size > 13 cm in larger axis 
    * Platelet count < 150,000/mm3
  * Extrahepatic portal vein thrombosis (PVT) does not preclude the diagnosis 
    * Portal vein thrombosis is mostly a secondary event ([Dig Dis Sci 2008;53:2784](18270830)) 
  * Diseases that cause liver microvascular damage, affect the hepatic vein or are associated with vascular malformations must be excluded ([J Hepatol 2022;77:1124](35690264)) 
    * History of bone marrow transplantation 
    * Budd-Chiari syndrome 
    * Hepatic venous outflow obstruction 
    * Hepatic schistosomiasis 
    * Cardiac failure 
    * Abernethy syndrome 
    * Hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia 
    * Chronic cholestatic diseases 
    * Sarcoidosis 
    * Hepatic schistosomiasis 
    * Congenital hepatic fibrosis 
    * Various neoplastic conditions 
  * Coexisting liver disease in early fibrotic stages is permitted ([J Hepatol 2022;77:1124](35690264)) 
    * Alcohol misuse 
    * Metabolic syndrome 
    * Viral hepatitis 
  * PSVD is observed in 15 - 20% of patients with unexplained abnormalities in liver function tests ([Clin Res Hepatol Gastroenterol 2020;44:447](32335045)) 
  * Delay in PSVD diagnosis occurs (> 1 year in 38% of patients; > 2 years in 29% of patients) ([J Hepatol 2025;82:72](39181213)) 
  * PSVD remains significantly underdiagnosed ([Korean J Radiol 2023;24:31](36606618)) 

Laboratory

  * Patients with portal hypertension ([J Hepatol 2022;77:1124](35690264)) 
    * Hematological findings 
      * Thrombocytopenia due to hypersplenism caused by splenomegaly; most common laboratory abnormality 
      * Anemia can result from chronic gastrointestinal bleeding (e.g., variceal bleeding) or hypersplenism 
      * Leukopenia also associated with hypersplenism 
    * Evaluation of antiendothelial cell antibodies (AECAs) has been proposed ([Liver Int 2021;41:2171](34173316)) 
    * Liver tests 
      * Elevated transaminase levels occur in 66% of patients and indicate mild liver injury 
      * Elevated gamma glutamyl transferase occurs in 75% of patients (often below 200 IU/L) 
      * Bilirubin is usually normal in PSVD related portal hypertension unless complicated by other liver dysfunctions 
      * Prolonged prothrombin time (or low international normalized ratio) may indicate impaired synthetic liver function 
      * Albumin is normal in most cases but may be decreased in advanced liver disease or portal hypertension related complications 
  * Patients without portal hypertension 
    * Mildly elevated liver enzymes 
    * Mild thrombocytopenia or anemia, often associated with hypersplenism 

Radiology description

  * Multislice computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging 
    * May identify alterations suggestive of PSVD: focal nodular hyperplasia-like lesions and hyperenhancement in the hepatobiliary phase using gadolinium contrast ([Eur Radiol 2023;33:1422](36166087)) 
    * Radiological scoring system helps to differentiate PSVD from cirrhosis ([Eur Radiol 2023;33:1422](36166087)) 
  * Ultrasound findings in patients with portal hypertension 
    * Ascites, portosystemic collaterals, splenomegaly and portal vein thrombosis 
    * Low liver stiffness measurement (< 12 kPa) detected by transient elastography helps to differentiate PSVD from cirrhosis ([Dig Liver Dis 2012;44:855](22721839), [J Hepatol 2017;67:399](28223101)) 
    * Advanced cases may show nodularity resembling cirrhosis ([AJR Am J Roentgenol 2012;198:370](22268180)) 
  * Patients without portal hypertension have normal liver stiffness measurement or mildly elevated values on elastography 
    * Absence of significant findings of portal hypertension on imaging studies

Prognostic factors

  * Mortality is more often due to extrahepatic diseases (50%) associated with PSVD than to complications directly related to this entity ([Clin Res Hepatol Gastroenterol 2020;44:447](32335045), [J Hepatol 2025;82:72](39181213)) 
  * Clinical outcomes in patients with portal hypertension 
    * Higher incidence of portal vein thrombosis 
    * Overall survival: 84% at 3 years ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2022;20:e251](33279774)) 
    * Liver related mortality: 4% at 3 years ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2022;20:e251](33279774)) 
      * Significantly lower in PSVD patients compared to cirrhotic patients 
      * Variceal hemorrhage is the main cause of death ([Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2012;35:1424](22536808)) 
    * Hepatic decompensation: 11% at 3 years 
      * Minority of patients may develop liver failure 
      * Liver transplantation may be necessary in severe cases ([J Hepatol 2016;64:179](26516032)) 
  * Clinical outcomes in patients without portal hypertension 
    * May follow benign and stable course over medium term follow up ([J Hepatol 2025;82:e286](39892820)) 
  * Development of hepatocellular carcinoma is rare and screening is not currently recommended ([Clin Exp Hepatol 2023;9:297](38774199)) 

Case reports

  * 23 year old man with Crohn's disease and mercaptopurine induced hepatoportal sclerosis ([J Crohns Colitis 2013;7:590](22841133)) 
  * 28 year old man with hematemesis and melena without cirrhosis or other common causes of liver pathology ([Int Med Case Rep J 2025;18:289](40046239)) 
  * 36 year old woman with hepatoportal sclerosis and hepatocellular carcinoma in the absence of cirrhosis ([Hepatol Forum 2023;4:35](36843893)) 
  * 69 year old woman with JAK2 positive essential thrombocythemia and splenic vein thrombosis ([Eur J Case Rep Intern Med 2023;10:004138](38077713)) 

Treatment

  * Treatment of associated disease is necessary 
  * Management of portal hypertension ([J Hepatol 2016;64:179](26516032)) 
    * Focuses on preventing complications 
    * Prophylaxis for variceal bleeding with nonselective beta blockers (NSBB) or variceal band ligation if NSBBs are contraindicated 
  * Monitoring is proposed for patients without portal hypertension ([Clin Res Hepatol Gastroenterol 2020;44:447](32335045)) 
  * Management of portal vein thrombosis ([J Hepatol 2016;64:179](26516032)) 
    * Anticoagulant therapy may be used in cases of portal vein thrombosis or high risk thrombophilia 
    * Routine prophylactic use of anticoagulant therapy is not recommended due to bleeding risks 
  * Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt is considered for severe complications, showing similar effectiveness as in cirrhosis ([J Hepatol 2016;64:179](26516032)) 
  * Liver transplantation is reserved for end stage cases or refractory complications ([Transplantation 2023;107:1330](36479977)) 
    * Transplant free survival of 97% (1 year), 83% (5 years) and 72% (10 years) ([J Hepatol 2025;82:72](39181213)) 

Gross description

  * Liver surface can be normal or nodular ([Histopathology 2003;42:251](12605645)) 
  * Organized thrombi in the large portal vein branches can be seen ([Histopathology 2003;42:251](12605645)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * When dealing with biopsies from patients with suspected PSVD, some authors suggest that histology should be assessed by an expert / experienced pathologist ([J Hepatol 2022;77:1124](35690264)) 
    * Histologic alterations can be subtle and unevenly distributed 
      * This increases the risk of underrecognizing PSVD if a pathologist is not aware of possible findings in the histology 
    * Normal liver histology is also described in PSVD with or without portal hypertension ([J Hepatol 2016;64:179](26516032)) 
  * Consensus on the definition and nomenclature for the portal / periportal microvascular histologic changes was established by the International Liver Pathology Study Group ([Histopathology 2019;74:219](30129657)) 
    * Portal vein (PV) stenosis: variable degrees of luminal narrowing with incomplete or complete obliteration of portal vein branches (with or without thickening of the wall), disappearance of PV or fibrotic portal tract 
      * Normal liver: portal vein is 20 - 30 microns in diameter and occupies 67% of portal tracts; portal veins are present in 70% of portal tracts (dyads) 
      * For this finding to be considered pathologic, it must be observed consistently across multiple portal tracts 
    * Herniated portal vein: portal vein from the portal tract directly abutting periportal parenchyma 
      * In normal liver, portal vein is entirely confined to the portal tract 
    * Hypervascularized portal tracts: multiple thin walled vascular spaces in a portal tract 
    * Periportal abnormal vessels: single or multiple thin walled vascular spaces of different caliber outside but in close contact with the portal tract 
  * Consensus recommends not using the following terms for microscopic description: phlebosclerosis, obliterative portal venopathy, portal vein obliteration, hepatoportal sclerosis, aberrant vessels, shunt vessels, fragmentation of portal vein radicles, portal tract angiomatosis, angiomatoid lesion; angiomatous transformation of the portal tract, cavernous transformation, paraportal shunting vessels and megasinusoids 
  * Microscopic findings (described below) are considered either specific or nonspecific 
    * Specific microscopic findings ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019;4:399](30957754)) 
      * Portal vein stenosis 
      * Nodular regenerative hyperplasia 
        * Diffuse nodularity with small, evenly distributed regenerative nodules throughout the liver parenchyma 
          * Best appreciated at low magnification 
          * Reticulin stain is helpful to assess nodularity 
        * Nodules lack surrounding fibrous septa 
        * Sinusoids within nodules may appear dilated or compressed 
        * Hyperplastic hepatocytes in the nodules are larger with surrounding atrophic parenchyma 
      * Incomplete septal fibrosis 
        * Thin and incomplete fibrous septa without nodule formation 
        * This feature can only be assessed in liver explants, not in liver biopsies 
    * Nonspecific microscopic findings ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019;4:399](30957754)) 
      * Portal tract abnormalities (multiplication, increased number of arteries, periportal vascular channels, aberrant vessels) 
      * Architectural disturbance (irregular distribution of the portal tracts and central veins) 
      * Nonzonal sinusoidal dilatation 
      * Mild perisinusoidal fibrosis 
  * Nodular regenerative hyperplasia (33%) and portal vein stenosis (22%) are the most frequent histologic alterations ([J Hepatol 2025;82:72](39181213)) 
    * They often coexist, can be irregularly distributed in the liver and vary in severity ([Histopathology 2019;74:219](30129657)) 
  * Sinusoidal dilatation and fibrosis can be demonstrated in up to 48% of cases ([J Hepatol 2025;82:72](39181213)) 
  * There remains ongoing discussion about histologic components of PSVD ([Liver Int 2025;45:e16196](39665793))

Practice question #2

A 50 year old woman presents with symptoms of portal hypertension, including splenomegaly and esophageal varices. She has no history of significant alcohol use, viral hepatitis or autoimmune liver disease. Liver function tests are within normal limits and imaging shows a patent portal vein with no evidence of cirrhosis. A liver biopsy is performed and reveals portal vein sclerosis and portal vein herniation in the portal tracts but no advanced fibrosis. Which of the following is the most important step in confirming a diagnosis of portosinusoidal vascular disease?   
  
  1. Doppler ultrasound of the hepatic and portal veins 
  2. Genetic testing for inherited thrombophilia 
  3. Hepatic venous pressure gradient measurement 
  4. Immunohistochemistry for CD34 to assess sinusoidal capillarization 

Practice answer #2

**C**. Hepatic venous pressure gradient measurement. The liver biopsy crucially excluded cirrhosis (a common cause of portal hypertension) and identified specific histological findings characteristic of portosinusoidal vascular disease (PSVD), such as portal vein sclerosis and portal vein herniation. These findings, when correlated with clinical and hemodynamic data (e.g., hepatic venous pressure gradient measurement to confirm portal hypertension), are essential for the diagnosis of PSVD. Answer A is incorrect because while Doppler ultrasound can exclude other causes of portal hypertension, such as portal vein thrombosis or Budd-Chiari syndrome, it is not sufficient to confirm PSVD. Answer B is incorrect because while thrombophilia testing may be useful in specific cases of portal vein thrombosis, it does not directly contribute to the diagnosis of PSVD. Answer D is incorrect because sinusoidal capillarization is not specific for PSVD and is not part of the diagnostic algorithm.
