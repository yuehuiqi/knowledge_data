Definition / general

  * Autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disease resulting from underlying _LIPA_ gene mutation ([Mol Genet Metab 2012;105:450](22227072)) 

Essential features

  * Lysosomal storage disease encompassing a spectrum of symptoms and severity 
    * Wolman disease in infancy, < 1% lysosomal acid lipase (LAL) activity, fatal within 6 - 12 months ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2012;54:700](22395188)) 
    * Cholesteryl ester storage disease (CESD) in children and adults, 1 - 12% LAL enzyme activity, leads to fibrosis and eventually micronodular cirrhosis ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2012;54:700](22395188)) 

Terminology

  * Wolman disease in infants 
  * Cholesteryl ester storage disease (CESD) in children and adults 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [E75.5](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/E00-E89/E70-E88/E75-/E75.5) \- other lipid storage disorders 

Epidemiology

  * Relative incidence of CESD and Wolman disease is ~2.5 per 100,000 live births and < 1 per 100,000 live births ([Curr Chem Genom Transl Med 2017;11:1](28401034)) 
  * Infantile onset lysosomal acid lipase deficiency (LALD) is generally fatal within the first 6 - 12 months of life 

Sites

  * Liver, spleen, heart, digestive tract ([Atherosclerosis 2014;235:21](24792990)) 

Pathophysiology

  * LAL is the sole enzyme known to degrade neutral lipids in the lysosome 
  * > 100 LIPA loss of function mutations have been identified ([Atherosclerosis 2017;265:124](28881270)) 
  * Deficiency of LAL results in diminished or absent hydrolysis of cholesteryl esters and triglycerides, trapping cholesterol esters and triglycerides (TG) within the lysosome 
  * Encoded by the _LIPA_ gene located at 10q23.31 ([Genomics 1993;15:245](8432549)) 

Etiology

  * Loss of function mutations have been identified ([Atherosclerosis 2017;265:124](28881270)) 

Clinical features

  * Hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, vomiting, gastrointestinal bleeding, steatorrhea, premature atherosclerosis 
  * Infants have severe malnutrition, adrenal calcifications, hepatosplenomegaly and death within the first few months of life 
  * Late onset form includes dyslipidemia (elevated low density lipoprotein cholesterol [LDLC], low high density lipoprotein cholesterol [HDLC]), elevated liver transaminases, hepatomegaly and splenomegaly 
  * Reference: [Ann Diagn Pathol 2017;31:66](28318950)

Diagnosis

  * Deficient LAL enzyme activity on enzyme assay and genetic testing that identifies mutations of the _LIPA_ gene ([J Hepatol 2013;58:1230](23485521)) 
  * _LIPA_ gene analysis ([Atherosclerosis 2017;265:124](28881270)) 

Laboratory

  * Elevation of total cholesterol, triglycerides and elevated LDLC 
  * Increased levels of transaminases 
  * Cholesteryl ester storage disease is milder with residual baseline LAL activity (1 - 12% of normal) 
  * Wolman disease in infancy, < 1% LAL activity ([Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2017;2:670](28786388)) 

Radiology description

  * Hepatomegaly 
  * Adrenal calcification in infantile form (Wolman disease) 
  * Reference: [Pediatr Radiol 1992;22:541](1491919)

Prognostic factors

  * Late onset form has a more favorable prognosis ([Genet Med 2016;18:452](26312827)) 

Case reports

  * 4.5 month old Caucasian boy presenting with fever, icterus and hepatosplenomegaly ([J Med Case Rep 2023;17:369](37641143)) 
  * 14 year old girl was diagnosed as LALD with the findings of hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, elevated liver enzyme levels and abnormal lipid profile ([Turk J Pediatr 2020;62:831](33108087)) 
  * 43 year old Brazilian man who was diagnosed with cirrhosis and then diagnosed with LALD at 53 years old ([Medicine (Baltimore) 2022;101:e30315](36107601)) 
  * 53 year old Brazilian man who was diagnosed with liver cirrhosis of unknown etiology ([Ann Hepatol 2019;18:230](31113597)) 

Treatment

  * Enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) ([Ther Adv Rare Dis 2021;2:26330040211026928](37181111)) 
  * Sebelipase alfa, a recombinant human lysosomal acid lipase, was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2015 to treat LALD ([Hepatology 2013;58:950](23348766)) 

Gross description

  * Hepatomegaly and greasy liver ([Endotext: Lysosomal Acid Lipase Deficiency [Accessed 11 March 2025]](27809430)) 

Frozen section description

  * Birefringent crystals in hepatocytes and Kupffer cells under polarized light ([J Hepatol 2013;58:1230](23485521)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Diffuse microvesicular steatosis in the hepatocytes 
  * Foamy macrophages, containing lipid and ceroid, are present in the sinusoids and portal tracts 
  * Large Kupffer cells with increased vacuoles, lipid droplets or cholesterol crystals 
  * Progresses to fibrosis and to micronodular cirrhosis ([Histopathology 2012;60:1107](22621222))

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * > 100 _LIPA_ loss of function mutations ([Atherosclerosis 2017;265:124](28881270)) 
  * Most commonly reported mutation is a splice junction mutation in exon 8 with CESD and null mutations with Wolman Disease respectively ([J Hepatol 2013;58:1230](23485521)) 
  * Mutations correlate with age at presentation and severity 
  * Genetic modifiers are lipid metabolic genes (i.e., apolipoprotein E, LDLR) and inflammation / immune response genes 
  * Diet, obesity and metabolic syndrome can modulate the severity and timing of clinical features ([J Lipid Res 2000;41:23](10627498)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Liver, nontargeted / random, biopsy: 
    * Microvesicular steatosis with scattered clusters of PASD positive histiocytes (see comment) 
    * Comment: Sections show hepatocytes with diffuse microvesicular steatosis and accentuated cell membrane. Foamy material within hepatocytes is PAS negative, indicating that it is not glycogen and arguing against glycogenic hepatopathy. Scattered clusters of PASD positive histiocytes are present. CD68 of histiocytes highlights negatively stained crystals and lipids. Electron microscopy demonstrates membrane bound lipids and cholesterol ester crystals in hepatocytes. Overall findings are suggestive of lysosomal acid lipase deficiency. Molecular testing is recommended to confirm the diagnosis.

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/liverlysosomalacidlipasedeficiencyOshimaKarimi02.jpg

Which of the following histologic findings is seen on liver biopsies of patients with lysosomal acid lipase deficiency?   
  
  1. Eosinophilic intracytoplasmic globules 
  2. Macrovesicular steatosis 
  3. Microvesicular steatosis 
  4. Stellate cell hyperplasia 

Practice answer #1

**C**. Microvesicular steatosis. In lysosomal acid lipase deficiency as depicted in the image, microvesicular steatosis is seen on biopsy. Answer B is incorrect because macrovesicular steatosis is usually seen in alcoholic fatty liver disease, metabolic dysfunction associated steatotic liver disease, etc. Answer A is incorrect because eosinophilic intracytoplasmic globules are seen in alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency. Answer D is incorrect because stellate cell hyperplasia is seen in hypervitaminosis A.

Practice question #2

What is the most common molecular abnormality in cholesteryl ester storage disease?   
  
  1. _ATP7B_ gene mutation 
  2. p.C282Y mutation in the _HFE_ gene 
  3. _SERPINA1_ mutation 
  4. Splice junction mutation in exon 8 of _LIPA_ gene 

Practice answer #2

**D**. Splice junction mutation in exon 8 of _LIPA_ gene. Lysosomal acid lipase deficiency (LALD) is an autosomal recessive genetic disease that arises due to mutations in the LAL locus of chromosome 10q23.2. While there are more than 100 loss of function mutations identified in the _LIPA_ gene, splice junction mutation in exon 8 of _LIPA_ gene is the most common mutation identified ([Atherosclerosis 2017;265:124](28881270), [Arterioscler Thromb Vasc Biol 2007;27:1866](17634524)). Answer C is incorrect because _SERPINA1_ mutation is seen in alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency. Answer B is incorrect because this mutation is seen in hereditary hemochromatosis. Answer A is incorrect because _ATP7B_ is the gene mutation involved in Wilson disease.
