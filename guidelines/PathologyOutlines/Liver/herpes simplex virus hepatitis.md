Definition / general

  * Rare complication of herpes infection that results in geographic (nonzonal) hemorrhagic necrosis, which is usually fatal 
  * 1% of acute liver failure ([Clin Transplant 2009;23:37](19930315)) 

Essential features

  * Geographic (nonzonal) hemorrhagic necrosis 
  * Usually pregnant women or immunocompromised patients 
  * Often clinically unsuspected ([Liver Transpl 2007;13:1428](17902129)) 
  * Often not diagnosed until autopsy due to nonspecific clinical features 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [B00.81](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/A00-B99/B00-B09/B00-/B00.81) \- herpesviral hepatitis 

Epidemiology

  * Immunocompromised patients are most susceptible 
  * Immunocompetent patients can be affected ([Am J Clin Pathol 1986;85:694](3518404)) 
  * Risk factors include malignancy and pregnancy (mean: 31 weeks) ([Hum Pathol 1992;23:183](1740303)) 
  * Neonates can also acquire herpes congenitally (as it is one of the TORCH infections), with roughly a quarter of them at risk for developing fulminant hepatitis 

Sites

  * Liver 

Pathophysiology

  * Theories include overwhelming of immune system due to large inoculum and unchecked disease due to deficiency of immune system ([Cureus 2019;11:e4313](31183293)) 

Etiology

  * Herpes simplex virus 1 or 2 (HSV1 or HSV2) 

Clinical features

  * Uncommon cause of liver failure in adults ([Liver Transpl 2008;14:1498](18825709)) 
  * Anicteric (not accompanied by jaundice) 
  * Fever (98%), coagulopathy (84%) and encephalopathy (80%) ([Liver Transpl 2008;14:1498](18825709)) 
  * Clinical presentation in adults is typically that of shock 

Diagnosis

  * HSV DNA can be detected in the blood 
  * 58% diagnosed at autopsy via histologic and immunohistochemical analysis of liver tissue ([Liver Transpl 2007;13:1428](17902129)) 

Laboratory

  * Marked elevation of liver serum markers (aspartate aminotransferase [AST], alanine aminotransferase [ALT], bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase [ALP], gamma glutamyl transferase [GGT]) 
  * Leukopenia 
  * HSV DNA identified via polymerase chain reaction (PCR) 
  * Reference: [J Dig Dis 2018;19:446](29923691)

Radiology description

  * May show innumerable nodules / lesions, mimicking abscesses, throughout liver ([Clin Imaging 2014;38:191](24387920), [ Case Reports Hepatol 2016;2016:8348172](27872770))

Prognostic factors

  * Rapid downhill course with acute liver failure, usually fatal (up to 81% of adults) due to massive hepatic necrosis, even with rapid antiviral therapy or liver transplantation ([Clin Transplant 2009;23:37](19930315), [Liver Transpl 2008;14:1498](18825709)) 
  * Better survival after transplantation in children (69%) than adults (38%) ([J Hepatol 2011;55:1222](21703210)) 
  * Worse prognosis ([Liver Transpl 2008;14:1498](18825709)) 
    * Male gender 
    * Age > 40 years 
    * Immunocompromised 
    * ALT > 5,000 U/L 
    * Platelet count < 75 x 103/L 
    * Coagulopathy 
    * Encephalopathy 
    * Absence of antiviral therapy

Case reports

  * 43 year old man who underwent liver transplant ([Int J Surg Pathol 2025 Aug 12 [Epub ahead of print]](40791108)) 
  * 50 year old man on upadacitinib ([Case Reports Hepatol 2025;2025:1414531](40741531)) 
  * 60 year old man with persistent fever, history of liver transplant ([Am J Case Rep 2024;25:e945140](39618065)) 

Treatment

  * Empiric antiviral therapy (acyclovir) 
  * Liver transplantation with lifelong acyclovir prophylaxis 

Gross description

  * Foci of necrosis in a geographic (nonlobular) distribution ([J Clin Med Res 2016;8:921](27829961))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Geographic (nonzonal) hemorrhagic necrosis 
  * Viable and nonviable areas distributed in a patchy, seemingly random fashion, sometimes with a clean separating border 
  * Classic nuclear features of herpes infection (margination, multinucleation, molding) are seen in hepatocytes, though multinucleation is not always observed 
  * Reference: [J Dig Dis 2018;19:446](29923691)

Sample pathology report

  * Liver, orthotopic transplantation: 
    * Widespread liver necrosis consistent with herpetic hepatitis (see comment) 
    * Comment: Geographic (nonzonal) hemorrhagic necrosis of the liver is present, along with viral cytopathic effect in surviving hepatocytes. Immunohistochemistry for HSV1 is positive, confirming the diagnosis.

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/caseofweek/case481image1.jpg

Herpes simplex hepatitis causes necrosis of hepatocytes in what pattern?   
  
  1. Nonzonal necrosis 
  2. Zone 1 necrosis 
  3. Zone 2 necrosis 
  4. Zone 3 necrosis 

Practice answer #1

**A**. Nonzonal necrosis. Such geographic necrosis is typical of herpes simplex hepatitis. Answers B, C and D are incorrect because such discrete zonal patterns are not found in herpes simplex hepatitis, though other conditions can cause them (e.g., zone 1 necrosis in eclampsia, zone 2 necrosis in yellow fever and zone 3 necrosis in shock).
