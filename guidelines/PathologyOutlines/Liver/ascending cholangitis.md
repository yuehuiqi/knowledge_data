Definition / general

  * Infection of the extrahepatic biliary system, often due to obstruction ([Infect Dis Clin North Am 2000;14:521](10987108)) 

Essential features

  * Highly morbid infection of large bile ducts 
  * Histologic correlate is neutrophils within epithelium and lumen of interlobular bile ducts 
  * Typically a secondary process in an injured bile duct 

Terminology

  * Also called acute cholangitis, suppurative cholangitis 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [K83.09](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K80-K87/K83-/K83.09) \- other cholangitis 

Epidemiology

  * Fewer than 200,000 cases per year in the United States ([World J Gastrointest Pathophysiol 2018;9:1](29487761)) 

Etiology

  * Common infectious causes include _E. coli_ , _Klebsiella_ , enterococci ([Postgrad Med J 2007;83:773](18057178)) 
  * Rarely due to adenovirus ([Pediatr Dev Pathol 2003;6:156](12574911)) 
  * Typically a secondary process that occurs in an already diseased bile duct (stricture, etc.) ([Postgrad Med J 2007;83:773](18057178)) 
  * Can occur following the Kasai procedure to correct [extrahepatic biliary atresia](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/liverextrahepaticbiliaryatresia.html) ([J Pediatr Surg 1989;24:729](2769536), [J Pediatr Surg 2004;39:1800](15616934)) 

Clinical features

  * Symptoms include Charcot cholangitis triad: jaundice, fever and right upper quadrant abdominal pain ([World J Gastrointest Pathophysiol 2018;9:1](29487761)) 
  * May lead to a hepatic pyogenic abscess 

Diagnosis

  * Based on the Tokyo Guidelines, which incorporate systemic inflammation, cholestasis and imaging ([J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 2018;25:17](29032610), [ANZ J Surg 2017;87:554](28337833)) 

Laboratory

  * Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) obtained bile can be cultured to guide antibiotic therapy ([Dig Dis 2019;37:155](30282078)) 
    * Blood culture may be less successful 

Case reports

  * 64 year old woman with _Ascaris_ infection ([Eur J Intern Med 2016;34:e7](27157828)) 
  * 66 year old woman with ascending cholangitis and history of gastric bypass ([J Surg Case Rep 2025;2025:rjaf365](40486690)) 
  * 68 year old woman with recurrent ascending cholangitis ([Ann R Coll Surg Engl 2025 Mar 25 [Epub ahead of print]](40131729)) 
  * 73 year old man who had recently done heavy lifting ([Case Rep Gastroenterol 2017;11:500](29033769)) 
  * 93 year old woman with biliary enteric fistula ([ACG Case Rep J 2018;5:e60](30214909)) 

Treatment

  * Biliary drainage / decompression and antibiotic therapy 
    * Utility of antibiotic treatment is somewhat in doubt ([Diagnostics (Basel) 2025;15:695](40150038)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Neutrophils within lumens of interlobular bile ducts and also infiltrating duct (not ductule) epithelium ([Lefkowitch: Scheuer's Liver Biopsy Interpretation, 9th Edition, 2015](https://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0702055484/pathologyoutl-20)) 
    * Note: neutrophils sprinkled throughout ductular reaction is a typical component of ductular reaction and should not be considered evidence of infection or of ascending cholangitis 
  * Larger ducts often but not always unaffected 
  * Findings of other concomitant biliary processes (e.g., large duct obstruction) may also be present

    * Hepatic parenchyma with cholestasis and neutrophils involving bile duct lumens and epithelium (see comment) 
    * Comment: The findings are concerning for acute (ascending) cholangitis in the proper clinical context. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

Neutrophils filling the lumens of native interlobular bile ducts are most suggestive of which diagnosis?  
  
  1. Ascending cholangitis 
  2. Cholangiocarcinoma 
  3. Primary biliary cholangitis 
  4. Primary sclerosing cholangitis 
  5. Recurrent pyogenic cholangitis 

Practice answer #1

**A.** Ascending cholangitis. This finding is essentially pathognomonic for ascending cholangitis. Answer B is incorrect because cholangiocarcinoma is an infiltrative glandular malignancy. Answer C is incorrect because primary biliary cholangitis shows florid duct lesions (granulomatous destruction of bile ducts). Answer D is incorrect because primary sclerosing cholangitis shows onion skin fibrosis around interlobular bile ducts. Answer E is incorrect because recurrent pyogenic cholangitis shows variable fibrosis and inflammation around bile ducts.

Practice question #2

A patient presents with fever, jaundice and abdominal pain. Liver enzymes are elevated and a liver biopsy is performed. Findings are visible in the figure above. Based on this, which of the following steps may be included in the patient's therapy?   
  
  1. Antibiotics 
  2. Bile duct excision 
  3. Platinum based chemotherapy 
  4. Steroids 
  5. Ursodeoxycholic acid 

Practice answer #2

**A.** Antibiotics. This image demonstrates ascending cholangitis. Typical therapy for this condition is antibiotics, although this is somewhat in doubt. Answer B is incorrect because decompression, not excision, of the bile duct is included in therapy for these patients. Answers C, D and E are incorrect because these treatment modalities are not implicated in patients with ascending cholangitis.
