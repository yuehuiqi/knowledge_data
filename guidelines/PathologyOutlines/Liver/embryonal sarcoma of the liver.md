Definition / general

  * Malignant mesenchymal neoplasm, comprised of undifferentiated cells 

Essential features

  * Mostly a pediatric liver tumor: the third most common in children; some cases in adults 
  * Right liver lobe is more frequently affected 
  * Extensive differential diagnosis 

Terminology

  * First described by Stocker and Ishak in 1978 ([Cancer 1978;42:336](208754)) 
  * According to the WHO 
    * Recommended terminology: embryonal sarcoma of the liver 
    * Acceptable terminology: undifferentiated (embryonal) sarcoma of the liver 
    * Old terminology and not recommended: malignant mesenchymoma, mesenchymal sarcoma, primary sarcoma of the liver 

ICD coding

  * ICD-O: [8991/3](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/f63d7652-1254-47f4-8ab7-3c65ad60d57d/content#page=84) \- embryonal sarcoma 
  * ICD-11: [XH42Q2](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#989583162) \- embryonal sarcoma (of liver) 

Epidemiology

  * Estimated annual incidence is 1 per million ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * Third most common liver malignancy in children ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * Occurs primarily in children, usually 5 - 12 years old ([Cancer 1978;42:336](208754), [Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * Represents 9 - 15% of all childhood liver tumors ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * Slight male predilection; some series report even gender distribution ([Am J Surg Pathol 1991;15:1](1702267), [Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * ~70 cases were described in adults, 24 - 84 years old ([Case Rep Surg 2018;2018:8362012](29971180), [Cancer 2008;112:2274](18361435)) 

Sites

  * Right liver lobe is the most affected, although it can involve both lobes ([J Pediatr Surg 2013;48:2202](24210186)) 
  * Usually liver confined but metastases may be detected at presentation in up to 15% of cases ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * Metastasis occurs more commonly in the lungs, pleura and peritoneum ([Radiographics 2011;31:483](21415193)) 
  * Invasion of the inferior vena cava is rare ([World J Surg Oncol 2006;4:9](16504010)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Oncogenesis remains uncertain ([PLoS Genet 2020;16:e1008642](32310940)) 
  * Can arise from mesenchymal hamartoma of the liver ([Mod Pathol 1994;7:490](8066077), [Cancer 1978;42:336](208754), [Pediatr Dev Pathol 2011;14:111](20925497)) 
  * Chromosomal changes in the noncoding region on chromosome 19q13.4 were described in embryonal sarcoma and mesenchymal hamartoma, suggesting a common origin ([PLoS Genet 2020;16:e1008642](32310940), [Exp Mol Pathol 2013;95:319](24120702)) 
  * Pathogenetic model was proposed, with _C19MC_ translocation as the first event, leading to a benign neoplasm, with a malignant transformation after p53 mutation / loss 

Etiology

  * Can arise from mesenchymal hamartoma of the liver ([Mod Pathol 1994;7:490](8066077), [Cancer 1978;42:336](208754))

Clinical features

  * Symptoms related to rapid growth ([Case Rep Surg 2018;2018:8362012](29971180)) 
  * Abdominal mass with distension or pain ([Cancer 1978;42:336](208754)) 
  * Hepatic rupture ([J Pediatr Hematol Oncol 2007;29:63](17230070)) 
  * Nonspecific symptoms such as fever and weight loss ([Cancer 1978;42:336](208754)) 

Diagnosis

  * Imaging and laboratory tests are nonspecific 
  * Tissue biopsy is recommended to make the diagnosis 

Laboratory

  * Liver tests can be normal 
  * No specific marker in the serum ([Case Rep Surg 2018;2018:8362012](29971180)) 
  * CA 125 and alpha fetoprotein levels are usually normal but an increase has been found in some cases ([Eur J Surg Oncol 2004;30:421](15063896)) 

Radiology description

  * Radiology is nonspecific 
  * On plain radiographs, it appears as a large mass with no calcification ([Radiographics 2011;31:483](21415193)) 
  * Ultrasound (US) shows a solid and iso to hyperechoic lesion ([Radiographics 2011;31:483](21415193)) 
  * On computed tomography (CT) scan, the tumor is heterogenous with abnormal contrast intake 
  * Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is usually performed for resectability evaluation, assessing local structures involvement ([Radiographics 2011;31:483](21415193))

Prognostic factors

  * Combined therapy and complete excision is a predictor of prognosis ([Cancer 1978;42:336](208754), [Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * Tumor size ≥ 15 cm is associated with worse prognosis ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * In children, the 5 year overall survival is 86% ([Pediatr Blood Cancer 2017;64:e26272](27781381)) 
  * Children have higher overall survival in comparison with adults ([Cancers (Basel) 2021;13:2918](34208030)) 

Case reports

  * 10 year old girl with abdominal pain and anorexia ([Case #134](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/caseofweek/case134.htm)) 
  * 13 year old girl with a liver mass ([BMC Gastroenterol 2022;22:2](34979950)) 
  * 43 year old woman presented with a large epigastric mass ([GE Port J Gastroenterol 2023;31:54](38476302)) 
  * 60 year old woman with constitutional symptoms and incidental finding on imaging ([Case Rep Surg 2018;2018:8362012](29971180)) 
  * 74 year old woman with a tumor arising from mesenchymal hamartoma ([Int J Surg Pathol 2012;20:297](22134632)) 

Treatment

  * Treatment is based on complete resection followed by chemotherapy; liver transplant may be performed ([J Gastrointest Surg 2007;11:73](17390190), [J Pediatr Hematol Oncol 2013;35:451](23138115)) 
  * Multimodal treatments have shown better survival ([Cancer 1978;42:336](208754), [Radiographics 2011;31:483](21415193), [Cancer 2002;94:252](11815984))

Gross description

  * 10 - 29 cm, with mean of 14 cm ([Radiology 1997;203:779](9169704), [Cancer 1978;42:336](208754)) 
  * Solitary, right sided, well demarcated, often with a fibrous pseudocapsule ([Radiographics 2011;31:483](21415193)) 
  * Predominantly solid; cut surface is heterogeneous gray-white with cystic, gelatinous, hemorrhagic and necrotic foci ([Radiographics 2011;31:483](21415193), [Radiology 1997;203:779](9169704))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Heterogeneous tumor with a variable cellularity 
  * High grade undifferentiated cells, spindle or stellate shaped, supported by a myxoid stroma 
  * Multinucleated cells and bizarre cells with hyperchromatic nuclei are frequent and scattered within the tumor 
  * Necrosis and mitotic figures are frequent 
  * Eosinophilic hyaline globules that are periodic acid-Schiff positive and diastase resistant (PASD) are a characteristic feature ([Liver Cancer: Undifferentiated Embryonal Sarcoma of the Liver in Adults [Accessed 22 September 2025]](33905202)) 
  * Background stroma is myxoid with thin walled blood vessels 
  * Hepatocytes and bile ducts are frequently entrapped in the periphery 
  * ~50% have areas of extramedullary hematopoiesis ([Cancer 1978;42:336](208754)) 
  * Osteoid formation has been reported ([Asian J Surg 2013;36:174](24054758))

Cytology description

  * Cellular aspirates showing mesenchymal groups with different sizes encompassing small round cells and polygonal large cells, ocasionally multinucleated giant cells ([Diagn Cytopathol 1995;13:44](7587875), [Acta Cytol 1997;41:1273](9990257)) 
  * Intracytoplasmic and extracytoplasmic eosinophil globules may be seen ([Acta Cytol 1997;41:1273](9990257)) 
  * Biliary pigment is absent ([Diagn Cytopathol 1995;13:44](7587875)) 

Positive stains

  * Embryonal sarcoma of the liver does not have a specific immunophenotype; immunohistochemistry is helpful in ruling out the entities in the differential diagnosis ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:269](25611111)) 
Negative stains

Electron microscopy description

  * Overlapping findings in children and adults ([Ultrastruct Pathol 2006;30:403](17182431)) 
  * Expanded rough endoplasmic reticulum ([Hum Pathol 2003;34:246](12673559), [Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:269](25611111)) 
  * Electron dense deposits in lysosomes, corresponding to the PASD resistant eosinophilic globules ([Hum Pathol 2003;34:246](12673559), [Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:269](25611111), [Ultrastruct Pathol 2006;30:403](17182431)) 
  * Myofilaments and intermediate filaments, indicating smooth muscle differentiation, were identified in some tumors ([Hum Pathol 2003;34:246](12673559)) 
  * Lipid droplets are seen in most cases ([Ultrastruct Pathol 2006;30:403](17182431))

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * By comparative genomic hybridization (CGH), gains of chromosomes 1q, 5q, 6q, 8q and 12q and losses of chromosomes 9q, 11p and 14 ([Cancer Genet Cytogenet 2001;126:128](11376805)) 
  * Highly aneuploid genome with recurrent structural alterations of chr19q13, leading to chromosome 19 microRNA cluster overexpression ([Exp Mol Pathol 2013;95:319](24120702), [PLoS Genet 2020;16:e1008642](32310940)) 
  * Recurrent _TP53_ mutation or copy number loss when C19MC changes are present ([PLoS Genet 2020;16:e1008642](32310940)) 
  * Translocations involving the _MHLB1_ locus have been identified in cases arising from mesenchymal hamartoma ([Cancers (Basel) 2024;16:897](38473259), [Exp Mol Pathol 2013;95:319](24120702), [Genes Chromosomes Cancer 2007;46:508](17311249)

Management of undifferentiated embryonal sarcoma of the liver

Sample pathology report

  * Liver, hepatectomy: 
    * Embryonal sarcoma (see comment) 
    * Comment: There is a neoplasia with ill defined borders. It is variably cellular with anaplastic, spindled / oval cells and bizarre cells with prominent hyaline globules (PAS / diastase+). The stroma is variably myxoid with numerous thin walled veins. At the periphery, entrapped hepatocytes and bile duct structures are seen. No vascular invasion was seen. Nontumoral parenchyma without changes.

Practice question #1

What is the most common lobe affected by the embryonal sarcoma of the liver?   
  
  1. Inferior 
  2. Left 
  3. Right 
  4. Superior 

Practice answer #1

**C.** Right. Embryonal sarcoma of the liver usually arises in the right lobe. Answers A and D are incorrect because it usually arises in the right lobe of the liver. Answer B is incorrect because it usually arises in the right lobe of the liver, although it may arise in the left lobe or involve both lobes.
