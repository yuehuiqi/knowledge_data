Definition / general

  * WHO recognized histological variant of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in which the tumor cells show histologic features resembling nonneoplastic steatohepatitis 
  * This variant usually arises in the setting of steatohepatitis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2010;34:1630](20975341)) 

Essential features

  * Variant of hepatocellular carcinoma with histologic features resembling nonneoplastic steatohepatitis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2010;34:1630](20975341)) 
  * Arises in the setting of nonalcoholic steatohepatitis as well as alcoholic steatohepatitis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2010;34:1630](20975341), [Am J Surg Pathol 2020;44:1406](32618599)) 
  * May have yellow appearance on cut surface of tumor 
  * Ubiquitin immunohistochemical stain can be used to highlight Mallory-Denk bodies 
  * Associated with S1 molecular subclass (TGFβ pathway activation) ([Liver Int 2016;36:108](26058462)) 

Terminology

  * Steatohepatic variant of hepatocellular carcinoma 

ICD coding

  * No specific code for steatohepatitic HCC 

Epidemiology

  * Age sixth to seventh decade (similar to conventional HCC) ([Am J Surg Pathol 2010;34:1630](20975341)) 
  * M > F (similar to conventional HCC) ([Am J Surg Pathol 2010;34:1630](20975341)) 
  * Represents 5 - 20% of HCC 

Sites

  * Liver 

Pathophysiology

  * Similar to [HCC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/livertumorHCC.html)

Etiology

  * Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2010;34:1630](20975341)) 
    * Obesity 
    * Diabetes 
    * Hypertension 
    * Dyslipidemia 
  * Alcoholic steatohepatitis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2020;44:1406](32618599)) 
  * May arise in absence of fatty liver disease or metabolic syndrome ([Hum Pathol 2015;46:1769](26410018)) 

Clinical features

  * See Etiology

Diagnosis

  * Similar to [HCC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/livertumorHCC.html)

Laboratory

  * Similar to [HCC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/livertumorHCC.html)

Radiology description

  * Prominent fatty deposits on MRI ([Clin Radiol 2021;76:160.e15](33046228)) 

Prognostic factors

  * Similar prognosis to conventional [HCC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/livertumorHCC.html)

Case reports

  * 49 year old woman with hepatitis C related chronic liver disease ([Trop Gastroenterol 2014;35:49](25276908)) 
  * 65 year old man with history of diabetes and hypertension with 4.8 cm liver mass ([Indian J Surg Oncol 2014;5:161](25114474)) 

Treatment

  * Similar to [HCC](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/livertumorHCC.html)

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Hepatocellular carcinoma with ≥ 50% of tumor showing 3 or more of the following steatohepatitis-like features ([Am J Surg Pathol 2010;34:1630](20975341)): 
    * Steatosis 
    * Hepatocyte ballooning 
    * Mallory-Denk bodies 
    * Inflammation 
    * Pericellular fibrosis

Positive stains

  * Similar to conventional hepatocellular carcinoma ([arginase1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsarginase1.html), [HepPar1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsheppar1.html), [glypican 3](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsglypican3.html), canalicular staining pattern with [CD10](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd10.html) and polyclonal [CEA](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainscea.html), [albumin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsalbumin.html) in situ hybridization, [reticulin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsreticulin.html) can highlight thickened trabeculae) 
  * **Ubiquitin** stain highlights Mallory-Denk bodies 
  * **C reactive protein** ([J Hepatol 2017;67:727](28532995)) 
Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * Associated with S1 molecular subclass (TGFβ pathway activation) ([Liver Int 2016;36:108](26058462)) 
  * Associated with G4 subgroup (JAK / STAT pathway activation, lack of mutations in _CTNNB1, TP53_ and _TERT_ promoter) ([J Hepatol 2017;67:727](28532995)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Liver mass, needle biopsy: 
    * Moderately differentiated hepatocellular carcinoma, steatohepatitic variant

  2. Autoimmune hepatitis 
  3. Chronic hepatitis C 
  4. Primary biliary cholangitis

Practice answer #1

**E.** Steatohepatitis. Steatohepatitic hepatocellular carcinoma most commonly arises in the setting of steatohepatitis, either alcoholic or nonalcoholic.

Practice question #2

Which of the following is true regarding staining of steatohepatitic hepatocellular carcinoma?   
  
  1. Oil red O stain must be performed on formalin fixed paraffin embedded tissue 
  2. PAS stain will show that the clear droplets contain dense glycogen 
  3. Trichrome stain generally shows a thick fibrous capsule with minimal intratumoral fibrosis 
  4. Ubiquitin stain can be used to highlight Mallory-Denk bodies 

Practice answer #2

**D.** Ubiquitin stain can be used to highlight Mallory-Denk bodies. Mallory-Denk bodies may not be present in all cases of steatohepatitic hepatocellular carcinoma but immunohistochemical stain for ubiquitin can be used to confirm or highlight their presence. The cytoplasmic vacuoles in steatohepatitic hepatocellular carcinoma contain predominantly lipid, not glycogen, so they would not be expected to stain with PAS. Oil red O and Sudan Black stains must be performed on frozen tissue (most fat is removed during tissue processing for paraffin embedding). Steatohepatitic hepatocellular carcinoma classically shows a pericellular fibrosis pattern.
