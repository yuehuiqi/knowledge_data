Definition / general

  * Rare genetic disorder that is a syndromic form of paucity of intrahepatic bile ducts 
  * Multisystem disease variably involving other organs, with cardiac, facial, ocular, vertebral and vascular abnormalities 

Essential features

  * Paucity of intrahepatic bile ducts 
  * Neonatal cholestasis as a common feature but with variable severity and multisystem clinical presentations ([Hepatol Commun 2020;4:387](33313463), [J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2011;52:84](21119543)) 
  * Mutations in _JAGGED1_ (_JAG1_) or _NOTCH2_ in most cases 
  * Autosomal dominant with variable penetrance and expressivity 

Terminology

  * Alagille syndrome (ALGS) 
  * Arteriohepatic dysplasia ([Arch Dis Child 1973;48:459](4712776)) 
  * Alagille-Watson syndrome ([StatPearls: Alagille Syndrome [Accessed 4 September 2025]](29939604)) 
  * Watson-Miller syndrome ([StatPearls: Alagille Syndrome [Accessed 4 September 2025]](29939604)) 
  * Syndromic bile duct paucity ([StatPearls: Alagille Syndrome [Accessed 4 September 2025]](29939604)) 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10 
    * [Q44.71](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/Q00-Q99/Q38-Q45/Q44-/Q44.71) \- Alagille syndrome 
    * [Q44.5](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/Q00-Q99/Q38-Q45/Q44-/Q44.5) \- other congenital malformations of bile ducts 
  * ICD-11: [LB20.0Y](https://icd.who.int/browse/2025-01/mms/en#2041553070%2Fother) \- other specified structural developmental anomalies of liver 

Epidemiology

  * Incidence of 1 in 30,000 - 50,000 live births ([Eur J Hum Genet 2014;22:e1](23881058)) 
  * Symptoms usually present in infancy and early childhood 
  * No significant gender, race, ethnicity or geographic predilection 

Sites

  * Intrahepatic bile ducts 

Pathophysiology

  * 2 genes, _JAG1_ and _NOTCH2_ , encode a cell surface ligand (JAGGED1) and a cell surface receptor respectively, which participate in the Notch signaling pathway 
  * Notch signaling pathway, mediated by cell - cell Jagged / Notch interactions is critical for determination of cell fates in early development, including bile duct development ([Nat Genet 1997;16:235](9207787), [Gene 2016;576:381](26548814)) 
  * Genetic alterations in either _JAG1_ or _NOTCH2_ , which may lead to deregulation of the Notch signaling pathway, likely drive hepatoblasts toward the hepatocyte rather than cholangiocyte lineage, resulting in intrahepatic bile duct paucity ([Gastroenterology 2004;127:1775](15578515), [Transl Gastroenterol Hepatol 2021;6:22](33824926)) 

Etiology

  * Autosomal dominant inheritance with variable penetrance 
  * Vast majority (~95%) of the cases (ALGS1) harbor pathogenic variants in the gene _JAGGED1_ (_JAG1_) on chromosome 20p12 ([Gene 2016;576:381](26548814)) 
  * Small proportion (~2.5%) of cases (ALGS2) are due to mutations in the gene _NOTCH2_ (on chromosome 1p13); ALGS2 may present with more prominent renal phenotype and less commonly dysmorphic facial features and skeletal abnormalities than ALGS1 ([Gene 2016;576:381](26548814), [Am J Hum Genet 2006;79:169](16773578))

Clinical features

  * Broad range of multisystem manifestations, variably involving liver, heart, eyes, vertebrae, facial features, vascular system and sometimes kidneys ([Diagnostics (Basel) 2020;10:907](33172025), [Am J Med Genet A 2012;158A:85](22105858), [Pediatr Radiol 2002;32:114](11819079), [Am J Med Genet 2002;112:163](12244550), [J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2002;35:495](12394373), [Eur J Hum Genet 2012;20:251](21934706)) 
    * Hepatobiliary 
      * Neonatal cholestasis 
      * Early onset jaundice, chronic cholestatic disease, portal hypertension, pancreatic insufficiency, pruritus, xanthomas may be seen in later stage 
      * Variable severity, ranging from mildly abnormal liver enzymes to severe neonatal cholestasis ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2011;52:84](21119543), [Clin Liver Dis 2018;22:625](30266153)) 
    * Heart (e.g., peripheral pulmonary artery stenosis, tetralogy of Fallot) 
    * Ophthalmologic (e.g., posterior embryotoxon) 
    * Skeletal (e.g., butterfly vertebrae, hemivertebrae) 
    * Dysmorphic facial characteristics (e.g., prominent forehead, triangular face appearance) 
    * Vascular (e.g., intracranial bleeding, vascular anomalies) 
    * Kidney (e.g., dysplastic kidneys, renal tubular acidosis) 
    * Growth failure (likely multifactorial, due to malabsorption, renal or cardiac disease) 
    * Neurocognitive deficits 

  * Genotype - phenotype correlation studies have not shown a link between mutation type and clinical manifestation or severity 

Diagnosis

  * Diagnosed based on a combination of clinical features, laboratory tests and genetic findings 
  * Liver biopsy may be helpful to confirm intrahepatic bile duct paucity 
  * Next generation sequencing (NGS) for cholestatic liver disease to identify mutations in _JAG1_ or _NOTCH2_ genes 

Laboratory

  * Liver enzyme or function test abnormalities: elevated serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP), gamma glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT) and direct hyperbilirubinemia ([Int J Mol Sci 2023;24:11758](37511516)) 
  * Fat soluble vitamin deficiencies (vitamins A, D, E and K) (may cause increased prothrombin time [PT] or activated partial thromboplastin time [aPTT]) 
  * Hypertriglyceridemia and hypercholesterolemia (primarily due to cholestasis) ([J Endocr Soc 2022;6:bvac005](35155971)) 

Radiology description

  * Hepatobiliary: ultrasound may frequently show small gallbladder, a finding also commonly seen in biliary atresia ([Br J Radiol 2017;90:20170406](28749707)) 
  * Bone: butterfly appearance of vertebral bone due to incomplete fusion lateral chondrification centers during embryogenesis ([Br J Radiol 2022;95:20201241](34609904))

Prognostic factors

  * Patients with cardiac disease and liver disease have lower life expectancy compared to those with no cardiac abnormality ([Hepatol Commun 2020;4:387](33313463)) 
  * No widely accepted prognostic factors, while prolonged hyperbilirubinemia beyond the first few years of life or early fibrosis might be associated with a more severe hepatic outcome ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2010;50:526](20421762), [Liver Int 2016;36:755](26201540)) 
  * 40% of patients develop portal hypertension by 20 years of age in one study ([Hepatol Commun 2020;4:387](33313463)) 
  * Small increase in risk of hepatocellular carcinoma ([Pathol Res Pract 2005;201:55](15807312)) 

Case reports

  * 3 year old boy with high transaminases ([Front Pediatr 2019;7:199](31157196)) 
  * 4 year old boy with neonatal cholestasis and atypical clinical and liver histologic findings ([Pediatr Dev Pathol 2018;21:79](29187043)) 
  * 10 year old boy presented with syndromic paucity of bile ducts ([J Med Case Rep 2016;10:342](27906097)) 
  * 19 year old man with Alagille syndrome and renal pathology ([Kidney Int Rep 2016;2:493](29318215)) 

Treatment

  * Supportive care for symptoms (e.g., pruritus, nutrition, fat soluble vitamin deficiencies, cholestasis) ([J Pediatr Gastroenterol Nutr 2019;69:498](31436707)) 
  * Liver transplantation in patients with progressive liver disease (indicators include synthetic dysfunction, portal hypertension, intractable severe pruritus, growth failure) ([Hepatol Int 2023;17:1098](37584849), [Clin Liver Dis 2018;22:625](30266153)) 

Gross description

  * Wide range of nonspecific findings from normal liver to hepatomegaly or bile tinged liver cut surface 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Paucity of interlobular bile ducts: the hallmark feature ([Clin Liver Dis 2018;22:625](30266153), [Arch Pathol Lab Med 1978;102:402](580878), [Eur J Hum Genet 2012;20:251](21934706)) 
    * Defined as presence of a bile duct in < 50% of evaluable portal tracts 
    * Potential pitfall: only seen in 60% of liver biopsies done prior to 6 months of age ([Hepatology 1999;29:822](10051485)) 
    * May be progressive and seen in 95% of liver biopsies done > 6 months of age 
  * Other nonspecific features which can be variably present 
    * Intrahepatic cholestasis 
      * Particularly zone 3 but may extend throughout the lobule ([Hepatol Commun 2023;7:e0314](38055640)) 
      * Biles in canaliculi, cytoplasm and Kupffer cells 
    * Bile duct proliferation: may be seen especially in early biopsies; when present, mimic biliary atresia 
    * Bile duct damage or degenerative change ([Pediatr Dev Pathol 2018;21:79](29187043)) 
    * Hepatocyte changes (many associated or secondary to cholestasis) 
      * Pseudoacinar / rosette formation 
      * Lobular disarray 
      * Giant cell transformation 
      * Hepatocyte ballooning 
    * Periportal copper accumulation 
    * Portal fibrosis 
      * With or without bridging, may be seen in later stages of the disease 
      * Mild periportal fibrosis may develop and remain stable over time ([Am J Pathol 2007;171:641](17600123)) 
      * Progression to cirrhosis is uncommon

Sample pathology report

  * Liver, needle biopsy: 
    * Paucity of intrahepatic bile ducts with cholestasis (see comment) 
    * Comment: The sample size is adequate and demonstrates liver parenchyma with absence of bile ducts in 12 out of 16 (> 50%) of evaluable portal tracts. Lobular cholestasis (canalicular and cytoplasmic) is present, with occasional giant cell transformation of hepatocytes. There is no significant bile duct / ductular proliferation, portal expansion or portal inflammation. The trichrome and reticulin stains demonstrate no significant portal fibrosis. PAS and PASD stains show no PAS positive diastase resistant cytoplasmic globules. Iron stain demonstrates mild iron deposition in portal / periportal region, within normal limits for age. 
    * Overall, the histologic findings are consistent with paucity of interlobular bile ducts. There is no histologic evidence of extrahepatic biliary obstruction. The differential considerations for paucity of bile ducts include both syndromic (e.g., most commonly Alagille syndrome) and various nonsyndromic etiologies (including infections, metabolic disorders, other genetic disorders and medications). Clinical correlation is needed. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

A 2 month old infant presents with neonatal cholestasis, triangular face and a systolic murmur. Liver biopsy reveals paucity of interlobular bile ducts. Genetic testing is initiated. Which of the following best describes the underlying genetic basis in the majority of patients with this condition?  
  
  1. Mutation in _ABCB4_ gene 
  2. Mutation in _ATP7B_ on chromosome 13q 
  3. Mutation in _JAGGED1_ gene on chromosome 20p12 
  4. Mutation in _NOTCH2_ gene 
  5. Mutation in _PKHD1_ gene 

Practice answer #1

**C.** Mutation in _JAGGED1_ gene on chromosome 20p12. ~95% of Alagille syndrome cases (ALGS1) are due to mutations in the _JAGGED1_ (_JAG1_) gene, located on chromosome 20p12. This gene is part of the Notch signaling pathway and plays a critical role in bile duct development and organogenesis. Answer B is incorrect because _ATP7B_ mutation is associated with Wilson disease. Answer D is incorrect because _NOTCH2_ mutations occur in only about 2.5% of Alagille syndrome cases. Answer A is incorrect because _ABCB4_ mutation is associated with progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis type 3 (PFIC3). Answer E is incorrect because _PKHD1_ mutation is associated with other diseases such as congenital hepatic fibrosis or Caroli disease.

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/liveralagillessyndromesalahi01.jpg

A 3 month old infant presents with prolonged jaundice and a liver biopsy shows paucity of interlobular bile ducts with no fibrosis. Which of the following statements regarding Alagille syndrome is correct?  
  
  1. Alagille syndrome is inherited in an autosomal dominant fashion with variable expression 
  2. Bile duct to portal tract ratio in affected infants is typically between 1.0 and 1.5 
  3. Most cases are due to mutations in the _NOTCH2_ gene on chromosome 1p13 
  4. Onion skin periductal fibrosis is a hallmark histologic feature of Alagille syndrome 
  5. Portal tracts typically show a dense inflammatory infiltrate with a prominent ductular reaction 

Practice answer #2

**A.** Alagille syndrome is inherited in an autosomal dominant fashion with variable expression. Alagille syndrome is caused in most cases by mutations in the _JAG1_ gene and follows autosomal dominant inheritance. Expression is highly variable, even among family members or identical twins. Answer E is incorrect because portal tracts in Alagille syndrome typically show minimal inflammation and paucity of bile ducts defined as presence of bile ducts in < 50% of evaluable portal tracts. Ductular reaction may be occasionally seen especially in early biopsies, increasing the challenge to differentiate from biliary atresia; however, it is not the characteristic feature. Answer C is incorrect because only ~2.5% of cases are due to _NOTCH2_ mutations; the majority (~95%) are due to _JAG1_ mutations. Answer B is incorrect because the bile duct to portal tract ratio in Alagille syndrome is significantly reduced (typically 0.0 - 0.4) compared to normal pediatric livers (0.9 - 1.8). Answer D is incorrect because onion skin fibrosis is characteristic of primary sclerosing cholangitis, not Alagille syndrome.
