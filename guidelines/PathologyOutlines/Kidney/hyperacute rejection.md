Definition / general

  * Immediate rejection of the kidney on reperfusion with the recipient's blood 
  * Variant of acute antibody mediated rejection (ABMR) ([StatPearls: Acute Renal Transplantation Rejection [Accessed 23 July 2025]](31971715)) 

Essential features

  * Occurring within minutes to hours (typically within an hour) 
  * Most severe and dreaded form of rejection where the graft injury is almost always irreversible 
  * Preformed donor specific antibodies (DSA) are sufficient at the time of transplant to cause fatal allograft injury ([Am J Transplant 2011;11:1131](21645250)) 
  * Type II hypersensitivity reaction 

Terminology

  * Also called hyperacute antibody mediated rejection 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [T86.11](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/S00-T88/T80-T88/T86-/T86.11) \- kidney transplant rejection 

Epidemiology

  * In the present day, with the availability of effective tissue crossmatch techniques, hyperacute rejection is encountered in < 0.1% of renal transplantation cases ([Curr Opin Organ Transplant 2022;27:405](35950887)) 

Sites

  * Limited to kidney allograft 

Pathophysiology

  * Capillary endothelium becomes the first target for injury by preformed antibodies 
  * Antibody antigen reactions activate the complement, resulting in vasoconstriction and a disseminated intravascular coagulation in the microvasculature 
  * Within 1 minute after transplantation, widespread blockage of the microvascular system by platelet and neutrophil aggregates and fibrin occurs in all caliber blood vessels ([J Clin Med 2023;12:4130](37373823)) 
  * Occlusion of the microvasculature of the graft results in an ischemic necrosis of the transplanted graft (mono-organ Shwartzman reaction) 
  * Severe injury to vascular endothelium is associated with the release of a variety of biologically active mediators of coagulation and inflammation, stimulation of platelet aggregation and modulation of inflammatory cell interaction with the endothelium ([Transplant Rev (Orlando) 2017;31:47](28126347)) 

Etiology

  * Pre-existing antibodies to donor endothelium at the time of implantation may be due to 
    * Antidonor ABO blood group or human leukocyte antigen (HLA) antibody (class I or class II) 
    * Rare cases of non-HLA or non-blood group antibody mediated hyperacute rejection may occur ([Clin J Am Soc Nephrol 2018;13:182](28446536))

Hyperacute rejection

Clinical features

  * Graft primary nonfunction (anuria) or anuria within hours after graft implantation 
  * Fever ([StatPearls: Acute Transplantation Rejection [Accessed 23 July 2025]](30571031)) 

Diagnosis

  * Based on histopathologic findings of neutrophils in the peritubular capillaries (PTCs) along with diffuse C4d positivity along the capillary walls on IHC examination ([StatPearls: Acute Transplantation Rejection [Accessed 23 July 2025]](30571031)) 

Laboratory

  * See [Microscopic (histologic) description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/kidneyhyperacuterejection.html#hyperacuterejectionmicro)

Prognostic factors

  * Carries a very poor prognosis; almost always results in rapid graft loss 

Case reports

  * 18 year old man with nephrotic syndrome treated with bilateral nephrectomy for proteinuria, develops hyperacute rejection on renal allograft transplant ([Cureus 2023;15:e50538](38222133)) 
  * 43 year old man with deceased donor kidney transplant with high anti-DQ DSA, developing hyperacute rejection ([Transplant Proc 2016;48:2384](27742303)) 
  * 61 year old man with suspected hyperacute allograft rejection caused by non-HLA antibodies ([Transplant Proc 2023;55:1089](37149471)) 
  * 63 year old man with ABO incompatible kidney transplant and hyperacute rejection ([Transpl Immunol 2021;69:101484](34678463)) 

Treatment

  * No effective treatment, the graft loss is irreversible; graft nephrectomy is required to save the life of the patient and prevent disseminated intravascular coagulation ([Transpl Immunol 2024;82:101922](37657691)) 
  * Preventive therapy in ABO incompatible or positive crossmatch transplants, including plasmapheresis, can be done to remove donor specific antibody; intravenous immunoglobulin, rituximab (anti-CD20), anticomplement drug (experimental) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Early (1 - 12 hours) 
    * Platelet and neutrophil margination occur in glomerular or peritubular capillaries, with neutrophils occasionally forming chain-like figures 
    * Endothelium is often stripped off the underlying basal lamina 
    * Capillaries filled with tightly packed red blood cells 
    * Scattered thrombi are noted in glomeruli and arterioles 
    * Hemorrhage is noted ([Nephron 2020;144:18](33264791)) 
  * Later (12 - 24 hours) 
    * Widespread thrombi in glomeruli and arteries 
    * Fibrinoid necrosis of arteries; larger arteries may be spared 
    * Cortical and medullary necrosis 
    * Features resemble severe acute antibody mediated rejection ([Transplant Proc 2016;48:2917](27932107))

Immunofluorescence description

  * C4d in the peritubular capillaries can be picked up on direct immunofluorescence (DIF) examination ([Nephrol Dial Transplant 2007;2:568](17164320)) 
  * Circumferential C4d staining along the peritubular capillaries in the unscarred renal cortex is scored as ([Am J Transplant 2017;17:28](27862883), [Am J Transplant 2011;11:56](21199348)) 
    * Diffuse expression: > 50% of PTCs are positive 
    * Focal expression: staining of ≥ 10 PTCs and < 50% 
    * Focal minimal expression: staining of ≥ 3 but < 10 PTCs 
    * Negative: staining of < 3 PTCs or completely negative

Positive stains

Electron microscopy description

  * Glomerular capillaries show fibrin and platelet thrombi 
  * Degranulated neutrophils with compacted red cells noted with the capillary lumina 
  * Endothelium is swollen and separated from the glomerular basement membrane and may be entirely absent ([Ultrastruct Pathol 1997;21:235](9183824)) 

Genetics

  * Human leukocyte antigens (HLAs) play a critical role in determining the risk of acute rejection following kidney transplantation 
    * HLA molecules, which are encoded by the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) on chromosome 6, are essential for the immune system's ability to distinguish between self and nonself antigens 
    * In the context of transplantation, mismatches between donor and recipient HLA alleles can lead to immune recognition of the graft as foreign, thereby increasing the risk of acute rejection 
  * HLA mismatches, particularly at the HLA-A, HLA-B and HLA-DR loci, are strongly associated with an increased risk of acute rejection 
    * This association underscores the importance of HLA matching in transplantation protocols to minimize immunogenicity and enhance graft survival 
  * Single nucleotide polymorphisms in the HLA alleles have also been associated with acute rejection (notable examples include variants in the _ABCB1, ACE, AIF, AT1R, CCDC67_ and _CCL2_ genes); however, many associations are weak and not consistently reproducible across studies ([Transpl Int 2018;31:263](29030886))

Sample pathology report

  * Kidney, transplant, nephrectomy: 
    * Morphological features and IHC finding suggestive of antibody mediated rejection (see comment) 
    * Comment: Since the rejection occurred a few hours posttransplant, probable hyperacute antibody mediated rejection is considered. Clinical correlation with donor specific antibodies, pretransplant serological and clinical findings recommended. 
    * Clinical history: 45 year old female, multipara, with chronic kidney disease on maintenance hemodialysis for 3 years undergoes ABO compatible deceased donor transplantation. Patient remained anuric in the postanesthetic care unit and the allograft ultrasound demonstrated no blood flow. Graft nephrectomy performed (same day). 
    * Gross description: Received an explant renal allograft (with measurements). External surface appears hemorrhagic. On cut section, widespread hemorrhagic necrosis is noted within the parenchyma. Medulla shows congestion and medium sized vessels show presence of thrombus. Representative sections taken. 
    * Light microscopy: The specimen consists of renal cortex and medulla. The cortex contains 30 glomeruli, 2 of them globally sclerosed. Sampled parenchyma shows large areas of hemorrhagic necrosis and stromal edema. 
      * Glomeruli: Many of the glomeruli show capillary lumina filled with compacted red blood cells. Thrombi formation within capillary lumina are seen. The rest show swollen endothelial cells stripped of endothelium. 
      * Tubules show compacted red blood cells; the tubular epithelial cells show vacuolation and sloughing. 
      * Interstitium shows widespread hemorrhage and edema with scant mononuclear inflammatory infiltrates; patchy necrosis is seen. 
      * Arteries and arterioles show widespread thrombi and fibrin deposition along the vessel wall; few of the vessels show transmural necrosis. 
      * Peritubular capillaries (PTCs) appear dilated throughout the sectioned parenchyma with presence of neutrophils within the lumina. 
      * Immunoperoxidase staining for C4d: strong diffuse staining in the peritubular capillaries 

Differential diagnosis

  * **Perfusion injury / surgical complications** : 
    * Shows prominent loss of endothelium and very rare thrombi 
    * No significant hemorrhage seen 
    * Ischemic injury can lead to necrosis, separated from the unaffected parenchyma by the demarcation zones, which may show increased neutrophils 
    * In contrast, cases of rejection show presence of neutrophils in the PTCs 
    * On IHC, [C4d](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsC4d.html) negativity is noted ([Semin Diagn Pathol 2020;37:148](32249077)) 
  * **Graft loss secondary to thrombosis of extrarenal arteries** : 
    * Occurs due to technical complications 
    * Necrosis / infarction noted with minimal / no hemorrhage 
    * Absence of thrombus formation in intraparenchymal vessels or glomerular capillaries ([Am J Clin Exp Urol 2022;10:129](35874286)) 
    * No accumulation of neutrophils in PTCs and glomeruli

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/kidneyhyperacuterejectionMicroPathak13.jpg

A 39 year old, multigravida woman with blood type B, with chronic glomerulonephritis, received a cadaveric kidney from a 25 year old man, blood type B, who died of a cerebrovascular accident. The lymphocytotoxic crossmatch was negative. The patient had a high PRA (panel reactive antibodies) value. The ischemia time for graft was 20 hours. After unclamping, the transplanted kidney became cyanotic and soft, then swelled and developed widespread hemorrhages. Graft nephrectomy was then done after 5 hours. Microscopically, the allograft showed nuclear fragments and inflammatory cells in many of the glomeruli. Only rare thrombi were present in glomerular capillaries. Cortical hemorrhage and necrosis were noted. IHC for C4d was done as shown in the image above. What is the probable diagnosis?   
  
  1. Hyperacute rejection 
  2. Perfusion injury 
  3. Thrombosis of extrarenal artery 
  4. Thrombotic microangiopathy 

Practice answer #1

**A.** Hyperacute rejection. C4d positivity in peritubular capillaries (PTCs) is seen on IHC, which indicates complement mediated injury as seen in antibody mediated rejection. Since this rejection happens within minutes to hours of transplantation, it is termed hyperacute rejection. Additionally, the recipient was a multigravida, which increases the likelihood of her having preformed antibodies. Also, her titers for panel reactive antibodies were high before transplantation, which could lead to more chances of rejection. Answer B is incorrect because perfusion injury doesn’t show significant cortical hemorrhage and neutrophils in the glomeruli / PTCs and is C4d negative (in PTCs). Answer C is incorrect because thrombosis of extrarenal artery shows absence of neutrophils in PTCs and C4d negativity on IHC. Answer D is incorrect because it shows absence of thrombus formation in intraparenchymal vessels or glomerular capillaries, no accumulation of neutrophils in PTCs and glomeruli and C4d negativity on IHC.

Practice question #2

A 60 year old man with end stage polycystic renal disease underwent an ABO incompatible live related renal transplant. The lymphocytotoxic crossmatch was negative and ischemia time was 32 hours. Posttransplant, the kidney became soft and dusky 15 - 20 minutes after unclamping and the patient became hypotensive. The kidney was removed after 5 hours. The resected allograft contained RBC platelet thrombi with neutrophils in 66% of the glomeruli, ~10% of which were necrotic as well. The possibility of hyperacute rejection of the renal allograft due to ABO incompatibility was considered. Which of the following types of hypersensitivity reaction is it?   
  
  1. Type I hypersensitivity reaction 
  2. Type II hypersensitivity reaction 
  3. Type III hypersensitivity reaction 
  4. Type IV hypersensitivity reaction 

Practice answer #2

**B.** Type II hypersensitivity reaction. Pre-existing antibodies in the recipient’s blood recognized red blood cell antigens on the donor graft and triggered an immediate damaging response. Answer D is incorrect because type IV is cell mediated delayed hypersensitivity reaction where Th1 cells secrete cytokines activating macrophages and cytotoxic T cells, which accumulate at the site of injury as seen in tuberculin reaction. Answer A is incorrect because type I is IgE mediated hypersensitivity, which is seen after exposure to an allergen. Answer C is incorrect as type III hypersensitivity is an immune complex mediated hypersensitivity, in which antibodies take a few days to develop.
