Definition / general

  * Mucosal injury to the colon secondary to nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) ingestion ([Int J Colorectal Dis 1996;11:99](8739836)) 

Essential features

  * Inflammatory response to NSAIDs that involves the colon ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2021;19:1759](32360808)) 
  * Improves with discontinuation of NSAIDs ([Arch Intern Med 1992;152:625](1546927)) 
  * Linked to development of microscopic colitis ([BMC Gastroenterol 2022;22:367](35907802)) 
  * Symptoms include abdominal pain, gastrointestinal bleeding and diarrhea ([Int J Colorectal Dis 1996;11:99](8739836)) 
  * Histologic findings include erosion / ulceration, neutrophilic inflammation and crypt disarray ([Am J Clin Pathol 1998;110:622](9802347), [Adv Anat Pathol 1999;6:213](10410174)) 

Terminology

  * NSAID induced colopathy, NSAID induced colitis 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [T39.395](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/S00-T88/T36-T50/T39-/T39.395) \- adverse effect of other nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs [NSAID] 

Epidemiology

  * More common in older patients, with women perhaps slightly more affected ([Arch Intern Med 1992;152:625](1546927)) 
  * Increased risk with long term use of NSAIDs ([Int J Colorectal Dis 1996;11:99](8739836)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Inhibition of COX1 and COX2 prevents prostaglandin synthesis and leads to direct mucosal injury ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2006;4:196](16469680), [Best Pract Res Clin Gastroenterol 2001;15:691](11566035)) 

Etiology

  * NSAIDs can cause colitis whether given orally or rectally ([J Clin Gastroenterol 1997;25:367](9412924), [Rev Esp Enferm Dig 2013;105:119](23659518)) 

Clinical features

  * Symptoms include abdominal pain, gastrointestinal bleeding and diarrhea ([Int J Colorectal Dis 1996;11:99](8739836)) 
  * Clinical presentation may mimic inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease), infection or ischemic colitis ([Mayo Clin Proc 2021;96:2724](34607638)) 
  * Linked to development of microscopic colitis and possibly inflammatory bowel disease ([BMC Gastroenterol 2022;22:367](35907802), [Ann Intern Med 2012;156:350](22393130)) 

Diagnosis

  * Essentially a diagnosis of exclusion, given the clinical and histologic overlap with other colitides 
  * Presumptive diagnosis easier to make in patients taking long term or high doses of NSAIDs 

Radiology description

  * Generally noncontributory, though rare patients may present with an inflammatory mass ([ACG Case Rep J 2023;10:e01151](37829168)) 

Prognostic factors

  * NSAID use may exacerbate known inflammatory bowel disease ([J Clin Gastroenterol 2016;50:152](26485106), [Ann Intern Med 2012;156:350](22393130)) 

Case reports

  * 55 year old man on diclofenac with 3 day history of melena ([Cureus 2021;13:e20278](35018270)) 
  * 71 year old man on diclofenac for chronic osteoarthritis with 2 year history of worsening diarrhea ([Mayo Clin Proc 2021;96:2724](34607638)) 
  * 84 year old man with lower abdominal pain, cecal mass on computed tomography (CT) and weekly use of ibuprofen ([ACG Case Rep J 2023;10:e01151](37829168)) 

Treatment

  * Withdrawal of NSAID therapy ([Z Gastroenterol 2000;38:957](11194886)) 
  * Sulfasalazine and mesalamine are sometimes given ([Cureus 2021;13:e20278](35018270))

Gross description

  * Very rarely leads to excision but may show extensive punched out colonic ulcers ([Mayo Clin Proc 2021;96:2724](34607638), [Cureus 2021;13:e20278](35018270)) 
  * May cause strictures or diaphragm-like strictures ([J Gastrointestin Liver Dis 2014;23:9](24689088)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Typical features include neutrophilic inflammation and mild crypt distortion ([Am J Clin Pathol 1998;110:622](9802347), [Adv Anat Pathol 1999;6:213](10410174)) 
  * Mild cases show architectural distortion with minimal inflammation 
  * Severe cases show erosion / ulceration with granulation tissue 
  * Pyloric metaplasia may be present ([Virchows Arch 2025;486:1221](39496819))

Sample pathology report

  * Colon, biopsy: 
    * Colonic mucosa with architectural distortion and mild acute inflammation (see comment) 
    * Comment: The patient's reported history of long term NSAID use is noted. While the histologic findings are not specific, they are compatible with NSAID associated colitis. 

Differential diagnosis

  * Most forms of colitis have histologic overlap and are challenging to distinguish without corresponding clinical information ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2012;136:854](22849731), [Surg Pathol Clin 2017;10:863](29103537)) 
Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/colonnsaidMicroGonzalez4.jpg

A 74 year old woman presents with abdominal pain. Her medical history reveals long term over the counter medication use for chronic pain but no known gastrointestinal issues. Colonoscopy shows scattered punched out ulcers in the cecum, which are biopsied. The colon is otherwise normal. Upper endoscopy is unremarkable. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. Cardiogenic shock 
  2. Celiac disease 
  3. Checkpoint inhibitor colitis 
  4. NSAID associated colitis 
  5. Ulcerative colitis 

Practice answer #1

**D.** NSAID associated colitis. This patient is known to be taking over the counter pain medication (likely NSAIDs) and has focal colonic ulcers with mild histologic injury. Of the available choices, NSAID associated colitis is the most likely etiology. Answer A is incorrect because the patient would likely present with more severe symptoms, along with histologic features of ischemic colitis. Answer B is incorrect because celiac disease should cause small bowel injury. Answer C is incorrect because the patient is not known to be taking a checkpoint inhibitor and histologic features implicating checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., increased intraepithelial lymphocytes, increased apoptoses) are absent. Answer E is incorrect because the patient presents with focal right sided ulcers, which would be a very atypical presentation for ulcerative colitis.

Practice question #2

A 50 year old man with a history of osteoarthritis of the knee presents with a complaint of hematochezia. The cause of bleeding is determined to be NSAID associated colitis. Which of the following findings is most likely on histopathologic examination of a colon biopsy?   
  
  1. Increased intraepithelial lymphocytes 
  2. Mild neutrophilic inflammation and crypt distortion 
  3. Noncaseating granulomas 
  4. Prominent crypt distortion with basal lymphoplasmacytosis 
  5. Viral cytopathic inclusions 

Practice answer #2

**B.** Mild neutrophilic inflammation and crypt distortion. Colonic NSAID injury is usually mild and fairly nonspecific histologically. More severe examples may demonstrate ulceration. Answer A is incorrect because increased intraepithelial lymphocytes is seen in colitis caused by other drugs (e.g., checkpoint inhibitors, angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors). Answer C is incorrect because noncaseating granulomas is more typical of Crohn's disease. Answer D is incorrect because prominent crypt distortion with basal lymphoplasmacytosis is more indicative of inflammatory bowel disease. Answer E is incorrect because viral cytopathic inclusions suggest certain infections (e.g., cytomegalovirus).
