Definition / general

  * Microsatellites (short tandem repeats) are repetitive DNA sequences composed of repeating motifs of 2 - 10 nucleotides 
  * Microsatellite instability (MSI) is one of the pathways implicated in colorectal adenocarcinoma (CRC) carcinogenesis; colorectal cancer with MSI is referred to as MSI / microsatellite unstable or mismatch repair (MMR) deficient (dMMR) 

Essential features

  * Microsatellite instability (MSI) results from abnormal function of 1 or more MMR genes (_MLH1_ , _PMS2_ , _MSH2_ , _MSH6_) 
  * 10 - 15% of colorectal adenocarcinomas are MSI / MMR deficient (dMMR) 
  * dMMR colorectal cancer arises due to sporadic hypermethylation of the _MLH1_ promoter or due to germline mutations in MMR genes ([Lynch syndrome](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumorlynch.html)) 
  * Colorectal cancer is initially screened for MSI by assessing the presence of MMR proteins using immunohistochemistry 
  * Identifying dMMR colorectal cancer is significant as these tumors have a better prognosis and can be treated with immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy ([J Clin Oncol 2022;40:2735](35649217)) 

Terminology

  * Lynch syndrome was formerly known as hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer (HNPCC) syndrome 
ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [Z15.09](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/Z00-Z99/Z14-Z15/Z15-/Z15.09) \- genetic susceptibility to other malignant neoplasm 

Epidemiology

  * 10 - 15% colorectal cancer is dMMR ([Clin Cancer Res 2012;18:1506](22302899)) 
    * 80% of dMMR colorectal cancer is sporadic 
      * Hypermethylation of _MLH1_ promoter 
      * ≥ 60 years old 
      * F > M 
    * 20% of dMMR colorectal cancer is familial 
      * Autosomal dominant, germline mutation (Lynch syndrome) 
      * ~ 40 - 50 years old 
      * M = F 

Sites

  * dMMR colorectal cancer has predilection for the right colon ([Hum Pathol 2018;78:125](29723603)) 
    * Lynch syndrome related colorectal cancer arises from [tubular adenomas](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumortubularadenoma.html) ([Mod Pathol 2017;30:1144](28548127)) 
    * Sporadic dMMR colorectal cancer arises from [sessile serrated adenomas / polyps](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumorsessileserrated.html) ([Histopathology 2007;50:113](17204026)) 

Pathophysiology

  * When there are defects in MMR genes (_MLH1_ , _PMS2_ , _MSH2_ , _MSH6_), mutations introduced into microsatellite regions during DNA synthesis are not repaired, which results in microsatellite instability (MSI) 
  * MMR pathway (see [diagram 1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumormolecularmicrosatellite.html#colontumormolecularmicrosatellitediag)) ([J Gastroenterol 2020;55:15](31494725)) 
    * Corrects DNA base substitution mismatch, insertions, deletions or slippage made during DNA replication 
    * Errors are corrected through the action of 2 protein complexes 
      * MutSα: heterodimer of mutS homologue 2 (MSH2) and mutS homologue 6 (MSH6) proteins 
      * MutSβ: heterodimer of MSH2 and mutS homologue 3 (MSH3) proteins 
    * MutSα or MutSβ bind to the area with the defect and recruit MutLα (heterodimer of mutL homologue 1 [MLH1] and postmeiotic segregation increased 2 [PMS2] proteins) 
    * PMS2 endonuclease makes a nick at 5' to the mismatch (upstream) 
    * Exonuclease 1 is recruited and activated by MSH2 or MLH1 
      * Catalyzes excision of the nascent DNA strand up to and slightly beyond the mismatch 
    * DNA excision gap is resynthesized by polymerase δ and nick is sealed by DNA ligase I 
  * MLH1 is required for the function of PMS2 (when MLH1 is lost, PMS2 must also be lost) 
  * MSH2 is required for the function of MSH6 (when MSH2 is lost, MSH6 must also be lost) 

Etiology

  * See [Pathophysiology](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumormolecularmicrosatellite.html#colontumormolecularmicrosatellitepath)
  * Colorectal cancer is MSS / MMR proficient (pMMR) or MSI / MMR deficient (dMMR) (see [diagram 2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumormolecularmicrosatellite.html#colontumormolecularmicrosatellitediag)) 
    * pMMR colorectal cancer: intact MMR genes 
    * dMMR colorectal cancer: abnormality affecting 1 or more MMR genes 
      * Sporadic 
        * _MLH1_ promoter hypermethylation 
        * 50% _BRAF_ V600E mutation 
        * _ARID1A_ may have a role in carcinogenesis ([Hum Pathol 2014;45:2430](25311944)) 
      * Familial - Lynch syndrome 
        * Germline mutation followed by somatic mutation in wild type allele 
        * Mutations in _MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, MSH6, EPCAM_ (adjacent to _MSH2_) 
      * Other 
        * Rare cases with isolated loss of PMS2 
          * Patients present at an older age and have a lower risk for carcinoma at other sites ([J Clin Oncol 2015;33:319](25512458)) 
        * dMMR tumors lacking germline mutations and _MLH1_ hypermethylation 
          * 70%: double somatic mutations in MMR gene ([J Pathol 2014;234:548](25111426), [Gastroenterology 2014;147:1308](25194673))

Clinical features

  * Lynch syndrome is associated with increased risk of carcinoma at other sites 
    * Gastrointestinal: colon, small intestine, stomach, hepatobiliary, pancreas 
    * Genitourinary: kidney, bladder, prostate 
    * Gynecological: endometrium, ovary 
  * Reference: [J Pathol 2020;250:518](32141610)

Diagnosis

  * Clinical assessment 
    * If there is clinical suspicion for Lynch syndrome, screening may be done using the Amsterdam criteria or Bethesda system 
      * Amsterdam criteria (1999) ([Gastroenterology 1999;116:1453](10348829)) 
        * At least 3 relatives with HNPCC associated cancer (colorectal cancer, cancer of endometrium, small bowel, ureter or renal pelvis) and all 5 following criteria should be met 
          * At least 1 should be first degree relative of the other 2 
          * At least 2 successive generations affected 
          * At least 1 diagnosed with colorectal cancer before age 50 
          * Tumor verified by pathological examination 
Prognostic factors

  * dMMR colorectal cancer 
    * Better stage adjusted survival compared to MSS tumors ([Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev 2001;10:917](11535541)) 
    * Prognosis for colorectal cancer associated with Lynch syndrome and _MLH1_ promoter hypermethylation is the same ([Genet Med 2016;18:863](26866578)) 

Case reports

  * 16 year old girl with advanced stage colorectal cancer due to de novo _MSH2_ mutation ([BMJ Case Rep 2020;13:e233935](32611652)) 
  * 25 year old man with early age of onset familial case of Lynch syndrome with splice site mutation in _MSH2_ ([Biomed Rep 2022;16:39](35386111)) 
  * 32 year old man with history of ulcerative colitis, known _MSH2_ mutation and family history of Lynch syndrome with multiple synchronous colorectal cancers ([Front Oncol 2022;12:1031606](36620534)) 
  * 50 year old man with peritoneal metastases from colon cancer in context of Lynch syndrome with complete pathologic response post immunotherapy and cytoreductive surgery ([BMC Gastroenterol 2022;22:17](35012456)) 

Treatment

  * Surgery 
    * pMMR colorectal cancer 
      * For node negative disease, surgical excision 
      * If higher stage (T3, T4) or LN positive, consider chemotherapy 
    * dMMR colorectal cancer 
      * Surgical excision 
      * Lynch: with or without prophylactic hysterectomy 
  * Immunotherapy 
    * dMMR solid tumor: pembrolizumab ([Science 2017;357:409](28596308)) 
    * Metastatic dMMR colorectal cancer: nivolumab ([Lancet Oncol 2017;18:1182](28734759)) 
  * Other 
    * Cases with _BRAF_ mutations have limited response to EGFR targeted therapies ([N Engl J Med 2015;372:2509](26028255)) 

Gross description

  * Colorectal carcinoma in Lynch syndrome 
    * Absence of diffuse polyposis ([Patholog Res Int 2012;2012:350309](22619739)) 
    * Mainly located in the proximal colon (right side predominant) 
    * Larger masses with a polypoid / exophytic appearance and classic colorectal cancer appearance: ulcerated lesion with heaped up / rolled edges ([Diagn Histopathol 2015;21:161](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mpdhp.2015.04.007))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Lynch syndrome / dMMR colorectal cancer histologic characteristics ([N Engl J Med 2018;379:764](30134129), [Cancer Treat Rev 2016;51:19](27838401)) 
    * Mucinous, poorly differentiated, signet ring or medullary subtypes 
    * Increased intratumoral / peritumoral lymphocytes 
    * Crohn's-like reaction 
    * Relative lack of intraluminal (dirty) necrosis 
    * Tumoral heterogeneity

MMR IHC assessment

  * Staining interpretation ([Dig Dis 2023;41:746](37231848)) 
    * Retained: moderate to strong nuclear expression in ≥ 10% of tumor cells 
    * Loss: no evidence of nuclear expression 
    * Indeterminate 
      * Staining in tumor cells is less intense compared to the internal control 
      * Expression is retained in < 10% of tumor cells 

Positive stains

  * MSS / pMMR colorectal adenocarcinoma shows retained staining pattern ([Mod Pathol 2019;32:1](30600322)) 
    * Intact nuclear expression of [MLH1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmlh1.html), [MSH2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh2.html), [MSH6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh6.html) and [PMS2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspms2.html)
    * Background stromal / inflammatory cells and normal mucosal epithelial cells at the crypt base provide a positive internal control 

Negative stains

  * MSI / dMMR colorectal adenocarcinoma shows abnormal staining patterns with 1 or more absent MMR proteins ([Mod Pathol 2019;32:1](30600322)) 
    * Combined [MLH1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmlh1.html) and [PMS2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspms2.html) loss 
      * Most common; no genetic abnormality in [PMS2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspms2.html)
      * _BRAF_ or _MLH1_ methylation testing analysis should be performed on [MLH1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmlh1.html) loss colorectal adenocarcinoma cases 
      * May be associated with additional loss of [MSH6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh6.html) or [MSH2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh2.html) / [MSH6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh6.html), usually due to additional double hit somatic mutation as a result of high number of mutations in dMMR 
        * Associated clonal loss of [MSH2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh2.html) / [MSH6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh6.html) and _BRAF_ / _MLH_ methylation testing analyses are recommended 
        * No germline testing is recommended 
      * Associated complete loss of [MSH2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh2.html) / [MSH6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh6.html) (null phenotype) on resection, germline testing is recommended as somatic [MLH1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmlh1.html) / [PMS2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspms2.html) loss has been associated with germline _MSH2_ / _MSH6_ mutation 
    * Germline testing is recommended for less common patterns of loss that are more likely associated with Lynch syndrome, including 
      * Isolated loss of [PMS2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspms2.html)
      * Loss of [MSH2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh2.html) and [MSH6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh6.html)
      * Isolated loss of [MSH6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh6.html)
      * Combined loss of [PMS2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainspms2.html) and [MSH6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsmsh6.html)
  * Note: several pitfalls exist when interpreting MMR ([Mod Pathol 2019;32:1](30600322)) 
    * Subclonal loss ([Hum Pathol 2020;96:104](31783044)) 
      * Heterogeneous staining (abrupt loss of staining adjacent to areas with retained staining) 
      * Possible causes 
        * Variable epitope expression 
        * Heterogeneous second hit mutation 
        * Accumulation of _MLH1_ methylation in select tumor areas 
        * Post neoadjuvant therapy 
      * CAP recommendation: MSI by PCR in specifically dissected area of tumor with MMR loss (see [Molecular / cytogenetics description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumormolecularmicrosatellite.html#microsatellitemolecular)) 
    * M1 clone of MLH1 may show artefactual granular (dot-like staining) in cases with MLH1 loss ([Pathol Res Pract 2020;216:152581](31402167)) 

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * _BRAF_ somatic mutation testing 
    * DNA change C.1799T>A 
    * Amino acid change: p.V600E (Val600Glu) 
  * _MLH1_ methylation testing 
  * MSI instability ([Dig Dis 2023;41:746](37231848)) 
    * PCR amplification of specific DNA sequences can determine instability by comparing length of nucleotide repeats in tumor cells and paired normal sample from the same patient 
    * MSI high (MSI-H) 
      * Shift in size of at least 2 of the 5 microsatellite loci in neoplastic cells compared to normal tissue (or > 30% of loci in larger panels) 
    * Microsatellite stable (MSS) 
      * No loci with instability (or < 10% of loci in larger panels) 
    * MSI low (MSI-L) 
      * Shift in size of 1 locus (or in 10 - 30% of loci in larger panels) 
      * Behaves similarly to MSS 
      * Does not appear to be a good predictor of Lynch syndrome 
      * Grouped with MSS, does not require further testing

Practice question #1

Sporadic MMR deficient colorectal carcinoma is due to promoter hypermethylation of which of the following genes?   
  
  1. _EPCAM_
  2. _MLH1_
  3. _MSH2_
  4. _MSH6_
  5. _PMS2_

Practice answer #1

**B**. _MLH1_. Hypermethylation of the _MLH1_ (heterodimer of mutL homologue 1) promoter is the most common cause of sporadic microsatellite unstable (MSI) colorectal adenocarcinoma. _MLH1_ is part of a heterodimer, which also includes postmeiotic segregation increased 2 (PMS). MLH1 / PMS2 are members of the DNA mismatch repair (MMR) pathway, which fixes mutations in microsatellite regions made during DNA replication. Defects in any of the MMR genes (including _MLH1_) prevent these mutations from being repaired, resulting in MSI / MMR deficient (dMMR) colorectal adenocarcinoma. In contrast to promoter hypermethylation that leads to sporadic MMR deficient colorectal carcinoma, Lynch syndrome is due to germline mutations in any of the MMR genes. Answers A, C, D and E are incorrect because mutations in _EPCAM_ , _MSH2_ , _MSH6_ or _PMS2_ are associated with Lynch syndrome rather than sporadic MMR deficient colorectal carcinoma.

Which mismatch repair (MMR) protein frequently shows diminished expression in post neoadjuvant treated colorectal carcinoma?   
  
  1. MLH1 
  2. MSH2 
  3. MSH6 
  4. PMS2
