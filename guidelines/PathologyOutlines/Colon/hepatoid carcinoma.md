Definition / general

  * Rare, aggressive subtype of adenocarcinoma that morphologically resembles hepatocellular carcinoma and produces alpha fetoprotein 

Essential features

  * Extremely rare subtype of colorectal carcinoma 
  * Resembles hepatocellular carcinoma morphologically and sometimes immunohistochemically 
  * Serum alpha fetoprotein may be elevated 

Terminology

  * Originally termed alpha fetoprotein producing tumor 

Epidemiology

  * More common in men 
  * Fewer than 20 colonic cases reported 

Sites

  * Hepatoid carcinoma has also been reported in other sites, including [stomach](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stomachhepatoid.html), gallbladder and uterus ([World J Gastroenterol 2013;19:321](23372352)) 
  * Also ampulla, bladder, lung, ovary 

Clinical features

  * Can occur in patients with ulcerative colitis ([World J Gastroenterol 2014;20:12657](25253971)) 
  * Serum alpha fetoprotein may be elevated ([Hepatobiliary Pancreat Dis Int 2008;7:539](18842504)) 

Case reports

  * 42 year old man with rectal hepatoid carcinoma with liver metastases ([Hepatobiliary Pancreat Dis Int 2008;7:539](18842504)) 
  * 59 year old man with colonic α fetoprotein producing carcinoma ([Dis Colon Rectum 2003;46:826](12794586)) 
  * 71 year old man with hepatoid carcinoma colliding with liposarcoma ([World J Surg Oncol 2007;5:42](17448253)) 
  * Rectal tumor arising in ulcerative colitis ([Dis Colon Rectum 2000;43:105](10813132)) 
  * Sigmoid alpha fetoprotein producing adenocarcinoma ([Pathology 1995;27:277](8532396)) 

Treatment

  * Surgery, possibly chemotherapy ([Pathol Oncol Res 2012;18:93](21667305)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Resembles hepatocellular carcinoma, with trabecular or solid pattern of large polygonal cells with abundant eosinophilic or clear cytoplasm 
  * Component of traditional adenocarcinoma also often present 
  * Lymphovascular invasion is common ([Histopathology 1997;31:47](9253624))
