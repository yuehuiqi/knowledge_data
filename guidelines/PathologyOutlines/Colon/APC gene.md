Definition / general

  * APC is a tumor suppressor "gatekeeper" gene located on 5q that regulates the level of [beta-catenin](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsbetacatenin.html) and directs the downstream activity of Wnt / beta catenin pathways ([Appl Clin Genet 2015;8:95](25931827)) 
  * APC contributes to orderly migration of intestinal cells within the crypt and beta-catenin plays a crucial role in this function; it also plays a role in intercellular adhesion

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * 90% of cases of classic FAP are caused by germline mutation of APC ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2014;12:1059](23962553)) 
  * More than 1500 germline mutations exist, the majority being point mutations ([Colon Cancer Gene Variant Databases](https://www.insight-group.org/variants/databases/)) 
  * Exon 15 is the most common target for mutations, at codons 1061 and 1309 ([Hum Mol Genet 2001;10:721](11257105)) 
  * Mutations are mostly nonsense or frameshift, resulting in a truncated protein 
  * Biallelic inactivation leads to a loss of protein function ([Genet Med 2014;16:101](24310308)), which causes aberrant transcription of c-myc, cyclin D1 and others 
  * APC mutations promote the T cell factor-lymphoid enhancer factor (TCF-LEF) pathway and inhibit the cellular adhesion complex, thereby stimulating cell proliferation ([Mol Cancer 2003;2:41](14672538)) 
  * The gold standard for mutation detection is direct DNA sequencing of all 15 coding exons 
  * Various other methods are available to detect point mutations, including PCR based methods ([Tech Coloproctol 2004;8:s305](15666112))

Differential diagnosis

  * In FAP-like patients without APC mutation, biallelic mutations of MUTY human homologue (MYH) gene may be found ([MUTYH associated polyposis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/colontumormyhassociated.html)) 
  * 10% of patients with classic FAP phenotype do not have identifiable mutations in APC or MYH
