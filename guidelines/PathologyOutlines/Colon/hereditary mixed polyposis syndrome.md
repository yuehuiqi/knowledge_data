Definition / general

  * Hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome (HMPS) is a rare inherited disorder characterized by the presence of various types of colorectal polyps (i.e., adenomatous, serrated, inflammatory, juvenile and polyps with mixed histologic types) and associated with an increased risk for colorectal cancer ([Gastroenterology 2017;152:1876](28242209)) 

Essential features

  * Autosomal dominant inheritance 
  * Upstream duplication of _GREM1_
  * Multiple polyps of different types, including polyps with mixed morphologies 
  * Increased risk of colorectal cancer 

Terminology

  * _GREM1_ associated mixed polyposis syndrome 
  * Hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome 1 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [D12.6](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/C00-D49/D10-D36/D12-/D12.6) \- benign neoplasm of colon, unspecified 
  * ICD-11: [2E92.40](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#790871642) \- polyposis syndrome 

Epidemiology

  * Rare condition with unknown incidence 
  * Mostly described in individuals of Ashkenazi Jewish descent ([Gastroenterology 2017;152:1876](28242209)) 
  * Polyps typically present in adults (mean age: 33 years) but may develop in childhood ([Hum Pathol 2017;60:75](27984123)) 
  * Affects both males and females 

Sites

  * Colorectum 
  * Extracolonic manifestations, if any, are not well defined 

Pathophysiology

  * Upstream duplication of _GREM1_
    * 40 kb duplication spanning the 3' end of the secretogranin V5 (_SCG5_) gene and a region upstream of the gremlin 1 (_GREM1_) locus found in individuals of Ashkenazi Jewish descent ([Nat Genet 2012;44:699](22561515)) 
    * Other duplications (16 kb and 24 kb) have been reported ([Mol Aspects Med 2019;69:10](30862463)) 
  * Duplication leads to an increase in GREM1 expression, which then decreases bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) pathway activity leading to tumorigenesis, a mechanism shared with juvenile polyposis ([Nat Genet 2012;44:699](22561515)) 
  * Germline pathogenic variants in _BMPR1A_ have rarely been described ([Am J Gastroenterol 2009;104:3027](19773747), [J Med Genet 2006;43:e13](16525031)) 

Etiology

  * Upstream duplication of _GREM1_ ([Mol Aspects Med 2019;69:10](30862463)) 
  * Germline pathogenic variants in _BMPR1A_ ([J Med Genet 2006;43:e13](16525031), [Am J Gastroenterol 2009;104:3027](19773747)) 

Clinical features

  * Patients may present with abdominal pain or gastrointestinal bleeding 
  * Colonic polyps are often found incidentally at colonoscopy 

Diagnosis

  * Multiple colorectal polyps, including polyps with mixed morphologies 
  * Identification of upstream duplication of _GREM1_ ([Fam Cancer 2019;18:63](29804199)) 

Prognostic factors

  * Increased risk of colorectal cancer in adulthood 

Case reports

  * 25 year old woman with multiple gastrointestinal tract polyps of varied histology ([Pol Przegl Chir 2012;84:262](22763303)) 
  * 38 year old man with 1 year of gastrointestinal bleeding, diarrhea and weight loss ([Rev Gastroenterol Mex 2005;70:430](17058983)) 
  * 63 year old woman with no Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry with multiple polyps and clinical concern for lynch syndrome found to have upstream _GREM1_ duplication ([Fam Cancer 2019;18:63](29804199)) 
  * 67 year old Japanese man with multiple polyps, including polyps with mixed histology and novel _BMPR1A_ pathogenic variant ([Jpn J Clin Oncol 2020;50:826](32378721)) 

Treatment

  * Surveillance colonoscopy beginning at age 25 - 30 years old, per National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines ([NCCN: Genetic / Familial High Risk Assessment - Colorectal, Endometrial and Gastric [Accessed 2 June 2025]](https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?category=2&id=1544)) 
  * Surgical resection when polyp burden is not amenable to endoscopic management 

Gross description

  * Typically < 15 polyps throughout the colorectum ([Gastroenterology 1997;112:327](9024286)) 
  * Polyps can be more numerous but rarely exceed 50 polyps ([Genet Res (Camb) 2016;98:e5](26947005))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Adenomatous polyps, indistinguishable from their sporadic counterparts 
  * Serrated polyps, including hyperplastic polyps and sessile serrated lesions 
  * Inflammatory polyps: nonspecific polypoid colonic mucosa with inflammation of lamina propria or crypts and variable crypt dilatation and distortion 
  * Juvenile polyps: inflamed or edematous stroma with cystically dilated glands 
  * Polyps with mixed morphologies are most distinguishing 
    * Mixed inflammatory / hyperplastic polyps, also termed atypical juvenile polyps, are the most common ([Hum Pathol 2017;60:75](27984123), [Gastroenterology 1997;112:327](9024286)) 
    * Mixed inflammatory / adenomatous polyp ([Hum Pathol 2017;60:75](27984123)) 
    * Mixed adenoma and serrated polyp ([Am J Gastroenterol 2003;98:2317](14572586), [Genet Res (Camb) 2016;98:e5](26947005))

Sample pathology report

  * Colon polyps, biopsy: 
    * Multiple fragments of tubular adenoma 
    * Separate fragments of polyp with features of inflammatory and hyperplastic polyp 
    * Separate fragments of hyperplastic polyp (see comment) 
    * Comment: The patient's history of multiple prior colon polyps is noted. The findings raise the possibility of a polyposis syndrome, including hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome. Clinical correlation and genetic testing, if clinically indicated, is recommended.

A 30 year old woman with a 1 year history of gastrointestinal bleeding and intermittent diarrhea underwent a colonoscopy, during which numerous polyps were discovered and removed. Microscopically, these polyps exhibited features of tubular adenoma, sessile serrated, hamartomatous / inflammatory and hyperplastic types. Genetic testing confirmed the diagnosis of hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome and she is now presenting for genetic counseling. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding the inheritance pattern and genetic mutation associated with this syndrome?   
  
  1. It follows an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern involving mutations in _MSH2_
  2. It involves autosomal dominant inheritance due to duplication upstream of the _GREM1_ gene 
  3. It involves biallelic mutations in the _APC_ gene, resulting in increased Wnt signaling 
  4. It is an X linked condition associated with mutations in _STK11_

Practice answer #1

**B**. It involves autosomal dominant inheritance due to duplication upstream of the _GREM1_ gene. Hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome is associated with a duplication upstream of the _GREM1_ gene, leading to overexpression of GREM1. GREM1 antagonizes bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) signaling, which normally acts to limit colorectal epithelial proliferation. Its overexpression disrupts this regulation, leading to various types of polyps, including juvenile, hyperplastic and adenomatous. Answer A is incorrect because mutations in _MSH2_ are associated with Lynch syndrome. Answer D is incorrect because it describes Peutz-Jeghers syndrome, which involves germline mutations in the _STK11_ gene. Answer C is incorrect because _APC_ gene mutations are associated with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP).
