Definition / general

  * Inflammatory colonic injury associated with angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB) use 

Essential features

  * Colitis caused by ARB use, most commonly olmesartan 
  * Histologic pattern typically resembles microscopic colitis (lymphocytic or collagenous colitis) 
  * ARB associated injury can occur in any part of the gastrointestinal tract (most commonly the duodenum) 
  * Patients improve clinically and histologically with cessation of ARB therapy 

Terminology

  * Olmesartan associated gastrointestinal injury 
  * Olmesartan associated enteropathy 
  * Sartan associated colitis 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [K52.1](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/K00-K95/K50-K52/K52-/K52.1) \- toxic gastroenteritis and colitis 

Epidemiology

  * ARB therapy causes gastrointestinal inflammation in an unknown (although likely low) proportion of general users ([Mayo Clin Proc 2012;87:1230](23218089), [Mayo Clin Proc 2014;89:1239](25023670)) 
  * Mean age is 68 years with a reported age range of 46 - 91 years ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:1242](26414468)) 
  * Case series report no differences in gender ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:1242](26414468)) 

Sites

  * Most commonly affects the duodenum but can occur anywhere in the gastrointestinal tract, including the liver ([Liver Int 2019;39:2204](31423708), [J Investig Med High Impact Case Rep 2021;9:23247096211037463](34378440)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Pathophysiology is uncertain but theorized to be attributed to loss of immune regulation via inhibition of TGF beta and dysregulation of IL15 pathway, as well as disruption of the tight junction protein ZO-1 ([Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2015;42:1303](26423313)) 
  * HLA-DQ2 and HLA-DQ8 have been speculated to predispose to immune mediated damage ([Mayo Clin Proc 2012;87:732](22728033), [Gastroenterol Rep (Oxf) 2019;7:162](31217979)) 

Etiology

  * Vast majority of cases are associated with olmesartan use (> 90% of cases) but rare cases have also been reported with other ARBs including valsartan, telmisartan, irbesartan, losartan and candesartan, suggesting the possibility of class effect ([Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2024;59:432](38185985), [ACG Case Rep J 2015;2:92](26157924), [Intest Res 2017;15:419](28670240), [J Neurogastroenterol Motil 2022;28:327](35362458), [Clin Case Rep 2021;9:e04645](34430010), [Case Rep Gastroenterol 2023;17:178](37035266)) 
    * Switching to a different antihypertensive drug class is therefore recommended 

Clinical features

  * Patients most commonly present with chronic nonbloody diarrhea and weight loss, which can be severe 
  * Other symptoms include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, bloating and fatigue 
  * Symptoms can develop months to years after initiating ARB therapy and relapse of symptoms occurs if drug is resumed ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:1242](26414468), [Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2014;40:16](24805127), [Am J Gastroenterol 2013;108:647](23644957)) 
  * Upper and lower endoscopy may demonstrate a normal examination, although villous atrophy, mucosal inflammation and ulceration / erosion can occur ([Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2014;40:16](24805127), [Endoscopy 2014;46:E97](24676835)) 
  * No improvement with gluten free diet 

Diagnosis

  * Clinical correlation of symptom onset and cessation with ARB use 

Laboratory

  * Negative celiac serologies, including antitransglutaminase, antigliadin and antiendomysial antibodies ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:1242](26414468), [Mayo Clin Proc 2012;87:732](22728033)) 
  * Up to ~70% of reported cases are positive for HLA-DQ2 or HLA-DQ8 ([Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2014;40:16](24805127), [Mayo Clin Proc 2012;87:732](22728033)) 
  * Detection of antienterocyte antibodies and antinuclear antibodies has been reported in a few cases ([Mayo Clin Proc 2012;87:732](22728033), [Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2014;40:1103](25199794)) 

Prognostic factors

  * There has been no association made between symptoms and drug dosage to date 

Case reports

  * 59 year old man with intractable diarrhea and weight loss ([Am J Case Rep 2019;20:111](30683835)) 
  * 73 year old man with diarrhea for 2 months and weight loss ([BMC Gastroenterol 2021;21:350](34556042)) 
  * 76 year old woman with severe nonbloody diarrhea for 12 months and weight loss ([Cureus 2021;13:e17004](34540405)) 

Treatment

  * Discontinuation of ARB use is the mainstay of treatment; symptoms usually resolve within weeks of cessation ([Mayo Clin Proc 2012;87:732](22728033)) 
  * Steroid therapy has been reported in a minority of cases for symptom management ([Cureus 2023;15:e41604](37559845), [Acta Gastroenterol Belg 2019;82:319](31314195))

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Microscopic colitis pattern with increased intraepithelial lymphocytes, lamina propria lymphoplasmacytosis or subepithelial collagen thickening ([Mayo Clin Proc 2012;87:732](22728033), [Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:1242](26414468)) 
  * Some cases may show variable active cryptitis, crypt apoptosis, crypt withering and mixed lamina propria inflammation ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2015;139:1242](26414468), [Hum Pathol 2016;50:127](26997446)) 
  * Similar histologic findings can be seen in biopsies of the duodenum (most common upper GI site of involvement) and stomach ([Mayo Clin Proc 2012;87:732](22728033), [Hum Pathol 2016;50:127](26997446), [Histopathology 2022;81:653](35945664))

Positive stains

  * H&E is sufficient for diagnosis 
    * Immunohistochemistry shows increased nonclonal T cells ([CD3](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd3.html), [CD8](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/cdmarkerscd8.html)) ([Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2014;40:1103](25199794)) 
Negative stains

  * Infectious stains, such as [CMV](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainscmv.html), [adenovirus](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsadenovirus.html), [EBER ISH](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainseber1.html), [GMS](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsgms.html), [AFB](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsacidfast.html)

Sample pathology report

  * Random colon, biopsy: 
    * Lymphocytic / collagenous colitis (see comment) 
    * Comment: This pattern of inflammation may be idiopathic or associated with the use of certain medications (e.g., NSAIDs, certain proton pump inhibitors [omeprazole, lansoprazole], selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, olmesartan). If medication related, patients usually improve upon withdrawal of offending agent. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

[  colitis/colonARBcolitisMicroOlivas1.jpg)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/colonARBcolitisMicroOlivas1.jpg)

A 65 year old woman on olmesartan for hypertension presents with intractable diarrhea and weight loss for 2 months. Laboratory studies are notable for negative celiac serologies and infectious workup is negative. Colonoscopic examination was unremarkable. A random biopsy of the colon is shown above. Which of the following is true regarding this entity?   
  
  1. Most patients test negative for HLA-DQ2 
  2. Patients improve clinically and histologically with cessation of ARB therapy 
  3. Symptoms always occur within days of initiating ARB therapy 
  4. Upper GI tract is never involved 

Practice answer #1

**B.** Patients improve clinically and histologically with cessation of ARB therapy. Resolution of symptoms and histologic normalization occur within weeks to months after discontinuation of ARB therapy. Answer C is incorrect because symptoms can occur months to years after starting ARB therapy. Answer A is incorrect because HLA-DQ2 is frequently positive in patients with ARB associated colitis. Answer D is incorrect because the upper GI tract is often involved, especially the duodenum.

Practice question #2

Which of the following is true regarding olmesartan associated colitis?   
  
  1. Colitis occurs in a high percentage of general patients taking olmesartan 
  2. Microscopic colitis pattern is the most common histologic finding 
  3. Steroids are the mainstay of treatment 
  4. Switching to losartan will likely alleviate symptoms 

Practice answer #2

**B.** Microscopic colitis pattern is the most common histologic finding. Microscopic colitis (lymphocytic or collagenous colitis) is the most common histologic finding in olmesartan associated colitis. Answer A is incorrect because colitis likely occurs in a relatively low proportion of general patients taking olmesartan. Answer C is incorrect because discontinuation of ARB therapy is the mainstay of treatment; steroid therapy has been reported in only a minority of cases. Answer D is incorrect because although the vast majority of colitis cases are associated with olmesartan use (> 90%), rare cases have also been reported with other ARBs including valsartan, telmisartan, irbesartan, losartan and candesartan, suggesting the possibility of class effect.
