Definition / general

  * This topic summarizes other anatomical and histological features of the eye and its associated structures within the orbit; these include the sclera, extraocular muscles, lacrimal apparatus, lens, vitreous and optic nerve ([cornea](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/eyecorneaanatomy.html) and [retina](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/eyeretinageneral.html) are covered elsewhere) 

Essential features

  * Orbit contains the globe, extraocular muscles (4 rectus, 2 oblique), optic nerve, lacrimal gland, blood vessels and connective tissue; muscles are innervated by cranial nerves III, IV, VI 
  * Lacrimal gland produces serous secretions for tear film; tears drain via puncta, canaliculi, lacrimal sac and nasolacrimal duct into nose 
  * Lens is biconvex and held behind iris by zonular fibers; it is enclosed by capsule, ~10 mm diameter and opacifies with age 
  * Vitreous humor is avascular gel in the posterior chamber, composed of water, collagen, hyaluronic acid; ora serrata marks anterior limit of retina 
  * Optic nerve is part of central nervous system, is the convergence site of ~1 million retinal axons and forms physiologic blind spot 

Anatomy & physiology

**Extraocular muscles and orbit**
  * 4 rectus muscles (superior, inferior, lateral, medial) and 2 oblique muscles (inferior, superior) 
    * Rectus muscles originate from a common fibrous ring (annulus of Zinn) in posterior orbit and insert into sclera; all muscles are surrounded by fascia 
    * Function to move the eyeball in multiple directions, maintain binocular vision and facilitate tracking ([Webvision: The Organization of the Retina and Visual System [Accessed 25 July 2025]](21413392)) 
      * Superior and inferior rectus muscles move the eyeball up and down 
      * Medial and lateral rectus muscles abduct and adduct the eyeball (side to side) 
      * Inferior oblique moves the eyeball up and out (away from the midline); superior oblique moves it down and in (toward the midline)
    * Innervated by cranial nerves III (superior, inferior and medial rectus + inferior oblique), IV (superior oblique) and VI (lateral rectus)
  * Orbit is the bony structure that contains the eyeball / globe and its fibrous covering (Tenon capsule) as well as the extraocular muscles, trochlea, lacrimal gland, optic nerve, blood vessels and fibroadipose connective tissue 
  * Orbit is formed by the maxilla, ethmoid, lacrimal, frontal, zygomatic, palatine and sphenoid bones ([Int Ophthalmol Clin 2018;58:5](29517643)) 
    * Floor of orbit is roof of maxillary sinus 
    * Medial wall of orbit (i.e., lamina papyracea) separates the orbit from the ethmoidal sinuses
  * Trochlea is an arc shaped cartilaginous structure on the superomedial aspect of the orbit; it acts as an anchor point / pulley through which the tendon of the superior oblique muscle passes before it inserts onto the eye

**Globe and sclera**
  * Eyeball without any appendages is termed the globe 
    * Dimensions: sagittal diameter (length) of ~24 - 25 mm, transverse diameter of ~24 mm ([Webvision: The Organization of the Retina and Visual System [Accessed 25 July 2025]](21413392)) 
    * Equator is defined as the midpoint between anterior and posterior poles
  * Outer protective tunic of the eyeball is formed by the cornea (anterior; discussed elsewhere) and the sclera 
    * Sclera is the fibrous white outer wall of the eye that extends posteriorly from the corneal limbus to the back of the eye where the optic nerve exits 
    * Its white, opaque appearance is due to the collagen fibers being oriented in different directions (compared to the cornea) 
    * 4 layers of sclera: episclera, stroma, lamina fusca, endothelium ([StatPearls: Histology, Eye [Accessed 25 July 2025]](31335063))

**Lacrimal apparatus**
  * Lacrimal gland is located in superotemporal aspect of orbit 
    * Contributes secretions to tear film, including IgA 
    * Accessory lacrimal glands (glands of Krause and Wolfring) are embedded in the conjunctival fornix and above the tarsal plate, respectively 
    * Secretions are serous with minor mucinous component; may have lymphocytes and plasma cells
  * Drainage of tear fluid from the lacrimal gland flows across the cornea inferior and medially toward the medial canthus, where it drains to the nose via the puncta, canaliculi, lacrimal sac and nasolacrimal duct ([StatPearls: Anatomy, Head and Neck - Eye Nasolacrimal [Accessed 17 September 2025]](29489208)) 
    * Puncta: small opening in inferior medial aspect of eyelid 
    * Canaliculi / lacrimal duct: tubular structures, 0.5 mm in diameter 
    * Lacrimal sac: formed by the merging of canaliculi and encased by bones of orbit 
    * Nasolacrimal duct: afferent drainage from the lacrimal sac to the inferior meatus of the nose, ~1 cm long 

**Lens**
  * Normal lens is a biconvex structure that is anatomically situated behind pupil / iris, anterior to vitreous and posterior chambers 
  * Usually, 9 - 10 mm in diameter and 4 - 5 mm wide ([BMJ Open Ophthalmol 2020;5:e000459](33024825)) 
  * Held in place by the zonular fibers, which connect to the pars plicata of ciliary body 
  * Lens capsule 
    * Completely envelopes the lens 
    * Posteriorly: thinner and lacking epithelial cells (2 - 9 µm thick) 
    * Anteriorly: eosinophilic acellular band overlying a single layer of epithelial cells (12 - 21 µm thick)
  * Opacifies with age due to degeneration of lens fibers, aggregation of crystallins, cortical clefts and formation of water vacuoles and globules

**Vitreous chamber**
  * Vitreous humor is an avascular gel-like material that fills the vitreous (most posterior) chamber of the eye (from behind the lens to sensory retina) 
    * Composed of water, protein, hyaluronic acid and randomly oriented type II collagen fibrils, with minor amounts of types V, IX and X 
    * May appear amorphous on H&E
  * Ora serrata appears as a thickening of tissue on the interior aspect of the sclera (adjacent to the vitreous); it represents the beginning of the retinal tissue on the posterior aspect of the eye and can vary in location depending on the extent of myopia present ([Ophthalmic Surg Lasers Imaging 2007;38:518](18050820))

**Optic nerve**
  * Optic nerve head is enclosed by meninges and is considered an extension of the central nervous system ([Lancet 2017;390:2183](28577860)) 
  * Surrounded by meninges; part of the central nervous system 
  * Not usually biopsied due to critical function and delicate structure 
  * Often shows psammoma bodies or optic disc drusen (calcified, acellular, globular deposits from axonal degeneration) 
  * Site of convergence for ~1 million axons from retinal nerve fiber layer 
  * Forms the physiologic blind spot in normal visual field 
  * Receives blood supply from branches of the ophthalmic artery 
  * Also supplied by short posterior ciliary arteries, providing segmental perfusion

Clinical features

  * Specimens can be obtained from surgical enucleation; these may be performed due to nonfunctionality, pain or infection or may be due to neoplasm, trauma, uveitis or chronic glaucoma 
    * Enucleation due to tumor (48%, most often melanoma), glaucoma (13%), phthisis bulbi (12%), recent trauma (11%) 
    * Specimens also received after evisceration or exenteration to manage malignant orbital tumors 
    * More recent data show a decrease in enucleation due to tumors from improved medical treatments and globe sparing treatments (e.g., radiation) ([Graefes Arch Clin Exp Ophthalmol 2010;248:593](20016910))
  * Lacrimal duct disorders often cause epiphora, induration, inflammation of lower eyelid 
    * Tumors tend to displace eye downward because adjacent orbit restricts growth 
    * High recurrence rate due to the difficulty of complete resection 
    * Lacrimal gland is considered a minor salivary gland for tumor reporting 
    * Regional lymph nodes: parotid, submandibular and cervical
  * Conditions that cause forward displacement of the eye or abnormal protrusion from the orbit 
    * Proptosis: forward displacement of eye due to any disease that increases orbital contents 
    * Exophthalmos: abnormal protrusion of the eye, most often due to thyroid disease but also can be a symptom of orbital disease, mucocele from paranasal sinus, hemangioma or inflammatory pseudotumor
  * Tumors of the eye and orbit should be reported using formats published for their counterparts elsewhere in body 
  * Lens 
    * Most surgical specimens are cataracts or prosthetic intraocular lens 
    * Lens specimens are usually submitted for gross examination only (do not section and report as transparent - yes / no) 
    * Presbyopia: normal progressive rigidity of the lens due to aging, resulting in a decreased ability to accommodate / focus light properly onto the retina ([Clin Ophthalmol 2021;15:2167](34079215))
  * Optic nerve 
    * Glaucoma is a heterogeneous group of diseases that causes optic nerve and visual field damage ([Lancet 2017;390:2183](28577860)) 
    * Diagnosed by ophthalmoscopy, tonometry and perimetry 
    * Treatment aims to lower intraocular pressure via topical drugs, laser therapy or surgery (in the case of progression)

Gross description

  * Globe 
    * Specimens are usually intact but free of extraocular muscles and orbital fat; occasionally, may be eviscerated, with only fragments available for microscopic study 
    * Initial pathologic processes may be obscured by subsequent pathologic processes (e.g., chronic inflammatory conditions leading to cataracts or retinal damage or a tumor causing retinal detachment) 
    * Orientation: posterior ciliary vessels run horizontally on either side of the optic nerve; this is important to note since the angle of the optic nerve stump may be altered during fixation 
    * Pathology report for a normal globe (e.g., enucleation) should include a description of the external surface, anterior chamber and posterior chamber as well as the globe dimensions and optic nerve stump length 
  * Lens ([BMJ Open Ophthalmol 2020;5:e000459](33024825)) 
    * Biconvex transparent structure 
    * ~9 - 10 mm in diameter and 5 mm thick 
    * Avascular 
  * Extraocular muscles 
    * Inferior oblique lacks a tendon on gross examination due to the tendon length (1 - 2 mm) being shorter than those of other extraocular muscles; the insertion of this muscle is the inferotemporal quadrant of the sclera under the lateral rectus muscle ([StatPearls: Anatomy, Head and Neck - Inferior Oblique Muscle [Accessed 17 September 2025]](31424837)) 
    * Superior oblique has a tendinous insertion at the superior temporal aspect of the sclera, ~ 6 - 7 mm from the optic nerve; the superior vortex vein exits temporal to this insertion ([StatPearls: Anatomy, Head and Neck - Eye Superior Oblique Muscle [Accessed 17 September 2025]](30725837)) 
    * On gross examination, the attached remnants of oblique muscles at their insertion can be helpful to determine orientation of the eye ([Eagle: Eye Pathology - An Atlas and Text, 3rd Edition, 2016](https://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1496337174/pathologyoutl-20))

Microscopic (histologic) description

**Accessory structures within the orbit**
  * Extraocular muscles: striated skeletal muscle with tightly aligned myofibrils with well arranged sarcomeres, Z lines and H zones 
  * Lacrimal gland: serous, compound tubuloacinar exocrine gland comprising lobules separated by connective tissue septa ([Cornea 2006;25:S82](17001201)) 
    * Lobules contain acini lined by cuboidal epithelial cells with secretory granules and are surrounded by myoepithelial cells 
    * Ducts are lined by simple cuboidal to columnar epithelium
  * Canaliculi / lacrimal duct: tube shaped structures, 0.5 mm in diameter, composed of nonkeratinized squamous epithelium surrounded by fibrous tissue 
  * Lacrimal sac: histologically composed of stratified columnar epithelium with goblet cells 
  * Nasolacrimal duct: ~1 cm long duct, composed of stratified columnar epithelium with goblet cells 

**Layers of the eye**
  * External / outer layer: sclera and cornea (discussed elsewhere) 
    * Sclera is composed of dense connective tissue (mostly type 1 collagen fibers oriented in different directions) ([J Biomech Eng 2015;137:041006](25531905))

  * Intermediate / middle layer: uvea (iris, ciliary body, choroid) 
    * Iris is composed of fibrovascular, pigmented stroma with pigmented epithelium deep to the stroma 
      * Sphincter pupillae is more distinct on histology and appears as a population of muscle cells at the inner rim of the iris 
      * Dilator pupillae is composed of myoepithelial cells that are radially oriented within the posterior region of the iris
    * Ciliary body 
      * Ciliary muscle is a ring of smooth muscle at the corneal limbus that attaches to the zonular fibers (i.e., zonules of Zinn or ciliary zonules), which suspend the lens within the eye and control accommodation 
      * Also controls the flow of aqueous humor
    * Anterior chamber is located between the cornea and iris, while the posterior chamber is between the iris and lens; both are filled with aqueous humor ([Webvision: The Organization of the Retina and Visual System [Accessed 25 July 2025]](21413392)) 
    * Choroid consists of loose connective tissue and a dense network of blood vessels 
      * Choriocapillary (deepest) layer supplies the retina 
      * Bruch membrane is located between the choroid and the retina; drusen can form between this layer and the retinal pigmented epithelium, blocking nutrient exchange to the retina (this is associated with increased risk of age related macular degeneration [AMD]) ([StatPearls: Drusen Bodies [Accessed 25 July 2025]](32644513))

  * Internal / inner layer: lens, vitreous, retina (discussed elsewhere) 
    * Lens comprises an outer capsule, inner cortex and innermost nucleus 
      * Capsule epithelial cells are the basement membrane of the cells deeper in the lens (basal surface toward center); it is a single layer of cuboidal cells on the anterior surface 
      * As the cells of the epithelium dedifferentiate to lens fibers, they lose their cellular organelles and push older cells toward the center
    * Vitreous chamber of the eye is filled with vitreous humor, which is composed of water, protein, hyaluronic acid and randomly oriented type II collagen fibrils 
      * Vitreous may appear amorphous on H&E
    * Ora serrata appears as a thickening of tissue on the interior aspect of the sclera, adjacent to the vitreous 
      * Histologically, it is where the retinal layers can begin to be observed
    * Optic nerve 
      * Cross section of nerve at 2 - 5 mm2 behind the globe (not including leptomeninges) is ~8 mm2 with a standard deviation of 2 mm2 and an average nerve fiber count of 1,159,000 with a standard deviation of 196,000 ([Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 1990;31:736](2335441)) 
      * Lamina cribrosa 
        * Canals 40 - 65 µm in diameter 
        * Composed of fine collagen fibers, through which axons of retinal ganglion cells pass through the sclera 
        * Myelination begins just posterior to the lamina cribrosa 
        * Can be highlighted with Luxol fast blue or other myelin stains

Electron microscopy description

  * Lamina cribrosa ([Eye (Lond) 1990;4:42](2182351)) 
    * Transmission electron microscope shows trabeculae surrounding the canals that are formed by fibrous astrocyte processes 
    * Scanning electron microscope (SEM) at low magnification 
      * Collagenous elements appear as dark bands, while neuroglia are pale 
      * Canals measure 25 - 125 µm

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/eyeanatomyotherStringham08.jpg

A patient presents with progressive visual field loss and is diagnosed with glaucoma. Damage is primarily localized to which of the following structures?   
  
  1. Bruch membrane 
  2. Ciliary body 
  3. Macula lutea 
  4. Optic nerve head 
  5. Ora serrata 

Practice answer #1

**D**. Optic nerve head, also known as the optic disc, is characteristically damaged in glaucoma due to increased intraocular pressure and impaired axonal transport. Answer A is incorrect because thickening and lipid accumulation in Bruch membrane are central to age related macular degeneration (AMD) pathogenesis. Answer B is incorrect because ciliary body controls accommodation and aqueous humor production. While a blockage in the canal of Schlemm could increase intraocular pressure, ciliary body does not commonly underlie glaucoma pathophysiology. Answer E is incorrect because ora serrata marks the anterior limit of the retina. Answer C is incorrect because macula lutea is the site for central to high acuity vision and macular pigment deposition but is more affected in AMD than in glaucoma.

Practice question #2

During orbital surgery, an anatomic structure is identified that passes through the trochlea and inserts onto the sclera. It functions to move the eyeball downward and medially. Which muscle is this?   
  
  1. Inferior oblique 
  2. Inferior rectus 
  3. Medial rectus 
  4. Superior oblique 
  5. Superior rectus 

Practice answer #2

**D**. Superior oblique passes through the trochlea and functions to move the eyeball downward (depression) and medially (intorsion). Answer A is incorrect because inferior oblique elevates and abducts the eye. Answer B is incorrect because inferior rectus depresses the eye but does not pass through the trochlea and does not move the eye medially. Answer C is incorrect because medial rectus adducts the eye without affecting vertical movement or torsion. Answer E is incorrect because superior rectus elevates the eye and does not pass through the trochlea.
