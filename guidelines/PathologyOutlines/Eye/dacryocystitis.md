Definition / general

  * Inflammatory lesion of lacrimal sac 

Essential features

  * Dacryocystitis, an inflammatory state of nasolacrimal sac, is typically caused by an obstruction within the nasolacrimal duct, often with unknown etiology; however, it may be secondary to infection, inflammatory conditions such as sarcoidosis or granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA), trauma, dacryolith or neoplasm ([Ophthalmol Ther 2022;11:1735](35788550)) 
  * Main clinical symptoms include pain, redness and swelling of lacrimal sac at medial canthus and epiphora due to inadequate drainage of tears, which in the end results in severe inflammation ([Clin Ophthalmol 2018;12:1859](30310264)) 
  * Main microscopic features include 
    * Neutrophilic accumulation in acute type 
    * Chronic inflammatory thickening of the wall, hyperplasia of lining epithelium, hypersecretion of mucosa and granulation tissue in chronic type 

Terminology

  * Acute dacryocystitis 
  * Chronic dacryocystitis 
  * Acquired dacryocystitis 
  * Congenital dacryocystitis 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10 
    * [H04.3](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/H00-H59/H00-H05/H04-/H04.3) \- acute and unspecified inflammation of lacrimal passages 
    * [H04.4](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/H00-H59/H00-H05/H04-/H04.4) \- chronic inflammation of lacrimal passages 

Epidemiology

  * Bimodal ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 
    * After birth: congenital 
    * Adults after age 40 
  * F > M ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 

Sites

  * Lacrimal sac 

Pathophysiology

  * Unknown 
  * Obstruction of canal ([Int J Ophthalmol 2013;6:817](24392331)) 
  * Direct extension from conjunctiva or nasal cavity ([Clin Pediatr (Phila) 2006;45:567](16893863)) 

Etiology

  * Acute dacryocystitis is caused by an acute infectious state ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 
  * Chronic dacryocystitis results from chronic obstruction ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 
  * Acquired states are typically due to repeated trauma, surgeries, medication and neoplasm ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 
  * Congenital forms are due to a membranous obstruction at the valve of Hasner in the distal portion of nasolacrimal duct ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 

Clinical features

  * Persistent epiphora ([Dis Mon 2020;66:101043](32622678)) 
  * Mucoid or purulent secretion by pressure ([J Clin Diagn Res 2016;10:EC01](27630848)) 

Diagnosis

  * History taking 
  * Physical examination 
  * Culture and Gram stain ([Ital J Pediatr 2024;50:10](38247004)) 
  * Biopsy ([Bosn J Basic Med Sci 2017;17:1](27754826)) 

Laboratory

  * Blood culture should be considered in patient with fever or acute visual changes ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 
  * Serologic testing such as antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (C-ANCA) if granulomatosis with polyangiitis is suspected and antinuclear antibody (ANA) testing can be pursued if systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is suspected ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 

Prognostic factors

  * Generally good prognosis 
  * Highly successful with simple probing techniques 
  * Dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) successful in > 93 - 97% of cases 
  * In congenital cases, conservative measures alone result in ~90% resolution by the age of 1 ([StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)) 

Case reports

  * 4 year old girl with a voluminous and erythematous, fluctuant and warm mass located inferiorly to the medial canthus of the right eye ([Ital J Pediatr 2020;46:15](32014022)) 
  * 61 year old diabetic man who presented with severe pain, swelling and inflammatory signs above the left medial canthal ligament tendon ([Arq Bras Oftalmol 2022;85:306](34586245)) 
  * 84 year old man admitted to the hospital with chronic dacryocystitis of the right eye due to tearing and purulent discharge for > 1 month ([BMC Ophthalmol 2023;23:445](37926842)) 

Treatment

  * Treatment of acute dacryocystitis includes conservative measures such as warm compress and massage for uncomplicated cases 
    * Oral antibiotics should be considered 
    * Empiric antibiotics should include Gram positive and Gram negative coverage 
  * Chronic dacryocystitis is almost always managed surgically 
  * Treatment of congenital dacryocystitis includes conservative measures 
    * Topical antibiotics can be considered for acute flares 
    * If conservative measures fail, a referral is made to an ophthalmologist for nasolacrimal probing 
  * Reference: [StatPearls: Dacryocystitis [Accessed 18 July 2025]](29261989)

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Types 
    * Acute: purulent exudate 
    * Chronic: inflammatory thickening of the wall, hyperplasia of lining epithelium, hypersecretion of mucosa and granulation tissue ([J Clin Diagn Res 2016;10:EC01](27630848)) 
  * Patterns of inflammation 
    * Suppurative ([Ophthalmol Ther 2022;11:1735](35788550)) 
    * Granulomatous ([Am J Ophthalmol 2022;241:e4](35752318)) 
    * Necrotizing ([BMC Ophthalmol 2023;23:445](37926842)) 
  * Complication: fistular tract formation ([Ophthalmic Plast Reconstr Surg 2022;38:270](34652315))

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

A 45 year old woman presents with pain, redness and swelling at the medial canthus of her right eye. She reports a recent upper respiratory tract infection. Examination reveals tenderness and erythema over the lacrimal sac region with purulent discharge expressed upon palpation. Which of the following is the most likely cause of her condition?   
  
  1. Bacterial infection secondary to nasolacrimal duct obstruction 
  2. Chronic blepharitis 
  3. Orbital cellulitis 
  4. Viral conjunctivitis 

Practice answer #1

**A.** Bacterial infection secondary to nasolacrimal duct obstruction. The patient's presentation is classic for acute dacryocystitis, which is most often caused by a bacterial infection due to nasolacrimal duct obstruction. The obstruction leads to stasis of tears, creating a favorable environment for bacterial growth, commonly involving _Staphylococcus aureus_ or _Streptococcus_. Answer B is incorrect because chronic blepharitis involves inflammation of the eyelid margins, typically presenting with redness, irritation, crusting and scaling at the base of the eyelashes. It does not cause swelling or erythema over the medial canthus. Answer D is incorrect because viral conjunctivitis presents with conjunctival injection, watery discharge and preauricular lymphadenopathy. It does not cause tenderness or swelling over the lacrimal sac or purulent discharge upon palpation. Answer C is incorrect because orbital cellulitis presents with symptoms such as proptosis, restricted eye movement and decreased vision, often accompanied by a systemic sign like fever.

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/eyedacryocystitisMicroMohsenifard11.jpg

A 58 year old woman presents with chronic tearing and recurrent swelling over the medial canthus of her left eye. She underwent dacryocystectomy and the histopathologic image of the lacrimal sac tissue is shown above. Based on the clinical history and histological findings, which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. Chronic dacryocystitis caused by nasolacrimal duct obstruction 
  2. Fungal dacryocystitis due to aspergillus 
  3. Granulomatous dacryocystitis due to tuberculosis 
  4. Sarcoidosis involving the lacrimal sac 

Practice answer #2

**A.** Chronic dacryocystitis caused by nasolacrimal duct obstruction. The histopathologic slide shows features consistent with chronic dacryocystitis, including inflammatory infiltrates of lymphocytes and plasma cells, fibrotic and edematous stroma and vascular proliferation. This condition is commonly due to obstruction of the nasolacrimal duct, leading to stasis of tears and chronic inflammation. Answer C is incorrect because granulomatous dacryocystitis would show granulomas with caseating necrosis on histopathology, which are absent here. Tuberculosis is a rare cause of dacryocystitis and usually has systemic features such as chronic cough or night sweats. Answer B is incorrect because fungal infections would demonstrate fungal hyphae on histopathology and are more likely in immunocompromised patients or tropical environments. Answer D is incorrect because sarcoidosis typically presents with noncaseating granulomas on histopathology and often involves other systemic findings like hilar lymphadenopathy or uveitis.
