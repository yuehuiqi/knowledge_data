Definition / general

  * Benign cystic lesions of the eyelid, most of which are caused by blockages of ducts (hidrocystomas) or hair follicles (epidermal cyst) or are consequence of entrapped benign, embryologic tissue (dermoid cyst) 
  * Includes epidermal cyst, dermoid cyst, eccrine hidrocystoma and apocrine hidrocytoma, all of which are generally cured by excision

Terminology

  * Epidermal (infundibular) cyst, epidermal inclusion cyst, follicular cyst, infundibular type, epidermoid cyst, clinical sebaceous cyst 
  * Hidrocystoma, sudoriferous cyst, sweat ductal cyst

Epidemiology

  * Epidermal cysts are common, sporadic lesions that typically affect adults with a slight male predominance ([StatPearls: Epidermoid Cyst [Accessed 18 August 2022]](29763149)) 
  * Periocular dermoid cysts are most often encountered in pediatric patients, with many cases arising in the superotemporal region within the first year of life ([Ophthalmic Epidemiol 2019;26:117](30260262)) 
  * Eccrine hidrocytomas generally arise along the medial or lateral eyelid in middle aged to older adults ([Eye (Lond) 2005;19:77](15205675)) 
  * Apocrine hidrocystomas often occur near the canthus in middle aged and older adults but may rarely be seen in the pediatric population ([MedGenMed 2006;8:57](17406184)) 

Etiology

  * Most epidermal cysts are thought to arise secondary to occlusion of hair follicles ([Arch Ophthalmol 1988;106:270](3341987)) 
  * Dermoid cysts are caused by entrapment of embryologic epithelium ([Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg 2005;132:938](15944568)) 
  * Eccrine hidrocystomas occur secondary to blockages of eccrine sweat glands ([Eye (Lond) 2005;19:77](15205675)) 
  * Apocrine hidrocystomas result from blockages of apocrine sweat glands ([MedGenMed 2006;8:57](17406184)) 

Clinical features

  * Epidermal cysts typically present as solitary, subcutaneous nodules and keratinaceous contents may appear cheese-like ([StatPearls: Epidermal Inclusion Cyst [Accessed 18 August 2022]](30335343)) 
  * Dermoid cysts present as subcutaneous nodules, often superotemporally in the vicinity of the zygomaticofrontal suture ([Int Ophthalmol 2011;31:93](21267629)) 
  * Most hidrocystomas appear as single, dome shaped lesions with varying tints (including transparent, skin colored, brown, blue and blue-black hues) though examples of multiple hidrocystomas do occur ([MedGenMed 2006;8:57](17406184)) 

Diagnosis

  * Histopathologic examination of excised tissue 

Case reports

  * 8 and 11 month old boys with upper eyelid lesions ([Ophthalmic Plast Reconstr Surg 2009;25:146](19300165)) 
  * 15 month old boy with a right upper eyelid mass ([Saudi J Ophthalmol 2015;29:312](26586987)) 
  * 9 year old girl with drooping of the right upper eyelid ([Indian J Ophthalmol 2017;65:884](28905840)) 
  * 35 year old man with a large right upper eyelid swelling over a period of 10 years ([Indian J Ophthalmol 2012;60:211](22569383)) 
  * 70 year old man with a large, cystic swelling of the forehead ([Indian J Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg 2019;71:59](31741931)) 

Treatment

  * Excision is usually curative

Gross description

  * Benign eyelid cysts are often received as small excisions with nodules, papules or grossly identifiable cysts 
  * Keratinaceous cyst contents in epidermal and dermoid cysts may appear yellow to white, cheese-like or chalky ([StatPearls: Epidermal Inclusion Cyst [Accessed 18 August 2022]](30335343)) 
  * Hidrocystomas are thin walled and usually contain clear cyst contents ([MedGenMed 2006;8:57](17406184))

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/eyeeyelidcystsNix11.jpg

A 68 year old man presents with a skin colored nodule of the lateral left lower eyelid. Based on the histologic image, what is the most likely etiology of the lesion?   
  
  1. Blockage of an apocrine sweat gland 
  2. Blockage of an eccrine sweat gland 
  3. Entrapped embryologic tissue 
  4. Obstruction of a pilosebaceous unit 

Practice answer #1

**B**. Blockage of an eccrine sweat gland. The histologic image shows an epithelial lining composed of a dual layer of cuboidal cells, consistent with the diagnosis of eccrine hidrocystoma. Eccrine hidrocystomas are caused by blockages of eccrine sweat glands. Apocrine hidrocystomas result from blockages of apocrine sweat glands (A). Entrapped embryonal tissue is encountered in a dermoid cyst (C) and obstruction of a pilosebaceous unit may result in an epidermal cyst (D).

Practice question #2

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/eyeeyelidcystsNix12.jpg

A 1 year old girl undergoes excision of a superotemporal periocular nodule and the histologic findings are provided in the associated image. What is the diagnosis?   
  
  1. Apocrine hidrocystoma 
  2. Dermoid cyst 
  3. Eccrine hidrocystoma 
  4. Epidermal cyst 

Practice answer #2

**B**. Dermoid cyst. Periocular dermoid cysts most often occur in pediatric patients and are diagnosed histologically by the presence of adnexal structures in association with a bland stratified squamous epithelial cyst lining. Apocrine hidrocystomas demonstrate a cyst lining with an inner layer of elongated, eosinophilic cells with apical snouts and decapitation secretions (A). Eccrine hidrocystomas feature a cyst lining composed of a dual layer of cuboidal cells (C). Epidermal cysts have a cyst lining of bland keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium with a granular layer in the absence of adnexal structures (D).
