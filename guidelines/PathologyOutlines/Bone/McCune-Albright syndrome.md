Definition / general

  * McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS) is a rare condition that results from postzygotic activating somatic mutations in the alpha subunit of the _GNAS_ gene, leading to continued stimulation of endocrine glands 
    * Typically codon 201 and rarely codon 227 ([Histopathology 2007;50:691](17493233)) 
  * Exact presentation and severity depends on the extent of mosaicism and the involved tissues ([Curr Osteoporos Rep 2015;13:146](25851935)) 
  * Most patients are diagnosed in childhood or adolescence 

Essential features

  * Classically defined as the triad of 
    * Polyostotic fibrous dysplasia of bone 
    * Precocious puberty 
    * > 3 café au lait macules 
  * However, a more inclusive definition has been proposed, consisting of 
    * Monostotic fibrous dysplasia of bone or polyostotic fibrous dysplasia of bone 
    * Hyperfunctional endocrinopathy or café au lait macules ([J Bone Miner Res 2006;21:P99](17229019)) 

Terminology

  * McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS) has no synonyms 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [Q78.1](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/Q00-Q99/Q65-Q79/Q78-/Q78.1) \- polyostotic fibrous dysplasia 
  * ICD-11: [FB80.0](https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en#/http%3a%2f%2fid.who.int%2ficd%2fentity%2f1704766818) \- fibrous dysplasia of bone 

Epidemiology

  * Very rare disorder affecting an estimated 1 in 100,000 to 1 in 1,000,000 people ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2008;3:12](18489744)) 
  * F = M 

Sites

Pathophysiology

  * Constitutively active mutant _GNAS_ results in increased cyclic adenosine monophosphate, which 
    * Promotes proliferation and inhibits differentiation of fibroblasts, which replace normal bone and produce fibrous dysplasia lesions 
    * Promotes proliferation and autonomous function of endocrine organs 
  * Fibrous dysplasia elaborates fibroblast growth factor 23, which 
    * Inhibits renal resorption of phosphate 
    * Causes hypophosphatemia 
    * Causes hyperphosphaturia 
  * Examples: 
    * Unregulated production of cortisol, leading to [Cushing syndrome](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/adrenalcushings.html)
    * Unregulated production of gonadotropin releasing hormone, leading to precocious puberty ([Curr Osteoporos Rep 2015;13:146](25851935)) 

Etiology

  * Results from de novo mutations in _GNAS_ ([Curr Osteoporos Rep 2015;13:146](25851935)) 
    * No inherited case has ever been reported 
    * Germline _GNAS_ mutation is presumed embryonic lethal 
  * Has no risk factors 

Clinical features

  * 2 most common presentations are fibrous dysplasia related pain and precocious puberty 
Diagnosis

  * Essentially a clinical diagnosis 
  * Genetic testing: 
    * Not routinely performed but available 
    * False negatives are common due to mosaicism and sampling 
    * Positive results merely confirm a clinical diagnosis 
    * No genotype / phenotype correlation, therefore no prognostic value ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2008;3:12](18489744)) 
  * When there is clinical suspicion: 
    * Skeletal survey to identify lesions suspicious for fibrous dysplasia 
    * CT is more sensitive for craniofacial fibrous dysplasia 
    * Biopsy can confirm fibrous dysplasia 
    * Skin survey to identify café au lait macules 
    * If not apparent at presentation, endocrinopathies may become apparent with laboratory studies and focused examinations 

Laboratory

  * Some patients have multiple endocrinopathies; others have none 
  * The following laboratory abnormalities may be present in any combination ([Orphanet J Rare Dis 2008;3:12](18489744)): 
    * Depressed serum phosphate 
    * Elevated urine phosphate 
    * Elevated cortisol 
    * Elevated sex steroid hormones 
    * Elevated thyroid hormone 
    * Depressed thyroid stimulating hormone

Prognostic factors

  * Severity of hypophosphatemia and number / extent of fibrous dysplasia lesions correlates with the risk of bone pain and pathologic fracture ([J Bone Miner Res 2016;31:2167](27340799)) 
  * Sites of fibrous dysplasia are established early and burden of disease typically plateaus by age 15 ([Expert Opin Investig Drugs 2007;16:761](17501688)) 

Case reports

  * Preterm neonate girl with Cushing syndrome, hyperthyroidism and café au lait macules ([J Med Case Rep 2015;9:189](26341786)) 
  * Neonate girl with severe cholestasis requiring liver transplant ([J Clin Res Pediatr Endocrinol 2019;11:100](29991465)) 
  * 4 year old girl also diagnosed with Mazabraud syndrome ([Acta Biomed 2017;88:198](28845837)) 
  * 39 year old man whose autopsy revealed extensive mosaicism for _GNAS_ R201C mutation and subclinical disease manifestations in multiple organs ([J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2014;99:E2029](25062453)) 

Treatment

  * There is no specific therapy 
  * Various endocrinopathies are treated just as their sporadic counterparts 
  * Fibrous dysplasia is treated just as its sporadic counterpart ([StatPearls: McCune Albright Syndrome [Accessed 27 April 2022]](30725777))

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * Mutation specific restriction enzyme digest (MSRED) method may identify mutations in codon 201 or 227 of the _GNAS1_ gene ([Histopathology 2007;50:691](17493233)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Bone, biopsy: 
    * Fibrous dysplasia of bone (see comment) 
    * Comment: Fibrous dysplasia is most commonly sporadic but is also a typical presenting feature of McCune-Albright syndrome and Mazabraud syndrome. Clinical correlation is advised.

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/bonefibrousdysplasiamccunealbrightdavis03.jpg

A 23 year old man diagnosed at age 8 with McCune-Albright syndrome is considering starting a family and seeks genetic counseling. Which of the following best describes his likelihood of having an affected child?   
  
  1. 0% chance of having an affected child 
  2. 25% chance of having an affected female child 
  3. 25% chance of having an affected male child 
  4. 50% chance of having an affected child 
  5. 100% chance of having an affected child 

Practice answer #1

**A.** 0% chance of having an affected child. McCune-Albright syndrome results from postzygotic mutations and an inherited case has never been reported. Germline mutation in _GNAS_ is believed to be embryonic lethal. The likelihood that the patient will have an affected child is effectively 0% ([Curr Osteoporos Rep 2015;13:146](25851935)).

Practice question #2

A 6 year old girl is brought to her pediatrician by her father who is concerned about her early breast development. On exam, the patient's breasts are Tanner stage 3 and the clinician also notes a large, irregularly contoured pigmented macule on her left flank. In addition to starting letrozole, which of the following is an appropriate next step?  
  
  1. Diagnose the patient with McCune-Albright syndrome and order a skeletal survey 
  2. Perform a complete skin survey in clinic and order a skeletal survey 
  3. Perform a punch biopsy at the edge of the macule 
  4. Send a blood sample for sequencing of _GNAS_
  5. Send a tissue sample for sequencing of _GNAS_

Practice answer #2

**B.** Perform a complete skin survey in clinic and order a skeletal survey. A complete skin survey and skeletal survey would potentially reveal additional findings that would rule in or provisionally rule out McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS). As presented in the question, the patient has findings suggestive of but not diagnostic for MAS, making answer A wrong. A punch biopsy, as in answer C, would likely reveal melanocyte proliferation but would not aid in diagnosis or direct treatment. Sequencing studies, as in answers D and E, if negative, would not rule out MAS and if positive, would only confirm a clinical diagnosis; thus, neither is contributory ([Curr Osteoporos Rep 2015;13:146](25851935)).
