Definition / general

  * Benign neoplasm; rare (< 1% of primary bone neoplasms), arising in second to third decades of life, with slight male predilection 
  * Treatment with surgical curettage is generally curative; recurrence varies with location 

Essential features

  * Rare (< 1% of primary bone neoplasms) 
  * Peaks in second decade of life, with slight male predilection 
  * Common location includes epiphysis of long bones > metaphysis; may involve skull in older patients 
  * Sheets of chondroblasts admixed with osteoclast-like giant cells and eosinophilic chondroid matrix; pericellular chicken wire type calcification may be present 
  * K36M mutations commonly identified 

ICD coding

  * ICD-O: [9230/0](https://apps.who.int/iris/bitstream/handle/10665/96612/9789241548496_eng.pdf?sequence=1#page=88) \- chondroblastoma, NOS 
  * ICD-11: [2E82.Z](https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en#/http%3a%2f%2fid.who.int%2ficd%2fentity%2f1687031913%2fmms%2funspecified) \- benign chondrogenic tumors, site unspecified 
  * ICD-11: [XH4NK2](https://icd.who.int/browse/2024-01/mms/en#484764712) \- chondroblastoma, NOS 

Epidemiology

  * Rare (< 1% of primary bone neoplasms) ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2017;141:867](28557595)) 
  * Peaks in second and third decades of life (10 - 25 years of age) ([Hum Pathol 1993;24:944](8253461)) 
  * Slight male predominance (M:F = 2:1) 

Sites

  * 75% of cases involve long bones; generally confined to a single bone ([Skeletal Radiol 1985;14:1](4023729), [J Bone Joint Surg Am 1985;67:748](3997927)) 
  * Common locations: epiphysis of proximal and distal femur, proximal humerus, proximal tibia; rarely involves the metaphysis ([Skeletal Radiol 1985;14:1](4023729)) 
  * Other locations: pelvis, ribs, scapula, navicular, carpal bones, patella and skull ([Hum Pathol 1993;24:944](8253461), [Virchows Arch A Pathol Anat Histopathol 1992;421:355](1384228)) 

Pathophysiology

  * May arise from cartilage germ cells, epiphyseal cartilage cells or possibly multipotent mesenchymal cell or tendon sheath synovial cell ([Eur J Radiol 2007;63:63](17482405), [Skeletal Radiol 1995;24:220](7610417)) 

Clinical features

  * Pain in the affected area ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2017;141:867](28557595)) 
  * Joint effusions can occur if involving articular cartilage or synovium 

Diagnosis

  * Xray (anterior posterior, lateral and oblique views) or MRI; CT scan less common ([Acta Orthop Belg 2016;82:68](26984656)) 
  * Open surgical biopsy / surgical curettage is frequently performed ([Acta Orthop Belg 2016;82:68](26984656)) 
  * Fine needle aspiration (FNA) biopsies are less common 

Radiology description

  * Xray: well defined lucent lesion with a thin sclerotic rim, with or without matrix calcifications, commonly arising eccentrically within the epiphysis of long bones; extension to the metaphysis can be seen ([AJR Am J Roentgenol 1977;128:613](403790)) 
  * CT scan: can show matrix calcification and solid periosteal reaction ([J Comput Assist Tomogr 1984;8:907](6470260)) 
  * MRI: associated with bone marrow edema, periosteal and soft tissue reactions, varying signal intensity on T2 weighted sequences ([Skeletal Radiol 2002;31:88](11828329))

Prognostic factors

  * Can be locally aggressive ([Mod Pathol 2020;33:2295](32601382)) 
  * Rare lung metastases have been identified ([J Pediatr Orthop 2020;40:e894](32658156), [Pathol Res Pract 1987;182:113](3588400)) 
  * Local recurrences vary, depending on location ([Acta Orthop Traumatol Turc 2018;52:415](30249436)) 

Case reports

  * 11 year old girl with chondroblastoma of distal femur ([J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 2020;50:275](32354316)) 
  * 17 year old boy with recurrent chondroblastoma of sacrum ([World J Clin Cases 2021;9:5709](34307629)) 
  * 30 year old man with chondroblastoma of patella with secondary aneurysmal bone cyst ([BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2021;22:381](33892701)) 

Treatment

  * Excision or curettage, with or without bone grafting, is the mainstay of treatment ([Acta Orthop Belg 2016;82:68](26984656)) 
  * En bloc resection or amputation is reserved for advanced cases 

Gross description

  * When resected with bone: usually 3 - 6 cm, well circumscribed, white-blue-gray, firm 
  * When resected as curetted material: reddish, friable material ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2017;141:867](28557595)) 
  * Variable calcification, necrosis, cystic areas 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Composed of round or polyhedral chondroblasts with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and well defined cell borders; spindle shaped cells may be focal ([Ann Diagn Pathol 2003;7:205](12913842), [Cancer 1972;30:401](5051664)) 
  * Nuclei are oval, hyperlobulated with grooves 
  * Pericellular lace-like or chicken wire type calcification among degenerative chondroblasts 
  * Chondroid matrix almost always present (pink rather than blue matrix) 
  * May have marked cellularity, intracytoplasmic glycogen granules, mitotic figures, necrosis and osteoclast type giant cells 
  * No significant nuclear atypia as compared with malignant chondroblastoma ([Mod Pathol 2020;33:2295](32601382)) 
  * Aneurysmal bone cyst-like change is common ([Skeletal Radiol 2010;39:583](19936740))

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/bonechondroblastomapatton02.jpg https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/bonechondroblastomapatton01.jpg

An 18 year old man presents with a 3 cm, well defined lucent lesion within the epiphysis of the distal femur. Microscopic features of the surgical curettage specimen are represented by the H&E images. The best diagnosis is   
  
  1. Chondroblastoma 
  2. Chondroblastoma-like osteosarcoma 
  3. Chondromyxoid fibroma 
  4. Giant cell tumor of bone 

Practice answer #1

**A.** Chondroblastoma. Chondroblastomas commonly present in the second decade of life as a singular, well defined lucent lesion within skeletally immature long bones. Characteristic histopathological features include sheets of chondroblasts admixed with osteoclast-like giant cells in a chondroid matrix and focal chicken wire type calcification. Chondroid fibromas typically lack chicken wire type calcification. Chondroblastoma-like osteosarcomas generally display an infiltrative growth pattern with marked cytologic atypia. Chondroid matrix is not commonly seen in giant cell tumor of bone.

Practice question #2

Which of the following is a feature of chondroblastoma?  
  
  1. Aggressive lesion with an infiltrative growth pattern 
  2. Commonly arises in the epiphysis and metaphysis of long bones 
  3. Generally shows a high degree of cytologic atypia 
  4. Lacks a pericellular chicken wire calcification 

Practice answer #2

**B.** Commonly arises in the epiphysis and metaphysis of long bones. Chondroblastomas show pericellular chicken wire calcification and generally display well demarcated borders and a lesser degree cytologic atypia when compared with chondroblastoma-like osteosarcoma.
