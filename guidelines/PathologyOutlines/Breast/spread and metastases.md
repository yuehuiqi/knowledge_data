Definition / general

  * Incidence of metastatic breast carcinoma continues to increase worldwide despite early detection and intervention, with distant bone metastasis most frequently involved 
  * Biomarkers (ER, PR, HER2) need to be performed in new metastases, with indication for PDL1 testing in some scenarios 

Essential features

  * 4 molecular subtypes of breast cancer (with surrogate immunohistochemical profiles) 
    * Luminal A: ER / PR positive, HER2 negative, low Ki67 proliferative index (< 20%) 
    * Luminal B: ER / PR positive, HER2 variable, high Ki67 proliferative index (> 20%) 
    * HER2 overexpressed: ER and PR negative, HER2 positive 
    * Triple negative: ER / PR / HER2 negative 
  * Prognosis is strongly impacted by biomarker status 
    * Luminal A tumors have the lowest incidence of metastasis 
    * Triple negative tumors have the worst prognosis with early risk of relapse 
  * Bones are the most common site of spread in metastatic breast cancer 
  * Per College of American Pathologists (CAP) protocol staging 
    * Distant metastasis (pM1): distant nodal, visceral or skeletal metastasis 
    * Regional lymph nodes metastasis (pNx): axillary, subpectoral, supraclavicular, infraclavicular and internal mammary stations 
  * References: [Kufe: Holland-Frei Cancer Medicine, 6th Edition, 2003](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK13196), [Nutrients 2022;14:2376](35745105), [CAP: Protocol for the Examination of Specimens From Patients With Invasive Carcinoma of the Breast [Accessed 12 September 2025]](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/cp-breast-invasive-18protocol-4100.pdf), [ Hall: Molecular Imaging and Therapy, 1st Edition, 2023](38190472)

Epidemiology

  * Breast cancer is the most common cancer in female patients in the United States 
    * Accounts for 32% of new cancer diagnoses 
    * Incidence by race (2017 - 2021) 
      * White (137.9 per 100,000) 
      * Black (131.3 per 100,000) 
      * American Indian / Alaska Native (123.6 per 100,000) 
      * Asian American / Pacific Islander (108.3 per 100,000) 
      * Hispanic (104.1 per 100,000) 
  * Incidence of breast cancer continues to increase worldwide despite early detection and intervention 
    * Rising by 1% overall (2012 - 2021) 
    * Rise is predominantly seen in the below demographics 
      * Age < 50 years (1.4% per year) 
      * Hispanic (1.6% per year) 
      * Asian American / Pacific Islander (2.6% per year) 
  * Breast cancer death rate has declined by 44% during 1989 - 2022 
    * Mortality by race (2017 - 2021) 
      * Black (26.8 per 100,000) 
      * American Indian / Alaska Native (20.5 per 100,000) 
      * White (19.4 per 100,000) 
      * Asian American / Pacific Islander (11.9 per 100,000) 
      * Hispanic (13.7 per 100,000) 
  * Metastatic disease is seen in predominantly women > 75 years old ([CDC: Metastatic Female Breast Cancer Incidence [Accessed 12 September 2025]](https://www.cdc.gov/united-states-cancer-statistics/publications/metastatic-breast-cancer.html)) 
  * Most cases of metastasis arise from secondary spread of a previously treated early breast cancer 
  * References: [InformedHealth.org: Overview - Metastatic Breast Cancer [Accessed 12 September 2025]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK361001), [Clin Breast Cancer 2021;21:302](33750642), [CA Cancer J Clin 2025;75:10](39817679), [Nutrients 2022;14:2376](35745105), [Mayrovitz: Breast Cancer, 1st Edition, 2022](36122161), [CA Cancer J Clin 2024;74:477](39352042)

Sites

  * Can occur at any body site 
  * Regional metastasis: ipsilateral breast, chest wall, axillary, subpectoral, supraclavicular, infraclavicular and internal mammary lymph nodes 
  * Distant metastasis 
    * Most frequent: brain, bone, distant lymph nodes, liver 
    * Less frequent: thyroid, bladder, gastrointestinal tract, gynecologic organs, peritoneum, retroperitoneum 
  * Patterns of metastasis are associated with biomarker status of tumor 
    * Luminal A: bone (66.6%), liver (23.8%) and pleura / peritoneum (28.2%) 
    * Luminal B: bone (71.4%), liver (32.0%) and lung (30.4%) 
    * HER2 positive, ER / PR positive: bone (65.0%), liver (44.4%) and lung (36.8%) 
    * HER2 positive, ER / PR negative: bone (59.6%), lung (47.1%) and liver (45.6%) 
    * Triple negative: bone (43.1%), lung (35.8%) and distant lymph nodes (35.8%) 
      * Highest probability of developing metastatic disease 
      * Predominantly seen in Black women 
  * References: [J Mol Pathol 2022;3:293](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmp3040025), [J Clin Oncol 2010;28:3271](20498394), [Breast Cancer Res Treat 2020;181:115](32193802)

Pathophysiology

  * Modulated by activator and suppressor genes 
  * Multistep process 
    * In blood 
      * Cancer stem cells degrade extracellular matrix 
      * Intravasation 
        * Disrupt vessel endothelium though cell division 
        * Tumor cells detach into circulation 
        * Altered integrins support oncogenic growth factor receptor (GFR) signaling and GFR dependent invasion 
      * Circulation 
        * Most are found as individual tumor cells 
        * Larger clusters are more likely to form metastases 
      * Evasion of immune response 
        * Tumor clusters contain stromal and immune components 
        * Coated with neutrophils and platelets 
        * Leads to heterogeneity to increase tumor survival 
      * Travel to distant organs 
        * Chemokines and cytokines facilitate tumor migration 
        * Promotion of prometastatic neutrophilic inflammation 
          * Loss of _TP53_ in tumor cells 
          * Secretes WNT ligands 
          * Stimulates IL1β 
        * Suppression of CD8+ T lymphocytes 
          * Tumor cells induce IL1β 
          * Gamma delta (γδ) T cells release IL17 
          * Promotes metastasis 
      * Invasion / colonization 
        * Establishes vascular network 
          * Overexpression of _SERPINE2_ and _SLPI_
        * Period of dormancy: arrest phase 
          * Due to delayed acclimatization of cancer cells in new microenvironment 
          * Inhibits WNT protein driven signaling pathway and enters dormant period 
        * Activation: generation of secondary tumors 
          * Persistent inflammation in microenvironment 
          * Establishment of neutrophilic extracellular traps 
          * High levels of ERK1 / ERK2 
    * In lymph node 
      * Mechanisms for survival and growth within lymph nodes is not clear 
      * In mice 
        * Tumor cells shift to fatty acid oxidation 
        * Adaptation activated by yes associated protein (YEP) transcription factor 
  * Bone metastasis pathway 
    * Tumor derived factors (osteoponin [OPN], parathyroid hormone related protein [PTHrP], heparanase, IL1, IL6 and prostaglandin E2 [PGE2]) enhance osteoclast formation 
    * Bone resorption 
    * Releases bone derived growth factors (insulin like growth factor [IGF1], platelet derived growth factory [PDGF], transforming growth factor beta [TGFβ] and calcium) 
    * Stimulates tumor growth 
    * Tumor cells secrete PTHrP 
    * Osteolysis and stimulate osteoblasts 
    * RANKL is upregulated, activate RANKL / RANK signaling pathway 
    * Further bone resorption 
  * References: [Hematol Oncol Stem Cell Ther 2021;14:275](33744312), [NPJ Precis Oncol 2018;2:4](29872722), [Signal Transduct Target Ther 2020;5:28](32296047), [Mol Biomed 2025;6:20](40192949)

Etiology

  * 10% of breast cancer is due to hereditary genetic mutations 
    * _BRCA1_ (17q21) and _BRCA2_ (13q13) 
      * Involved in homologous DNA repair 
      * Risk of breast cancer by the age of 80 is 72% and 69%, respectively 
      * Risk of breast cancer in men with _BRCA2_ mutation is 6% 
      * Prevalence of mutation 
        * Highest in Ashkenazi group (10.2%) 
        * Lower in Asian group (0.5%) 
      * _STK11_ , _TP53_ , _PTEN_ , _CHECK2_ , _ATM_ , _PALB2_
  * Lifestyle / environmental factors 
    * Early menarche 
    * Nulliparity / lack of breast feeding 
    * Late onset menopause 
    * Radiation exposure 
    * Modifiable risk factors 
      * Obesity 
      * Physical inactivity 
      * Cigarette and alcohol use 
  * References: [Nat Rev Dis Primers 2019;5:66](31548545), [Signal Transduct Target Ther 2025;10:49](39966355)

Diagnosis

  * American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM: most common staging system for breast cancer 
    * T: tumor size and degree of locoregional invasion 
    * N: regional lymph node metastasis 
    * M: distant metastasis 
  * Numerous imaging modalities used for staging advanced breast cancer 
    * Bone scintigraphy 
    * Computed tomography (CT) 
    * Positron emission tomography (PET) scan 
    * Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) 
    * Hybrid imaging 
  * Followed by fine needle aspiration (FNA) and core needle biopsy of metastatic sites 
    * Evaluate cytohistologic morphology 
    * Immunohistochemical markers 
    * Obtain material for ancillary testing 
  * Hypothetical 
    * Future role of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) 
      * Detect early and characterize tumor 
      * Monitor genomic profile and detect genetic alterations 
      * May guide clinical decision making 
      * Can be found in nonneoplastic inflammatory diseases (lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis) 
  * References: [Insights Imaging 2020;11:79](32548731), [Mod Pathol 2019;32:58](30600317), [Cancer Treat Rev 2019;73:73](30682661)

Laboratory

  * Cancer antigen (CA) 15-3 and CA 27-29: elevated in > 70% of metastatic breast cancer patients 
  * Alkaline phosphatase (ALP): elevated with bone involvement 
  * Liver function tests (LFTs): elevated with liver involvement 
  * Reference: [Kufe: Holland-Frei Cancer Medicine, 6th Edition, 2003](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK13196)

Radiology description

  * Several imaging modalities (MRI, CT, PET, bone scintigraphy) every 2 - 4 months to determine tumor burden and treatment response 
  * Findings are based on site of metastasis 
  * Usually found as numerous nodules but can be a solitary mass 
  * MRI 
    * Liver: hyperintense lesions 
    * Brain: lesions predominantly arising at the gray - white matter junction and watershed zones 
  * CT 
    * Bone: lucency, sclerosis, well defined radiodensities 
    * Liver: irregular, hypodense lesions with peripheral contrast enhancement 
    * Lung: lymphangitic carcinomatosis, pleural effusions 
  * Reference: [Insights Imaging 2020;11:79](32548731)

Prognostic factors

  * Breast cancer death rate continues to decline (1989 - 2022 by 44%) 
  * 5 year survival rate dependent on state at initial diagnosis 
    * 99% for localized stage disease 
    * 87% for regional stage disease 
    * 32% for distant stage disease 
  * 20 - 30% of patients experience relapse with distant metastasis 
    * Metastatic disease has a poor prognosis overall 
  * Favorable prognostic factors 
    * Luminal A tumors 
      * Best prognosis compared to other hormone receptor (HR) profiles 
      * Highest rate in White women 
      * Longest median duration of survival (2.2 years); 70% of patients alive at 10 years 
    * HR+ carcinomas have better outcomes than HR- 
      * Risk of relapse after 5 - 7 years increases with HR+ tumors 
    * Bone only metastasis 
  * Unfavorable prognostic factors 
    * Presence and number of metastatic axillary lymph nodes are the single most important prognostic factors in metastatic breast cancer 
    * Primary tumor characteristics 
      * Size: > 2.0 cm 
      * Lymphovascular invasion 
      * Basal-like or triple negative breast cancer 
    * _EGFR_ mutations 
    * Site and number of distant metastases 
      * Multiple or visceral metastasis (< 22 month survival) 
      * Nonvisceral metastasis (> 33 month survival) 
    * Disadvantages in social determinants of health: hazardous environment, alcohol, less breast feeding, low socioeconomic status 
    * Age: > 50 years 
    * Black women have 38% higher mortality compared to White women 
      * Lowest survival for every breast cancer subtype and stage 
  * References: [Hematol Oncol Stem Cell Ther 2021;14:275](33744312), [Ann Oncol 2008;19:2012](18641006), [CA Cancer J Clin 2024;74:477](39352042), [J Clin Oncol 2010;28:3271](20498394)

Treatment

  * Treatment is based on disease stage, number and site of metastasis, biological characteristics of tumor, age and overall health 
    * Systemic therapy (hormone therapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted drugs) 
    * Palliative treatment 
    * Surgical resection 
      * Usually for local treatment 
      * In metastatic disease: removable chest wall recurrences or solitary brain mass 
  * Complete recovery is rare with distant metastasis 
  * Local treatment 
    * Lumpectomy / mastectomy with sentinel lymph node dissection in primary tumors 
    * Emerging: thermal ablation / cryoablation to destroy tumor 
      * Thermal ablation: examples include radiofrequency ablation, microwave ablation, laser ablation and high intensity focused ultrasound 
      * Cryoablation: inserts a cryoprobe which alternates between freeze thaw cycle to destroy target lesion 
      * May replace surgical excision in some situations 
  * Systemic therapy 
    * HER2+: trastuzumab, pertuzumab and taxanes 
    * HR+, premenopausal: tamoxifen, ovarian ablation, CDK4/6 inhibitors 
    * HR+, postmenopausal: endocrine therapy, chemotherapy and other 
    * Triple negative breast cancer 
      * PDL1+: pembrolizumab 
      * PDL1-: chemotherapy 
  * References: [Kufe: Holland-Frei Cancer Medicine, 6th Edition, 2003](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK13196), [InformedHealth.org: Overview - Metastatic Breast Cancer [Accessed 12 September 2025]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK361001), [Ther Adv Med Oncol 2019;11:1758835919833519](30911337), [Mod Pathol 2021;34:62](33110239), [JCO Glob Oncol 2024;10:e2300285](38206277), [Signal Transduct Target Ther 2025;10:49](39966355)

Frozen section description

  * Can be used during intraoperative frozen section for sentinel lymph nodes 
    * Sentinel lymph nodes should be sectioned at 2 mm intervals with minimum of 1 H&E level 
    * Can have false negative results (isolated tumor cells or micrometastasis) 
  * Interpretation can be challenging 
    * Therapy related changes (fibrosis, fat necrosis, granulation tissue) 
    * Tissue artifact 
  * Histology of metastasis varies: individual cells to glandular formation, cytologic atypia, prominent nucleoli 
  * References: [Arch Pathol Lab Med 2021;145:1018](33212474), [Surg Pract Sci 2023;15:100224](39844810), [Cureus 2024;16:e72412](39588452)

Cytology description

  * Effusion specimen 
    * Discrete population of atypical cells 
    * Cells may be single, linear, cohesive cannon balls with smooth borders 
    * Typically hyperchromatic, high N:C ratio, round to oval nuclei 
    * Metastatic lobular carcinoma 
      * Mild cytologic atypia 
      * Monotonous appearance 
      * Single cytoplasmic vacuole 
      * Can mimic reactive mesothelial cells 
  * Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) specimen 
    * One of the most common carcinomas in CSF 
    * Single cells or loose clusters 
    * Moderate pleomorphism, high N:C ratio, fine chromatin, prominent nucleoli and distinct cell borders 
  * FNA specimen 
    * Cytomorphology dependent on primary histologic subtype 
    * Can be found as cohesive tumor fragments to individual cells 
  * Reference: [J Mol Pathol 2022;3:293](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmp3040025)

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * Molecular testing for predictive biomarkers 
    * Germline _BRCA1_ / _BRCA2_
      * Performed on all new metastatic patients 
      * Predictive for PARP inhibitors plus platinum based chemotherapy 
    * _PIK3CA_ , _PTEN_ and _ESR1_ mutations: predictive for targeted therapies or certain hormone therapies 
      * _PIK3CA_ : second line option in ER positive, HER2 negative breast cancer 
    * Tumor mutational burden (TMB) high status 
      * Defined as TMB of 10 mut/mB or greater 
      * High TMB is seen in 5% of breast cancers 
        * Most often triple negative and metastatic tumors (metastatic lobular > metastatic ductal) 
      * Predictive for response to pembrolizumab 
    * Rare 
      * _NTRK_ rearrangement 
        * Seen in metastatic secretory breast carcinoma 
        * Rare subtype, < 0.15% of invasive breast cancer 
        * Targeted therapy with larotrectinib and entrectinib 
      * Microsatellite instability (MSI) high or deficiency in mismatch repair proteins (dMMR) 
        * Predictive for single agent pembrolizumab 
  * Molecular testing on circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) in serum approved to detect certain biomarkers, including 
    * _PIK3CA_
    * _ESR1_
  * In situ hybridization for HER2 amplification is performed on cases with equivocal (score 2+) HER2 IHC 
    * HER2 IHC is performed on every metastasis 
    * 25% of cases with a score of 2+ have HER2 amplification 
  * References: [Clin Lab Med 2023;43:299](37169447), [JAMA Oncol 2021;7:316](33331847), [Breast Cancer Res Treat 2020;179:3](31522348), [Pathobiology 2022;89:278](35500561)

A woman with a history of breast ductal carcinoma 10 years ago is found to have suspicious lymph node. A fine needle aspiration (FNA) of her left supraclavicular lymph node is performed. Which of the following immunohistochemical stains could be used to support the diagnosis of metastatic breast cancer?   
  
  1. AE1 / AE3, claudin4 
  2. SOX10, S100 
  3. TRPS1, GATA3 
  4. TTF1, Napsin A 

Practice answer #1

**C**. TRPS1, GATA3. Breast carcinomas are often positive for TRPS1 and GATA3. Answer B is incorrect because SOX10 and S100 would be positive in triple negative breast carcinoma or melanoma. Answer D is incorrect because TTF1 and Napsin A are positive in lung adenocarcinoma. Answer A is incorrect because while AE1 / AE3 and claudin4 are positive in carcinomas, they are not specific to the breast.

Practice question #2

A woman is found to have a new metastasis of her primary breast carcinoma. What additional prognostic marker(s) should be ordered with any new metastasis?   
  
  1. ALK 
  2. BRAF V600E 
  3. Cyclin D1 
  4. ER, PR, HER2 

Practice answer #2

**D**. ER, PR and HER2 are prognostic markers in breast metastasis. Answers A, B and C are incorrect because these are not routinely ordered in breast metastasis.

Practice question #3

Which of the following is the most common site for metastatic luminal A breast cancer?   
  
  1. Bone 
  2. Liver 
  3. Pleura 
  4. Thyroid 

Practice answer #3

**A**. Bone. The most common site of metastatic breast cancer is bone (66.6%). Answer B is incorrect because the liver is the second most common metastatic site (23.8%). Answer C is incorrect because pleura is the third most common metastatic site (28.2%). Answer D is incorrect because thyroid is not a frequent site of metastasis for breast cancer.
