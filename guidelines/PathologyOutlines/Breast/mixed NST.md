Definition / general

  * Primary breast carcinoma with a mixture of no special type ductal (NST) morphology and special type histology, most commonly of lobular morphology 

Essential features

  * Tumors presenting with areas of 10 - 90% of a special histology admixed with at least 10% NST 
  * Mixed NST most often presents with lobular as the special histology ([Breast Cancer Res Treat 2013;138:719](23535842)) 

Terminology

  * Invasive carcinoma with mixed ductal and lobular features (WHO) 
  * Mixed invasive ductal and lobular carcinoma 
  * Invasive breast carcinoma - no special type (IBC NST) / invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC) 
  * Mixed ductal and lobular carcinoma (mDLC) 
  * Invasive carcinoma with ductal and lobular features 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [C50.919](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/C00-D49/C50-C50/C50-/C50.919) \- malignant neoplasm of unspecified site of unspecified female breast 

Epidemiology

  * Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer in female patients and is the leading cause of female cancer related death worldwide ([Br J Cancer 2023;128:1030](36604587), [CA Cancer J Clin 2018;68:394](30207593)) 
  * Most breast cancers are sporadic; however, up to 10% of breast cancers are hereditary ([Am J Hum Genet 1991;48:232](1990835), [Semin Oncol 2016;43:528](27899183), [Eur J Hum Genet 2009;17:722](19092773)) 
  * Mixed NST constitutes ~5% of primary breast carcinomas ([Oncologist 2019;24:e441](30518616), [Breast 2024;75:103732](38653060)) 
  * Mixed NST is identified in younger women when compared to invasive lobular carcinoma ([Breast 2024;75:103732](38653060)) 
  * Endogenous and exogenous estrogen exposure increases risk ([Breast Cancer Res 2015;17:37](25848941)) 

Sites

  * Breast 
  * Can occur in axillary accessory breast tissue 

Pathophysiology

  * Exact pathophysiology is unknown and this entity may represent distinct lesions (i.e., collision) or a single lesion with divergent differentiation 

Etiology

  * Risk factors (no different risk factors for mixed NST; risk factors listed are for mammary carcinoma, NST) 
    * Hormone related 
      * Prolonged estrogen exposure (early menarche, late menopause, obesity, estrogen producing ovarian tumors, nulliparity) ([Br J Cancer 2009;100:538](19190634), [BJOG 2006;113:1160](16945118)) 
    * Genetic 
      * _BRCA1_ / _BRCA2_ mutations associated with early onset breast carcinoma account for up to 60% of familial breast cancer cases 
      * Other mutations associated with breast carcinoma: _TP53, STK11, CD1, PTEN, MDM2, RB, CHEK2_ ([Acta Oncol 2019;58:135](30606073)) 
    * Other 
      * Proliferative breast disease; in situ lesions are a precursor to invasive carcinoma 
      * History of carcinoma of the contralateral breast or endometrial carcinoma 
      * Radiation exposure 
      * High density breast tissue ([Breast Cancer Res 2011;13:R132](22188651)) 

Clinical features

  * Commonly presents as a palpable spiculated mass, with or without calcifications 
  * Skin retraction or change in color / texture, nipple discharge or inversion and change in size / shape of breast may be seen 
  * Similar to invasive lobular carcinoma, mixed NST more often presents as a larger mass than invasive ductal carcinoma ([Br J Cancer 2023;128:1030](36604587)) 
  * Mixed NST tends to clinically behave similarly to invasive lobular carcinoma ([Br J Cancer 2023;128:1030](36604587)) 
  * Mixed NST most often presents with lobular special type histology ([Breast Cancer Res Treat 2013;138:719](23535842)) 
  * May be bilateral or multifocal 

Diagnosis

  * Diagnosis is made on histologic evaluation of lesional tissue 

Radiology description

  * Mammographically may present as architectural distortion or as a spiculated mass with or without calcifications, often underrepresenting any lobular component as it is typically not mass forming 
  * Ultrasound may identify a hypoechoic mass with spiculated to well defined margins 
  * MRI may better visualize the lobular component and may identify multifocal disease 

Prognostic factors

  * Mixed NST is associated with increased likelihood of hormone sensitivity and a lack of HER2 amplification ([Breast 2024;75:103732](38653060)) 
  * Histologic grade of mixed NST is an important prognostic factor, particularly between grade II and grade III ([Acta Oncol 2007;46:803](17653904)) 
  * Generally, survival outcomes of mixed NST does not significantly vary from that of invasive ductal carcinoma or invasive lobular carcinoma ([Breast Cancer 2015;22:374](23925582)) 
  * However, overall survival of mixed NST compared to pure invasive ductal carcinoma and invasive lobular carcinoma may be predicted by molecular subtype ([Breast Cancer 2021;28:187](32812198)) 
    * For hormone receptor (HR)- HER2-, mixed NST showed poorer survival than invasive ductal carcinoma and invasive lobular carcinoma 
    * For HR+ HER2-, invasive lobular carcinoma showed better survival than both mixed NST and invasive ductal carcinoma 
    * For HER2+, no survival differences were seen 
  * Mixed NST overall has superior survival outcomes when stratified by menopausal status, especially in postmenopausal women ([Oncologist 2019;24:e441](30518616)) 
  * Mixed NST has an increased continuous long term risk of relapse compared to invasive ductal carcinoma, which may be attributed to the lobular component of mixed tumors ([Oncologist 2019;24:e441](30518616)) 
  * Mixed NST is less likely to present with intra-abdominal relapses when compared to invasive lobular carcinoma ([Oncologist 2019;24:e441](30518616)) 

Case reports

  * 64 year old woman with calcifications on screening mammogram with palpable breast anomaly ([Radiol Case Rep 2021;16:2450](34093928)) 

Treatment

  * Complete surgical excision, to include breast conserving surgery and mastectomy, for invasive carcinomas 
  * Local radiotherapy, systemic chemotherapy and other targeted therapies (e.g., anti-HER2) may be offered; however, consideration and selection of treatment is based on several factors such as location, histologic grade, genetic alterations and hormone receptor status 

Gross description

  * May present as an ill defined and poorly circumscribed mass with irregular or stellate borders 
  * Lobular component may not be grossly appreciated and therefore may be underestimated 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Tumor is composed of 10 - 90% of a special histology (most commonly invasive lobular carcinoma) admixed with at least 10% NST 
  * Ductal (NST) component 
    * Histologic and cytologic features are often quite variable 
    * Variable architecture of tubules, nests, clusters or sheets may be seen 
    * Absence of myoepithelial layer 
    * Calcification and desmoplasia may be seen 
  * Lobular component (alternate special histology may be present) 
    * Tumor cells are small, discohesive and typically monomorphic with mild atypia 
    * Tumor cells can dissect intervening tissue in a single file fashion, arranged in cords or as single cells 
    * Other nonclassical patterns may be seen 
  * Overall histologic grade of tumor based on Nottingham / modified Bloom & Richardson as follows ([Histopathology 1991;19:403](1757079)) 
    * Tubule formation (1 - 3 pts) 
      * > 75% (1 pt) 
      * 10 - 75% (2 pts) 
      * < 10% (3 pts) 
    * Nuclear pleomorphism (1 - 3 pts) 
      * Small, regular, uniform nuclei, similar in appearance to normal ductal nuclei, 2 - 3x red blood cell (RBC) (1 pt) 
      * Moderate increase in size and variability (2 pts) 
      * Large nuclei with marked variation, vesicular chromatin, prominent nucleoli (3 pts) 
    * Mitotic count (1 - 3 pts), cutoff points determined by field area assessed using the diameter of the high power field (HPF) ([Virchows Arch 2022;480:33](34196797)) 
  * Total score (add points for tubule formation, nuclear pleomorphism and mitotic count) 
    * 3 - 5 pts: grade 1 
    * 6 - 7 pts: grade 2 
    * 8 - 9 pts: grade 3 
  * Reporting the specific special type histology and its percentage of the overall tumor is suggested 
  * Reporting biomarker status of both components is advised 
  * Reference: [Schnitt: Biopsy Interpretation of the Breast, 3rd Edition, 2017](https://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1496365755/pathologyoutl-20)

Invasive ductal and lobular carcinoma

Cytology description

  * There may be clusters of cohesive atypical cells that represent the ductal component or mildly atypical cells arranged in chains or as single cells that represent the lobular component 
  * Ductal component may be discohesive on cytology and may not accurately represent the mixed nature of the tumor 

Positive stains

  * Low molecular weight cytokeratins ([CK8](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsck8.html), [CK18](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsck18.html), [CK19](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsck19.html) and [CK7](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsck7.html)) and pancytokeratins, such as [AE1 / AE3](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsae1ae3.html)
Negative stains

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * Mixed NST is a molecularly heterogenous group of tumors ([Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A 2024;121:e2322068121](39042692)) 
  * Invasive ductal carcinoma is commonly with 1q, 11q13 gains and 16q, 13q losses 
  * Invasive lobular carcinoma is commonly with 1q gain, 16p gain and 16q loss 
    * _CDH1_ gene encoding E-cadherin is located at 16q22.1 ([Br J Cancer 1997;76:1131](9365159)) 
    * _CDH1_ gene mutations are common; however, promoter hypermethylation is seen ([Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A 2024;121:e2322068121](39042692)) 
  * 5 - 10% of breast cancers are hereditary with mutations in genes such as _BRCA1, BRCA2, TP53, STK11, CD1, PTEN, MDM2, RB, CHEK2_ ([Acta Oncol 2019;58:135](30606073)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Left breast, mastectomy: 
    * Invasive carcinoma with mixed ductal and lobular features, grade 2, measuring 1.1 cm (see comment and synoptic report) 
    * Ductal carcinoma in situ, nuclear grade 2, solid and cribriform patterns, with focal necrosis and rare calcifications 
    * Resection margins are negative for carcinoma 
    * All margins are > 0.2 cm for invasive carcinoma and ductal carcinoma in situ 
    * No lymphovascular invasion is identified 
    * Extensive lobular carcinoma in situ, classic type 
    * Biopsy site changes, biopsy clips and SAVI reflector 
    * Pathologic stage (AJCC 8th edition): pT1c N1mi(sn) 
    * Comment: The invasive carcinoma demonstrates admixed 15% ductal and 85% lobular components. While the majority of the tumor demonstrates a lobular phenotype with loss of E-cadherin expression, a smaller ductal component is identified adjacent to ductal carcinoma in situ in block 1G, demonstrating retained E-cadherin expression. This may represent a collision tumor of 2 separate invasive carcinomas versus a single malignant process with divergent differentiation.

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/breastmixedNSTbachert01.jpg

Which of the following best fits the 2019 WHO definition of mixed NST of the breast?   
  
  1. At least 5% NST admixed with 10 - 95% special histology 
  2. At least 5% special histology admixed with 10 - 95% NST 
  3. At least 10% NST admixed with 10 - 90% of carcinoma in situ 
  4. At least 10% NST admixed with 10 - 90% special histology 
  5. At least 50% special histology admixed with 10 - 49% NST 

Practice answer #1

**D.** At least 10% NST admixed with 10 - 90% special histology. Per the 2019 WHO classification, mixed NST is defined as tumors presenting with areas of 10 - 90% of a special histology admixed with at least 10% NST. Answer E is incorrect because this is criteria that is no longer used for mixed NST. Answers A, B and C are incorrect because these combinations do not satisfy the current definition of mixed NST.

Practice question #2

Which of the following immunoprofiles would generally be expected of mixed NST of the breast and would aid in its diagnosis?  
  
  1. 2 distinct components, with both negative for CK20 
  2. 2 distinct components, with both positive for E-cadherin 
  3. 2 distinct components, with both positive for ER, PR and HER2 
  4. 2 distinct components, with one positive and the other negative for cytokeratin AE1 / AE3 
  5. 2 distinct components, with one positive and the other negative for E-cadherin 

Practice answer #2

**E.** 2 distinct components, with one positive and the other negative for E-cadherin. E-cadherin can be used to distinguish the ductal NST component from a lobular special type histology. Answers A - D are incorrect because these combinations of immunohistochemical stains are not useful in distinguishing NST from special type histology.
