Definition / general

  * Infection or inflammation of breast tissue, usually bacterial, often seen in association with lactation 
  * If untreated, may form abscess and fistulous tracts 

Essential features

  * Infection or inflammation of breast tissue, usually bacterial, often seen in association with lactation 
  * If untreated, may form abscess and fistulous tracts 
  * _Staphylococcus aureus_ most common organism 
  * Microscopic examination reveals a mixed dense inflammatory infiltrate, predominantly neutrophils, in breast tissue 

Terminology

  * Puerperal mastitis 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: 
    * [N61.0](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/N00-N99/N60-N65/N61-/N61.0) \- mastitis without abscess 
    * [N61.1](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/N00-N99/N60-N65/N61-/N61.1) \- abscess of the breast and nipple 
    * [O91.1](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/O00-O9A/O85-O92/O91-/O91.1) \- abscess of the breast associated with pregnancy, the puerperium and lactation 
    * [O91.211](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/O00-O9A/O85-O92/O91-/O91.211) \- nonpurulent mastitis associated with pregnancy, first trimester 
    * [O91.22](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/O00-O9A/O85-O92/O91-/O91.22) \- nonpurulent mastitis associated with the puerperium 
  * ICD-11: 
    * [GB21.Z](https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en#/http%3a%2f%2fid.who.int%2ficd%2fentity%2f259204343%2fmms%2funspecified) \- inflammatory disorders of breast, unspecified 
    * [GB21.Y](https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en#/http%3a%2f%2fid.who.int%2ficd%2fentity%2f259204343%2fmms%2fother) \- other specified inflammatory disorders of breast 
    * [JB45.1](https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en#/http%3a%2f%2fid.who.int%2ficd%2fentity%2f823561621) \- nonpurulent mastitis associated with childbirth 
    * [JB45.0](https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en#/http%3a%2f%2fid.who.int%2ficd%2fentity%2f1169939818) \- abscess of breast associated with childbirth 

Epidemiology

  * Incidence in 2 - 33% of lactating women ([Am J Epidemiol 2002;155:103](11790672), [Med Microbiol Immunol 2018;207:83](29350290)) 
  * 3% with mastitis have an abscess ([BJOG 2004;111:1378](15663122)) 
  * Risk factors: age (21 - 35 years old), mastitis with previous child, cracked or sore nipples, use of ointments, incorrect breastfeeding practices, peripartum antibiotic therapy ([Med Microbiol Immunol 2018;207:83](29350290)) 

Sites

  * Subareolar (nonpuerperal) 

Pathophysiology

  * Infection via the mammary duct system; typically occurs within 2 - 3 weeks after the start of lactation ([Breastfeed Med 2014;9:239](24911394)) 
  * _Staphylococcus aureus_ is most common organism transmitted from skin or infant ([Breastfeed Med 2014;9:239](24911394)) 
  * Accumulation of inadequately drained milk in ducts and lobules creates a microenvironment that fosters bacterial growth ([Hoda: Rosen’s Breast Pathology, 4th Edition, 2014](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1451176538/pathologyoutl-20)) 
  * Infrequent complication is abscess formation (localized collection of purulent material) 
  * Milk samples show loss of bacterial diversity ([J Hum Lact 2015;31:406](25948578)) 
  * Subareolar or nonpuerperal abscesses most often a consequence of [squamous metaplasia of lactiferous ducts (SMOLD)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastsmold.html) ([J Pathol Transl Med 2015;49:279](26095437)) 

Etiology

  * Associated with lactation and cracks in nipple 
  * Common scenarios: infrequent feeding, oversupply of milk, rapid weaning, illness in mother or child, clogged duct ([J Hum Lact 1998;14:101](9775842)) 
  * Also associated with eczema, nipple dermatoses and postreduction mammoplasty 
  * Bacteria often not isolated from culture 
  * Pregnancy related infections are usually due to _Staphylococcus_
    * Usually unilateral, often methicillin resistant _Staphylococcus aureus_ (MRSA) ([Arch Surg 2007;142:881](17891866), [Asian J Surg 2009;32:55](19321404)) 
    * Also _Staphylococcus epidermidis_ ([BMC Microbiol 2009;9:82](19422689), [J Hum Lact 2015;31:406](25948578)) 
  * _Streptococcus_ usually causes diffuse infections leading to scarring but is uncommon today ([J Hum Lact 2020;890334420965147](33201760)) 
  * Tuberculosis may occur in endemic areas ([Med Princ Pract 2009;18:245](19349731), [Int J Infect Dis 2019;87:135](31442629)) 
  * Nonpregnancy related infections may be polymicrobial or associated with diabetes ([Am J Med Sci 2009;338:123](19680016)) 

Clinical features

  * Erythema, swelling, firmness, breast pain, fever, malaise, decreased milk outflow 
  * Abscess: well circumscribed fluctuant mass in addition to above features 
  * African Americans may have higher risk of abscess formation ([World J Surg 2009;33:2582](19669231)) 
  * Obesity (BMI > 30) and smoking risk factors for abscess ([World J Surg 2009;33:2582](19669231)) 
  * Zuska disease (periductal mastitis): clinical scenario of recurring subareolar abscesses and sinus formation ([Am J Surg 1951;81:312](14819475)) 
  * Fungal mastitis: rare 
    * Actinomycosis may develop in nipple and form a sinus tract 
    * _Candida_ infection may interfere with breast feeding but _Candida albicans_ is not present in milk ducts in women with clinical symptoms of ductal candidiasis ([Breastfeed Med 2009;4:57](19500049)) 

Diagnosis

  * Based on history and clinical findings 
  * Ultrasound preferred imagining modality ([Radiol Bras 2020;53:405](33304009)) 
  * Biopsy may be warranted for suspected abscess, atypical presentation, recurrent infection or treatment failure; Gram stain and culture (aerobic and anaerobic) with sensitivities to guide antibiotic selection (rarely needed) 
  * FNA can be used to drain ([Am Fam Physician 2008;78:727](18819238)) 

Radiology description

  * Ultrasound (preferred modality): abscess shows hypoechoic lesion of purulent material, well circumscribed, macrolobulated, irregular or ill defined with septa and thick echogenic rim ([Radiographics 2011;31:1683](21997989), [Radiographics 2007;27 Suppl 1:S101](18180221)) 
  * Mammography of limited value due to nonspecific findings

Prognostic factors

  * Recurrent breast abscesses are more likely to be smokers and have mixed bacterial and anaerobic infections ([World J Surg 2009;33:2582](19669231))

Treatment

  * Symptomatic treatment: effective milk removal via breastfeeding, pumping or hand expressing to fully empty breasts, analgesia, warm compress ([Am Fam Physician 2008;78:727](18819238)) 
  * Kneading and dispersing manipulation for early stage disease ([Zhong Xi Yi Jie He Xue Bao 2009;7:1130](20015432)) 
  * Antibiotics targeting _Staphylococcus_ species are mainstay of treatment, although effectiveness is debated ([J Hum Lact 2008;24:311](18689718), [Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2013:CD005458](23450563)) 
  * Aspiration or incision and drainage; now considered last resort for abscess management ([Radiographics 2011;31:1683](21997989), [Expert Rev Anti Infect Ther 2014;12:753](24791941)) 
  * Urokinase irrigation may be useful for large abscesses ([J Ultrasound Med 2009;28:449](19321672)) 
  * Ultrasound guided drainage ([Radiology 2004;232:904](15284435), [Br J Radiol 2005;78:186](15730981)) 
  * Probiotics ([Benef Microbes 2014;5:169](24463206), [Appl Environ Microbiol 2008;74:4650](18539795), [Clin Infect Dis 2010;50:1551](20455694))

Lactational abscess

Gross description

  * Excisions rare; specimens usually from incision and drainage procedures and typically received in fragments 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Mixed dense inflammatory infiltrate, predominantly neutrophils, in breast tissue 
  * May obscure underlying normal breast tissue 
  * Tissue necrosis may be present 
  * Gram stain for microorganisms may reveal bacterial forms (gram positive cocci) associated with neutrophilic infiltrate 
  * Granulation tissue and chronic inflammation with resolution 
  * Lymphocytes, giant cells and granulomas are not typical ([J Pathol Transl Med 2015;49:279](26095437))

  * Background of acute inflammation and necrotic debris ([Cytopathology 1992;3:281](1288653)) 

Positive stains

Sample pathology report

  * Right breast, core needle biopsy: 
    * Breast tissue with abundant acute inflammation and bacterial forms identified on Gram stain (see comment) 
    * Comment: The histologic findings are compatible with acute mastitis and abscess formation in the appropriate clinical context. Clinical correlation and correlation with microbiology studies (if performed) is recommended.
