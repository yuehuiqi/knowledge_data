Definition / general

  * Benign enlargement of the male breast, typically presenting as a palpable subareolar mass 
  * Histologically identical gynecomastoid hyperplasia can be seen in the female breast 

Essential features

  * Most common male breast lesion 
  * Benign ductal and stromal proliferation with characteristic stromal cuffing, 3 layered ductal lining and pseudoangiomatous stromal hyperplasia (PASH) 
  * No evidence of precancerous potential 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [N62](http://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/N00-N99/N60-N65/N62-/N62) \- hypertrophy of breast 
  * ICD-11: [GB22](https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en#/http%3a%2f%2fid.who.int%2ficd%2fentity%2f2078176266) \- hypertrophy of breast 

Epidemiology

  * Trimodal age distribution 
    * Infancy and neonatal period 
    * Puberty (peak incidence between ages 13 - 14) 
    * Older men, ages 50 - 80 (likely due to increased adiposity, decreased testosterone, medications)
  * Estimated lifetime prevalence is 32 - 65% of men, depending on method of assessment ([Indian J Endocrinol Metab 2014;18:150](24741509)) 

Sites

  * Most cases are retroareolar and bilateral 
    * Rare cases of unilateral gynecomastia related to trauma or of unknown pathophysiology ([Andrologia 2006;38:31](16420240), [J Plast Reconstr Aesthet Surg 2011;64:1684](21782537), [GMS Interdiscip Plast Reconstr Surg DGPW 2012;1:Doc01](26504685))
  * Can be localized or generalized 

Etiology

  * Imbalance of estrogens and androgens by various mechanisms 
  * Idiopathic in > 40% of cases ([Biomed Res Int 2018;2018:8364824](30003107)) 
  * Physiologic: in newborns and infants, due to in utero estrogen exposure and tends to resolve spontaneously; hormonal changes in puberty ([Pediatr Endocrinol Rev 2017;14:371](28613047)) 
  * Drugs: finasteride, anabolic steroids, digitalis, spironolactone, metronidazole ([Eur J Clin Pharmacol 2015;71:569](25827472), [Biomed Res Int 2018;2018:8364824](30003107)) 
  * Systemic disorders: obesity, hyperthyroidism, hypogonadism, cirrhosis, chronic renal failure, chronic pulmonary disease 
  * Syndromes: androgen insensitivity, Klinefelter, Carney complex, Peutz-Jeghers, spinobulbar muscular atrophy ([Andrology 2016;4:1178](27611179), [Case Rep Pediatr 2015;2015:439239](26366315), [Am J Transl Res 2017;9:2639](28560011), [J Neurol 2018;265:1026](29464380)) 
  * Paraneoplastic hormone production by various testicular germ cells, pulmonary and adrenocortical neoplasms 
  * Mechanical: repeated impact against the body as seen in male rifle shooters with unilateral gynecomastia ([GMS Interdiscip Plast Reconstr Surg DGPW 2012;1:Doc01](26504685)) 

Clinical features

  * Most common male breast lesion 
  * Mastalgia in 88% of cases ([J Family Med Prim Care 2021;10:648](34041055)) 

Diagnosis

  * Histologic findings are necessary for the diagnosis; important to exclude malignancy 

Laboratory

  * Usually not needed, except to identify underlying causes 
    * Estrogen to testosterone ratio (E2 / TTE), follicle stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), prolactin, thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH), human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), alpha fetoprotein (AFP), dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), cortisol, karyotyping

Radiology description

  * 3 patterns of mammographic findings ([Radiographics 1999;19:559](10336188), [Andrology 2021;9:1444](34033252)) 
    * Nodular: 72%, corresponds to florid histologic phase; fan shape radiating from nipple into surrounding fat 
    * Dendritic: 18%, corresponds to fibrous histologic phase; flame shaped opacity with radiating projections penetrate surrounding fat / upper outer quadrant 
    * Diffuse: ~10%, typically in transgender females undergoing gender affirming hormonal therapy; resembles dense female breast but heterogeneous and without Cooper ligaments
  * Imaging useful to exclude pseudogynecomastia and carcinoma ([Radiographics 2024;44:e230181](38752766))

Prognostic factors

  * May recur if underlying cause is not addressed 
  * No strong evidence of increased risk of breast carcinoma 
    * Present in 3 - 46% of male breast carcinoma cases ([Cancer 2004;101:51](15221988), [Cancer 1996;77:490](8630956))

Case reports

  * 17 year old transgender boy on gonadotropin releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist and testosterone with pubertal gynecomastia ([AACE Clin Case Rep 2021;7:350](34765730)) 
  * 20 year old man with persistent gynecomastia after 1 month of low dose finasteride ([JCEM Case Rep 2024;2:luae050](38660487)) 
  * 20 year old man with concomitant gynecomastia and granulomatous mastitis ([J Int Med Res 2022;50:3000605221075815](35098766)) 

Treatment

  * Some regress spontaneously within 2 years and do not require treatment 
  * Sudden or symptomatic gynecomastia may be treated medically 
    * Aromatase inhibitor, antiestrogen, androgen therapy (tamoxifen, danazol) ([Breast 2006;15:276](15998589), [J Pediatr Endocrinol Metab 2013;26:803](23729603))
  * Identify and treat any underlying causes 
  * Excision in adult patients to rule out malignancy and for cosmesis; however, recurrence is common if the underlying cause is not identified and treated

Gross description

  * Soft, rubbery to firm gray-white subareolar mass 
  * Can be ill defined subareolar induration 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Increased number of ducts with characteristic 3 layer epithelium ([Am J Surg Pathol 2012;36:762](22314184)) 
    * Luminal flattened epithelium expressing CK5/6, CK14 
    * Intermediate cuboidal to columnar epithelium expressing ER, PR, AR 
    * Outer myoepithelium expressing CK5/6 and CK14
  * 3 patterns of proliferative changes, without correlation to age or duration of lesion ([Am J Clin Pathol 1972;57:431](5012934), [ J Plast Surg Hand Surg 2018;52:166](28876176)) 
    * Florid: irregular branching ducts, mild to moderate epithelial hyperplasia with a sawtooth appearance, cellular / myxoid / edematous stroma cuffing around ducts; can have papillary or cribriform architecture, myoepithelial hyperplasia 
    * Intermediate: mixture of florid and fibrous patterns 
    * Fibrous: quiescent epithelium, hyalinized hypocellular stroma
  * Other findings can include [pseudoangiomatous stromal hyperplasia](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/breastpash.html), edema, apocrine or squamous metaplasia

  * Biphasic epithelial and stromal fragments in a background of scattered single bipolar nuclei, similar to fibroadenoma 
  * Spindle cells, apocrine cells, foamy macrophages can be present

  * Intermediate epithelial cells: [ER](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainser.html), [PR](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsprog.html), [AR](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsAR.html), [BCL2](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsbcl2.html), [cyclin D1](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainscyclind1.html)
    * Often more diffusely [ER](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainser.html) positive than female benign breast epithelium, even in absence of atypia ([J Clin Endocrinol Metab 1996;81:3063](8768875)) 
  * Luminal epithelial cells and myoepithelial cells: [CK5](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsck5and6.html), [CK14](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsck14.html)

Negative stains

  * Intermediate epithelial cells: [CK5/6](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsck5and6.html) negative; this should not be confused with atypia

Practice question #1

Which of the following is the most common etiology in the development of gynecomastia?   
  
  1. Cirrhosis 
  2. Estrogen exposure in utero 
  3. Hyperthyroidism 
  4. Idiopathic 
  5. Klinefelter syndrome 

Practice answer #1

**D.** Idiopathic. Gynecomastia arises in the setting of hormone imbalance due to a variety of causes. > 40% of cases have no identifiable cause. Answers A, B, C and E are incorrect because other less common etiologies include systemic disorders like cirrhosis and hypothyroidism, perinatal physiologic exposure to maternal estrogens and congenital hypoandrogenism due to Klinefelter syndrome.

Practice question #2

What is the immunohistochemical profile of the intermediate epithelial cells of gynecomastia?   
  
  1. ER / PR / AR+, CK5/6+ 
  2. ER / PR / AR+, CK5/6- 
  3. ER / PR / AR-, CK5/6+ 
  4. ER / PR / AR-, CK5/6- 

Practice answer #2

**B**. ER / PR / AR+, CK5/6-. The hyperplastic epithelium of gynecomastia consists of 3 phenotypically distinct cell layers. ER / PR / AR+, CK5/6- is the correct immunoprofile of the intermediate epithelial layer, which is positive for hormone receptors but almost always negative for high molecular weight keratins (HMWK). Answer A is incorrect because it describes the inner luminal layer, which is reactive for HMWKs and weakly reactive for hormone receptors. Answer C is incorrect because it describes the peripheral myoepithelial cells. Answer D is incorrect because it is not a typical immunoprofile for any of the cell layers.
