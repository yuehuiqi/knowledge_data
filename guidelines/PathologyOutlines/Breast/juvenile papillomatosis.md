Definition / general

  * Nonneoplastic, localized proliferation encountered mainly in women under 30 
  * Grossly distinct, multinodular mass with clustering of cystic formations that resemble Swiss cheese and contain epithelial proliferations 

Essential features

  * Clinically resembles fibroadenoma 
  * Clustered cysts with epithelial proliferations, grossly resembling Swiss cheese 
  * Associated with family history of breast carcinoma and concurrent or subsequent carcinoma in 10% of patients 
  * Excision not required for radiologic pathologic concordant cases without atypia or carcinoma 
  * Not part of WHO breast classification 

Terminology

  * First described by Rosen in 1980 ([Am J Surg Pathol 1980;4:3](7361994)) 
  * Swiss cheese disease 
  * Juvenile epitheliosis (not recommended) 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [N64.89](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/N00-N99/N60-N65/N64-/N64.89) \- other specified disorders of breast 

Epidemiology

  * Occurs predominantly in female patients in their 20s and 30s 
  * Rare cases in male patients, including infants, children and adults 
  * 26 - 58% of patients have family history of breast carcinoma ([Oncol Lett 2014;8:2587](25364432), [Am J Clin Pathol 1986;86:745](3788861)) 

Sites

  * Most commonly occurs in upper outer quadrant of breast ([Breast J 2018;24:1136](30051564)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Exact pathophysiology is unknown 
  * Rare reports in patients with Cowden syndrome and Proteus syndrome, raising possibility of association with _PTEN_ and PI3K pathway alterations ([Breast J 2018;24:1136](30051564)) 

Clinical features

  * Firm, well defined, mobile mass often in the periphery of the breast, often mistaken for fibroadenoma 
  * Typically unifocal 
  * 4.4% have bilateral disease (synchronous or metachronous) ([Cancer 1985;55:1345](3971303)) 
  * Rare cases with spontaneous nipple discharge or pain ([Pediatr Surg Int 2000;16:104](10663852)) 

Diagnosis

  * Radiologic mass lesion (suggestive of fibroadenoma) or distortion 
  * Histologic examination of tissue obtained via needle core biopsy, lumpectomy or mastectomy 
  * Definitive diagnosis requires evaluation of the entire lesion 
  * Reference: [Am J Surg Pathol 1980;4:3](7361994)

Radiology description

  * Preoperative imaging is often not performed in this age group (especially if the lesion is clinically consistent with fibroadenoma) but may be done 
  * Mammogram: localized increased density not as well defined as fibroadenoma 
  * Ultrasound 
    * Ill defined, heterogeneous mass with small, rounded, echolucent cystic areas at the borders, without acoustic enhancement or attenuation 
    * Numerous, aggregated internal cysts ([Br J Radiol 1996;69:867](8983594)) 
  * Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) ([Radiographics 2007;27:S101](18180221), [Jpn J Radiol 2013;31:419](23475601)) 
    * Complex solid and cystic mass 
    * Multiple small cysts on T2 
    * Continuous or clumped enhancement

Prognostic factors

  * Recurrence after incomplete excision in 6% of cases ([Breast J 2018;24:1136](30051564)) 
  * Coexisting or subsequent breast carcinoma (in situ or invasive carcinoma) in ~10% of cases ([Breast J 2018;24:1136](30051564), [Int J Surg Pathol 2025;33:220](38839253)) 
  * Higher risk of carcinoma if recurrent, bilateral, multicentric or family history of breast cancer ([Am J Clin Pathol 1990;93:599](2158224)) 

Case reports

  * 7 and 14 month old boys with juvenile papillomatosis ([Pediatr Surg Int 2000;16:104](10663852)) 
  * 13 year old girl and 24 year old woman with juvenile papillomatosis of the breast ([Med Arch 2024;78:170](38566878)) 
  * 26 year old woman with left breast mass, with combined features of benign phyllodes tumor, juvenile papillomatosis and juvenile fibroadenoma ([Int J Surg Pathol 2025;33:220](38839253)) 
  * 45 year old man with left breast mass, diagnosed as juvenile papillomatosis ([Int J Surg Pathol 2017;25:536](28420303)) 

Treatment

  * Excision is not required for cases without atypia and with radiologic pathologic concordance 
  * Clinical and radiologic monitoring for breast carcinoma development is recommended by some authors ([Eur J Breast Health 2018;14:51](29322120)) 

Gross description

  * Multinodular mass with fluid cysts of different sizes (1 mm - 2 cm) intermingled with fibrotic areas, giving it a Swiss cheese-like appearance 
  * Typically 1 - 8 cm in diameter; average is 4 cm ([Am J Clin Pathol 1990;93:599](2158224))

Cytology description

  * Cyst fluid but mass persists after aspiration 
  * Sheets of hyperplastic breast epithelium with areas resembling fibroadenoma, macrophages and apocrine cells ([Diagn Cytopathol 1993;9:457](8261855))

Positive stains

  * H&E diagnosis; stains are typically not needed 
  * Myoepithelial stains will show intact myoepithelium throughout 

Negative stains

  * H&E diagnosis; stains are typically not needed 

Molecular / cytogenetics description

  * _PIK3CA_ / _AKT1_ pathway mutations commonly observed ([Hum Pathol 2020;98:64](32088208)) 
  * Shared _PIK3CA_ E542K in 1 case of coexisting juvenile papillomatosis, ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) and invasive carcinoma, suggesting clonal relatedness ([J Pathol Clin Res 2021;7:113](33263939)) 

Sample pathology report

  * Breast, right, 5 o'clock 4 cm from nipple, excision: 
    * Juvenile papillomatosis (see comment) 
    * Comment: Histologic sections show a circumscribed proliferation of variably dilated ducts and cysts. Some ducts exhibit florid ductal hyperplasia, apocrine metaplasia, duct ectasia and sclerosing adenosis. No atypia is identified. Given the patient age and histologic findings, this is most consistent with juvenile papillomatosis.

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/breastjuvenilepapillomatosisSandeep002.jpeg

A 19 year old woman presents with a palpable, mobile breast mass. Ultrasound reveals well circumscribed mass with multiple cystic areas. A core biopsy is performed. Histological examination shows numerous cysts with usual and papillary ductal hyperplasia, apocrine metaplasia and dilated ducts filled with macrophages. No cytologic atypia or malignancy is identified. Which of the following statement is most accurate regarding this condition?   
  
  1. It is a precursor lesion to invasive ductal carcinoma 
  2. It is associated with family history of breast cancer 
  3. It is most commonly associated with _BRCA2_ mutations 
  4. It is typically present in postmenopausal women 

Practice answer #1

**B**. It is associated with family history of breast cancer. The findings are diagnostic of juvenile papillomatosis, which has been associated with family history of breast carcinoma in 26 - 58% of patients and concurrent or subsequent carcinoma in 10% of patients. Answer A is incorrect because although patients with juvenile papillomatosis and family history of breast cancer are at increased personal risk of breast cancer, it is not thought to be a direct precursor to carcinoma. Answer D is incorrect because juvenile papillomatosis is usually present in patients under the age of 30. Answer C is incorrect because there is no documented association with _BRCA1_ / _BRCA2_ mutations.
