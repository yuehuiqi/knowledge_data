Definition / general

  * Lobules of epithelial cells mimicking pancreatic acinar cells at the gastroesophageal junction / distal esophagus (outside of the pancreas) 

Essential features

  * Lobules of epithelial cells with basally situated round nuclei, apical granular acidophilic cytoplasm and basal basophilic cytoplasm 
    * These cells resemble pancreatic acinar cells and are seen in the lamina propria at the gastroesophageal junction and in the distal esophagus 

Terminology

  * Pancreatic acinar metaplasia 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [Q45.3](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/Q00-Q99/Q38-Q45/Q45-/Q45.3) \- other congenital malformations of pancreas and pancreatic duct 

Epidemiology

  * Incidence of pancreatic acinar metaplasia at the gastroesophageal junction / distal esophagus ranges from 15.7% to 24% ([Hum Pathol 2023;139:1](37364824), [Am J Surg Pathol 1996;20:1507](8944044)) 
  * At the gastroesophageal junction / distal esophagus, pancreatic acinar metaplasia has been associated with a protective effect against intestinal metaplasia ([Hum Pathol 2023;139:1](37364824)) 
  * Pancreatic acinar metaplasia may be age dependent and associated with _Helicobacter pylori_ infection as well as female gender; it also may be linked to gastroesophageal reflux if located above the gastroesophageal junction ([J Gastroenterol 2010;45:291](20012917)) 
  * Significant association has been found between pancreatic acinar metaplasia and chronic use of proton pump inhibitors and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, as well as with chronic carditis ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2019;143:510](30525934)) 

Sites

  * Gastroesophageal junction and distal esophagus ([Hum Pathol 2023;139:1](37364824), [Am J Surg Pathol 1996;20:1507](8944044)) 

Pathophysiology

  * 2 major hypotheses, which include potential congenital and acquired etiologies; however, there is some controversy over its origin 
  * No histologic or endoscopic association with either reflux disease or diagnosis of gastritis, demonstrating that it likely arises from a congenital origin rather than an acquired (metaplastic) one ([Virchows Arch 2013;463:643](23989798)) 

Etiology

  * Etiology of pancreatic acinar metaplasia remains unclear 
  * Major hypotheses include gastroesophageal reflux disease and congenital etiology ([Hum Pathol 2023;139:1](37364824)) 
  * Incidental finding unrelated to clinical or histologic abnormalities, favoring a congenital origin ([Am J Surg Pathol 1996;20:1507](8944044)) 

Clinical features

  * Not visible endoscopically 
  * No association has been noted between the presence of pancreatic acinar cells at the gastroesophageal junction and clinical symptoms or complaints or with an endoscopic diagnosis of esophagitis or Barrett esophagus ([Virchows Arch 2013;463:643](23989798)) 

Diagnosis

  * Histopathologic diagnosis based on the characteristic histologic features including epithelial cells arranged in lobules with basophilic cytoplasm at the base and finely granular acidophilic cytoplasm at the apex, mimicking pancreatic acinar cells 

Radiology description

  * Not visible radiologically 

Prognostic factors

  * Not all pathologists report on the presence of pancreatic acinar metaplasia in their reports, indicating that many believe it has no clinicopathologic / prognostic significance 

Case reports

  * 41 year old woman with a longstanding history of gastroesophageal reflux disease ([Am J Gastroenterol 2021;116:S1514](http://doi.org/10.14309/01.ajg.0000789176.09481.be)) 
  * 57 year old man with gastroesophageal reflux disease ([J Med Cases 2022;13:257](35837081)) 
  * Case series of 184 patients with pancreatic acinar cells at the gastroesophageal junction ([Virchows Arch 2013;463:643](23989798)) 
  * Large case series showing that pancreatic acinar metaplasia at the gastroesophageal junction imparts decreased susceptibility to intestinal metaplasia in patients with GERD ([Hum Pathol 2023;139:1](37364824)) 
  * Case series of 71 patients with pancreatic acinar metaplasia and chronic nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug use ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2019;143:510](30525934)) 

Gross description

  * Not visible grossly 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Epithelial cells arranged in lobules with basally situated round nuclei, apical granular acidophilic cytoplasm and basal basophilic cytoplasm, resembling pancreatic acinar cells ([Hum Pathol 2023;139:1](37364824)) 
  * Located in the lamina propria

Practice question #1

Which of the following statements regarding the histologic features of pancreatic acinar metaplasia is true?   
  
  1. Elderly patients with pancreatic acinar metaplasia tend to show more frequent development of esophageal ulcer caused by pancreatic acinar metaplasia 
  2. Lobules of epithelial cells mimicking pancreatic acinar cells in the lamina propria are the most common finding in biopsy samples 
  3. Pancreatic ducts and islets of Langerhans are occasionally seen in this lesion 
  4. Proximal esophagus is the most common site of involvement 

Practice answer #1

**B**. Lobules of epithelial cells mimicking pancreatic acinar cells in the lamina propria are the most common finding in biopsy samples. The histologic findings of pancreatic acinar metaplasia are characterized by lobules of epithelial cells mimicking pancreatic acinar cells in the lamina propria at the esophagogastric junction / distal esophagus. Answer A is incorrect because pancreatic acinar metaplasia is not associated with esophageal ulcer. Answer C is incorrect because pancreatic acinar metaplasia is composed only of pancreatic acinar cells. Answer D is incorrect because distal esophagus and esophagogastric junction are the most common sites of involvement.

Practice question #2

Which of the following statements about pancreatic acinar metaplasia is true?   
  
  1. Can be highlighted by BCL10 immunostain 
  2. Patients often present with dysphagia 
  3. Positive for pepsinogen I 
  4. Usually identified as a small nodule 

Practice answer #2

**A**. Can be highlighted by BCL10 immunostain. Pancreatic acinar metaplasia can be highlighted by immunostains such as BCL10, lipase, trypsin, amylase and chymotrypsin. Answer D is incorrect because pancreatic acinar metaplasia is not visible grossly. Answer B is incorrect because no association has been noted between pancreatic acinar metaplasia and clinical symptoms. Answer C is incorrect because pancreatic acinar metaplasia is negative for pepsinogen I, while gastric chief cells are positive for pepsinogen I.
