Definition / general

  * Columnar mucosa with intestinal metaplasia (goblet cells) endoscopically visible as salmon colored mucosa extending into the tubular esophagus (≥ 1 cm proximal to the gastroesophageal junction per American College of Gastroenterology [ACG]; no length requirement per American Gastroenterological Association [AGA]) ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * British Society of Gastroenterology (BSG) definition: columnar mucosa with or without intestinal metaplasia extending ≥ 1 cm proximal to the gastroesophageal junction ([Gut 2014;63:7](24165758)) 

Essential features

  * Secondary to longstanding gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) 
  * Barrett esophagus requires both endoscopically abnormal mucosa in the tubular esophagus and histologic (metaplastic columnar epithelium) correlation for diagnosis ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Necessity of intestinal metaplasia for diagnosis varies 
    * Intestinal metaplasia required in United States and many other countries; however, intestinal metaplasia is not necessary in United Kingdom and Asia Pacific region ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Increased risk for esophageal adenocarcinoma, through progression from metaplasia - dysplasia - carcinoma sequence ([Am J Surg Pathol 2016;40:e45](26813745)) 
  * Histologic assessment on H&E slide remains gold standard for diagnosing Barrett esophagus and associated neoplasia ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 

Terminology

  * Long segment: any segment measuring ≥ 3 cm ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Short segment: any segment measuring < 3 cm ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Z line: squamocolumnar junction 
  * Gastroesophageal junction: anatomic point where the esophagus connects to the stomach

Epidemiology

  * Estimated population prevalence ranges from 0.4% to 5.6% ([Gastrointest Endosc Clin N Am 2021;31:1](33213789)) 
  * Among patients with chronic gastroesophageal reflux disease, 5 - 12% will harbor Barrett esophagus ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Symptom duration of gastroesophageal reflux disease is a risk factor ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Screening endoscopy is recommended in patients with chronic gastroesophageal reflux disease and at least 3 additional risk factors (male sex, age > 50 years, white race, central obesity, smoking history or a family history of Barrett esophagus or esophageal adenocarcinoma in first degree relative) ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 

Sites

  * Distal esophagus, gastroesophageal junction 

Pathophysiology

  * Exact mechanism is not completely characterized 
  * Likely related to gastroesophageal reflux disease induced tissue damage, which drives cellular reprogramming and change in cell phenotype 
  * Transdifferentiation of squamous cells into columnar cells may occur directly or via dedifferentiation ([Am J Surg Pathol 2016;40:e45](26813745)) 
  * Reprogramming of progenitor cells, which may originate from esophagus, distal stomach or bone marrow ([Am J Surg Pathol 2016;40:e45](26813745)) 
    * Tissue injury activates signaling pathways (such as Hedgehog, BMP4 and NFκB) and downregulates Notch signaling 
    * Signals lead to increased expression of SOX9 (induces columnar differentiation) 
    * Upregulation of _FOXA2_ , _CDX1_ and _CDX2_ induces intestinal differentiation 
  * Multilayered epithelium (4 - 8 layers of basally located squamous cells with associated superficial columnar, mucinous epithelium) shows morphologic and ultrastructural features of both squamous and columnar epithelium and has been suggested as a precursor ([Am J Surg Pathol 2001;25:569](11342767)) 

Etiology

  * Known risk factors ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
    * Chronic (> 5 years) gastroesophageal reflux disease symptoms 
    * Advancing age (> 50 years) 
    * Male gender 
    * Tobacco usage 
    * Central obesity 
    * Caucasian race 
    * Family history of Barrett esophagus or esophageal adenocarcinoma in first degree relative 
  * Prevalence in those with gastroesophageal reflux disease plus at least 1 additional risk factor is substantially higher than those with gastroesophageal reflux disease alone ([Gastrointest Endosc 2019;90:707](31152737)) 
  * Alcohol consumption does not increase risk; wine may be protective ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777))

Clinical features

Diagnosis

  * Upper endoscopy showing characteristic appearance (at least 1 cm segment of abnormal mucosa proximal to gastroesophageal junction) and histologic findings (metaplastic epithelium with intestinal metaplasia in U.S.) ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
    * At least 8 random biopsies recommended 
    * When 8 biopsies are not obtainable (as in short segment Barrett esophagus), at least 4 biopsies per cm of circumferential Barrett esophagus and 1 biopsy per cm in tongues of Barrett esophagus recommended 
    * Biopsies of normal Z lines or Z lines with < 1 cm of variability is not recommended 
    * Routine use of high definition white light and chromoendoscopy is recommended 
    * Advanced endoscopic imaging techniques (e.g., confocal laser endomicroscopy, volumetric laser endomicroscopy) may be utilized 
  * Unsedated transnasal endoscopy ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
    * Minimally invasive screening with a sensitivity of 91% and specificity of 96% 
    * Low cost alternative, not regularly implemented in clinical practice 
  * Swallowable cell collection device combined with a biomarker (e.g., cytosponge, Esophacap, EsoCheck) ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
    * Dissolvable capsule containing an expandable polyurethane sponge (Cytosponge, EsophaCap) or inflatable balloon (EsoCheck) 
    * Swallowed and orally withdrawn to obtain cytology samples, which are assessed for protein markers expressed in intestinal metaplasia, including trefoil factor 3 (TTF3) expression by immunohistochemistry or methylated DNA markers (MDMs) analyzed by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing 

Prognostic factors

  * Risk of cancer progression for nondysplastic Barrett esophagus is 0.2 - 0.5% per year ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Annual risk of progression with low grade dysplasia is 0.7% per year ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Progression to dysplasia / carcinoma is associated with increasing age, smoking and increased length of Barrett esophagus ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2018;16:1046](29199147)) 
  * Proton pump inhibitor and statin use are associated with lower risk of progression ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2018;16:1046](29199147)) 
  * Risk of progression is extremely low in patients with normal or Z line irregularity < 1 cm ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 

Case reports

  * 26 year old White man with Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome and young onset Barrett esophagus ([Cureus 2020;12:e11709](33391942)) 
  * 47 year old man with bulimia nervosa and Barrett esophagus ([World J Clin Cases 2022;10:5841](35979135)) 
  * 66 year old man with concurrent esophagitis dissecans superficialis and Barrett esophagus ([R I Med J (2013) 2022;105:44](35476736)) 
  * 73 year old man with coincident achalasia and Barrett esophagus ([ACG Case Rep J 2024;11:e01341](38690567)) 

Treatment

  * Proton pump inhibitor therapy, once daily dosing ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * Aspirin, other nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) or antireflux surgery not routinely prescribed or performed ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * For nondysplastic Barrett esophagus, surveillance endoscopy at 3 - 5 year interval ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
    * High definition / high resolution white light endoscopy is recommended 
    * 4 quadrant biopsies at 2 cm intervals in patients without dysplasia 
  * Endoscopic ablative therapy not recommended for nondysplastic Barrett esophagus ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 
  * For Barrett esophagus dysplasia, see [Barrett esophagus dysplasia](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusdysplasia.html) for surveillance guidelines

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Esophageal squamous epithelium replaced by columnar epithelium with intestinal metaplasia (goblet cells) 
  * True goblet cells: rounded shape, bluish cytoplasmic mucin, discrete cells scattered in columnar epithelium, mucin usually indents nucleus 
    * Pitfall is pseudogoblet cells: distended foveolar type cells, which can mimic goblet cells but typically cytoplasmic mucin is pinker and cells appear clustered together or in linear array 
  * Intestinal metaplasia in Barrett esophagus is typically incomplete (goblet cells interspersed between gastric foveolar type cells with cytoplasmic mucin), whereas intestinal metaplasia in gastric cardia is usually complete (intestinal type epithelium with goblet cells interspersed between nonmucinous cells with brush border) 
  * Nondysplastic reactive Barrett esophagus shows the presence of 4 lines ([Histopathology 2021;78:453](32841414)) 
    * First line: gastric foveolar type mucin droplet 
    * Second line: base of the foveolar mucin vacuole 
    * Third line: cytoplasm below the mucin vacuole 
    * Fourth line: row of nuclei 
  * Basal glands with intestinal metaplasia may appear atypical, with nuclear enlargement, hyperchromasia, pseudostratification or mitotic figures, compared to adjacent gastric type glands 
    * Distinguished from [dysplastic Barrett esophagus](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusdysplasia.html) by the lack of crowded or complex architecture, retained nuclear polarity and maturation to the surface 
  * Duplication of muscularis mucosae is common ([Am J Surg Pathol 2007;31:1719](18059229))

Barrett esophagus

Cytology description

  * Cytology has good sensitivity (82%) and specificity (88%) for identifying intestinal metaplasia with moderate interobserver agreement ([J Am Soc Cytopathol 2015;4:113](31051691)) 
  * Improvements in brushing devices may help to decrease the nondiagnostic rate ([Diagn Cytopathol 2011;39:60](21162095)) 
  * Cytology in combination with biomarkers (TTF3 immunohistochemistry or methylated DNA markers [MDM]) useful for screening and diagnosis ([Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:559](35354777)) 

Positive stains

  * H&E remains the gold standard for diagnosis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2017;41:e8](28248814)) 
Negative stains

  * Special stains typically not needed for diagnosis ([Am J Surg Pathol 2016;40:e83](27096258)) 
Electron microscopy description

  * Goblet cells: circular cells containing mucin with few microvilli; web-like structure with mucin granules ([Gastroenterology 1986;90:1932](2422089)) 
  * Gastric-like cell: convex ballooning projection of mucin with few stubby microvilli 
  * Intestinal-like cell: covered in densely packed, small, slender and uniform microvilli 
  * Variant cell: range of appearances with variable amount of mucin and microvilli

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/esophagusBarrettsHutchings02.jpeg

A 60 year old man with a clinical history of reflux symptoms undergoes upper endoscopy, which reveals salmon colored tongues extending > 1 cm in the distal esophagus. Biopsies of the distal esophagus are pictured. What is the diagnosis?  
  
  1. Barrett esophagus, negative for dysplasia 
  2. Gastric carditis with intestinal metaplasia 
  3. High grade dysplasia arising in Barrett mucosa 
  4. Squamous mucosa with changes consistent with reflux esophagitis 

Practice answer #1

**A.** Barrett esophagus, negative for dysplasia. The biopsy shows columnar mucosa with intestinal metaplasia, which is compatible with Barrett esophagus in conjunction with the endoscopic findings. No features of dysplasia are present. The glandular architecture is maintained. The basal glands with intestinal metaplasia have mildly enlarged nuclei with pseudostratification and scattered mitotic figures; however, cell polarity is maintained and there is maturation of cells at the surface. Answer D is incorrect because the biopsy shows columnar, not squamous mucosa. Answer C is incorrect because no features of dysplasia are present. Answer B is incorrect because the biopsies were obtained from the esophagus in an area of abnormal mucosa.

Practice question #2

What is the best stain to diagnose Barrett esophagus on a biopsy?   
  
  1. CDX2 immunostain 
  2. CK7 immunostain 
  3. H&E stain 
  4. Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) with Alcian blue special stain 

Practice answer #2

**C.** H&E stain is the best stain to identify goblet cells. Answer D is incorrect because goblet cells are typically readily identifiable on routine H&E stain, although PAS / Alcian blue special stain may be helpful in distinguishing true goblet cells from pseudogoblet cells in challenging cases. Answer A is incorrect because CDX2 may show reactivity in nongoblet columnar cells, which is of uncertain clinical significance and is an unnecessary added expense. Answer B is incorrect because the pattern of CK7 and CK20 immunoreactivity may help distinguish gastric intestinal metaplasia from Barrett esophagus; however, this is not sufficiently specific for clinical use.
