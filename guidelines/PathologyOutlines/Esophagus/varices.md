### Varices

[Definition / general](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusvarices.html#definitiongeneral132241) | [Clinical features](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusvarices.html#clinicalfeatures132242) | [Treatment](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusvarices.html#treatment132243) | [Gross description](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusvarices.html#grossdescription132244) | [Gross images](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusvarices.html#grossimages132245) | [Microscopic (histologic) images](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusvarices.html#microscopichistologicimages132246) | [Differential diagnosis hematemesis](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/esophagusvarices.html#differentialdiagnosishematemesis132247)

Definition / general

  * Dilated tortuous vessels, usually submucosal, that develop due to portal hypertension (prolonged or severe), which induces formation of collaterals between portal and caval systems ([Wikipedia: Esophageal Varices [Accessed 15 February 2019]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophageal_varices), [eMedicine: Portal Hypertension [Accessed 15 February 2019]](http://emedicine.medscape.com/article/182098-overview), [eMedicine: Esophageal Varices Imaging [Accessed 15 February 2019]](http://emedicine.medscape.com/article/367986-overview), [Mayo Clinic: Esophageal Varices [Accessed 15 February 2019]](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/esophageal-varices/symptoms-causes/syc-20351538)) 
  * Collaterals in lower esophagus divert flow from portal vein, through coronary veins of stomach, into esophageal veins, then azygous veins, then into vena cava 

Clinical features

  * Present in 90% of cirrhotic patients, usually due to alcoholism; acute alcohol intake may cause death, often outside the hospital ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2002;126:1197](12296758)) 
  * May rupture and cause massive hemorrhage (cause of death in 50% with advanced cirrhosis) 
  * 40% die in first episode of bleeding varices; rebleeding occurs in 50% of survivors within 1 year with 40% mortality 
  * Blood stains at death scene and unusual body positions of deceased are clues to fatal esophageal variceal hemorrhage 
  * #2 cause of bleeding varices worldwide is hepatic schistosomiasis 
  * "Downhill" varices occur in upper esophagus from other causes ([Rev Esp Enferm Dig 2006;98:359](16944996)) 

Treatment

  * Repeated sclerotherapy (injection of thrombotic agents, [Ann Surg 2006;244:764](17060770)), banding / ligation ([Arq Gastroenterol 2005;42:72](16127560)), balloon tamponade, beta blockers or octreotide to reduce portal blood flow, TIPS (**T** ransjugular **I** ntrahepatic **P** ortosystemic **S** hunt) procedure, liver transplant 

Gross description

  * Varices may protrude into lumen, mucosa may be normal or inflamed
