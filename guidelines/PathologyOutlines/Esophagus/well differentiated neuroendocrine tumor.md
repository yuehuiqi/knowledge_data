Definition / general

  * Well differentiated epithelial neoplasm of the esophagus arising from endocrine cells 

Essential features

  * Exceedingly rare epithelial tumor of the esophagus 
  * Classified according to the 2019 WHO classification as G1 (low grade), G2 (intermediate grade) and G3 (high grade) based on proliferative activity (mitoses and Ki67 index) 
  * Presents asymptomatically as an incidental finding or as an obstructive mass 
  * Distal esophagus is the most common site 

Terminology

  * Historically referred to as carcinoid tumors (atypical and typical types) ([Prz Gastroenterol 2018;13:196](30302162)) 

ICD coding

  * ICD-10: [D3A.8](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/C00-D49/D3A-D3A/D3A-/D3A.8) \- other benign neuroendocrine tumors 

Epidemiology

  * Extremely rare, < 0.01% of all esophageal cancers ([Dis Esophagus 2023;36:doad001](36688874), [Prz Gastroenterol 2018;13:196](30302162)) 
  * < 1% of all esophageal neuroendocrine neoplasms ([Cancer 1997;79:813](9024720)) 
  * Incidence has increased over the past 2 decades ([Dis Esophagus 2023;36:doad001](36688874), [Cancer 1997;79:813](9024720)) 
  * M > F ([Dis Esophagus 2023;36:doad001](36688874), [Prz Gastroenterol 2018;13:196](30302162)) 
  * Mean age at diagnosis: 60 years ([BMC Cancer 2014;14:569](25098730)) 

Sites

  * Distal esophagus ([Prz Gastroenterol 2018;13:196](30302162)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Higher density of neuroendocrine cells in distal esophagus ([BMC Cancer 2014;14:569](25098730)) 
  * Not typically associated with familial syndromes (type 1 multiple endocrine neoplasia [MEN 1], neurofibromatosis [NF] type 1), unlike neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) in other sites ([BMC Cancer 2014;14:569](25098730)) 

Etiology

  * No consensus etiology for NETs in the esophagus 

Clinical features

  * Dysphagia is most common ([J Formos Med Assoc 2021;120:508](32600867), [BMC Cancer 2014;14:569](25098730)) 
  * Hoarseness, epigastric pain, dyspepsia, weight loss, reflux symptoms ([J Formos Med Assoc 2021;120:508](32600867)) 
  * Asymptomatic, incidental finding on routine endoscopy ([Crit Rev Oncol Hematol 2019;137:92](31014519)) 

Diagnosis

  * Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) with biopsy 
  * Staging via CT, MRI and PET imaging 
  * Histological classification on biopsy results ([Proc (Bayl Univ Med Cent) 2021;35:80](34970043)) 

Radiology description

  * Barium esophagography: polypoid tumor expanding the esophageal lumen ([J Gastrointest Cancer 2017;48:299](28656561)) 

Prognostic factors

  * Low proliferative index and mitotic count is associated with a more favorable prognosis 
  * Complete resection improves prognosis ([J Gastrointest Cancer 2017;48:299](28656561)) 

Case reports

  * 43 year old woman successfully treated with endoscopic submucosal dissection ([Dig Endosc 2015;27:527](25283957)) 
  * 46 year old man with pedunculated esophageal well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2022;20:e651](33662595)) 
  * 71 year old woman with Barrett esophagus and incidental finding of low grade neuroendocrine tumor ([Proc (Bayl Univ Med Cent) 2021;35:80](34970043)) 

Treatment

  * Endoscopic resection for tumors < 1 cm in size with no lymphovascular invasion or lymph node metastases ([J Formos Med Assoc 2021;120:508](32600867)) 

Gross description

  * Elevated polypoid or nodular lesions with smooth glistening surfaces ([Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2022;20:e651](33662595), [Dig Endosc 2015;27:527](25283957)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * Round to oval nuclei with low N:C ratio, dispersed chromatin 
  * Rare or few mitotic figures 
  * Delicate, highly vascularized stroma arranged in nests and trabeculae ([Endocr Pathol 2022;33:115](35294740)) 
  * Grading is based on mitotic count and Ki67 index ([Endocr Pathol 2022;33:115](35294740)) 
    * NET, grade 1 (low grade) < 2 mitoses/2 mm2 or Ki67 < 3% 
    * NET, grade 2 (intermediate grade) 2 - 20 mitoses/2 mm2 or Ki67 3 - 20% 
    * NET, grade 3 (high grade) > 20 mitoses/2 mm2 or Ki67 > 20% 
  * No necrosis

Cytology description

  * Flat sheets or loosely cohesive groups / cords of monotonously uniform plasmacytoid cells 
  * Abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm, round to oval nuclei with small, nonprominent nucleoli and salt and pepper chromatin pattern ([Neoplasia 2017;19:991](29091800), [J Gastrointest Cancer 2017;48:299](28656561)) 
  * Rosette formations ([J Gastrointest Cancer 2017;48:299](28656561))

Sample pathology report

  * Distal esophagus, mass, biopsy: 
    * Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor, WHO grade 1 of 3 (G1) (see comment) 
    * Comment: Sections show proliferation of tumor cells with fairly uniform, round to oval nuclei, dispersed chromatin and moderate pale eosinophilic cytoplasm. The tumor cells are diffusely highlighted by pankeratin and synaptophysin stains. There are < 2 mitoses per 10 high power fields and the proliferative index assessed by Ki67 immunohistochemistry is 1.5%. The morphologic features and immunophenotype are those of a well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor, WHO grade 1 of 3 in this biopsy sample. 

Differential diagnosis

Practice question #1

https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/esophaguscarcinoidMontgomery01.jpg https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/esophaguscarcinoidMontgomery02.jpg

A 66 year old man presents with dysphagia and weight loss and upper endoscopy reveals a submucosal mass in the distal esophagus. Histological examination of a biopsy sample shows uniformly round to oval nuclei, fine granular chromatin, low mitotic activity and a Ki67 index of < 3%. There is no intestinal metaplasia in the background. Immunohistochemical stains are positive for synaptophysin and chromogranin A. What is the most likely diagnosis?   
  
  1. Adenocarcinoma 
  2. Neuroendocrine carcinoma 
  3. Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor, WHO grade 1 of 3 
  4. Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor, WHO grade 2 of 3 

Practice answer #1

**C**. Well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor, WHO grade 1 of 3. Neuroendocrine tumor is supported via histology showing uniform round to oval nuclei and granular (salt and pepper) chromatin, with positive staining for synaptophysin and chromogranin A. While the low Ki67 index and mitoses confirms a well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor grade 1 of 3. Answer A is incorrect because adenocarcinoma of the esophagus typically arises in the background of Barrett mucosa and is characterized by glandular architecture and mucin production. Answer B is incorrect because neuroendocrine carcinoma would have brisk mitotic activity and typically a proliferative index > 50%. Answer D is incorrect because the Ki67 is < 3% and grade 2 tumors have a Ki67 that is 3 - 20%.

Practice question #2

Which of the following features is utilized in determining the grade of a well differentiated neuroendocrine tumor in the esophagus?   
  
  1. Ki67 index 
  2. Nested architecture 
  3. Positive staining for synaptophysin and chromogranin A 
  4. Tumor size 

Practice answer #2

**A**. Ki67 index. The 2019 WHO grading scheme for neuroendocrine tumors is determined via mitotic count and Ki67 index. Answer B is incorrect because architecture is not used to grade neuroendocrine tumors. Answer C is incorrect because positive staining for synaptophysin and chromogranin A are important for confirming neuroendocrine differentiation but do not determine grade. Answer D is incorrect because tumor size is relevant to stage but not related to mitotic count or Ki67 used in determining grade.
