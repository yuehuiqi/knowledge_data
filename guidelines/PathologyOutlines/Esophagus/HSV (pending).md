Definition / general

  * Sensitive and specific method for diagnosing herpes simplex virus (HSV) infections ([Diagn Pathol 2022;17:17](35094696)) 
  * Positive in tissues infected with herpes simplex virus 1 and 2 (HSV1 and HSV2), which belong to the ubiquitous _Herpesviridae_ family of viruses ([Am J Dermatopathol 2015;37:1](25171431)) 

Essential features

  * Sensitive and specific method for diagnosing HSV infections ([Diagn Pathol 2022;17:17](35094696)) 
  * Positive in tissues infected with herpes simplex virus 1 and 2 ([Am J Dermatopathol 2015;37:1](25171431)) 
  * Exact sensitivity of IHC is hard to determine since few concurrent specimens have tissues sent for viral culture, the gold standard for diagnosis ([Am J Clin Pathol 2017;147:96](28007703)) 
  * Cells exhibit both nuclear and cytoplasmic staining ([Am J Dermatopathol 2015;37:1](25171431)) 

Pathophysiology

  * Enveloped, double stranded linear DNA virus with an icosahedral capsid and protein tegument ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2011;135:1357](21970493), [Cancers (Basel) 2023;15:3692](37509353)) 
  * Neurocutaneous tropism; replicates and spreads rapidly, causing extensive cell lysis and can establish latent infection primarily in neurosensory ganglia ([Diagn Pathol 2022;17:17](35094696)) 
  * Rely on the host cell nucleus for DNA replication and transcription via RNA ([Arch Pathol Lab Med 2011;135:1357](21970493)) 
  * 2 mechanisms are involved in the formation of intraepidermal vesicles: the ballooning of keratinocytes and the reticular degeneration of the epidermis ([Am J Dermatopathol 2015;37:1](25171431)) 

Clinical features

  * HSV1 and HSV2 are very common and can lead to illness through primary infection or reactivation of latent virus ([Am J Clin Pathol 2017;147:96](28007703)) 
  * HSV H&E viral cytopathic effects are characterized by multinucleated cells, prominent glassy viral intranuclear inclusions and chromatin margination ([Am J Clin Pathol 2017;147:96](28007703), [Am J Dermatopathol 2015;37:1](25171431), [Arch Pathol Lab Med 2011;135:1357](21970493)) 
  * Is a neurotropic virus and primarily infects neurons in their latent forms ([Infect Dis Clin North Am 2010;24:373](20466275)) 
  * Reactivation is common among transplant recipients ([Infect Dis Clin North Am 2010;24:373](20466275)) 

Interpretation

  * Cells exhibit both nuclear and cytoplasmic staining ([Am J Dermatopathol 2015;37:1](25171431)) 
  * Most intense staining is at the edge of the nuclei ([Am J Dermatopathol 2015;37:1](25171431)) 

Uses by pathologists

  * To exclude herpes simplex virus infection 
  * Most ordered on gastrointestinal, dermatologic, oral / head & neck, gynecological, urologic, pulmonary and central nervous system (CNS) specimens ([Am J Clin Pathol 2017;147:96](28007703), [Cancers (Basel) 2023;15:3692](37509353)) 
  * IHC detects more cases with virally infected cells than H&E alone; however, the clinical significance in these cases is unclear ([Am J Clin Pathol 2017;147:96](28007703)) 

Microscopic (histologic) description

  * H&E correlation will reveal cells with HSV cytopathic changes, including marginated chromatin, multinucleated cells, nuclear molding and possibly eosinophilic intranuclear (Cowdry A) inclusions ([Am J Dermatopathol 2014;36:609](25051039))

Positive staining - disease

  * Tissues infected with HSV, including gastrointestinal (e.g., herpes esophagitis, hepatitis), oral / head & neck (e.g., herpes labialis), gynecological / urological (e.g., herpes genitalis), dermatologic (e.g., herpes folliculitis), pulmonary (e.g., herpes pneumonia) and CNS specimens (e.g., herpes encephalitis) ([Am J Clin Pathol 2017;147:96](28007703), [Cancers (Basel) 2023;15:3692](37509353)) 
  * Positivity rates will vary based on ordering practices ([Am J Clin Pathol 2017;147:96](28007703)) 
  * Sensitivity of IHC is hard to determine since few concurrent specimens have tissues sent for viral culture, the gold standard for diagnosis ([Am J Clin Pathol 2017;147:96](28007703)) 

Negative staining

  * Any tissue not infected by HSV

Practice question #1

[ /stainsHSVBaniak04.jpg)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/imgau/stainsHSVBaniak04.jpg)

Which of the following is true given the above image?   
  
  1. Due to cytoplasmic staining, the protocol needs to be adjusted 
  2. Infection is caused by HSV1 
  3. Other viruses may be cross reacting with the stain 
  4. Staining pattern is diagnostic of HSV pneumonia 

Practice answer #1

**D**. Staining pattern is diagnostic of HSV pneumonia. Diffuse positive staining for HSV is indicative of HSV infection. Answer B is incorrect because HSV IHC detects both HSV1 and HSV2. Answer C is incorrect because HSV IHC is specific. Answer A is incorrect because HSV IHC stains are both nuclear and cytoplasmic.
